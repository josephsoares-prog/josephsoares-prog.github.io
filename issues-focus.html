Issues Focus

A standing watch on the world's live decision-points — where power, capital, and institutions are under the most strain right now. Not news for its own sake: the through-line each situation shares with the choices leaders face in any boardroom or cabinet room under pressure.

Last updated: August 3, 2026 · Reviewed and improved in place, not re-published as a new post

The two fronts
Active war

Iran

The 2026 Iran war forced a leadership transition in real time. Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was killed in a US-Israeli strike in late February; the Assembly of Experts named his son, Mojtaba Khamenei, as successor on March 8 — a succession decided inside an active war rather than through the normal process. Diplomatically, the picture has kept reversing within days of itself: on August 2, Tehran publicly denied a claim that talks with Washington would resume, even after planned US strikes were paused. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei has said the Strait of Hormuz will not simply return to its pre-war status — a marker of how Tehran is holding leverage while its institutions absorb the shock at the top.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Feb 28 · Al Jazeera, Mar 8 · Washington Post, Aug 2 · IRNA via GlobalSecurity, Aug 2
Attritional war

Russia–Ukraine

Now past its fourth year, the war has settled into a pattern of costly Russian territorial advances against a backdrop of shifting U.S. policy. The International Crisis Group's 2026 assessment describes Russian forces continuing to gain ground at heavy cost, while the current U.S. administration's approach is reshaping both the level of support flowing to Kyiv and the diplomatic pressure applied to both capitals. Watch for how far Washington is willing to condition further support on a negotiated settlement.

Sources: International Crisis Group, "Ten Conflicts to Watch 2026" · GlobalSecurity, Aug 1
Also watching

Israel–Palestine

A fragile Gaza ceasefire is holding under strain, while West Bank annexation questions remain a flashpoint for the Israeli government's coalition politics.

Venezuela

A U.S. military buildup and blockade posture around Venezuela has raised the prospect of direct confrontation tied to Nicolás Maduro's hold on power.

Sudan

The war between the army and the paramilitary RSF continues to grind toward a de facto partition, alongside one of the world's largest humanitarian emergencies.

Sahel — Mali & Burkina Faso

Military-led governments in Bamako and Ouagadougou remain unable to subdue jihadist insurgencies; a partial blockade of Mali's capital points to a deepening regional crisis.

Syria

Syria's post-Assad leadership is working to secure a sectarian peace and rebuild the economy while managing Turkish and Israeli intervention risk.

Myanmar

The military junta continues to consolidate control despite an ongoing civil war, with Beijing's backing shaping the odds for both opposition forces and the Rohingya.

Afghanistan–Pakistan

An October ceasefire between Kabul and Islamabad is holding, but cross-border militant violence keeps the bilateral relationship under strain.

Ethiopia–Eritrea

Rising tension between the two neighbours over internal interference and seaport access carries risk of igniting a wider regional conflict.

Watchlist source: International Crisis Group, "Ten Conflicts to Watch 2026"
