01
HORMUZ

Iran and Oman reach an understanding on the Hormuz shipping route as both sides finalise joint-management arrangements

The Hindu · thehindu.com ↗

Iran and Oman have agreed on the coordinates of a shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz and are finalising arrangements to jointly manage the passage, Tehran’s foreign ministry said Wednesday — a concrete step beyond the “near deal” language of recent weeks. Trump has said an announcement could come this week, and Iran separately says it is ready to return to its commitments under the June ceasefire. A fifth of global oil transits Hormuz; a confirmed reopening caps the risk premium priced into energy markets since February, while any slippage keeps the paused US bombing option live.

02
LEBANON

Two Israeli soldiers are killed in south Lebanon — the first Israeli deaths since June’s truce — triggering the heaviest Israeli bombardment of Lebanon since the ceasefire

TimesLIVE · timeslive.co.za ↗

Two Israeli soldiers were killed and others wounded when an improvised explosive device detonated inside a booby-trapped building in the southern village of Majdal Zoun — the first Israeli combat deaths since the June truce with Hezbollah. Israel responded with its heaviest bombardment of Lebanon since the ceasefire, striking Tyre district, Mansouri and Burj al-Shemali and issuing evacuation warnings; a strike on a Tibnin cemetery prayer hall killed one person and wounded twelve. A truce that has held for seven weeks without either side disarming is now being tested by exactly the kind of incident that ends them.

03
RUSSIA

Trump rejects Zelensky’s request for more Patriot interceptors, citing depleted US stockpiles and Middle East needs, days after Ukraine’s defences missed all 28 missiles in a barrage that killed 17

United24 Media · united24media.com ↗

Trump has rejected Zelensky’s personal request for several hundred additional Patriot interceptors, telling him American stocks are limited and are needed to protect US facilities in the Middle East and Gulf amid the Iran standoff. The rejection lands days after Ukrainian air defences failed to intercept a single one of the 28 missiles Russia fired in the barrage that killed 17 near Kyiv. With Hormuz and Ukraine now drawing on the same finite interceptor pool, expect continued strikes on Ukrainian cities and logistics through the fall.

04
MARKETS

Asian shares slide as Kospi drops 4.6% on a chip-sector selloff, with Hormuz uncertainty and Friday’s US jobs report both weighing on sentiment

U.S. News (AP) · usnews.com ↗

South Korea’s Kospi dropped more than 4.6% led by chipmaker SK Hynix, with Nikkei (-1.2%) and Hang Seng (-1.8%) also lower, as analysts describe a combination of profit-taking ahead of Friday’s US non-farm payrolls report and lingering uncertainty over the direction of the Iran standoff despite Hormuz reopening hopes. The Shanghai Composite was roughly flat and Australia’s ASX 200 gained. Treat this as positioning ahead of two binary catalysts — the jobs number and a Hormuz announcement — rather than a fundamental repricing of AI-chip names.

05
SHIPPING

Houthis strike an eighth Saudi oil tanker since their blockade began, pushing Gulf shipping traffic down even as Hormuz talks progress

Daily Sabah · dailysabah.com ↗

Yemen’s Houthis say they struck the Saudi oil tanker Wafa off Yanbu with ballistic missiles — the eighth Saudi tanker targeted since their Red Sea blockade began July 22 — and have vowed to escalate against tankers rerouted to the northern Red Sea. Gulf shipping traffic has fallen in response. Even as diplomats close in on a Hormuz reopening, Saudi Arabia’s only other maritime export route is under active attack — the chokepoint risk operators should be pricing in is regional, not single-strait.

NORTH KOREA

A North Korean missile unit begins deploying to Russia for the Ukraine war as the Kremlin seeks 30,000 more North Korean troops

The Spokesman-Review · spokesman.com ↗

A North Korean missile unit equipped with up to 120 ballistic missiles and six launchers has begun deploying to western Russia, Ukrainian military intelligence says, with preparations underway in Russia’s Voronezh region to receive roughly 30,000 additional North Korean troops the Kremlin has requested. Pyongyang already has close to 11,000 troops in Kursk and has supplied millions of artillery shells and hundreds of ballistic missiles since 2023. The scale of this expansion turns North Korea from an auxiliary supplier into a standing combatant partner in the war.

Defense News · defensenews.com ↗

It lands alongside a wave of smaller, incremental defence awards (NRO satellite-imagery contracts, a $615 million Space Force award for airborne-target tracking) that show the Pentagon spreading bets across exquisite and distributed systems simultaneously.

EUobserver · euobserver.com ↗

The financing track is running in parallel to — and increasingly substituting for — the interceptor-supply track that Washington is now rationing; European capitals should expect to carry a larger share of Ukraine’s air-defence and reconstruction costs as US hardware gets pulled toward the Gulf.

The Moscow Times · themoscowtimes.com ↗

Ukrainian drones hit oil refineries in Russia’s Yaroslavl region and Bashkortostan overnight, part of a deep-strike campaign that has separately started a fire at Russia’s main Roscosmos research centre near Moscow. The refinery campaign is now sustained enough that Moscow’s own mayor is publicly resisting calls to put the civilian economy on a war footing — a sign the strikes are reaching the political threshold, not just the industrial one.

Commentary Hooks

Two Chokepoints, One Story

Hormuz is close to a deal while the Houthis escalate against Saudi tankers in the Red Sea — diplomats are solving one chokepoint while a second one under-covered by wire headlines gets worse. The real story isn’t which strait reopens; it’s that Gulf shipping risk has become a permanent operating cost, not a single-crisis problem.

The Interceptor Ceiling

Trump’s refusal to resupply Zelensky’s Patriots, explicitly citing Middle East demand, is the clearest evidence yet that US air-defence stockpiles are a shared, rationed resource across two simultaneous theatres. Any ally or partner relying on American interceptors should be asking today how their request gets prioritised against the Gulf.

Truce Debt Comes Due

Two Israeli deaths breaking a seven-week Lebanon lull, arriving the same week Gaza remains ‘unsafe for civilians’ and Ukraine posts its deadliest month for civilians yet, show how fragile ceasefires get when neither side disarms and diplomatic bandwidth is elsewhere. Read all three together: this is what a world running multiple unresolved ceasefires at once actually looks like.
