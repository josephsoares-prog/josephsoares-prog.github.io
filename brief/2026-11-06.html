01
IRAN WAR

Three More Tankers Hit in Hormuz as Reuters Sources Say Trump Leans Toward Resuming Iran Strikes

MarineLink · marinelink.com ↗

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said two tankers struck mines and a third caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, the fifth vessel incident since Sunday — a pace matching October’s record month for the nine-month war. Two Reuters sources say Washington is still leaning toward resuming strikes rather than settling, even as Vice President Vance blamed the war for the GOP’s midterm losses and oil, down more than $20 a barrel from last week on deal hopes, ticked back up. For anyone hedging Gulf shipping, insurance or Q1 fuel costs, last week’s price relief looks premature against an attack rate that isn’t actually slowing.

02
MARKETS

Dow Sheds 1,833 Points and Nasdaq Enters Correction as AI Data-Center Fears Collide With Midterm Regulation Risk

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

The Dow fell 3.44% to 51,523 and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.52% into correction territory Thursday, with the Philadelphia semiconductor index down 12.25% — its worst session since the pandemic — as hedge funds rushed to lock in year-end AI gains and priced in new data-center regulatory risk after Tuesday’s Democratic sweep. SpaceX and Tesla fell alongside the chip names on the same regulatory-hurdle logic, while Vanda Research clocked the heaviest single-day net retail selling since March 2020. For any board or allocator still running “AI exposure” as one trade, Thursday is the clearest evidence yet that positioning, not fundamentals, is now driving the swings — and that the next leg depends on whether funds are done de-risking into year-end.

03
CANADA BUDGET

Carney’s Second Budget Pencils In $289 Billion in Deficits Over Five Years, Bets Big on Pipelines and LNG

EnergyNow · energynow.ca ↗

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne tabled a budget Thursday projecting $62.3 billion in red ink for 2026-27, falling to $52.9 billion by 2030-31 for a $288.7 billion five-year total — well under the Parliamentary Budget Officer’s pre-budget $333.3 billion estimate, aided by a budget assumption of $85 WTI versus $73 in the spring forecast. The government paired the deficit path with a proposed 1,250-km Pacific Link pipeline to the B.C. coast, a doubling of LNG Canada’s Kitimat capacity, and a $36 billion Productivity Mega Deduction meant to cut the marginal effective tax rate on new investment from roughly 13% to 6.4%. For executives weighing Canadian capital commitments, the real test is whether the one-year regulatory-review promise for major projects survives contact with 28 nation-building files worth over $194 billion — not the deficit number itself.

UKRAINE

Death Toll From Russian Strike on Ukrainian Aid Hub Reaches 40 as UN Says Bombing ‘Likely’ Broke International Law

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

President Zelenskyy raised the toll from Monday’s Russian strike on a humanitarian aid hub in Komyshuvakha to 40 dead, with more than 30 people still feared under the rubble of what Al Jazeera calls one of the deadliest single strikes on civilians in the nearly five-year war; the UN’s human rights mission in Ukraine said Tuesday the bombing “likely violated international law.” Kyiv is now preparing for US-brokered talks with Moscow in Abu Dhabi next week even as Zelenskyy says the strike shows Russia “does not want de-escalation.” For anyone pricing reconstruction or European energy exposure to a settlement, the gap between this week’s atrocity and next week’s negotiating table is the risk to watch — the two are moving on separate tracks.

CRYPTO

Bitcoin Falls Below $76,000 as Equity Rout Spills Into Crypto

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Bitcoin fell more than 5% to briefly dip below $76,000 Thursday, tracking the broader flight from risk assets as the Nasdaq entered correction territory on the same AI-regulation fears rattling equities. The move erases the modest post-midterm bounce crypto had been running on and confirms that, for now, bitcoin is trading as a risk-on proxy for the AI trade rather than the uncorrelated hedge its bulls pitch. For treasurers and allocators holding bitcoin as a diversifier, Thursday is a reminder to check that correlation assumption before year-end rebalancing, not after.

TAIWAN

Taiwan Tracks 22 Chinese Aircraft and 12 Ships in 24 Hours as Beijing Ramps Up Pressure Before Local Elections

Taiwan News · taiwannews.com.tw ↗

Taiwan’s defence ministry tracked 22 Chinese military aircraft and 12 naval and coast guard vessels in the 24 hours to Wednesday, with 14 of the aircraft sorties crossing the Taiwan Strait median line — bringing the month’s running total to 90 aircraft and 37 ship incursions with three weeks still to go before Taiwan’s November 28 local elections. Taipei responded by deploying its own aircraft, ships and coastal missile systems, while prosecutors separately accused a retired major general of taking Chinese funding to influence local races. For anyone with Taiwan-adjacent supply chain or semiconductor exposure, the gray-zone pressure campaign is scaling with the electoral calendar, not independent of it — treat the next three weeks as the window to watch, not a one-off spike.

M&A

Tata Motors Completes €3.91 Billion Takeover of Iveco, Creating a 590,000-Vehicle Global Commercial Rival

Electrive · electrive.com ↗

Tata Motors now holds roughly 89.42% of Iveco after raising its offer to €14.40 a share and completing the acquisition this week, with a second acceptance window running through November 6 that could lift the stake further; the combined group projects annual sales above 590,000 vehicles and roughly €21 billion in revenue, with Europe supplying 46% and India 32%. Tata committed to keeping Iveco’s headquarters in Turin and preserving its factories and jobs for two years, giving the Agnelli family’s Exor an exit after its 27.1% stake proved no match for Tata’s all-cash offer. For commercial-vehicle buyers and suppliers, the practical question now is how fast Tata integrates an Iveco that generates nearly three-quarters of its revenue in a market — Europe — where Tata previously had no manufacturing footprint at all.

AI & M&A

Meta Acquires AI Coding Startup Conductor to Bolster Its Agentic Software Push

TechCrunch · techcrunch.com ↗

Meta confirmed it has acquired Conductor, a startup whose platform lets developers run multiple AI coding agents from labs like Anthropic and OpenAI in parallel workspaces, though neither side disclosed terms; Conductor’s cloud service shuts down November 20, with existing users’ data then deleted. Founder Charlie Holtz is joining Meta’s Superintelligence Labs under Alexandr Wang and Nat Friedman, the same unit behind Muse Code, Meta’s own coding agent that has already topped 10 million users. For enterprise software buyers, Meta’s pattern — Moltbook in March, now Conductor — shows it building out agentic AI tooling by acquisition rather than internal development alone, which means the roadmap for any Conductor-dependent workflow needs a new plan before November 20.

09
AI SAFETY

Anthropic Open-Sources FlashProve, a Cryptographic System to Prove Which AI Model Produced an Answer

TechCrunch · techcrunch.com ↗

Anthropic published research on FlashProve, an open-source system that generates a cryptographic proof — a published model “fingerprint” that doesn’t expose the underlying weights — letting anyone verify that an AI output actually came from the model it claims to, even against a lab secretly swapping in a cheaper model. The design is explicitly aimed at third-party verification claims too, such as a vendor asserting a frontier model scanned its code for vulnerabilities, with no deployment plans yet at Anthropic or any other lab. For enterprises buying AI-verification or compliance claims from vendors, FlashProve is the first credible technical answer to “how do we actually check that” — worth tracking as a due-diligence standard before it becomes one.

10
SUDAN

Sudan Accuses Ethiopia of Hosting RSF Drone Bases as Attacks Hit a Record 97 Drones in October

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Sudan’s government told the UN Security Council that 70 drone attacks using 187 one-way drones were launched from Ethiopian territory between February and October, with October alone accounting for 28 attacks and 97 drones — a toll Khartoum says has killed 176 soldiers and 484 civilians since mid-February and knocked out more than 90% of power stations in Sudanese cities. Addis Ababa denies the allegation and accuses Khartoum of backing Tigrayan rebels, while UN Secretary-General Guterres said he was “appalled” by the civilian toll, including 26 women and children killed in a single October 3 strike near the border. For anyone tracking Horn of Africa stability or Nile-basin water politics, the real escalation risk is a formal Sudan-Ethiopia rupture layered onto an already three-year civil war — a scenario officials on both sides say they are still trying to avoid.
