01
MARKETS

Wall Street braces for the July jobs report, the day's one scheduled catalyst, with hiring seen rebounding to about 120,000 but unemployment ticking up to 4.3%

Continuum Economics · continuumeconomics.com ↗

The July non-farm payrolls report lands at 8:30 a.m. ET, and economists expect roughly 120,000 jobs added — a sharp rebound from June's 57,000 — but with unemployment edging up to 4.3% from 4.2% and wage growth holding near 3.5% year-over-year. It is the last major labour reading before the Fed's September meeting, and with markets already leaning toward a cut, the risk is asymmetric: a hot print that pushes the Fed back to hold would hit rate-sensitive and AI-chip names hardest. Treat today as a binary and size positions for the surprise, not the consensus.

02
HORMUZ

Trump says a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz 'has not yet been reached' but could come 'soon,' as the White House seeks over $30 billion in supplemental funding for the Iran conflict

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

President Trump walked back recent optimism, saying a deal to reopen Hormuz 'has not yet been reached' though it could come 'soon,' and claimed the US holds an 'unlimited supply' of missiles even as the White House asks Congress for more than $30 billion in supplemental funding for the Iran conflict. The gap between the 'unlimited supply' rhetoric and a $30 billion funding request is the tell: the standoff is being budgeted as a prolonged operation, not a deal about to close. Anyone pricing energy or Gulf-shipping risk on an imminent reopening should widen the timeline.

03
GULF SECURITY

Saudi Arabia warns of 'imminent coordinated attacks from the north and south' by Iraqi militias and Houthis, targeting energy infrastructure, ports and airports

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

Senior Saudi officials warned of imminent coordinated attacks from the north and south — Iraqi militias and Houthis operating under Iranian supervision — against civilian and economic sites including energy infrastructure, ports and airports. Coming while Hormuz talks are stalled, it signals Tehran is preparing pressure points well beyond the strait itself. Operators with Gulf exposure should read the threat surface as the whole peninsula, not a single chokepoint, and stress-test continuity plans for infrastructure strikes rather than shipping delays alone.

04
ENERGY

Oil edges higher on Red Sea shipping risk, with WTI at $75.81 and Brent at $80.23 as tanker attacks keep a war premium in the price

TheStreet · thestreet.com ↗

West Texas Intermediate rose 0.78% to $75.81 a barrel and Brent gained 0.98% to $80.23, driven by ongoing Red Sea shipping concerns rather than any single fresh shock. The market is holding a persistent risk premium even as diplomats talk down the odds of a Hormuz closure — a sign traders no longer expect the Gulf risk to resolve on one headline. For budgeting and hedging, the operative assumption is a structurally elevated floor under crude through the standoff, not a snap-back to pre-crisis levels.

05
YEMEN

Houthi missile and drone strikes on Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces kill at least 58 in the worst fighting in three years

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

Houthi forces launched ballistic-missile and drone attacks on Saudi-backed Yemeni government troops, killing at least 58 people in what officials called the worst fighting in three years; a Houthi spokesman said the strike answered an alleged military buildup. The internal Yemen front reopening while the Houthis simultaneously blockade Red Sea shipping stretches the group across two theatres at once. The read-through for the region: the Houthis are escalating, not consolidating, and their appetite for Red Sea disruption is unlikely to ease.

06
LEBANON

Lebanon truce talks stall in Rome as Israel refuses further withdrawals and the two sides fail to agree who verifies Hezbollah's disarmament

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

After three days of US-mediated talks in Rome, Israel and Lebanon failed to agree which countries would sit on the committee verifying Hezbollah's disarmament, and Israel refused further troop withdrawals pending a mechanism. The dispute is no longer about whether to disarm but about who is trusted to confirm it — the kind of procedural deadlock that quietly hardens into a frozen line. With Israeli combat deaths already testing the truce this week, the verification gap is where it most plausibly breaks.

CANADA

Carney says 'all strategic sectors' are on the table in US talks as a fresh tariff threat looms over Canada

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney said 'all strategic sectors' — steel, aluminium, autos, lumber and energy among them — are being discussed with Washington as a new US tariff threat looms, after Trump's 35% duty took force on non-CUSMA goods on August 1. With roughly 90% of Canadian exports still shielded by CUSMA compliance, the negotiation is now about protecting the exposed remainder in the highest-value sectors. For Canadian exporters, the practical task is confirming rules-of-origin coverage line by line before the next deadline resets the exposure.

TRADE

Trump's sweeping new tariffs on some 60 trade partners take hold as the prior round of global duties expires

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

A new tariff schedule covering roughly 60 US trade partners has taken hold as the previous round of country-by-country duties expired, cementing the highest average US tariff wall in generations as a standing feature of the trade environment rather than a passing negotiating threat. For supply chains, the planning assumption should flip: tariffs are now the baseline cost structure to design around, not a temporary disruption to wait out. The competitive edge goes to firms that have already re-mapped sourcing to the post-tariff map.

FINANCE

TheStreet · thestreet.com ↗

JPMorgan chief executive Jamie Dimon warned that margin debt is 'the highest it has ever been,' pointing to leverage that has quietly built across the system while equity indexes trade at records. Coming from the head of the largest US bank rather than a regulator, the warning is less a forecast than a positioning signal: the people closest to the plumbing are flagging fragility. For allocators, it argues for stress-testing exposure to a fast, leverage-driven unwind — the kind that turns an ordinary selloff into a disorderly one.

10
LABOR

US layoff announcements fall to a two-year low of 33,429 in July even as AI is blamed for a third of the cuts

Challenger, Gray & Christmas · challengergray.com ↗

US employers announced 33,429 job cuts in July — down 27% from June and 46% below a year ago, the lowest monthly total in two years — while announced hiring rose to 16,095. Yet AI was cited for 10,970 of the cuts, a third of the total and the fifth straight month it led the reasons given. The signal underneath the soft headline: the labour market is cooling gently in aggregate but restructuring hard around automation. Workforce planning that reads only the falling top-line will miss where the displacement is actually concentrated.

The divergence — cyclicals and value holding up while AI-chip names wobble — is the market quietly hedging its own AI concentration. For anyone overweight the megacap-tech complex, the rotation under the surface matters more than the flat index headline.

Commentary Hooks

Jobs Friday Meets a War Premium

Today stacks two very different risks on the same tape: a scheduled data binary (the July payrolls) and an open-ended geopolitical one (oil holding a war premium while Hormuz talks stall). The interesting trade isn't either alone — it's that a soft jobs number and a Gulf flare-up could hit rate-cut hopes and energy costs at once, the exact combination that squeezes both sides of a balanced book.

'Unlimited Supply,' $30 Billion Bill

Trump says the US has an 'unlimited supply' of missiles and then asks Congress for $30 billion more for the Iran conflict. Read the budget request, not the adjective: governments don't ask for supplemental war funding for a standoff they expect to end this week. The tell for how long the Gulf risk lasts is in the appropriations, and it points to months, not days.

The Soft-Landing Headline Hides the Restructuring

July layoffs fell to a two-year low — the reassuring headline — while a third of the cuts that did happen were blamed on AI, the fifth straight month automation led the reasons. The aggregate is cooling gently; the composition is changing hard. Anyone reading only the top line will miss that the labour market's softness and its automation-driven reshaping are two different stories running at once.
