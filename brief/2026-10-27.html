01
IRAN

Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish Officials Meet Iran’s President in Tehran as Washington Weighs New Strikes

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Saudi Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman, Pakistani army chief Gen. Asim Munir and Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin met President Masoud Pezeshkian in Tehran on October 26 — the first high-level contact between Iran and the three Mecca Joint Defence Agreement signatories — as Washington weighs new strikes once it judges the window right, and the State Department has already pulled non-emergency staff from Saudi Arabia. Munir reportedly floated a proposal that could let Qatar and the Mecca pact states act as guarantors between Washington and Tehran, but analysts quoted by Al Jazeera call it an “11th-hour” bid unlikely to resolve the core US-Iran standoff even if it buys Riyadh a reprieve from Houthi strikes. Executives with Gulf exposure should read this as a signal that the region is managing, not ending, an active war — plan contingencies around renewed strikes, not around a settlement.

02
OIL

Brent Hits Six-Month High Above $116 as Houthi Strikes Force Aramco to Evacuate Its HQ

The National · thenationalnews.com ↗

Brent settled up 3.14% at $116.72 a barrel on October 23 — briefly topping $117, a six-month high, and up 6.24% for the week — after Houthi strikes claimed to hit Saudi Arabia’s Defence Ministry HQ, King Fahd airport in Dammam, Abha airport and an Aramco loading terminal at Yanbu, killing two people and forcing Aramco to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters for the first time since the Iran war began. WTI rose 3.82% to $103.27. Energy buyers, refiners and anyone pricing Gulf-supply risk should treat the Dhahran evacuation — not just the price move — as the real escalation marker: Aramco moving its own staff out signals a materially higher assessment of direct-hit risk than markets have priced into crude so far.

03
ISRAEL

Israelis Vote Today in a Knesset Election That Could End Netanyahu’s Rule

Gulf News · gulfnews.com ↗

About 7.3 million Israelis are voting today for a new 120-seat Knesset, with a 10-poll average showing Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud tied with Gadi Eisenkot’s new Yashar party at 22 seats apiece and Netanyahu’s bloc polling 49-55 seats — short of the 61 needed to govern — against an opposition bloc at 54-56. Official results aren’t due until November 4 and coalition talks could run weeks or months; if no government forms, Netanyahu — on trial for corruption and facing an ICC warrant — would keep full caretaker authority, including on war decisions against Iran and its proxies. Anyone with Israel-policy or defense-sector exposure should treat tonight’s exit polls as directional at best and plan for a prolonged coalition vacuum, not a clean handover.

04
ANTHROPIC

Anthropic Files for IPO, Igniting Wall Street’s Best Day in a Month

The National · thenationalnews.com ↗

Anthropic’s IPO filing — disclosing steep losses alongside strong revenue growth — sent the S&P 500 and Nasdaq toward their biggest one-day gain in over a month on October 26, with the Philadelphia semiconductor index up more than 3% and CoreWeave and Nebius reversing early losses. The filing lands in what one analyst called “a challenging environment, to say the least,” given uncertain IPO timing and the same debt-fueled AI-infrastructure questions that have hit neocloud stocks this month. Portfolio managers and boards weighing AI-sector exposure should read the rally as a reminder that Anthropic’s actual listing terms — not the filing itself — will be the real test of whether public markets back frontier-lab economics at scale.

05
AI INFRASTRUCTURE

Meta Launches $95 Billion Stock Offering With Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom as Cornerstone Investors

The Irish Times · irishtimes.com ↗

Meta began raising up to $95 billion on October 26 — $35 billion through underwritten public offerings now, $60 billion more via an at-the-market programme — with its own chip suppliers Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom signed on as cornerstone investors, a week after Meta projected up to $145 billion in 2026 capital spending and warned it will need “hundreds of billions of dollars” more over several years. Meta shares fell 7% on the spending disclosure before the offering news. Boards and investors tracking AI capex should note that chipmakers funding their own largest customer’s equity raise blurs the line between vendor financing and genuine demand — a structure worth scrutinizing with the same skepticism applied to the neocloud debt story this month.

06
FEDERAL RESERVE

Fed Expected to Hold Rates Wednesday as Inflation Tops Target for a Fifth Year, Days Before Midterms

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

The Fed is expected to hold its target range at 3.75%-4% on October 28 even as markets price at least one more hike before year-end, with PCE inflation running at a three-year high of 3.9% — missing the 2% target for five straight years — which economists link to tariffs and the Iran war’s effect on energy prices. The decision lands four days before November 3 midterms in which Democrats are contesting control of Congress; new Chair Kevin Warsh, confirmed after the criminal probe into predecessor Jerome Powell was dropped, has so far avoided the public criticism Trump directed at Powell. Rate-sensitive investors and corporate treasurers should watch whether Warsh signals a December hike in his press conference — that, not Wednesday’s hold itself, is the actionable data point.

07
ENERGY M&A

EQT to Buy Range Resources in $50 Billion Deal, Creating America’s Largest Gas Producer

Bloomberg · bloomberg.com ↗

EQT said on October 26 it will acquire Range Resources in an all-stock deal valued at more than $50 billion, creating what the companies call the largest vertically integrated natural gas producer in the United States, as AI-driven power demand reshapes dealmaking across the sector. The combined company would command Appalachian gas reserves at a scale positioned to feed both LNG export terminals and the data-center buildout now straining US power grids. Energy investors and utility planners should treat this as confirmation that gas, not just renewables or nuclear, is consolidating around AI-era power demand — expect further roll-up activity among mid-cap Appalachian producers.

08
BANK OF CANADA

Bank of Canada Set to Hike to 2.50% Wednesday as Inflation Hits 3.1%, Reuters Poll Shows

Reuters · kitco.com ↗

Nineteen of 34 economists in an October 21-23 Reuters poll now expect the Bank of Canada to hike its key rate a quarter-point to 2.50% on October 28 — its first move in a year — reversing a prior poll where 80% saw a hold through year-end, after September inflation came in at 3.1%, above the 1-3% target range, and oil hit $117 a barrel. Sixteen of 29 respondents expect one more hike by end-March 2027, taking the rate to 2.75%, with growth forecast at just 1.0% for 2026. Canadian borrowers, mortgage holders and bond investors should treat this as a genuine regime change from the hold-and-cut consensus of the past year — reprice floating-rate exposure now rather than waiting for Wednesday’s announcement.

09
AI CHIPS

Tencent, Alibaba and ByteDance Order $35 Billion in Nvidia Chips Nvidia May Not Be Able to Deliver

Reuters · news.bloomberglaw.com ↗

Tencent, Alibaba and ByteDance have ordered more than 3 million of Nvidia’s new export-compliant RTX Pro 5500 chips — worth over $35 billion at list price — but Nvidia has told customers and partners it cannot guarantee it can fill the orders, Reuters reported October 20. Washington still bars Nvidia’s most powerful accelerators from China, so the order itself signals how much pent-up Chinese AI-compute demand is chasing whatever chips remain legal to sell. Supply-chain planners and chip investors should treat Nvidia’s own delivery caveat as more decision-relevant than the headline order size — allocation, not demand, is now the binding constraint.

10
VENEZUELA

Machado Returns to Venezuela After 10 Months in Exile as Rubio Warns Against Delaying Elections

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Nobel laureate María Corina Machado returned to Venezuela on October 25 after more than 10 months in exile and seven failed prior attempts, with US-backed reconciliation talks credited for her safe passage; Secretary of State Marco Rubio is now pressing interim president Delcy Rodríguez — in office since Maduro’s January capture, her 90-day mandate long expired — to call the elections she promised at the UN in September but has not scheduled. The return follows nationwide protests of a scale not seen in nearly two years and comes as Rodríguez’s government cooperates with Washington on a deal covering more than 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan oil reserves. Energy traders and anyone with Venezuela exposure should watch the election-timing standoff, not Machado’s popularity, as the variable that determines whether the current oil-access arrangement holds or gets renegotiated.

11
CANADA-US TRADE

Trump Eases Tariffs on Canadian Goods Effective October 30, Says Trade Deal Coming “Pretty Soon”

The White House · whitehouse.gov ↗

A proclamation signed October 23 and taking effect October 30 removes plywood, some paper products, and certain machinery and refrigeration equipment from Section 338 tariffs on Canadian goods while adding valves, carpets and waste products to the list — the tariffs still apply regardless of USMCA qualification and stack on top of Section 232 duties. President Trump told reporters October 25 he expects the trade war with Canada to end “pretty soon,” days after a Canadian official said an Alberta referendum delay had slowed talks. Canadian exporters in the affected sectors should confirm classification under the narrowed scope before the October 30 effective date, rather than waiting for the broader deal Trump is promising.

CANADA BUDGET

Canada’s Budget Watchdog Cuts 2026 Growth Forecast to 0.9%, Sees Deficit Topping $63 Billion

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

The Parliamentary Budget Officer cut its 2026 real GDP growth forecast to 0.9% (from 1.1% in June) and 2027 to 1.5% on October 20, citing new US tariffs and Canadian countermeasures, while projecting the federal deficit at $63.3 billion for 2026-27, peaking at $70.2 billion in 2027-28 — about $3 billion a year higher than June’s estimate, driven by new spending that outpaces stronger revenues. The downgrade lands ahead of Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne’s budget. Provincial finance officials and bond investors should treat the PBO’s weaker growth-and-deficit combination, not the government’s own targets, as the baseline to price Canadian federal borrowing against.

UKRAINE

European Defence Ministers Pledge Air-Defence Buildup for Ukraine as Zelenskyy Confirms November Peace Talks

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Defence ministers from France, Germany, Italy, Poland and the UK met in Paris on October 26 and pledged to help build an air-defence system supplementing Ukraine’s US-made Patriots, assemble winter aid packages, and press Washington to keep supplying intelligence and satellite data — while Zelenskyy said on October 25 that Russia has agreed in principle to trilateral US-Russia-Ukraine talks restarting in November, after three earlier rounds produced little beyond prisoner exchanges. No dollar figures or delivery timelines were disclosed for either the defence package or the talks. Governments and contractors in the European defence-industrial base should read the Paris pledge as directional political cover, not funded procurement — the figures that would make this actionable haven’t been published yet.

ALBERTA

Alberta Voters Reject Independence Referendum by Roughly 76% to 23%

CBS News · cbsnews.com ↗

Alberta voters rejected a referendum question on beginning a separation process by roughly 76% to 23%, with more than 1 million votes counted after advance and special balloting drew 1.35 million voters — 43% of those eligible — ahead of final results reported October 20. The decisive margin removes the Alberta-secession variable that had been complicating Ottawa’s trade negotiations with Washington, after a Canadian official had blamed the referendum for delaying progress on a US deal. Investors and executives tracking Canadian political risk can discount the separatist scenario for now, but should watch whether Premier Danielle Smith’s government uses the result to press Ottawa harder on energy and equalization grievances rather than dropping them.

15
GAZA

UN Commission Says Israel Still Committing “Genocidal Acts” in Gaza Despite Ceasefire

The National · thenationalnews.com ↗

The UN’s Independent International Commission of Inquiry said October 26 that Israel is “continuing to commit genocidal acts” in Gaza despite the ceasefire that took effect a year ago, reporting that Israeli forces have expanded territorial control from 53% to at least 60% of the territory and that fire along the advancing “Yellow Line” has killed at least 196 Palestinians, including 43 children, since the truce — separately, Gaza’s health ministry counts more than 1,400 killed in the past year. The commission also found Israeli West Bank airstrikes killed 254 Palestinians between October 2023 and September 2026, versus six in the prior 15 years. Israel rejected the findings and called for the commission’s dissolution; the report cannot prosecute but could feed future international proceedings, so corporates and investors with reputational exposure to the region should treat it as a documented, if contested, escalation in legal and diplomatic risk, not a one-off claim.

TAIWAN

Taiwan Drives Away 16 Chinese Vessels in Coordinated “Hybrid Warfare” Operation

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Taiwan’s coastguard said it confronted and drove away 16 Chinese coastguard, research and fishing vessels in coordinated operations around Taiwan’s main island and the disputed Pratas Islands, calling it “hybrid warfare” and accusing Beijing of trying to unilaterally change the status quo; Taiwanese data show China deployed a record 244 ships around the main island in July, versus 58 a year earlier. The incident follows Taiwan’s October 1 use of water cannon on a Chinese vessel near Pratas and comes as Chinese warships operate around Taiwan almost daily. Firms with Taiwan Strait shipping or semiconductor supply-chain exposure should track the pace of these incursions, not any single incident, as the leading indicator of risk — the trend line, now running at roughly four times last year’s rate, matters more than any one confrontation.

TORONTO

Olivia Chow Wins Second Term as Toronto Mayor, 54.1% to 41.1%

CP24 · cp24.com ↗

Olivia Chow won re-election as Toronto mayor with 54.1% of the vote to challenger Brad Bradford’s 41.1% — a margin of roughly 125,500 votes with 98.2% of polls reporting — after a race that had tightened to a statistical tie in a mid-October poll before breaking decisively her way on election night, October 26. Chow, first elected in a 2023 byelection, avoids becoming the first post-amalgamation Toronto mayor to lose a re-election bid and says her first priority is uniting the 25-member council. Business and real-estate interests with Toronto exposure now have policy continuity to plan around rather than the 50-50 outcome polling had suggested through mid-October.

DATA CENTERS

CBRE Buys Data-Center Services Firm Onnec for Up to £760 Million

CBRE · cbre.com ↗

CBRE announced October 20 it will pay £460 million upfront plus up to £300 million in earnouts for Onnec, a UK, European and Asia-Pacific data-center infrastructure services firm expected to generate about £370 million in 2027 revenue, extending CBRE’s Critical Infrastructure Services line toward nearly $4.5 billion in projected 2027 revenue and giving it data-center reach to match its existing US footprint. The deal is expected to close by year-end and to be immediately accretive to core earnings. Real-estate services investors and data-center operators should read this as confirmation that infrastructure-services consolidation, not just new-build construction, is now a primary way incumbents are capturing AI-driven data-center growth.
