01
APEC TRILATERAL

Trump, Xi and Putin Confirm First-Ever Trilateral at APEC, Agenda Set on Nuclear Arms

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

The White House confirmed Friday that Trump, Xi and Putin will hold an unprecedented three-way meeting on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18–19, with the stated agenda centred on nuclear non-proliferation after New START lapsed in February. A senior US official cautioned the topic “will start the conversation” but conceded “we do not expect to get any kind of agreement,” while Ukraine is being kept off the table since Beijing isn’t party to that track — a dynamic a Russian lawmaker has already likened to a “new Yalta.” For boards pricing geopolitical risk into next week, treat this as a test of whether the three leaders can agree on an agenda at all, not as a venue where anything gets decided.

02
AI IPO DEBUT

Anthropic Sets November 19 Nasdaq Debut, Discloses $81 Billion Run-Rate Revenue

The Next Web · thenextweb.com ↗

Anthropic set its Nasdaq debut for November 19 under ticker ANT, pricing shares at $70 to $78 for a raise of up to $64.4 billion plus a separate $26 billion private placement from cornerstone investors — a combined $90.4 billion that would rank among the largest tech listings on record. The updated prospectus discloses run-rate revenue of roughly $81 billion as of October 31, up $10 billion in a single month, against nearly $900 billion in computing commitments, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading the offering. The number that matters for anyone pricing the AI trade isn’t the valuation headline — already guided down from near $2 trillion six weeks ago — it’s whether that revenue run-rate can keep compounding fast enough to justify the compute Anthropic has already committed to buy.

03
US-CHINA TRADE

China Extends US Tariff Truce Two More Months, Critical-Minerals Waivers to January

gCaptain · gcaptain.com ↗

China’s State Council confirmed that the suspension of its 24% additional tariff on US imports is extended two more months from November 10, while export-control waivers on critical minerals, the removal of 11 US firms from its “unreliable entities” list and 16 from its export-control list, and special port fees on US-linked vessels are all extended to January 10, 2027. Beijing framed the move as implementing “the outcomes and consensus reached at the Washington meeting,” while warning it will “adjust relevant tariff countermeasures” if Washington doesn’t reciprocate — Treasury Secretary Bessent had called the extension a bid for “more time to see what we can do on the economic front.” For supply chains built around Chinese critical minerals, the operative date is no longer an abstract deadline — it’s January 10, and nothing beyond it is yet guaranteed.

04
CANADA-US TRADE

Carney Says Trade Talks Must Run Through Trump, Not Congress, as Contacts Resume

WCAX (AP) · wcax.com ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney said “the people who decide on trade agreements are the executive in the United States over the next two or three years,” pushing back on Quebec’s plan to lobby US governors and senators directly and insisting “it’s always a good idea to have one negotiator.” Carney confirmed he spoke with Trump over the weekend and may meet him at upcoming summits, with Washington having dropped its demands on French-language protections and cultural programming since Canada suspended talks in August over 50% US tariffs — though Carney cautioned “there are still some tough issues to resolve.” For Canadian exporters still absorbing those tariffs, the signal is that any deal will be negotiated in the Oval Office, not reopened by provincial outreach to Capitol Hill, however appealing that route looks after the Democrats’ midterm gains.

05
UKRAINE-EUROPE

Zelenskyy Warns Trump’s Proposed Europe-Russia Security Deal Risks a “Yalta 2.0”

Kyiv Post · kyivpost.com ↗

President Zelenskyy told CNN that Trump’s proposed Russia-Europe security framework — reportedly broad enough to cover the whole continent — risks dividing Europe into spheres of influence, declaring “we don’t want to have Yalta 2.0” and insisting the war end first, with Ukraine-specific security guarantees agreed, before any wider Moscow-Europe arrangement is negotiated. Jared Kushner confirmed Washington is working on such a broader framework, while EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas countered in Warsaw that “Putin had no veto over European security.” For any government or institution exposed to the shape of a postwar European order, the fight over sequencing — guarantees for Ukraine first, or a continental deal first — is now the real negotiation, not the ceasefire terms themselves.

06
CRYPTO MARKETS

Bitcoin Extends Slide to $69,135, Down 5.6% in a Day as Rate-Cut Bets Unwind

Fortune · fortune.com ↗

Bitcoin fell to $69,134.72 as of Friday morning, down $4,073.52 — a 5.6% single-day drop from $73,208.24 — extending a slide that began when a hotter-than-expected October inflation print pushed the 10-year Treasury yield toward 4.99% and traders began repricing toward a Fed hike rather than a cut. The move has erased most of the token’s gains since the summer and tracks the same rate-path repricing now hitting equity duration trades across the broader market. Treasury desks and crypto-exposed funds should treat this less as an idiosyncratic crypto event than as one more data point in a market-wide reassessment of how much higher rates stay for how long.

07
US HOUSING

US Existing Home Sales Fall 5.5% in October to Lowest Level Since 2010

National Association of Realtors · nar.realtor ↗

The National Association of Realtors reported that existing-home sales fell 5.5% in October, the weakest pace since August 2010, with NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun attributing the slide to mortgage rates that “have climbed to their highest levels since 2000, aside from a single month in 2023.” Yun struck a cautiously constructive note regardless, arguing the roughly 88 million American homeowners are “largely in a comfortable financial position” and that “there could be sizable pent-up demand if mortgage rates decline.” For anyone underwriting US consumer or construction exposure, the read-through is that this is a rate-driven freeze, not a housing-demand collapse — which makes the sector’s recovery timeline a direct bet on the Fed’s own path.

08
INDONESIA POLITICS

Indonesia’s VP Gibran Steps Down After Courts Void His Diploma, Assembly to Pick Successor Dec. 2

The Jakarta Post · thejakartapost.com ↗

Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka told President Prabowo he intends to step down, after the Supreme Court annulled the education certificate he used for his 2024 candidacy and the Constitutional Court found no convincing evidence his overseas schooling met the eligibility requirement; he formally stepped down two days later while separately seeking to contest the documents’ validity in administrative court. Prabowo has given coalition party leaders a week to prepare recommendations for a successor, with the People’s Consultative Assembly due to select the new vice president on December 2. For investors tracking Indonesian policy continuity, the three-week vacancy is short by design, but it hands Prabowo effectively unchecked discretion over who holds the country’s second-highest office.
