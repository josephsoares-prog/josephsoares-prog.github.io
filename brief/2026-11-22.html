MARKETS & MACRO

Nvidia Posts a Record $115.8 Billion Quarter as the AI Boom Outruns Its Own Boardroom Crisis

NVIDIA · nvidianews.nvidia.com ↗

Nvidia reported record fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year, with Data Center revenue alone at $107.6 billion, in the company’s first earnings call under interim CEO Colette Kress since Jensen Huang’s ouster. Nvidia guided fourth-quarter revenue to $130 billion, assuming zero Data Center sales to China, and its board authorized an additional $150 billion in buybacks — the clearest signal yet that markets are still pricing Nvidia’s fundamentals, not its governance crisis. Treat any further AI-sector wobble as a sentiment story until the numbers themselves say otherwise.

TECHNOLOGY & AI

Nvidia’s Board Still Hasn’t Named a Permanent CEO Ten Days After Ousting Jensen Huang

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Nvidia’s market value briefly fell below $4 trillion after the board forced out Jensen Huang over his ties to a suspected Chinese intelligence operative, naming CFO Colette Kress interim CEO and venture investor Harvey C. Jones as the company’s first-ever board chair while a permanent search — open to internal and external candidates — continues. With Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent separately pressing the board to look outside the company and a shareholder class action already filed, the succession question is no longer just a leadership story but a live governance and export-compliance risk that customers and partners should be pricing into any long-term Nvidia commitment.

03
TECH & MARKETS

Jensen Huang’s Downfall Is Testing Whether the AI Trade Can Survive Without Him

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

The Globe and Mail reports that analysts increasingly see Jensen Huang’s resignation — amid allegations he had affairs with a suspected Chinese spy and with a company executive — as a potential trigger for the AI bubble to burst, given AI infrastructure spending is projected to reach $7.6 trillion within five years and Nvidia has spent billions underwriting its own customers’ chip demand. The risk for boards everywhere exposed to the AI trade is that Huang’s personal scandal, not a rate shock or an accounting fraud, becomes the catalyst historians point to — which means governance risk at the industry’s central supplier now deserves the same weight as valuation risk.

GAZA CEASEFIRE

Israel’s Ground Push Into Central Gaza Enters Its Fifth Day as the Ceasefire’s Civil Administration Stays Unbuilt

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Israeli forces killed three Palestinians when they launched their first broad ground operation in central Gaza since the ceasefire was signed over a year ago, with tanks operating near Salah al-Din Street east of the Nuseirat refugee camp; the offensive is now entering its fifth day with no sign of a pullback. This lands on top of the UN-backed Board of Peace’s own warning that Israel has blocked the ceasefire’s interim civil administration body and left civilian policing undecided — for anyone tracking regional stability or shipping risk, the operative question is no longer whether the ceasefire holds on paper but whether it still constrains anyone’s actions on the ground.

HORMUZ STANDOFF

Tankers Keep Burning in the Strait of Hormuz as the US-Iran Standoff Defies Hopes of De-escalation

Al-Monitor (Reuters) · al-monitor.com ↗

Reuters reporting carried by Al-Monitor found at least 10 tankers hit in the Strait of Hormuz in the first ten days of November alone, after a record 37 attacks in October, even as falling oil prices suggested markets expected de-escalation; satellite imagery has confirmed oil spills reaching the shores of Oman and the UAE, and the Iran-backed Houthis have struck Saudi Aramco facilities in parallel. The gap between what oil prices are pricing in and what is actually happening on the water is now wide enough that shippers and insurers should be treating that spread itself, not the headline crude price, as the signal to watch.

ENERGY & OIL

Oil Settles Near a Four-Week Low as Brent Holds Near $92 and the Mideast Risk Premium Fails to Stick

Gulf News · gulfnews.com ↗

WTI fell 1.10% to $85.05 a barrel and Brent slipped 0.57% to $92.61 on Friday, with the Brent-WTI spread widening to $7.55, even as tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz keep escalating — a disconnect that tells us physical supply fears are currently losing to demand-side and recession worries in setting price. For anyone hedging energy exposure into year-end, the message is that the market is not yet treating Hormuz as a tail risk worth paying up for, which is itself the risk to watch if that complacency breaks.

CANADA & TRADE

Canada and China Expand Tariff Relief on Steel, Aluminum and EVs as Carney Hedges Against Washington

Al Jazeera (Reuters, AP) · aljazeera.com ↗

Meeting on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, Carney and Xi Jinping announced new tariff remissions — China cutting duties on Canadian pork and seafood, Canada extending exemptions on Chinese steel and aluminum — building on a January agreement to repair the relationship. Coming the same week Carney told reporters a Canada-US deal remains ‘possible, not there yet,’ the pattern is unmistakable: Ottawa is actively diversifying away from reliance on Washington, and companies planning around CUSMA alone should treat that diversification as Canadian government policy, not just rhetoric.

CANADA & TRADE

Carney Says a Canada-US Trade Deal Is ‘Possible, Not There Yet’ as Auto-Sector Protections Remain the Sticking Point

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

Speaking in Manila after a ‘long, substantive conversation’ with President Trump, Ambassador Jamieson Greer and chief of staff Stephen Miller, Prime Minister Carney said Canada and the US are making progress toward a bilateral deal but ‘we are not there yet,’ naming protections for Canada’s auto sector — in the wake of Stellantis’s sale of its Brampton assembly plant — as a non-negotiable condition on Ottawa’s side. With no fixed deadline attached to that condition, the planning risk for Canadian auto-sector suppliers is an open-ended one: budget for continued tariff exposure until a deal is actually signed, not announced.

CANADA ECONOMY

Bank of Canada’s October Hike to 2.5% Sets Up a Pivotal December 9 Decision

Bank of Canada · bankofcanada.ca ↗

The Bank of Canada raised its policy rate 25 basis points to 2.5% — its first hike in three years — citing GDP growth of 3.3% in the second quarter and resilient consumer spending, while flagging that new US tariffs will slow fourth-quarter growth before a 2027 rebound. With the Bank’s final scheduled decision of the year due December 9 and unemployment steady near 6.5%, treasurers and lenders should be pricing a live two-sided risk into year-end, not assuming the October move was a one-off before a pause.

CANADA ECONOMY

Ottawa’s Budget Shifts Temporary Residents to Permanent Status as Critics Say the Levels Math Doesn’t Add Up

BNN Bloomberg (Canadian Press) · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

Canada’s updated 2027-2029 immigration plan, released with the federal budget, holds broad admission targets steady while offering permanent status to up to 200,000 longtime temporary residents who have already applied — a mechanism that converts existing temporary residents rather than adding net new arrivals. For employers and provinces planning around labour supply, the practical effect is a status conversion, not a demand shock; the open question is whether the targets this masks for new arrivals still match the housing and services capacity Ottawa has separately promised to fix.

BUSINESS & M&A

Skydance Merges Warner Bros. and Paramount’s TV Studios Into a 120-Series Giant

The Boston Globe · bostonglobe.com ↗

Skydance will combine Warner Bros. Television Group and Paramount Television Studios into a single unit, Warner Bros. Television Studios, housing more than 120 series including ‘The Pitt,’ ‘Ted Lasso’ and ‘Emily in Paris,’ with the merger set to close in early 2027 — the latest integration step since Skydance’s Paramount closed its $81 billion takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery last month. For studios, streamers and talent agencies negotiating with either legacy entity, the operative fact is that counterparty consolidation in Hollywood is still accelerating, not settling, which should push any multi-year content deal into renegotiation sooner rather than later.

BUSINESS & RETAIL

Walmart Raises Its Outlook for a Third Straight Time as E-Commerce Carries the Holiday Quarter

AJC (AP) · ajc.com ↗

Walmart posted comparable sales growth of 2.9% for the quarter — an improvement on last quarter’s 2.6% but still well below the 4.5% growth of a year earlier — and raised its annual guidance for the third time this year, though the outlook was more conservative than analysts expected and shares slipped nearly 1%. The read for other retailers heading into the holiday season: trading-down and cost-conscious shopping are still the dominant consumer behaviour, and e-commerce is doing more of the work to hit targets than in-store traffic is.
