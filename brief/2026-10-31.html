01
ISRAEL

Israel’s Election Ends in Deadlock — Netanyahu’s Bloc Takes 53 Seats, Eisenkot’s 48, as Arab Parties Win a Record 19

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

With all votes counted from Tuesday’s election, Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc has 53 seats and challenger Gadi Eisenkot’s 48, leaving both well short of the 61-seat majority needed to govern; Arab-led parties won a record 19 seats but every Zionist bloc party except The Democrats has ruled out partnering with them. President Herzog receives the official results Wednesday and will then consult party leaders before tasking someone with forming a government, a process that could run past the new Knesset’s November 10 swearing-in and drag on for more than two months. For anyone pricing Israeli policy continuity — on Gaza ceasefire enforcement, on Iran, on US diplomacy — the operative fact is that neither man can govern alone, and the horse-trading period itself is now the variable to watch, not just its eventual outcome.

02
IRAN WAR

Pentagon Puts the Iran War’s Publicly Disclosed Cost at $47.1 Billion Through October 16

The Boston Globe · bostonglobe.com ↗

The Pentagon’s latest internal accounting puts the publicly disclosed cost of the US campaign against Iran at $47.1 billion through October 16, continuing a steady climb from roughly $29 billion in May and $37.5 billion in July. The figure covers direct military operations only — munitions, fuel, deployments — and excludes the broader economic toll of the Hormuz disruption itself, which is being felt separately in fuel prices from the Gulf to Western Europe. For defense committees and contractors alike, the trajectory matters more than any single number: at this rate of increase, Congress will be asked for a new supplemental well before year-end, and appropriators should be modeling that request now rather than waiting for the formal ask.

03
ENERGY

EIA Raises Its Q4 Brent Forecast to $105 a Barrel, $14 Higher, on Hormuz Shut-Ins

US Energy Information Administration · eia.gov ↗

The US Energy Information Administration’s October Short-Term Energy Outlook now projects Brent crude averaging $105 a barrel in the fourth quarter of 2026, a $14 increase from last month’s forecast, citing roughly 4.5 million barrels a day of Middle East production shut in by the Strait of Hormuz disruption, attacks on Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline, and elevated tanker war-risk insurance costs. This is the US government’s own benchmark forecast, not a trading desk’s call, which makes it the number treasurers and procurement teams should be using to re-price winter fuel and freight budgets right now. Diesel markets in particular remain tight, the EIA notes, meaning the squeeze will show up in trucking and shipping costs before it shows up at the pump.

04
MARKETS

Intel Beats on Q3 Revenue and Profit, Then Sheds a Fifth of Its Value on Weak Q4 Guidance

Shacknews · shacknews.com ↗

Intel beat Wall Street on both lines for the third quarter — $16.5 billion in revenue against a $16.44 billion estimate, and 52 cents a share against 39 cents expected — but guided fourth-quarter revenue to just $15-16 billion with GAAP earnings of only 10 cents a share, well below what analysts had priced in. Shares fell as low as $73.50 in after-hours trading from a $92.07 close, a roughly 20% drop, as investors concluded the beat was backward-looking while the guidance is the forward signal that matters. For anyone holding Intel exposure through a supply chain, a pension allocation, or a competing chipmaker’s relative valuation, the lesson is now the same one the market applied to Alphabet two days earlier: a beat on trailing numbers no longer buys a pass on a soft forward outlook.

05
MARKETS

Roblox Beats on Loss-Per-Share but Guides Q4 Bookings Down 10-15% as a Shareholder Firm Opens an Investigation

MarketBeat · marketbeat.com ↗

Roblox posted a smaller-than-expected per-share loss for the third quarter but revenue of $1.57 billion missed the $1.63 billion consensus, and the company swung to a $227 million net loss while guiding fourth-quarter bookings down 10-15% year over year on tougher comparisons and softer monetization among younger users. Shares fell about 2% on the news alone, a muted initial reaction that undersells the real story: a shareholder-rights law firm has already announced an investigation, and that combination of rising liabilities, slowing user spend, and legal exposure is the trio that tends to compound into a much larger drawdown once the market has had a weekend to digest it. Any institutional holder should be reading the guidance call transcript, not just the headline beat, before Monday’s open.

06
CANADA-UAE

Carney Launches ‘Strategic Partnership’ With the UAE, Touting the $70 Billion Pledge Turning Into Real Projects

Prime Minister of Canada · pm.gc.ca ↗

Mark Carney and Abu Dhabi Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan launched a new Canada-UAE Strategic Partnership on Parliament Hill, layering defence, diplomatic-training, and justice-cooperation agreements on top of the Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement the two countries negotiated in a record 47 days — a deal Ottawa says will double two-way trade from roughly $3.4 billion to $7 billion over the next decade. Carney framed the visit as turning the UAE’s November 2025 pledge of $70 billion in Canadian investment into concrete commitments, including a DP World-Arctic Gateway memorandum to develop a Churchill-to-Hudson Bay transportation corridor and a Masdar energy-cooperation arrangement with Natural Resources Canada. For Canadian exporters and infrastructure players, the corridor and energy MOUs are the pieces worth tracking for actual contract flow, since the partnership framework itself carries no binding commitments on its own.

07
CANADA STEEL

Joly Says ‘That Doesn’t Work,’ Threatens Cleveland-Cliffs With Court Action and Forced Divestment Over Stelco

CP24 · cp24.com ↗

Industry Minister Mélanie Joly escalated Ottawa’s standoff with Cleveland-Cliffs, telling CTV’s Power Play that large companies cannot simply walk away from commitments made to the government and that Canada, mid-trade-war, needs to send a message — including going to court and considering forced divestment of assets or financial penalties if the Hamilton layoffs continue. Cleveland-Cliffs maintains it is in full compliance with its 2024 Investment Canada Act jobs pledge and has asked for a direct meeting between Joly and CEO Lourenco Goncalves to chart a path back to full employment; Joly says Ottawa believes it has a strong legal case. Any company that accepted foreign-investment conditions as the price of a takeover approval should treat this as the live test of how far Ottawa will actually go to enforce them once market conditions make compliance unprofitable.

08
TRADE LITIGATION

New Jersey Food Importer Becomes First Plaintiff to Sue Over Trump’s 50% Canada Tariffs

KSAT (AP) · ksat.com ↗

Gellert Global Group, a New Jersey-based food importer, has filed the first lawsuit directly challenging President Trump’s 50% tariffs on Canadian goods, arguing the administration unlawfully invoked a 1930s-era trade law — Section 338 — to impose duties that courts were never meant to let a president levy without congressional sign-off. The case lands as the fifth Section 338 proclamation on Canada took effect this week, meaning the legal theory underpinning that entire escalating tariff track is now, for the first time, squarely before a judge. Importers of Canadian goods who have been eating these costs or passing them to customers should watch this docket closely — a ruling against the administration would be retroactive leverage for refund claims, not just a forward-looking fix.

09
AI REGULATION

OpenAI Asks BC Court to Quash Privacy Commissioner’s Probe Into the Tumbler Ridge Shooting

MLex · mlex.com ↗

OpenAI has asked the BC Supreme Court to quash the provincial privacy commissioner’s investigation into ChatGPT’s interactions with the teenager involved in the Tumbler Ridge school shooting, arguing the regulator exceeded its authority by examining what is really an online-safety issue and that the probe duplicates an earlier federal-provincial review. The motion itself is what forced the previously quiet investigation into public view, and it is now a live test of whether a privacy commissioner’s jurisdiction extends to AI-safety failures or stops at data-handling questions. Any company whose product has generated harm-adjacent litigation in Canada should treat the eventual ruling as the template for which regulator gets to investigate first — the privacy office or a dedicated safety authority — the next time a similar incident reaches a courtroom.

10
FRANCE

Macron Ties France’s Worst Unrest in Years to the Gulf War’s Fuel-Price Shock

International Business Times UK · ibtimes.co.uk ↗

In an October 18 address after riots and arson spread from Limoges to Paris in what officials called France’s worst unrest in years, President Macron tied rising fuel prices directly to the Hormuz-driven Gulf conflict, which France has not joined militarily, and announced a Gulf tour — Kuwait, the UAE, Bahrain, and Qatar — to secure alternative fuel supply. The address came the same week President Trump posted that “France is burning” and blamed immigration, a claim French authorities have not substantiated, and it illustrates a pattern worth tracking beyond France alone: the Hormuz shock is now a domestic political liability for European governments, not just an energy-markets story. Companies with European consumer or logistics exposure should expect fuel-cost pass-through to collide with public tolerance for price increases well before any Hormuz resolution arrives.
