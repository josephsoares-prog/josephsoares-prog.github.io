MARKETS

Palantir detonates its Q2 — revenue up 93% to $1.94 billion, guidance raised to about $8.15 billion, and the stock jumps ~13% after hours

Fortune · fortune.com ↗

Palantir posted $1.94 billion in Q2 revenue, up 93% year-over-year, with US commercial sales up 149% and government up 90%, earned 41 cents a share against a 35-cent estimate, and lifted full-year 2026 guidance to roughly $8.15 billion from about $7.19 billion — sending the stock up ~13% after hours to $142.01. We read it as the day’s tone-setter: AI software demand is being validated by cash flow, not slideware, and it resets the bar every other AI name now has to clear this week. But at a valuation this stretched the upside is priced in — own the theme through diversified AI exposure rather than chasing a single 200-times-sales name into the print.

GEOPOLITICS

Trump calls Iran’s leadership ‘unbelievably duplicitous’ after Tehran denies new talks — the de-escalation narrative is fraying, not firming

NBC News · nbcnews.com ↗

After President Trump said US-Iran talks would resume and a deal on Strait of Hormuz shipping and Iran’s nuclear programme was close, Tehran denied any new negotiations were set to begin — prompting Trump to brand its leadership ‘unbelievably duplicitous.’ The decision-grade read is that the two capitals are describing different realities, so the de-escalation trade is running ahead of the facts: keep a thin war premium in oil and treat any ‘imminent deal’ headline as reversible until Tehran confirms the same terms, because a Hormuz ceasefire has already collapsed once.

TECHNOLOGY

AMD reports Q2 after the close today — Wall Street wants roughly $11.3 billion in revenue and the AI-chip read-through the whole complex is trading on

TipRanks · tipranks.com ↗

AMD reports second-quarter results after today’s close, with analysts modelling revenue near $11.3 billion (up about 47%) and earnings around $1.62 a share (up more than 235%) after a stock that has surged over 120% year-to-date on data-center and AI wins. This is the breadth test that follows Palantir: a strong data-center number and confident guidance widen the AI rally into silicon and ease fears of narrow leadership, while any softness or cautious outlook would puncture a market priced for continued acceleration heading into Friday’s jobs report.

ECONOMY

US factories expand at their fastest pace in over four years — the July ISM manufacturing PMI hits 55.6

PR Newswire · prnewswire.com ↗

The Institute for Supply Management’s July manufacturing PMI came in at 55.6%, the sharpest expansion in more than four years and a clear break from the sector’s recent stop-start pace. The decision-grade point is that a hot factory read cuts against the September rate-cut trade: stacked on top of resilient growth, it forces the market to question cut bets it has already partly priced — so if Friday’s payrolls confirm the strength, the asymmetry into the autumn tilts more hawkish than the consensus assumes.

MACRO

Friday’s July jobs report is the week’s hinge for the Fed — payrolls seen near 85,000, with ADP Wednesday the tell before it

Bloomberg · bloomberg.com ↗

Economists look for roughly 85,000 jobs added in July with unemployment near 4.3%, the capstone of a data week that runs ADP on Wednesday into Friday’s official payrolls. With Big Tech earnings behind us and a 55.6 ISM in hand, the labour read is now the market’s swing factor: a soft number revives the September-cut case and lifts duration, a firm one — on top of hot manufacturing — arms the hawks and pressures the long end. This is the single print to size rate-sensitive risk around.

ENERGY

OPEC+ finishes unwinding its cuts with another 188,000 barrels a day from September as oil eases on the Iran pause — Brent near $84

Benzinga · benzinga.com ↗

OPEC+ approved a further 188,000 barrels-a-day increase for September, completing the restoration of the output it had voluntarily withheld, with Brent easing toward $84 and WTI near $81 — down from roughly $91 a week earlier as the market fades the Iran war premium. The read is that supply, not the Middle East headlines, is setting the price: the barrels are in the market and the group is signalling a possible fourth-quarter pause, so position an energy book for range-bound crude and treat a Hormuz flare-up as the tail that reprices it fast, not the base case.

TECHNOLOGY

Nvidia and OpenAI are in talks for a backstop of up to $250 billion to fund AI infrastructure — the scale of the capex bet keeps growing

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Nvidia and OpenAI are discussing a financing backstop of up to $250 billion to underwrite OpenAI’s data-center and compute ambitions — a sum that underscores how the AI build-out is now measured in figures once reserved for sovereigns. The decision-grade signal is circularity and concentration: the chipmaker helping finance its largest customer’s buildout is great for near-term demand but stacks systemic risk onto a handful of balance sheets, so this is the structure to watch as the read-through from Palantir and AMD tests whether AI revenue can eventually justify the spend.

CANADA

Canada trades through a technical recession with the 35% US tariff now live — the OECD sees growth strengthening to 1.2% this year

Global News · globalnews.ca ↗

Canada is in a technical recession — GDP slipped in the first quarter after a full-percent drop late last year — even as Trump’s 35% tariff on non-CUSMA-compliant goods is now in force, with the OECD projecting growth strengthens to about 1.2% in 2026 and 1.7% in 2027, helped by energy exports and public investment. For Canadian decision-makers the message is that the trade file, not the Bank of Canada, is the swing variable: plan for a soft-growth, steady-rate backdrop into the autumn, lean on the CUSMA carve-out that shields most exports, and treat any Mexico-style tariff extension as the upside catalyst to watch.

Commentary Hooks

The AI Earnings Bar Just Went Vertical

Palantir’s 93% growth and another guidance raise show the AI trade is being validated by cash flow, not hype — but a stock priced for perfection punishes any wobble. When AMD reports tonight, the question is no longer whether AI sells, but whether the earnings strength is broad enough to justify a market leaning on a handful of names.

Announcements Are Not Agreements: three conflicts, one discipline

Trump says an Iran deal is close while Tehran denies talks; Zelensky floats a ceasefire while Russia keeps striking; Gaza’s disarmament roadmap stalls on verification. Three conflicts, one discipline — the smart money keeps hedges on and prices the signed, verified outcome, which is why gold sits near its highs even as equities make records.

Two Data Points, One Fed

A 55.6 ISM says the economy is running hot; a payrolls number near 85,000 would say the labour market is cooling. The Fed has to reconcile them by September, and this week decides which signal wins. For allocators, the lesson is to stop trading the narrative and start trading the data — the cut is no longer the base case it was a month ago.
