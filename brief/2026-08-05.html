01
HORMUZ

US, Iran and Oman near an interim deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with a Wednesday announcement targeted

Axios · axios.com ↗

Washington, Tehran and Muscat are closing in on an interim deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with a US announcement targeted for Wednesday. Iran’s foreign minister Araghchi has called the Oman-mediated talks “on the way to being finalised,” but Tehran itself has not confirmed the deal’s terms, and Trump has warned Iran will be “hit very hard” if the strait is not open soon. A fifth of global oil transits Hormuz — a confirmed reopening would ease the tanker backlog and cap the risk premium in energy markets; a collapse revives the case for the bombing campaign Trump paused Saturday.

02
CANADA

Washington’s new 50% tariffs on Canadian goods take effect August 19

Wiley · wiley.law ↗

A fresh round of US tariffs — up to 50% under Section 338 of the 1930 Tariff Act — hits Canadian dairy, alcohol, furniture and roughly $16 billion in other goods starting August 19, framed as retaliation for alleged discrimination against US exports. It lands the same week Ottawa expects roughly $10 billion in refunds on an earlier, separately litigated round of tariffs (next item) — the two tracks show a relationship being renegotiated tariff-by-tariff rather than through a single framework. Exporters should not read the refund as a sign the trade war is winding down.

03
CANADA

Ottawa expects roughly $10 billion in refunds for Canadian exporters after courts found earlier tariffs illegal

National Post · nationalpost.com ↗

Canadian exporters are set to recover roughly $10 billion after US courts found an earlier round of Trump tariffs unlawful — a rare reversal in a relationship that has mostly moved in one direction. The refund restores working capital mid-litigation on the new Section 338 tariffs above, but does not touch them; treat it as a cash-flow event for exporters, not a policy signal from Washington.

The strike lands as Trump has reportedly declined Kyiv’s request for additional Patriot interceptors — a resupply gap that, if confirmed independently, leaves Ukrainian cities and logistics infrastructure more exposed through the fall.

Commentary Hooks

The Chokepoint Illusion

Diplomatic attention is fixed on Hormuz, but the Houthis just hit a Saudi tanker in the Red Sea and Lufthansa Cargo’s numbers show shippers already routing around both. The real story isn’t which strait reopens — it’s that global logistics has quietly priced in permanent chokepoint risk.

Tariff-by-Tariff Diplomacy

Canada is simultaneously absorbing a new 50% tariff round and collecting a $10B refund on an old one. That is not policy incoherence — it’s a preview of how trade relationships get renegotiated in an era of litigation-plus-executive-action rather than treaties.

Command Responsibility Has a Longer Reach Now

The ICC’s move against a former police chief, not just the head of state, in the Philippines case is a template. Leaders and their operational lieutenants across multiple theaters should read the Dela Rosa warrant as a signal about where accountability now stops.

Watchlist

  • Whether Washington issues its targeted Wednesday announcement on a Hormuz reopening deal, and whether Tehran itself confirms the terms.
  • Whether Iran’s ‘Axis of Resistance’ network makes any visible move signaling Tehran feels strong enough to walk from the Hormuz deal.
  • Whether the August 19 effective date for the new 50% US tariffs on Canadian goods holds, moves, or triggers a Canadian retaliatory announcement.
  • Whether the Ethiopia-Tigray fighting risk materializes into confirmed clashes, opening a second active front in the Horn of Africa alongside Sudan.
  • Whether SpaceX and other newly listed AI-infrastructure names see follow-through selling, testing whether markets treat the capex shock as isolated or sector-wide.
