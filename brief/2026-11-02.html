US MIDTERMS

US Midterms, One Day Out: Early Voting Tops 49.6 Million as DOJ Sues to Contest a Possible Alaska Senate Loss

CBS News · cbsnews.com ↗

With polls opening tomorrow, Tuesday, November 3, roughly 49.6 million Americans had already voted early as of Friday evening, and CBS’s latest tracking shows Democrats with an edge on the generic ballot while the Senate hinges on toss-ups in Maine, Ohio and Texas, where Senator Ted Cruz has warned Republicans risk a “bloodbath” if turnout sags. The sharpest preview of post-election litigation risk is in Alaska, where the Justice Department has sued to block certification if Republican Dan Sullivan loses, citing voter confusion with a similarly named independent candidate, while separately running a criminal probe into the matter. For boards and allocators pricing policy continuity into 2027, the practical read is that control of both chambers remains genuinely contested and results may still be in litigation well past election night.

02
OPEC+

OPEC+ Holds December Output Steady as Brent Hits a Six-Month High of $117.64 on the Worst Month Yet for Hormuz Attacks

The National · thenationalnews.com ↗

The seven OPEC+ members managing voluntary cuts agreed on November 1 to hold December output at roughly 31 million barrels a day, the same level as September through November, and reiterated their commitment to the group’s Declaration of Cooperation without announcing any timetable for raising supply. The decision lands as Brent rose 2.4% to $117.64 a barrel on Friday — a six-month high and a fourth straight monthly gain — while October logged at least 35 attacks on commercial vessels in and around the Strait of Hormuz, the worst month since the US-Israel war with Iran began in February. For procurement and treasury teams hedging Q1 2027 fuel costs, the signal is that OPEC+ is managing a market it cannot fully control: most members are already producing below target because of the war, so a steady-output vote is really a statement that there is little spare capacity to offer.

03
EXXON EARNINGS

ExxonMobil Posts $15.4 Billion Q3 Profit and Announces $20 Billion in 2027 Buybacks, Extending a 44-Year Dividend Streak

ExxonMobil · exxonmobil.com ↗

ExxonMobil reported third-quarter net income of $15.4 billion, or $3.72 a share, up from $3.48 a share in the second quarter, with year-to-date earnings of $34.1 billion versus $22.3 billion over the same period in 2025. The company generated $19.1 billion in free cash flow, returned $9.4 billion to shareholders through dividends and buybacks, extended its streak to 44 consecutive years of dividend increases, and announced $20 billion in share repurchases planned for 2027 “assuming reasonable market conditions.” CEO Darren Woods framed the gains as structural rather than cyclical, saying “these are lasting improvements, not simply the result of favorable prices and margins” — a claim allocators should test against the fact that elevated, war-driven Brent prices are still doing real work in these numbers.

04
CHEVRON EARNINGS

Chevron’s Profit Nearly Triples to $9.9 Billion as Average Realized Brent Jumps to $97 From $69 a Year Ago

SEC EDGAR (Chevron Corp. 8-K) · sec.gov ↗

Chevron’s third-quarter net income nearly tripled to $9.9 billion, or $5.06 a share, from $3.5 billion a year earlier, as the average Brent price it realized jumped to $97 a barrel from $69, even as total production fell to 3.98 million barrels of oil-equivalent a day on lower entitlements tied to the Middle East conflict. US refining was the other engine: crude throughput hit a record 1.09 million barrels a day and US downstream earnings rose nearly sixfold to $3.6 billion, while the company cut net debt by $7.8 billion and lifted its return on capital employed to 17.7% from 7.6%. CEO Mike Wirth called it “disciplined execution” while “reinvesting to bring more energy to a supply-constrained market” — the operative number is still the $28 jump in realized Brent, the war premium rather than operations doing most of the work, a distinction worth remembering before extrapolating this margin into 2027.

05
ANTHROPIC IPO

Anthropic Files for a Nasdaq IPO Under ‘ANT’ as Half-Year Revenue Hits $16.3 Billion, Up 1,300%

TNW · thenextweb.com ↗

Anthropic filed its Form S-1 with the SEC on October 26, confirming it will list Class A shares as “ANT” on Nasdaq with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading a 22-bank underwriting group, and the Wall Street Journal reports — though the filing itself gives no figure — that the offering could raise $100 billion or more at a $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion valuation, with a listing targeted for the week of November 16. The filing shows first-half 2026 revenue of $16.3 billion, up 1,300% from $1.16 billion a year earlier, alongside an $80.7 billion net loss driven almost entirely by a $72.4 billion non-cash charge on convertible-note revaluations rather than operating losses, which were a comparatively modest $693 million. For anyone benchmarking AI-sector valuations, the useful number is the $1.6 billion adjusted operating income Anthropic reports once those accounting items are stripped out — a real, if thin, 10% margin business sitting underneath what looks on its face like an enormous loss.

06
GULF ESCALATION

Qatar Says an LNG Tanker Was Struck in the Gulf as Iran Designates Britain’s Royal Navy, RAF and MI6 Terrorist Entities

The Associated Press · click2houston.com ↗

Qatar’s state LNG carrier Nakilat confirmed Sunday that its vessel Al Mayeda was struck by “unidentified objects” in the Persian Gulf on Friday night — damaging the ship without crew injuries — in what marks an escalation beyond the Strait of Hormuz itself, since Qatar is the world’s second-largest LNG exporter and this is an attack on its own flagged carrier rather than a third-party tanker in transit. The same day, Iran designated Britain’s Royal Navy, Royal Air Force and MI6 as terrorist entities, accusing London of backing US-Israeli strikes on Iran, an escalation that followed a US State Department official telling the Manama Dialogue that Washington remains open to negotiating with Tehran while preparing “other options.” For companies with LNG contracts or UK-linked assets anywhere near the Gulf, the war-risk perimeter that insurers price around just widened on two fronts at once.

07
GROCERY DELIVERY M&A

DoorDash to Buy Instacart for $17 Billion, Cementing a Delivery Market Where It Now Controls 59% of US Spending

ITV News (AP) · itv.com ↗

DoorDash agreed to acquire Instacart for $17 billion, with Instacart keeping its own brand and app while gaining access to the roughly 50,000 additional stores DoorDash already serves; the deal caps a five-year shift in which DoorDash’s share of US delivery spending rose from 44% to 59% while Instacart’s fell from 26% to 18%, per Consumer Edge data cited by the companies. CEO Tony Xu called grocery “one of the hardest things to get right online,” and the open question the companies haven’t answered is how existing partnerships survive contact with the merger — Instacart still delivers for Walmart, a retailer DoorDash stopped serving in 2022. For grocers negotiating delivery contracts, the practical effect is a sharp reduction in platform choice, and renegotiating terms before the deal closes is the window that matters now.

CANADA BUDGET

Three Days From Ottawa’s Budget, Carney Says He’ll Beat His Own Fiscal Anchor a Year Early — the PBO Says He’ll Miss It

Investment Executive · investmentexecutive.com ↗

With Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne set to table Carney’s second budget on Thursday, November 5, the two sides are now contradicting each other on the government’s central fiscal promise: the Parliamentary Budget Officer projects the operating budget won’t balance until 2028-29, a year later than the Liberals promised, while Carney maintains the government is on track to hit that target a year early. The PBO’s updated outlook puts the 2026-27 deficit at $63.3 billion, down from a June estimate of $71.8 billion mostly on higher gas-price-linked revenue, but still projects deficits averaging $3 billion a year higher than June’s forecast over the next five years and a rising deficit-to-GDP ratio in two of those years, concluding the government’s own anchors “have imposed limited fiscal discipline.” For bondholders and anyone pricing federal borrowing costs, Thursday’s actual numbers — not either side’s framing of them — are the only reliable tie-breaker.

09
GAZA

Israeli Strikes Kill Eight in Gaza, Including a 4-Year-Old, as Ceasefire-Era Toll Passes 1,570

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Israeli drone strikes killed at least eight Palestinians across Gaza on November 1 — five in an attack on a displaced-persons camp in al-Mawasi that Gaza’s Interior Ministry says targeted police officers, and three more, including a four-year-old boy, in Gaza City’s Rimal neighborhood — with Hamas calling the strikes a “deliberate war crime.” The Israeli military told AP it was targeting militants, and Gaza’s Health Ministry says more than 1,570 Palestinians have been killed since the US-brokered ceasefire took effect roughly a year ago, even as major fighting has largely stopped. For any organization planning Gaza reconstruction financing or logistics, the operative fact is that near-daily strikes continue inside a ceasefire most outside observers describe as holding — treat the truce as a reduction in intensity, not an end to risk.

10
UKRAINE

Zelenskyy Meets Erdogan in Istanbul, Hoping for a US-Russia-Ukraine Trilateral in Abu Dhabi Between November 6 and 10

The Star (Reuters) · thestar.com.my ↗

Zelenskyy said in his Friday night address that “important negotiations will take place in Turkey,” and Turkish officials confirmed he met President Erdogan in Istanbul on Saturday, November 1, as part of a diplomatic push the Ukrainian leader hopes will produce a trilateral meeting of Ukrainian, Russian and American negotiators in Abu Dhabi between November 6 and 10 — a date he describes as a hope, not a confirmed booking. Kyiv continues to insist Europe be included in any settlement talks, a position Washington has not committed to as it continues negotiating energy and territorial terms bilaterally with Moscow. For anyone pricing Ukraine-exposure risk or European energy contracts tied to a peace settlement, the operative fact remains timing: nothing is confirmed before November 6 at the earliest, and the format itself — who is actually in the room — is still unresolved.
