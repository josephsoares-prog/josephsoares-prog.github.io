01
HORMUZ

Iran holds firm on Hormuz conditions as US, Iran and Oman target a Wednesday deal announcement

NBC News · nbcnews.com ↗

Axios reports the US is targeting Wednesday for a deal announcement on the Strait of Hormuz, but Iran insists an Oman-brokered management arrangement will not be enough — full reopening still requires a US withdrawal and other long-sought concessions. Treat any Wednesday headline as a framework, not a reopening: the gap between an announced deal and ships actually moving through Hormuz has widened every time this month. Price shipping and insurance risk on the conditions, not the announcement.

02
CANADA

Washington's 50% tariff on Canadian goods takes effect August 19 as Carney calls talks 'quite firm'

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

The new US tariff hits nearly $20 billion of Canadian imports — dairy, alcohol, electronics, building materials, apparel — even on CUSMA-compliant goods, with no expiry date attached. Prime Minister Carney says talks remain constructive, but Ottawa has not secured relief and the deadline is nine days closer than any plausible Hormuz resolution. This is the nearer, harder shock for North American operators to plan around, not the Gulf story dominating headlines.

03
HUMANITARIAN

Colombia's earthquake death toll climbs past 181 as five cities remain under red alert

NPR · npr.org ↗

Colombia's 7.4-magnitude quake has killed at least 181 people and injured over 2,595, with 195 officially missing — though civilian-run databases put that figure closer to 4,000. Pereira, Cali, Quibdo, Manizales and Armenia remain under the country's highest disaster alert as a new government's rescue and reconstruction capacity is tested in real time. Expect the gap between official and civilian missing-persons counts to widen before it closes, and insurance/reconstruction financing questions to dominate the next two weeks.

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

A Houthi strike killed six people in the Red Sea — the first fatalities there in over a year — while the US separately struck a vessel it says violated the Iran blockade. This is the clearest signal yet that maritime risk is no longer contained to Hormuz: Cosco had just resumed Red Sea sailings despite Houthi threats. Any operator that had priced Red Sea transit as de-risked should reverse that assumption immediately.

Commentary Hooks

A Deal Announcement Is Not a Reopening

Wednesday's targeted Hormuz announcement will likely be a framework agreement on waterway management, not an actual reopening — Iran has repeatedly said Oman-brokered management arrangements don't satisfy its withdrawal and reparations demands. Anyone pricing a near-term normalisation should study the gap between every prior 'deal near' headline this year and what actually happened on the water.

Ottawa's Real Deadline Is Domestic, Not Diplomatic

The August 19 tariff deadline hits Canadian operators before any Hormuz resolution reaches the water, and Ottawa has not secured relief despite talks Carney calls constructive. Canadian business leaders should be building for the harder, nearer date rather than anchoring risk planning to the Gulf story dominating headlines.

The Silent War Over Battery Metals

Congo's critical-minerals rivalry, new US export controls on battery 'black mass,' and India's Parliament-level minerals push all landed this week — the real 2026 resource war isn't oil, it's the metals inside every EV and AI data centre, and it's being fought with export controls and financing deals, not warships.
