01
US MIDTERMS

Democrats Sweep Congress, Flipping the Senate and the House as Jeffries Nears First Black Speakership

The Boston Globe · bostonglobe.com ↗

Democrats flipped seven Republican-held Senate seats — North Carolina, South Carolina, Texas, Ohio, Kansas, Iowa and Maine — reaching 54 seats as of this writing after the AP called Michigan for Abdul El-Sayed, with Alaska, Mississippi, Montana and Nebraska still uncalled, while the House was called for Democrats around 1:30 a.m. ET with the party at 224 seats to Republicans’ 134 and counting still underway. Hakeem Jeffries is poised to become the chamber’s first Black speaker when the new Congress convenes in January. For boards and allocators, Trump’s final two years now arrive with a hostile Congress able to block his agenda and open investigations — a materially different two years than the one priced in a week ago.

02
IRAN WAR

US Strikes Near Hormuz as Iran Calls the Response ‘Not Convincing,’ a Tanker Attack Kills a Seafarer and Brent Craters to $105

CBS News · cbsnews.com ↗

US forces carried out limited defensive strikes on Iranian targets near Qeshm Island on Monday after Iran’s Revolutionary Guard fired on an American aircraft, and Tehran called Washington’s response “not convincing,” with Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei saying the Strait of Hormuz reopens only if the US meets Iran’s seven conditions. The shipping war intensified in parallel — an India-flagged cargo vessel was struck off Oman on Tuesday, killing one of 14 Indian seafarers aboard, while UKMTO logged a record 30 attacks on commercial vessels in October — even as Brent crude fell 7.4% to $105.03 a barrel on hopes a Qatari-mediated deal is close. For anyone pricing Gulf shipping or fuel-hedging risk, de-escalation talk and continued attacks are running simultaneously, and Qatari mediators expect responses from both sides within 36 hours.

03
MARKETS

Wall Street Futures Whipsaw Overnight as Democrats Sweep Congress, Then Calm Once Trump Accepts the Results

Reuters · investing.com ↗

US stock futures swung sharply overnight as results rolled in: Nasdaq-100 futures fell as much as 2.4% and S&P 500 futures dropped 1.4% on the initial Democratic sweep of Congress, before recovering once Trump publicly accepted the results, with Dow E-minis up 0.84% and S&P 500 E-minis up 0.35% by 4:22 a.m. ET. Yields on long-dated Treasuries moved lower on the day, a sign bond markets read the outcome as reducing policy uncertainty rather than adding to it. For anyone managing overnight risk, the lesson is that markets feared contested results more than a Democratic Congress itself.

04
GOVERNOR FLIPS

Democrat Gina Hinojosa Ousts Greg Abbott in Texas After 31 Years of GOP Rule, as Ohio Flips to Amy Acton

The Texas Tribune · texastribune.org ↗

Austin state lawmaker Gina Hinojosa defeated four-term Gov. Greg Abbott late Tuesday, ending 31 years of Republican control of the Texas governor’s mansion and becoming the state’s first Latina governor on a platform of reviving public schools, returning $1,500 per household from the state’s savings account and regulating data centers. Ohio flipped the same night: public-health physician Amy Acton beat biotech billionaire Vivek Ramaswamy despite more than $100 million spent against her and campaign stops by Trump and Vance, becoming the state’s first female governor and the first Democrat to hold the seat in 20 years. For any company weighing a Texas data-center buildout or an Ohio manufacturing incentive, the regulatory and tax assumptions embedded in those plans now need a second look.

CANADA-US TRADE

Carney Says the US Is ‘Asking Less, Offering More’ as Canada Drops Sovereignty Red Lines in Intensifying Trade Talks

The Canadian Press · ca.finance.yahoo.com ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney said Tuesday that trade negotiations with Washington have grown “more intense” since his weekend call with Trump, telling reporters the US is “asking less and offering more” even though “we’re not quite there,” and that Canada has already removed red lines on language, culture and its ability to strike trade deals with other markets. Trump told Fox News a deal is imminent and raised Keystone XL’s construction and tariff-free fertilizer access as discussion points, though neither side has detailed what remains on the tariffs covering roughly $28 billion of Canadian goods since the August talks collapsed. For Canadian exporters still absorbing 50% US tariffs, the open question is timing, not direction — Carney gave no date for his negotiator’s return to Washington.

ANTHROPIC IPO

Anthropic’s Updated IPO Filing Shows a $23.3 Billion Quarterly Loss Alongside $895 Billion in AI Infrastructure Commitments

The Associated Press · abcnews.com ↗

Anthropic’s updated S-1, filed Monday, shows third-quarter revenue of $17 billion with nine-month revenue up thirteenfold to $33.2 billion, but a nine-month net loss of $104 billion that includes a $94 billion non-cash charge tied to financing-agreement revaluations, against $895.4 billion in AI-infrastructure spending commitments stretching to 2045, of which $70 billion is due within the next 12 months. The filing arrived about a week after the original IPO filing, with the company still planning a Nasdaq debut under the ticker ANT this month, though it has not disclosed a share price or how much it expects to raise. For anyone benchmarking AI-sector spending against revenue, the ratio to watch is the $70 billion due next year against a single quarter’s $17 billion in sales.

07
ISRAEL

Arab Parties and Lieberman Line Up Behind Eisenkot, Putting Netanyahu’s Grip on Power in Real Jeopardy

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

The Joint List and Ra’am, the Arab-led parties that together won a record 19 Knesset seats, said Monday they will back Gadi Eisenkot’s opposition bloc to remove Netanyahu, while Avigdor Lieberman also recommended Eisenkot — moves that could let a minority government of fewer than 61 seats push out Netanyahu’s 53-seat bloc against Eisenkot’s 48. President Isaac Herzog will meet all eleven parties’ leaders this week in a livestreamed process before picking whichever candidate he judges most likely to form a government, who then gets 28 days — extendable by 14 — to do so, with the new Knesset sworn in regardless on November 10. For anyone pricing Israeli policy continuity, Eisenkot is now plausibly the frontrunner despite Netanyahu’s larger bloc, a reversal from where the race stood a week ago.

CANADA BUDGET

One Day From Ottawa’s Budget, Canadian Business Wants a Full Tax Review as Carney Still Faces the PBO’s Deficit Warnings

KPMG · kpmg.com ↗

With Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne tabling Thursday’s budget, a KPMG survey of 346 Canadian business owners and executives finds 87% want a comprehensive review of the tax system and 71% expect a business-friendly budget to improve their prospects, with the government’s own “Productivity Mega Deduction” already flagged as a welcome step that business leaders want extended with further tax or financing measures. The same survey found 84% support a special trade partnership with the EU and other stable partners even if it costs more, and 77% backed Ottawa’s summer decision to suspend US trade talks and impose counter-tariffs — a sign business has priced in a longer rupture with Washington than this week’s warmer trade-talk tone might suggest. For anyone modeling Canadian fiscal risk, Thursday’s actual numbers on Build Canada Homes funding and the “AI for All” strategy are the real test, not the pre-budget signaling.

UKRAINE

Zelenskyy Confirms Abu Dhabi Trilateral Talks for November 10–11 as Russia Sends Medinsky, With Europe Shut Out of the Room

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Standing with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz in Berlin, Zelenskyy confirmed that adviser-level talks between Russia, Ukraine and the US will proceed in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11, with the Kremlin confirming a delegation led by former culture minister Vladimir Medinsky, and said he hopes the format opens the way to a leaders-level meeting with Putin for the first time since the 2022 invasion. Merz said plainly “I do not see that happening so far” on Russia engaging seriously in peace talks, even as Zelenskyy said Ukraine needs an additional €70 billion ($77.5 billion) from European partners next year and European governments are expected to be shut out of the Abu Dhabi room entirely. For anyone pricing European energy or reconstruction exposure, the parties actually negotiating Ukraine’s future don’t currently include Europe.

GROCERY DELIVERY M&A

DoorDash’s $17 Billion Instacart Deal Headed for an In-Depth Antitrust Review, Regulatory Analysts Say

MLex · mlex.com ↗

MLex, a regulatory-intelligence service owned by the London Stock Exchange Group, reported October 30 that DoorDash’s proposed $17 billion acquisition of Instacart is likely to draw an in-depth investigation from US federal and state antitrust authorities, with regulators focused on whether the deal eliminates a growing competitor and raises food-delivery prices for consumers already facing high grocery costs. The report names no specific agency or timeline, but the signal is that this will not clear on a routine Hart-Scott-Rodino review alone. For grocers and delivery partners negotiating contract terms before closing, the prospect of an in-depth review — not the announced deal terms — is now the thing to watch.

AI CHIP EXPORTS

US Closes a Chip-Export Loophole, Requiring Licenses for Nvidia and AMD AI Chips Sold to China-Headquartered Firms Anywhere

South China Morning Post · scmp.com ↗

The US Commerce Department posted new guidance Sunday extending license requirements to advanced AI chips — including Nvidia’s Rubin and Blackwell processors and AMD’s MI350X — sold to any entity headquartered in China, even when that entity operates outside the country, closing a loophole that had let Chinese AI firms buy through subsidiaries in places like Malaysia. One industry source estimated hundreds of thousands of chips had moved through that channel over the past year before the rule change. For chipmakers and their resellers, the test now is whether a buyer’s beneficial ownership — not its shipping address — determines whether an export license is required.

PALANTIR EARNINGS

Palantir Tops $2 Billion in Quarterly Revenue for the First Time, but Shares Slide as Contract-Value Growth Slows Sharply

The Motley Fool · fool.com ↗

Palantir’s third-quarter revenue topped $2 billion for the first time and US commercial revenue rose 148% year over year to $984 million, overtaking government revenue for the first time, prompting the company to raise its 2026 sales guidance to $8.53 billion. But total contract value grew just 11% year over year, down sharply from 153% growth in the second quarter, and the stock fell 1.65% to $175.40 on roughly 67% higher volume as investors weighed whether the slowdown signals the AI-contracting boom is cooling. For anyone holding Palantir exposure, the revenue headline is strong, but the contract-value deceleration is the number that moved the stock.

GAZA

Israeli Strikes Kill Five in Gaza as Ceasefire-Era Toll Tops 1,593, With a UN-Backed Envoy Pushing for Faster Reconstruction

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Israeli strikes and a shooting killed five Palestinians across Gaza on Monday — in Khan Younis, al-Musaddar village and Tal al-Hawa — while the Israeli military said only that it “targeted Palestinian fighters in three strikes,” and the Gaza Health Ministry says at least 1,593 people have been killed and 5,748 wounded since the US-brokered ceasefire began roughly a year ago. Nickolay Mladenov, head of the US-established Board of Peace, arrived in Israel the same day seeking permission to bring in tents and push for faster reconstruction, as the UN says nearly two million people still need shelter assistance. For any organization planning Gaza-reconstruction financing or logistics, near-daily strikes inside a ceasefire most observers describe as holding remain the operative risk, not an exception to it.
