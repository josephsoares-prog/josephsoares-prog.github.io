What mattered on Sunday, 18 October 2026

01
HORMUZ

Iran's Hormuz Campaign Crashes Oil Flows as Brent Nears $110

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Gulf crude flows collapsed from 20.8 million barrels on October 8 to just 2.9 million on October 11 after Iran logged 15 attacks on shipping in nine days, reversing September's recovery and pushing Brent to $106.58. CENTCOM says it still controls the strait and moved 19 million barrels on October 13, but Tehran's security chief claims five or six vessels are hit nightly, and an Iranian official says the timing is partly aimed at the US midterms. Any board with Gulf-transit or marine-insurance exposure should budget for sustained triple-digit crude through Q4, not a spike that fades.

02
UKRAINE

Ukraine Ends Trump's Fuel-Deal Truce, Resumes Strikes on Russian Refineries

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Kyiv says it is resuming drone strikes on Russian oil refineries days after Trump signed an executive order waiving Graham Act sanctions to let a US-Russia diesel deal proceed, a move Zelensky has openly criticized as the two governments' relations cool. The reversal shows Washington's energy diplomacy with Moscow has no durable buy-in from Kyiv, which still treats refinery strikes as leverage regardless of White House deal-making. Energy-security planners should treat any US-brokered Russia fuel arrangement as provisional, not a floor under supply risk.

03
TANKERS

Washington Weighs Carve-Out for 'Legal Oil' on Sanctioned Shadow-Fleet Tankers

gCaptain · gcaptain.com ↗

The administration is drafting a Treasury licensing path that would let previously sanctioned shadow-fleet vessels carry non-Russian, non-Iranian crude, aiming to relieve record tanker freight rates that are inflating the cost of US crude for Asian buyers ahead of the midterms. Energy Secretary Chris Wright's team says the goal is cheaper logistics, not sanctions relief for Moscow, but Energy Aspects and shipping-industry sources doubt it moves rates much since EU and UK insurers remain bound by their own rules. Commodity desks should model this as a marginal, not structural, fix to freight costs.

04
FED

Fed's Daly Says More Hikes Likely Needed as Brokerages Pencil In a December Move

Reuters (via Investing.com) · investing.com ↗

San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly told Reuters the central bank will probably need to raise rates further to rein in inflation, but sees no urgency to act at the October 27-28 meeting; major brokerages now expect the next hike in December instead. Coming alongside hot CPI data and oil-driven cost pressure, this is the clearest signal yet that the Fed's tightening cycle isn't over, even as markets had been pricing a pause. Treasurers and credit desks should keep a hike scenario live into year-end rather than assume rates have peaked.

05
MARKETS

AI Financing Anxiety Rattles Wall Street as CoreWeave, Semis Slide Into Earnings Season

Reuters (via The Star) · thestar.com.my ↗

The S&P 500 posted its biggest weekly drop since June and the Philadelphia semiconductor index has fallen 12% this month, as worries about financing the AI buildout hit CoreWeave — which carries roughly $50 billion in debt — and prompted Deutsche Bank and RBC to pull back their tech recommendations. Five AI hyperscalers are still expected to lift capital spending to about $1.2 trillion next year per Goldman Sachs, and futures markets now lean toward the Fed waiting until December to move. Portfolio managers should distinguish this as a financing-quality repricing within AI names, not yet a broad market regime change.

06
OPENAI

OpenAI's Revenue Restated $20B Lower, Dragging the Nasdaq Down 1.25%

Wealth Professional (Reuters data) · wealthprofessional.ca ↗

OpenAI's annualized revenue came in near $50 billion at the end of September, roughly $20 billion below the nearly-$70-billion figure Reuters had previously reported was given to investors, because that earlier number included gross revenue from partners. The Nasdaq fell 1.25% on the news, its worst session since mid-August, with CoreWeave down nearly 8%, Oracle down 5.5% and Nvidia down 2.9%, while the S&P/TSX held roughly flat. Investors and partners sizing AI exposure off OpenAI's self-reported metrics should now demand the net figure, not the headline one.

07
CANADA

Bank of Canada's Oct. 28 Decision Turns 'Live' as Inflation Jumps and Jobs Fall

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

A Reuters poll expects September inflation, out Monday, to hit 3.2% — its fastest pace since December 2023, driven by gasoline and heating oil — even as Canada lost 68,000 jobs in September for a second straight monthly decline. Markets price only about a 34% chance of a quarter-point hike on October 28, down after the weak jobs data, but the Bank has warned it could tighten if the Middle East-driven energy spike feeds into broader prices. Corporate borrowers should treat October 28 as a genuine two-way risk, not a formality.

08
OILSANDS

Cenovus's $5.7B Athabasca Bet Signals Oilsands Consolidation Under Carney's Pro-Oil Pivot

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

Cenovus agreed to buy Athabasca Oil in a cash-and-stock deal worth roughly C$5.7 billion, a 13.4% premium, picking up 45,000 boe/d of thermal production plus full ownership of the Duvernay-area subsidiary; it follows Cenovus's C$8.6 billion MEG Energy purchase a year earlier. The deal reflects growing confidence in oilsands growth under Carney's fossil-fuel-friendly policy shift, though majors still haven't sanctioned the carbon-capture project Carney has tied to a proposed Pacific export pipeline. Expect further consolidation among Alberta's remaining independents as majors move first and ask forgiveness on carbon-capture timing later.

09
CHIPS

Microsoft to Deploy Homegrown Maia Chips at Scale From 2027, Cutting Nvidia Reliance

AFP (via The Star) · thestar.com.my ↗

Microsoft says its Maia 300 chip, launching in 2027, will deliver 40% more AI work per watt than leading systems and more than double its predecessor, with racks holding twice as many chips and TSMC manufacturing the compute components; Maia 200 already runs OpenAI, Microsoft's own, and open-source models inside Copilot. The move won't eliminate Nvidia's training-chip dominance, but it shifts the inference battle — where AMD, Amazon and Google are also pushing products — toward hyperscaler-owned silicon. Any firm pricing long-term AI infrastructure costs around Nvidia's pricing power should start modeling a multi-vendor compute stack by 2027.

10
EXPORTS

Beijing Proposes Sweeping Export Controls on AI Models, Chips and Agents

The Next Web · thenextweb.com ↗

China's commerce and science ministries published a draft adding 23 AI technologies to the national export-control list, covering model weights and source code for systems above 2 trillion parameters or in the global top 10 on performance benchmarks, AI chips at 14nm or below, and computing clusters; a temporary registration rule already took effect October 16, with comments on the full draft open until November 15. Open-source tech and published research are exempt, and Beijing says the rules target no specific country, but the move hands China the same chokepoint leverage over frontier AI that Washington has used against it. Multinationals building on Chinese-origin frontier models should map licensing exposure now, before the November 15 comment window closes.

11
TRADE

Canada-US Tariff War Frozen at 50%; Washington Says No New Talks Planned

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Dollar-for-dollar retaliatory tariffs that Prime Minister Mark Carney imposed on US steel, dairy, appliances and other goods remain in force against the US's own 50% levy on roughly $20 billion of Canadian exports, after talks collapsed when Carney called Washington's terms 'uneconomic, unfair.' US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer has said no new negotiations are planned, despite the Business Roundtable urging both sides back to the table. Exporters on either side of the border should plan for the current tariff wall to persist through year-end rather than betting on a near-term reset.

12
VENEZUELA

Venezuela's Acting President Touts $76B Oil-Investment Pledge as Transition Deepens

Reuters (via Investing.com) · investing.com ↗

Acting President Delcy Rodriguez announced Venezuela expects nearly $76 billion in oil investment over five years under newly signed accords, including a US bilateral deal to drill 250 rigs across 17 fields, as part of 61 agreements signed since Maduro's January ouster; Zulia state output has reportedly risen from 70,000 to 370,000 barrels a day. Venezuela holds the world's largest proven reserves but produces far below decade-ago levels after years of underinvestment and US sanctions, so these pledges are a bet on sanctions relief translating into real capital, not just headlines. Energy investors should watch for actual rig counts and lifted sanctions before pricing in Venezuelan supply growth.

13
JUSTICE

Maduro and Wife Plead Not Guilty to New US Torture Charges in Manhattan Court

Reuters (via West Hawaii Today) · westhawaiitoday.com ↗

Nicolas Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, pleaded not guilty October 15 to a new US indictment accusing them of conspiring to torture political opponents, each facing a mandatory minimum 50 years if convicted; the trial start has moved to June 2027. Captured in a January 3 US raid on their Caracas home and already facing drug-trafficking charges, the couple's prolonged US prosecution keeps Venezuela's legal transition unsettled even as acting leader Delcy Rodriguez courts foreign oil capital. Firms weighing Venezuela re-entry should price in years, not months, of legal and political overhang from the Maduro proceedings.

14
TAIWAN

China Sanctions Taiwan Allies' Ministers in Paraguay, Belize — a First

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Beijing barred Belize's foreign minister and Paraguay's information minister from entering China and froze their assets there, branding them 'anti-China politicians' for backing their countries' diplomatic ties with Taiwan — the first time China has sanctioned individual officials from Taiwan's allies this way. Taiwan called the move 'preposterous' interference in other nations' sovereignty, while Beijing gave no specific reason for targeting these two. Any firm with operations in Taiwan's remaining diplomatic allies should treat personal sanctions on host-government officials as a new tool in Beijing's pressure playbook.

SECURITY

Taiwan on Alert as China Runs Week-Long Live-Fire Drills Near Its Coast

Taipei Times · taipeitimes.com ↗

Taiwan's defence ministry is monitoring Chinese live-fire drills across five no-sail zones from the South China Sea to Liaoning this week — none directly around Taiwan itself — after Beijing condemned President William Lai's National Day speech as seeking 'independence through force.' Defence Minister Wellington Koo says the coast guard will lead in expelling Chinese militia vessels from Taiwan's waters, with navy support, continuing daily Chinese military activity that hasn't escalated to full war games since December 2025's $11 billion US arms sale. Firms with Taiwan Strait shipping or supply-chain exposure should keep contingency plans current rather than wait for a dramatic escalation to act.

16
ISRAEL

Israel Heads to Oct. 27 Vote as Netanyahu's Coalition Options Fray

Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

With Israel's election ten days out, opposition figures Avigdor Liberman and Gadi Eisenkot are trading accusations over whether an anti-Netanyahu bloc would govern with him after the vote, while Yair Lapid says he doesn't trust Netanyahu not to launch an Iran strike that could disrupt the election itself. Settler violence against Palestinians has risen sharply ahead of the vote, and smaller parties like Yoseph Haddad's are being courted by Likud with ministry offers tied to military-conscription guarantees. Investors and counterparties with Israel exposure should treat October 27-November as a period of genuine coalition uncertainty, including possible security-driven disruption.

17
GAZA

Gaza's Ceasefire 'In Name Only' a Year On, as Israel Holds Two-Thirds of the Strip

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

More than 100 humanitarian and rights groups say Gaza's ceasefire exists 'in name only' a year in, with 1,493 killed and 5,299 wounded since the truce began; Israel now controls nearly two-thirds of the Strip, well above the roughly 50% it was meant to hold before further withdrawal, and the promised international stabilization force still hasn't deployed. About 44% of essential medicines are out of stock and none of Gaza's 36 hospitals is fully operational, with over 18,500 patients needing medical evacuation. Any organization planning Gaza reconstruction or aid logistics should assume the stabilization and withdrawal milestones in the original ceasefire framework remain unmet, not pending.

IMMIGRATION

Carney Signals Higher Immigration Levels for November Despite Housing Backlash

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

Immigration Minister Lena Diab is signalling that Canada's immigration levels plan, due in November, will raise targets again after population growth slowed sharply over the past two years, even as housing affordability remains a top public concern. The signal comes as Canada separately expands Express Entry points for high-wage workers, suggesting Ottawa is prioritizing skilled-worker inflows even while the optics of 'more immigration' collide with an affordability narrative that has already strained Carney's coalition. Employers planning 2027 workforce strategy should watch the November levels plan for the skilled-stream details, not just the headline target number.

19
M&A

Copart Completes $1.9B ACV Auctions Buyout, Cementing Vehicle-Auction Consolidation

Auto Remarketing · autoremarketing.com ↗

Copart closed its $10.50-a-share, roughly $1.9 billion cash tender offer for ACV Auctions on October 16 after about 68% of shares were tendered, ending ACV's run on the NYSE; it will now operate as an independent Copart subsidiary under its existing leadership. The deal, announced just five weeks earlier on September 10, signals how fast consolidation is moving in vehicle remarketing as salvage and wholesale-auction platforms converge. Dealers and insurers using either platform should expect integration-driven changes to auction terms within two to three quarters, not years.
