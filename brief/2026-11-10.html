01
UKRAINE PEACE TALKS

US, Russia and Ukraine Open Third Round of Abu Dhabi Talks on Security Guarantees as Putin and Zelensky Stay Away

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Senior US, Russian and Ukrainian delegations met in Abu Dhabi Tuesday for a third round of talks this year — Washington represented by envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, Kyiv by intelligence chief Rustem Umerov, Moscow by Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky — with discussions centered on security guarantees for Ukraine, including a US proposal modeled on NATO’s Article 5, before any territorial settlement. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the process “has a ways to go,” while the Kremlin’s Dmitry Peskov made clear Russia does not want European officials — who are sending representatives anyway — inside the formal trilateral format. For anyone pricing reconstruction, sanctions relief or European energy exposure, the fact that security guarantees, not territory, are the stated sticking point is the detail that will decide how fast any deal can actually move.

02
IRAN WAR

Iran Says Mediator Travel Paused After US Midterms as CENTCOM Launches ‘Task Force Iron Gate’ and IRGC Lifts Missile-Range Limit

CBS News · cbsnews.com ↗

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said mediator travel to advance US-Iran talks has paused following the shock of the US midterm results, telling reporters Saturday that “American voters have made their choice; it’s time for the White House to make its own.” The pause comes as US Central Command announced a new “Task Force Iron Gate” to tighten the Gulf blockade — disabling a cargo ship it said tried to breach it — and as Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said it has lifted its self-imposed 1,200-mile missile-range restriction, dismissing the new task force as “merely a wordplay,” even as Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the two sides are “not close to a breakthrough.” For anyone pricing Gulf shipping or insurance risk, a diplomatic pause paired with an unrestricted Iranian missile posture is a materially different risk setting than the stalemate of the past two weeks.

03
OIL MARKETS

Brent Falls to Around $93 as Trump Calls New Iran Strikes ‘Unlikely,’ Treasurer Warns Prices Won’t Normalize Before 2030

ABC News Australia · abc.net.au ↗

Brent crude has fallen to roughly $93 a barrel, down sharply from about $112 a week earlier, after President Trump said new US strikes on Iran were “unlikely,” easing fears of an escalating Gulf war on top of the market-friendly reading of this week’s Democratic midterm sweep. RACQ economist Ian Jeffreys said prices “fell significantly last week, hitting a low on Wednesday before a slight rebound,” while UNSW’s Daniel Prior said “we’re seeing the $90s as sort of the more typical price of trade” now; Australia’s treasurer separately warned prices may not return to pre-crisis norms before 2030. For anyone hedging Q1 energy costs, the gap between this price relief and an unresolved Hormuz blockade and still-live IRGC threats is the thing to watch, not the headline number itself.

04
MARKETS

Wall Street Rallies as Last Week’s AI Rout Reverses, but Nvidia Falls on Board Probe Into Jensen Huang

Reuters via Honolulu Star-Advertiser · staradvertiser.com ↗

The Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq all rallied Monday — up 1.27%, 1.48% and 1.87% respectively in early trading — reversing most of last week’s AI-driven selloff, with Morgan Stanley and UBS calling that rout “overdone” and arguing heavy data-center regulation is unlikely under a Democrat-controlled Congress. Nvidia was the exception, falling nearly 6% after reports emerged of an internal board investigation into whether CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, raising questions about his position at the world’s second most valuable company; one analyst called the news “the cherry on top for investors looking to pull out of AI-linked stocks.” For boards still treating the AI trade as one basket, the split between a broad market relief rally and a company-specific governance shock at its most important name is the signal to track into Tuesday’s CPI print.

AI INFRASTRUCTURE

Marvell Strikes $115 Billion All-Stock Deal for Lumentum to Bet on Optical Networking for AI Data Centers

SEC EDGAR (Marvell 8-K) · sec.gov ↗

Marvell Technology agreed to acquire optical-components maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal valuing Lumentum at roughly $115 billion in implied enterprise value, or about $1,132 a share — a 24% premium — at a fixed exchange ratio of 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share, with Marvell shareholders holding about 67% of the combined company. Marvell CEO Matt Murphy said “AI infrastructure is undergoing a fundamental architectural shift toward optical connectivity,” while Lumentum’s Michael Hurlston pointed to a “$550 billion market opportunity”; the deal isn’t expected to close until the first half of 2028, pending shareholder and regulatory approval. For anyone tracking AI-capex exposure beyond the chip names themselves, this is a direct bet that optical interconnects, not just GPUs, become the next capacity bottleneck.

06
MEDIA M&A

Fox Completes $22 Billion Acquisition of Roku, Taking the Streaming Platform Private

SEC EDGAR (Fox 8-K) · sec.gov ↗

Fox Corporation completed its roughly $22 billion acquisition of Roku on November 6, with each Roku share converted into $95.30 in cash plus 0.9799 Fox Class A shares — terms adjusted from the original $96.00-cash-plus-0.9693-share structure announced in June — leaving former Fox shareholders with about 74% of the combined company and Roku shareholders with about 26%. Roku has ceased trading on Nasdaq and is now a wholly owned Fox subsidiary; Fox expects roughly $400 million in annual run-rate cost synergies within two years. For media and ad-tech watchers, the deal hands Fox direct control of one of the largest connected-TV distribution platforms just as streaming completes its shift from a content fight to a distribution-and-data fight.

07
VENEZUELA

ExxonMobil Signs Deal With Venezuela’s PDVSA in First Step Toward Returning to the Country After 20 Years

Reuters via BOE Report · boereport.com ↗

ExxonMobil signed a non-binding memorandum of understanding with Venezuela’s state oil company PDVSA to explore upstream opportunities, calling it “a meaningful first step” while citing the need for “stable policies, clear regulations, strong investment protections and competitive returns” before any binding contract follows. Exxon exited Venezuela roughly 20 years ago after Hugo Chávez’s government expropriated its assets, and the deal is widely read as a win for the Trump administration’s push to put US companies at the center of reshaping Venezuela’s energy sector since Maduro’s ouster earlier this year. For anyone pricing Venezuelan crude supply returning to global markets, a non-binding MOU is a long way from barrels — but it is the clearest signal yet of which companies will be first in line when contracts are signed.

08
CANADA JOBS

Canada’s Unemployment Rate Rises to 6.7% in October as Private-Sector Hiring Keeps Shrinking

Statistics Canada · statcan.gc.ca ↗

Statistics Canada reported the unemployment rate rose to 6.7% in October, up 0.2 points from September, as employment was little changed overall (-4,700) after two straight sharp monthly declines, marking a third consecutive drop in the employment rate to 60.5%. Private-sector employment fell by 53,000 while the public sector added 50,000, wholesale and retail trade lost 29,000 jobs, and youth unemployment climbed 0.7 points to 13.7%; average hourly wages rose 2.2% year over year to $37.89. For anyone pricing the Bank of Canada’s next move, a labour market where private hiring keeps shrinking even as headline job losses stabilize is a weaker picture than the stable topline number alone suggests.

CANADA-CHINA TRADE

Canada and China Extend Canola-for-Steel Trade Deal to 2028 as Canadian Exports Surge

BNN Bloomberg (The Canadian Press) · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

Canada and China extended their January 2026 market-access deal — Chinese tariff relief on Canadian canola meal, peas, lobster and crab in exchange for Canadian market access for certain Chinese steel and aluminum products — by two years to the end of 2028, Trade Minister Maninder Sidhu announced, as canola seed exports to China have jumped 84% and canola meal more than doubled since the deal took effect in March. China remains Canada’s second-largest single-country trading partner at $125.7 billion in two-way trade in 2025, and Ottawa is targeting a 50% increase in exports to China by 2030; the timing clears an irritant ahead of a visit from Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng and Prime Minister Carney’s planned trip to the APEC summit in Shenzhen. For exporters and processors in the affected sectors, a two-year extension rather than a short-term rollover is the detail that lets them actually plan production around it.

CANADA-US TRADE

Carney Says He Hasn’t Spoken With Democrats on Repealing Canada Tariffs Despite Midterm Wins

Global News · globalnews.ca ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney said Friday he has not spoken with Democrats about legislation to end US tariffs on Canada following their sweep of the House and Senate, telling reporters “the short answer is no,” while adding Canada “will continue to work with the Americans to get a deal.” Newly elected Democrats have called for repealing the Section 338 tariffs — currently 50% on more than $20 billion of Canadian goods — but since they were imposed by executive order, analysts note Congress cannot remove them unilaterally and a prior Senate resolution had no lasting effect; University of Calgary analyst Carlo Dade called the Democrats’ promises “symbolic.” For anyone modeling tariff relief into 2027 planning, the gap between Democratic rhetoric and their actual legislative power to reverse these tariffs is the thing to price, not the headline political win.

NORTH KOREA

North Korea Fires Fourth Round of Missiles in a Week, Tests New Air-Defense System

NK News · nknews.org ↗

South Korea’s Joint Chiefs of Staff detected North Korean missile launches from the Sondok area on November 4 — the fourth such launch event in a week — while state media KCNA said separately that Pyongyang tested an advanced air-defense missile and ran a “firepower subunit” live-fire drill it called part of “obligatory activities,” without disclosing the weapon types involved. US Pacific Command said it was aware of the launches and consulting with allies; analyst Leif-Eric Easley said the tests “seriously complicate engagement” amid speculation about a possible renewed Trump-Kim summit. For anyone tracking North Korean weapons development, a fourth launch inside a week signals a sustained testing tempo, not an isolated provocation.

CYBERSECURITY

FBI Blames North Korea for Record $6.3 Billion Binance Crypto Heist

Al Jazeera / Reuters · aljazeera.com ↗

The FBI attributed the theft of $6.3 billion in virtual assets from exchange Binance to North Korean state hackers it identified as the “TraderTraitor” group — the same actors blamed for last year’s $1.5 billion Bybit theft — saying stolen funds were being converted into Ethereum and laundered “in stages.” Binance co-CEO Richard Teng called it a “complex attack” involving a compromised laptop and evidence-wiping malware, and said the exchange has already replaced about 90% of the stolen funds using its insurance reserves and corporate treasury, with Binance posting that it “will absorb the financial impact of these stolen funds, not our users.” North Korea has denied the allegation. For anyone exposed to crypto-exchange counterparty risk, the speed and scale of Binance’s self-funded replacement is the more decision-relevant fact here than the headline theft figure itself.
