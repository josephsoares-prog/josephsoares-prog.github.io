01
HORMUZ

Iran's Supreme National Security Council sets sweeping new conditions for reopening Hormuz — end the war, withdraw US forces, pay reparations, unfreeze assets

NPR · npr.org ↗

Tehran's Supreme National Security Council has laid out its actual terms for reopening the strait: the United States must lift its naval blockade and sanctions, withdraw military forces from the region, pay war reparations, and release frozen Iranian assets. That is a demand to end the war outright, not a technical arrangement to let tankers through. Iran's foreign minister says negotiators are closing in on a temporary transit corridor, but Tehran has been explicit that any such arrangement would not amount to reopening the strait. For anyone pricing a near-term de-escalation, the message is that the shipping lane is now leverage in a much larger negotiation over the war's terms, not a separate technical track that can be settled on its own.

02
HORMUZ

The UAE accuses Iran's Revolutionary Guard of piracy after a missile strikes an ADNOC tanker in Hormuz — the latest of more than a dozen attacks on the state carrier's fleet since February

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Abu Dhabi says an Iranian missile hit an ADNOC tanker transiting Hormuz early Saturday; there were no casualties, but ADNOC says more than a dozen of its vessels have now been attacked since the US-Israeli war on Iran began in February, with one crew member killed and twenty wounded. The UAE Foreign Ministry called the strike an act of piracy by the IRGC and demanded Iran halt attacks and reopen the strait unconditionally. The incident lands the same day Iran's Supreme National Security Council issued its toughest reopening conditions yet — a reminder the shipping lane remains an active combat zone even as diplomats describe a deal as close.

03
HORMUZ

Houthi strikes on Marib kill two and wound fourteen in the deadliest 48 hours in months, a day after roughly 30 Yemeni troops died in coordinated camp strikes, as the UN warns of the widest war risk since the 2022 truce

NBC News · nbcnews.com ↗

The Iran-aligned Houthis fired missiles and drones into displacement camps and residential areas of Marib on Friday, a day after strikes on government military camps in Marib and Hadramawt killed roughly 30 troops — one of the deadliest escalations of the year. UN Special Envoy Hans Grundberg says the violence has put Yemen at its greatest risk of renewed large-scale war since the 2022 truce, warning it could draw the country deeper into the regional Hormuz conflict. This is the widening-war scenario risk desks have been pricing since February: a second active front that complicates any US-Iran-Oman settlement and keeps Gulf shipping insurance elevated regardless of what happens at the strait itself.

04
POLITICS

The US Senate passes a bipartisan stopgap funding bill 90-6, keeping the government funded through December 11 and blocking a $1 billion request for Trump's proposed new battleship class

Axios · axios.com ↗

The Senate voted 90-6 early Saturday to fund the government through December 11, pushing the next shutdown deadline past the November midterms and averting an October 1 lapse. The bill largely holds funding flat but carries negotiated riders: it blocks a proposed rule expanding political appointees' role in reviewing federal grants, bars diverting funds to Border Patrol, and rejects the White House's $1 billion request to start work on a new battleship class. The House must still concur after its August recess, but the lopsided vote signals a genuine bipartisan off-ramp rather than a bridge to another standoff — reducing near-term fiscal-policy tail risk into year-end.

05
MACRO

Odds of a September Fed rate hike collapse after the weak July payrolls print, unwinding weeks of speculation that war-driven energy inflation would force tightening

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Futures markets had been building in real odds of a September rate hike as Hormuz-driven oil prices pushed inflation risk higher, but the surprise 23,000 July payrolls decline flipped that calculus: CME futures now point toward a cut being the base case, not a hike. The reversal matters because it shows the Fed's reaction function is currently more sensitive to labour weakness than to energy-driven inflation — a call that will be tested hard if oil holds its war premium into September's inflation prints. Treat the next CPI release, not the jobs number alone, as the real swing factor for whether cut expectations survive contact with the data.

06
CHINA

China's consumer inflation cools to a six-month low of 0.5% year-on-year in July as easing oil prices from the Hormuz standoff feed through, even as producer prices climb 3.5%

Xinhua · news.cn ↗

China's CPI rose just 0.5% year-on-year in July, down from 1.0% in June and the softest reading in six months, with prices actually down 0.1% month-on-month; state media links the cooling directly to easing oil costs as the Hormuz crisis's worst price spikes recede. Producer prices, by contrast, were up 3.5% year-on-year, a divergence that points to input-cost pressure not yet reaching consumers. For a government targeting durable domestic demand, a softening CPI narrows Beijing's room to normalise stimulus and adds to the case for continued policy support — while the PPI print flags that any renewed spike in Gulf shipping costs would hit Chinese producers before it hits Chinese consumers.

07
CANADA

Ottawa and Washington trade written proposals as Canada races to strike a deal before Trump's threatened 50% tariff on $20 billion of goods across 554 tariff lines takes effect August 19

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

Canada's Dominic LeBlanc and Janice Charette met US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer this week, exchanging written proposals covering alcohol, dairy and autos in exchange for relief on Section 232 steel and aluminum tariffs, including a revived quota proposal. Officials describe the talks as tense and say a deal by August 19 is not assured, with Trump able to knock negotiations off track at any moment. The stakes are concrete: Section 338 tariffs would add 50% duties on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods across 554 tariff lines, from hockey sticks to cement, at 12:01 a.m. on August 19 if no deal lands — treat the date as a live cliff, not a formality, for any cross-border supply chain.

RUSSIA

Ukraine strikes a refinery in Russia's Krasnodar region as Russian drones kill a three-year-old boy and his grandparents near Kyiv and three more in Belgorod

The Moscow Times · themoscowtimes.com ↗

Ukrainian drones hit an oil refinery in Russia's Krasnodar region overnight, part of Kyiv's continuing campaign against Russian refining capacity, while Russian drone strikes killed a three-year-old boy and his grandparents in the Kyiv region and three more people in Belgorod, with 25 injured. Neither side is de-escalating the deep-strike campaign even as diplomatic energy is consumed by the Hormuz track; refinery strikes on both sides are a direct input into global product-market tightness layered on top of the Gulf premium. The read for energy allocators: Russian and Iranian supply risk are now compounding rather than substituting for each other.

Ukraine's president won fresh commitments from Serbian officials on a visit that underscored how far Belgrade has drifted from its historic alignment with Moscow, long the most Russia-sympathetic capital in the EU's neighbourhood. The shift matters beyond symbolism: Serbia sits astride Balkan transit routes and EU accession politics, and any further loosening of Russian influence there narrows Moscow's remaining diplomatic footholds in Europe. Watch Serbian energy-supply and defence-procurement decisions over the coming months as the concrete test of whether this is a durable realignment or a one-visit gesture.

Russian security services raided several Moscow-based crypto exchanges, alleging the platforms moved stolen funds to Ukraine; independent reporting says some of those detained believed they were themselves working with or for the FSB, pointing to a murky internal counter-fraud or influence operation rather than a clean law-enforcement action. The episode is a useful data point on how contested and opaque Russia's domestic financial-crime and sanctions-evasion enforcement has become under wartime pressure — treat official Russian framing of any single financial-crime case as unreliable without independent corroboration.

Defense News · defensenews.com ↗

Reporting on North Korea's expanding contribution to Russia's war — munitions production and continued combat-support roles — shows the relationship has moved from emergency stopgap to a durable wartime supply chain. For sanctions and non-proliferation policy, the practical effect is that North Korea is extracting technology transfer and hard currency in exchange, strengthening a regime under maximum sanctions pressure. Any Ukraine settlement calculus needs to account for a Moscow that has meaningfully diversified its materiel and manpower base away from pure domestic production.

DEFENCE

Rheinmetall says ramping up ATACMS production will take time even as the US works to rebuild depleted stockpiles for both its own forces and Ukraine

Defense News · defensenews.com ↗

The German defence manufacturer says scaling long-range ATACMS missile output is a multi-year proposition, not a switch that can be flipped quickly, as Washington tries to rebuild stocks drawn down by both Ukraine transfers and Middle East operations. The constraint is structural — specialised propellant and warhead production lines, not just capital — meaning Western long-range fires capacity stays tight through at least the next several budget cycles. For defence planners, this argues for prioritising which theatre gets scarce ATACMS allocations rather than assuming supply catches up with demand soon.

DEFENCE

Janes · janes.com ↗

Janes reports the PLA Ground Force has moved the KVD002 unmanned combat aerial vehicle from development into operational service, giving ground formations an organic armed-drone strike capability rather than relying solely on air force or naval aviation assets. For regional military planners, it is another increment in the PLA's push toward multi-domain integration at lower echelons.

SHIPPING

Chinese supertankers are rerouting around Africa to avoid Red Sea attacks, adding weeks and cost to crude shipments as the regional conflict reshapes global tanker routing

Caixin Global · caixinglobal.com ↗

Caixin reports Chinese-linked supertankers are increasingly taking the long route around the Cape of Good Hope rather than transiting the Red Sea, adding significant time and fuel cost per voyage to avoid the ongoing attack risk in that corridor. The rerouting is a quiet but material cost now embedded in global crude logistics, compounding the direct Hormuz war premium already priced into Brent — freight and insurance costs across multiple Middle East-adjacent chokepoints are moving together, not independently. Expect this to show up in landed crude costs for Asian refiners well before it shows up in headline benchmark prices.

Reuters · reuters.com ↗

Reuters reports Typhoon Dolphin is tracking toward China's east coast with flood warnings already issued, threatening disruption to a coastal corridor that includes major container and bulk ports. Storm-driven port closures compound an already stressed global shipping picture shaped by Red Sea rerouting and Hormuz-linked tanker risk, meaning even a short weather closure now has less slack in the system to absorb than it would have a year ago. Logistics planners should model port-dwell delays on top of, not instead of, the existing geopolitical routing premium.

China's newest AI models unsettled Wall Street's assumptions about US compute dominance, but analysts say the same breakthroughs could turbocharge Chinese industrial productivity

South China Morning Post · scmp.com ↗

SCMP reports that recent Chinese AI model releases have rattled US investors' confidence that American firms hold a durable compute and capability lead, echoing — on a smaller scale — the market shock of prior Chinese model breakthroughs. TechCrunch reports that permitting documents for a planned Amazon data centre project show projected emissions that would make it the largest single climate-polluting facility in the country, underscoring how AI-driven data-centre buildout is now a first-order emissions and energy-planning issue rather than a rounding error. Utilities and regulators in the host region face direct pressure to either accommodate the power demand with fossil generation — locking in emissions — or delay the buildout pending clean capacity, a trade-off playing out simultaneously across multiple US data-centre hubs. This is the leading edge of a policy fight that will recur at every major AI infrastructure siting decision for the next several years.

HUMANITARIAN

The UN's deputy chief warns Sudan is 'losing another generation' to war as the conflict grinds into its third year with no political resolution in sight

UN News · news.un.org ↗

UN Deputy Secretary-General Amina Mohammed delivered a stark warning that Sudan's war is costing the country an entire generation — through displacement, interrupted education, and famine risk — with humanitarian access still severely constrained. Sudan remains one of the world's largest and least-resourced displacement crises, competing for donor attention and funding against Gaza, Ukraine and now the Hormuz-region conflict, and international attention has structurally thinned as the war has continued. For anyone tracking humanitarian-funding allocation or African stability risk, Sudan is the crisis most likely to be underfunded relative to its actual scale in the current news environment.

Israel is accused of using archaeological excavation and heritage designations at West Bank sites to entrench settlement claims, a legal flashpoint independent of the Gaza front

BBC · bbc.co.uk ↗

BBC reporting details accusations that Israeli authorities are using archaeological digs and heritage site designations in the occupied West Bank as a tool to assert and legitimise territorial claims, a practice critics say blurs the line between genuine historical preservation and de facto annexation. The dispute sits squarely in international humanitarian law territory — the law of occupation restricts an occupying power's authority over cultural and territorial assets — and adds another legal front to Israel's West Bank posture that is separate from, but politically linked to, the Gaza conflict. This is a slow-moving but durable story for anyone tracking the accumulating legal case around West Bank annexation.

RFI · rfi.fr ↗

RFI reports the US administration is rolling back planned federal protections for workers exposed to extreme heat, reversing a rulemaking track that had been advancing under the prior administration. Commentary Hooks

The Strait Is Now a Peace Treaty, Not a Shipping Lane

Iran's stated conditions for reopening Hormuz — a full US withdrawal, war reparations, an unfrozen asset base — show the waterway has become a bargaining chip for ending the war outright, not a technical fix for global shipping. Anyone modelling a quick reopening on the strength of 'talks are close' headlines is modelling the wrong negotiation entirely.

Two Fronts, One War: Marib and the Strait Are the Same Conflict

The deadliest Houthi escalation in months landed in Yemen the same week Tehran hardened its Hormuz terms, and treating them as separate stories understates how far the regional war has widened. The real planning question for the rest of 2026 is not whether the strait alone reopens, but whether this becomes a durable two-front architecture that outlasts any narrow Hormuz deal.

Rate Cuts Are Back — But Not for the Reason That Usually Justifies Them

Markets spent weeks pricing a hawkish Fed move on war-driven oil inflation; one weak jobs report flipped that to near-certain cut odds. A Fed cutting into labour weakness while an active war keeps a floor under oil prices is a fundamentally different regime than a Fed cutting because inflation is genuinely tamed, and investors pricing the two scenarios identically are carrying hidden risk.
