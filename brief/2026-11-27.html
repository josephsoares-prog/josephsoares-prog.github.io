BUSINESS & M&A

Altria Confirms It Is Weighing a Cash Bid for Imperial Brands

Imperial Brands (regulatory filing via Investegate/RNS) · investegate.co.uk ↗

Altria confirmed Thursday, November 26 that it is considering an all-cash takeover offer for UK-listed Imperial Brands (Winston, Davidoff, JPS) and is separately in talks with an unnamed third party to acquire part of Imperial’s business; Imperial shares jumped as much as 17.6% to 3,562p, a roughly £26.8 billion market value, on the news. Under UK Takeover Panel rules Altria must declare a firm offer or walk away by 5pm London time on December 24—a hard four-week deadline that forces clarity on what could become the largest global tobacco consolidation in years, with direct read-through for industry M&A multiples and antitrust posture on both sides of the Atlantic.

ENERGY & SANCTIONS

US-Backed Consortium Moves to Absorb Lukoil’s Global Assets

The Associated Press (via BNN Bloomberg) · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

A consortium led by U.S. asset manager Eldridge—with the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation, UAE’s Catena Investment Holding, and New York’s Xtellus Capital Partners—agreed November 26 to acquire Lukoil International’s non-Russian assets: oil and gas fields, refineries and pipelines across 11 countries producing roughly 350,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day. The deal swaps restricted Russian Lukoil shares for the unit at zero cash cost to Moscow, just ahead of a Treasury divestment deadline, but Rep. Gregory Meeks, the top Democrat on House Foreign Affairs, says the DFC’s role breaches a congressional ban on U.S. investment in Russia—a first live template for how Western capital absorbs Russian-linked energy assets that boards tracking sanctions risk should watch closely.

UKRAINE

Washington Delays 48 Patriot Interceptors as Russia Rejects Ukraine Security Guarantees

Institute for the Study of War / Critical Threats Project · criticalthreats.org ↗

The Kremlin has rejected nearly every substantive security guarantee on the table for Ukraine—including any foreign troop presence—while Washington has delayed shipment of at least 48 NATO-funded Patriot interceptors, the system that reliably stops Russian ballistic missiles, per ISW’s November 25 assessment. Ukraine has struck 15 Russian oil refineries this month alone, forcing Moscow’s deputy prime minister to acknowledge fuel-market strain, even as Zaporizhzhia’s nuclear plant lost external power for the 17th time this year. For boards exposed to European energy, defense primes or Russia-linked commodity flows: no near-term ceasefire is coming, and continued volatility in refined-product markets and nuclear-safety risk should be priced in through the winter.

GAZA

Israel Confirms Targeted-Killing Campaign as Trump’s Peace Board Accuses Netanyahu of Sabotage

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

Prime Minister Netanyahu and Defense Minister Katz jointly confirmed on November 26 that an overnight strike killing Hamas economic chief Khaled Abu Nada was “part of a series of targeted killings,” vowing forces would “continue to thwart and exact retribution against every terrorist.” A day earlier, the Times of Israel reported that the Board of Peace—the Jared Kushner-led body Trump created to oversee the Gaza truce—had publicly accused Netanyahu’s own government of blocking implementation of the Hamas disarmament plan it exists to run. That rift between Washington’s peace architecture and Israel’s military campaign means the October 2025 truce is now a ceasefire in name only; expect elevated escalation risk for Gaza-adjacent supply chains, regional aviation and Eastern Mediterranean energy assets into the new year.

ENERGY & OIL

Brent Hits Three-Week High After Fatal Strike on Saudi Jazan Refinery

Reuters (via Arab News) · arabnews.com ↗

Brent crude hit $96.61 a barrel Friday—its highest since November 6—after a projectile struck Saudi Aramco’s Jazan refinery on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker, days after Houthi forces claimed strikes on the Ras Tanura and Jeddah facilities; WTI jumped 2.5% to $85.78. Two Reuters sources say OPEC+ will likely hold output targets steady through at least the first quarter of 2027 when it meets this Sunday, November 29. Analysts at ANZ say Brent only breaks $100 on confirmed supply losses, not infrastructure scares—so this is still a sentiment premium, not barrels actually coming off the market, and hedging desks should price it that way.

MARKETS & MACRO

S&P 500 Falls for a Fifth Straight Session as Hawkish Fed Minutes Hit Markets

Dow Jones Newswires (via Morningstar) · morningstar.com ↗

The S&P 500 closed at 7,323.63 on November 18, down for a fifth consecutive session and 6.33% below its October 6 record close, as traders unwound rate-cut bets following the newly hawkish Fed minutes; the dollar index jumped to 104, its highest since April 2025, the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.07%, and gold fell 3.2% to a roughly 14-month low. The “rate cuts are coming” tailwind that lifted equities through most of 2025 is now actively unwinding—treat higher-for-longer financing costs as the planning assumption into year-end.

TECHNOLOGY & AI

Dell Posts 103% Revenue Surge as AI Server Sales Triple, Raises Full-Year Outlook

Dell Technologies (SEC Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1) · sec.gov ↗

Dell reported fiscal Q3 2027 revenue of $54.9 billion, up 103% year-over-year, driven by AI-optimized server revenue of $22.0 billion, up 291%; it raised full-year guidance to $202.0 billion in revenue (up 78%) and $78.0 billion in AI server revenue (up 216%), with Q4 revenue guided to $56.3 billion. This is hard confirmation—not vendor hype—that enterprise AI-infrastructure build-out is still accelerating into year-end, a demand signal boards should weigh against AI-capex-bubble concerns when planning 2027 technology budgets.

TRADE & TARIFFS

US Trade Czar Says Canada’s China Deal Breeds "Concern" as Talks Go "Backwards"

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told Fox News the U.S. and Canada are “fairly close” to a trade deal but that Ottawa’s tariff-relief agreement with Beijing—extended November 17 to cover canola, pork, seafood and Chinese steel and aluminum access through 2028—“gives some concern and some worry,” adding talks have gone “a little bit backwards.” For executives with cross-border supply chains, this signals Washington may use Canada’s China ties as leverage to delay a permanent tariff deal, even as the incoming Democratic House majority is pushing to end the tariff war—a split Carney’s team can work against Washington at the table.

Workday (SEC Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1) · sec.gov ↗

Workday reported fiscal Q3 2027 revenue of $2.705 billion, up 11.2%, with AI products generating nearly $150 million in new annual contract value—roughly triple the year-ago level—and over 2,200 core customers now buying at least one AI product, up almost 25% quarter-over-quarter; non-GAAP operating margin expanded to 31.4%. Deere Signals Agriculture-Equipment Recovery Despite Profit Dip

Deere &amp; Company (press release via PR Newswire) · prnewswire.com ↗

Deere reported fiscal 2026 net income of $4.86 billion, down 3%, on November 25, including $383 million in tariff costs absorbed during the year, but CEO John May flagged early recovery signs in North American large-agriculture demand and guided fiscal 2027 net income to $4.50-5.25 billion, with large-ag sales seen up 5-15%. CANADA

Liberals Call Bill C-39 Nation-Building Act a "Slam Dunk" Despite Unified Opposition

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

Government House Leader Steven MacKinnon told CTV’s Question Period the Building Canada Strong Act (Bill C-39) is “a slam dunk with our own MPs” and will not need a whipped vote, after a House committee logged over 100 hours across seven days—including back-to-back 15-hour weekend sessions—to finish clause-by-clause review. Conservatives, the NDP, the Bloc Québécois and the Greens have all signalled they will vote against it, leaving Carney’s majority zero room for defections on what he calls the most consequential economic legislation his government has proposed; the bill fast-tracks major-project approvals and creates pre-approved “national interest” zones.

Ottawa Scrambles for a "Solution" as Stellantis Sues Unifor Over Brampton Blockade

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

Industry Minister Mélanie Joly reported a “productive meeting” with a Stellantis negotiator on November 25 and said the government is “in solution mode” over the automaker’s idled Brampton assembly plant, as Stellantis has separately sued union Unifor alleging it is blockading access to the site. CANADA

Supreme Court Set to Rule Today on Quebec’s Bill 21 in Landmark Secularism Case

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

The Supreme Court of Canada hands down its ruling today, Friday November 27, on the constitutionality of Quebec’s secularism law (Bill 21), which bars public employees in positions of authority—teachers, judges, police—from wearing religious symbols; legal experts call it one of the most important decisions in Canadian democratic history given its use of the notwithstanding clause. The outcome sets precedent on how far provinces can shield legislation from Charter challenges, with direct hiring, litigation and operating-risk implications for any organization active in Quebec’s public and quasi-public sectors, and could reignite federal-provincial and sovereigntist tensions regardless of which way it falls.

VENEZUELA

Nobel Laureate Machado Unveils Transition Plan While Shut Out of Washington’s Actual Talks

Infobae · infobae.com ↗

María Corina Machado, the 2025 Nobel Peace Prize winner barred from office by Venezuela’s Comptroller General, addressed 3,000 students in Caracas on November 25 to unveil a transition plan built around a depoliticized military, a federal state, and a long-term U.S. agreement covering security, energy, mining and migration. For allocators pricing a Venezuelan oil and mining reopening, the signal is that Washington is negotiating the durable post-Maduro order with Chavista holdovers, not with her.

TAIWAN

Experts Dismiss US "First Island Chain Withdrawal" Idea as Taiwan Security Debate Intensifies

Focus Taiwan (Central News Agency) · focustaiwan.tw ↗

Regional security experts and the chair of the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission pushed back on November 26 against a Defense Priorities proposal to pull U.S. forces back from the first island chain—which includes Taiwan—and wind down mutual defense treaties with Japan, South Korea, the Philippines and Australia. Analysts at Taiwan’s INDSR warned that abandoning the first chain would likely mean losing the second chain too, giving China a launch point for regional expansion within 10-20 years. The debate lands while Taiwan’s own $14 billion U.S. arms package remains stalled—a combination that should keep Taiwan-dependent semiconductor and hardware supply chains on a heightened geopolitical-risk watch into 2027.
