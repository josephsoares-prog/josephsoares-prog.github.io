What mattered on Sunday, 26 July 2026

19 items · 15 sources · ranked by decision impact · EN/FR
01
WAR

The US-Iran war grinds into a third week as Washington strikes Iran again, Tehran hits Gulf bases, and Trump threatens to bomb Iranian infrastructure over Hormuz

CNN · cnn.com ↗

The war is now measured in weeks, not headlines: fresh US strikes, Iranian drones and missiles reaching Kuwait and Jordan, and a defense secretary putting the running bill at $37.5 billion. For any allocator, this is no longer a tail risk to hedge — it is the base case, and every "ceasefire" rumour is a countertrend bounce to sell into, not a bottom to buy.

02
ENERGY

Brent slips back toward $97 from above $100 as Pakistan, backed by China, tries to revive US-Iran talks — but crude is still up 30% on the month

Trading Economics · tradingeconomics.com ↗

The pullback from triple digits is a diplomacy trade, not a supply change: nothing has physically eased in the Gulf, and Brent is still up roughly 30% in a month. Treat sub-$100 as a gift for hedging energy exposure, not a signal the risk premium is gone — the next tanker headline reprices it in hours.

03
MARKETS

Wall Street closes a second straight losing week — S&P 500 at 7,412, Nasdaq down 1.2% Friday — as oil and a chip selloff overpower solid earnings

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Blowout numbers from Intel and Alphabet could not lift the tape because the macro tax — $100 oil and rising yields — is now the dominant variable. When good earnings can't buy a green week, the message is that the market is trading geopolitics and rates, not fundamentals; position defensively into the Fed.

04
CANADA

Trump signs a 50% tariff on roughly US$20B of Canadian goods, starting a 30-day clock as Carney vows "whatever it takes"

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

The order hits wine, lumber, dairy, electronics and pharma inputs — sectors where Canada sends up to 90% of output south — and the 30-day window turns CUSMA renewal into a live crisis, not a 2026 talking point. Canadian exporters should model a 50% wall now and pull shipments forward; the negotiation, not the tariff, is where the value swings.

05
SEMICONDUCTORS

TSMC posts a record quarter, lifts 2026 growth guidance above 40%, and adds $100B to its Arizona build-out as AI demand runs hot

Tech Times · techtimes.com ↗

Revenue of $40.2B (+36%), 67.7% gross margins and HPC now two-thirds of the mix confirm the AI capex cycle is accelerating, not peaking — and the extra $100B for Arizona directly hedges the very Taiwan-Strait and tariff risk clients fear. If you own the AI trade, TSMC's guidance is the single most important tell that the buildout has years left to run.

06
BIG TECH

Alphabet's revenue jumps 24% to $119.8B with Cloud up 82% — yet the stock slips as a bigger 2026 capex bill unsettles investors

9to5Google · 9to5google.com ↗

Cloud at $24.8B and YouTube ads at $11.1B show the AI monetization the market wanted, but the selloff on higher capex reveals the new fault line: investors will reward AI revenue and punish AI spending in the same breath. The tell for the whole complex — Microsoft and Meta report next — is whether cloud growth is now outrunning the cost to build it.

07
SEMICONDUCTORS

Intel delivers a Q2 blowout — revenue up 25%, adjusted EPS double estimates, foundry and AI both surging — and shares jump 12%

Investing.com · investing.com ↗

$16.1B revenue, $0.42 adjusted EPS against a $0.22 consensus, data-center-and-AI up 59% and Foundry up 31% mark the clearest sign yet that Intel's turnaround is real, not hopium. For anyone who wrote Intel off, this is a repricing event — the foundry story now has revenue behind it, and the US chip-sovereignty trade just gained a credible second name.

08
AUTOS

Tesla sets a delivery record of 480,126 vehicles but profit misses badly — EPS $0.33 vs $0.53 — as regulatory-credit revenue collapses 67%

Electrek · electrek.co ↗

Record volume with collapsing margins is the whole story: automotive is now a low-margin business propped up by credits that are disappearing, and the bull case rests entirely on robotaxi and energy, not cars. Anyone holding Tesla on the auto multiple is mispricing it — the vehicle business just showed you its real earnings power.

09
MARKETS

The chip complex leads the market lower — the semiconductor ETF drops 3.2% on the week as capex fears hit Nvidia, Micron and Broadcom

TheStreet · thestreet.com ↗

Even with TSMC and Intel beating, the group sold off because the market now fears the AI bill, not the AI demand — a sentiment shift that matters more than any single print. When the highest-quality names in the leadership group can't hold gains, it's a warning that the crowded AI trade needs a rest; trim, don't chase.

10
FED

The Fed meets July 28-29 with markets caught between an oil-driven inflation scare and a softening economy

CBS News · cbsnews.com ↗

Powell walks into the worst possible setup: $100 oil and 50% tariffs pushing inflation expectations up just as growth data cools, leaving no clean path to cut or hold. The decision itself matters less than the language on tariffs and energy — that guidance will set the tone for bonds, the dollar and every rate-sensitive asset into the fall.

11
CHINA

China's Q2 growth slows to its weakest since 2022 as investment slumps, intensifying calls for fresh stimulus

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

A sub-4.5% print with collapsing fixed investment tells you the property drag is still winning and Beijing's calibrated approach isn't enough — a bigger stimulus package is now a question of when, not if. For commodity exporters and multinationals with China revenue, position for a policy jolt in H2; the current data is the floor, not the trend.

12
SECURITY

Russia and Ukraine trade deadly strikes — at least 11 killed in Ukraine, a Russian factory hit — as Moscow claims to down 571 drones overnight

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

With the world's attention on the Gulf, the Ukraine war has quietly escalated in intensity, not wound down, and the scale of the drone exchanges signals no near-term off-ramp. For European energy and defense planners, the second front is still open — the risk of a simultaneous Gulf-and-Europe supply shock is higher than the muted coverage suggests.

AI INFRASTRUCTURE

Broadband Breakfast · broadbandbreakfast.com ↗

The read-through: the picks-and-shovels of AI now extend well past chips, and legacy infrastructure owners are quietly becoming AI-capex beneficiaries. Look downstream of Nvidia for the next re-rating.

Variety · variety.com ↗

A near-year pause hands rivals time and injects deep uncertainty into a deal that would reshape Hollywood — and signals that this antitrust environment can stall even blessed megadeals. MOBILITY

Waymo moves to end its Uber robotaxi exclusivity in Atlanta and Austin, stepping up a direct rivalry

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

EARNINGS

IBM Newsroom · newsroom.ibm.com ↗

IBM won't set pulses racing, but steady software growth and a reaffirmed outlook make it the ballast in a tech portfolio whipsawed by AI-capex fear. OPEC

OPEC+ keeps unwinding its cuts, approving another output increase for August even as war supercharges prices

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

But with real supply at risk in Hormuz, extra OPEC quota means little if it can't ship; watch the spread between paper barrels and physical delivery, because that gap is where the next price shock lives.

COMMODITIES

Gold pushes above $4,100 an ounce on renewed safe-haven demand as the Gulf war drives investors out of stocks and currencies

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Gold near $4,130 and up nearly 20% on the year is doing exactly what it's supposed to in a geopolitical shock — and its strength is a quiet vote of no confidence in the "quick ceasefire" narrative. If you hold no gold in a portfolio facing $100 oil, a widening war and a cornered Fed, you are running an un-hedged book; this is the insurance the tape is telling you to own.

19
CRYPTO

Bitcoin retreats toward $64,000 as higher oil, tariffs and rising Treasury yields pull capital out of risk assets

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Crypto's slide, alongside $225M in spot-ETF outflows, is the cleanest read on the macro regime: when real yields rise, the opportunity cost of a non-yielding asset bites first and hardest. Bitcoin is trading as a high-beta liquidity proxy right now, not "digital gold" — a distinction that matters if you're using it to hedge the very war that's lifting actual gold.
