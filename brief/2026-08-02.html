01
TRADE

Canada's 35% US tariff is now in force after the August 1 deadline passed with no deal — but CUSMA-compliant goods, close to 90% of exports, remain exempt

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

The August 1 deadline passed with Ottawa and Washington still short of a deal, so Trump's 35% rate on non-exempt Canadian goods now governs — yet the number that matters is the carve-out: CUSMA-compliant trade, nearly 90% of Canada's exports, stays tariff-free. We read the decision as operational, not political: any firm with cross-border exposure should map its CUSMA compliance line by line this week, because that status — not the 35% headline — is what sets landed cost.

02
CANADA

Carney rolls back most of Canada's retaliatory tariffs on CUSMA-compliant US goods, matching Washington's approach and signalling a negotiated off-ramp

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

Prime Minister Carney has removed most of Canada's retaliatory tariffs on US goods that comply with CUSMA, mirroring the US exemption and pairing the move with support measures for affected Canadian businesses. The signal for decision-makers is de-escalation: rather than answer the 35% rate with symmetric retaliation, Ottawa is protecting the exempt trade corridor and keeping talks alive — read this as a negotiated path taking shape, and position for a managed dispute, not an all-out trade war.

03
MACRO

Friday's July jobs report is the week's decisive data — payrolls seen near 100,000 and unemployment at 4.2% after June's weak 57,000 print

Kiplinger · kiplinger.com ↗

Economists expect the July payrolls report, due Friday, to show about 100,000 jobs added and unemployment steady at 4.2%, following June's disappointing 57,000 against a 115,000 forecast, with ISM manufacturing, JOLTS and ADP setting the tone earlier in the week. With Big Tech earnings now behind us, payrolls is the swing factor for the Fed's September decision — a soft number revives rate-cut odds and lifts duration, a hot one arms the hawks and hits rate-sensitive names, so this is the one print worth sizing risk around.

SECURITY

Russia's overnight barrage on Kyiv kills at least nine and wounds 28, including four children, collapsing a residential block as Ukraine runs short of Patriot interceptors

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

An early-Saturday assault on Kyiv killed at least nine and wounded 28, four of them children, partially collapsing a residential building in the Shevchenkivskyi district — the second major strike on the capital that week, days after a suspected Russian cruise missile hit Poland. Coming as Ukraine runs critically short of Patriot interceptors, the decision-grade point is direction: the war is escalating, not settling, so treat European-security and energy risk as a rising premium, and read any 'peace' talk against a battlefield that is intensifying.

GEOPOLITICS

Gaza's Board of Peace advances a phased Hamas disarmament plan — a 14-day weapons inventory, a 200-to-300-day decommissioning and a 5,000-strong stabilization force under a US general

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

The Trump-chartered Board of Peace set out a roadmap under which Hamas weapons are inventoried within 14 days and decommissioned over 200 to 300 days, with each disarmament phase tied to an incremental Israeli withdrawal, a technocratic Palestinian National Committee governing Gaza, and a roughly 5,000-strong International Stabilization Force led by an American general. The read is cautious optimism with a hard condition: Israel has not publicly accepted the plan and Hamas says it will not move until Israel withdraws, so price the signed, verified settlement — not the announcement — before assuming any regional de-escalation dividend.

SECURITY

The US-Iran war grinds on with no ceasefire as the State Department warns American citizens across the Middle East — the calm markets keep pricing has not been signed

CNN · cnn.com ↗

With no ceasefire signed, the US State Department issued fresh warnings to American citizens across the Middle East, underscoring that the on-again, off-again lull in the US-Iran war remains a pause, not a settlement. For allocators the discipline is the same as with Gaza and Ukraine: a quiet stretch that has already broken more than once is not a trend, so any oil, gold or risk exposure that assumes de-escalation is mispriced until a durable, verified agreement — rather than a security advisory — is on the table.

07
TECHNOLOGY

Nvidia's $250 billion financing guarantee for OpenAI's 10-gigawatt Ohio data centre revives circular-financing fears — the whole project could top $500 billion

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Nvidia is providing a roughly $250 billion financing guarantee behind OpenAI's 10-gigawatt data centre in Piketon, Ohio — a project whose total cost, with chips, could exceed $500 billion — even as OpenAI remains unprofitable at an $852 billion valuation, and Nvidia's own stock fell 4.9% on the news. The decision-grade signal is that the AI buildout is increasingly financed by its own suppliers, and when the chipmaker underwrites its largest customer's demand, the risk is no longer just capex but circularity — size AI-infrastructure exposure to genuine end-demand, not to vendor-backed commitments.

MARKETS

ECONOMY

South China Morning Post · scmp.com ↗

China's official manufacturing PMI dropped to 49.2 in July from 50.3 in June — back into contraction after a four-month expansion — as the new-orders subindex slid to 48.5 and export orders eased to 49.6, signalling weaker domestic and external demand. Coming a day after Beijing flagged accelerated fiscal spending, the read is that the world's second-largest economy is losing momentum and stimulus is now likely but not yet delivered — size China-sensitive cyclicals and commodities to policy that is probable, not confirmed, and watch the follow-through.

GEOPOLITICS

The US Senate's 86-12 Russia sanctions bill clears tariffs of up to 100% on buyers of Russian energy — a secondary-tariff lever aimed straight at China and India

Kyiv Post · kyivpost.com ↗

By an 86-12 margin the Senate advanced the Graham bill, pairing sanctions on Russia's shadow fleet and banks with authority for tariffs of up to 100% on major buyers of Russian oil — chiefly China and India — with a House vote expected in September. As Russia escalates against Kyiv, the mechanism is the story for allocators: this converts Russia sanctions into a secondary-tariff weapon pointed at Asia's largest economies, so the real exposure is the trade and energy channels of the countries that keep buying, and House ratification is the trigger to watch.

Commentary Hooks

The Exemption Is the Policy: why the 35% headline still misreads the Canada tariff

The rate that fills the headlines governs a shrinking slice of trade. With CUSMA-compliant goods — near 90% of Canadian exports — exempt, and Ottawa now dropping its own counter-tariffs on compliant US goods, both capitals are quietly protecting the corridor while the number does the shouting. The firms that win are the ones that already know, line by line, which of their goods qualify.

Circular Money: when the chipmaker underwrites its own customer's demand

Nvidia's $250 billion backstop for OpenAI's Ohio data centre is a $500-billion bet financed, in part, by the very supplier that profits from it. Critics hear an echo of 1999 — companies buying from each other to manufacture the look of demand. The question for every AI-infrastructure holding is simple: strip out the vendor financing, and how much end-demand is actually left?

A Plan Is Not a Peace: three conflicts, one discipline

Gaza has a roadmap Israel has not signed, the US-Iran war has a lull nobody has settled, and Ukraine has a battlefield that is intensifying, not cooling. The market keeps reaching for the de-escalation dividend; the discipline is to price the signed, verified outcome, not the announcement. Gold near a record, even amid the equity rally, shows the smart money already knows the difference.
