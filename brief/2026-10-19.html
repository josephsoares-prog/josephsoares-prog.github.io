FRANCE

Macron Faces Political Crisis as French Protests Draw at Least 2,400 Arrests

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

Nationwide protests over the cost of living and strained public services turned violent across France over the weekend, with police making at least 2,400 arrests as President Macron publicly expressed “anger” and “shame” over the unrest. The demonstrations, which began last month over conditions at Paris-area high schools, have broadened into a direct challenge to Macron’s government as it heads toward a contested 2026 budget. Boards with French consumer, retail or public-sector exposure should expect continued social-stability risk to weigh on near-term growth and policy predictability.

UKRAINE

Ukraine Launches Over 1,000 Drones at Russian Oil Facilities in St. Petersburg

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

Ukraine launched more than 1,000 drones at Russian targets, striking oil facilities in St. Petersburg in one of its largest single retaliatory barrages of the war, even as Trump has publicly criticized Kyiv’s attacks on Russian oil infrastructure. The strike underscores that Kyiv will keep hitting Russian energy assets regardless of Washington’s diplomatic choreography with Moscow, including the recent diesel-sanctions waiver. Energy and insurance desks should treat Russian refined-product supply as structurally unreliable, not merely politically contingent.

SANCTIONS

Trump Defends Russian Sanctions Relief, Clashes Publicly With Zelenskyy

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

President Trump publicly defended his decision to ease sanctions on Russian diesel and accused Ukrainian President Zelenskyy of not wanting to end the war, as Zelenskyy’s envoys met with Washington officials. The exchange deepens the rift between the White House and Kyiv just as a bipartisan group of US lawmakers presses Treasury to reverse the diesel waiver on legal grounds. Compliance and energy-trading desks should keep contingency plans ready for the waiver to be narrowed or revoked with little notice.

CHINA

China’s Economy Grows 4.5% in Q3, Beating Forecasts on AI-Driven Export Boom

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

China’s GDP growth accelerated to 4.5% in the third quarter, beating forecasts as an AI-driven boom in exports lifted activity. The upside surprise complicates the “China is slowing” thesis many allocators have priced in, and signals Chinese industrial capacity tied to AI hardware and components is scaling faster than consensus expected. Portfolios positioned for a soft China and a capped AI supply chain should revisit both assumptions this quarter.

ALBERTA

Albertans Vote Today on Secession Referendum, With ‘Remain’ Favoured in Polls

BBC News · bbc.co.uk ↗

Albertans go to the polls today on a referendum on whether the province should pursue separation from Canada, with pre-vote polling suggesting most Albertans favour remaining part of the country even as the debate itself has proven deeply divisive. A “remain” outcome would relieve near-term uncertainty over interprovincial trade, pipeline jurisdiction and federal transfers tied to Alberta’s energy sector; a stronger-than-expected “leave” showing would still reset the risk premium on Alberta-exposed capital projects regardless of the final result. Energy and infrastructure allocators should watch tonight’s results, not just the binary outcome.

BANK OF CANADA

BoC’s Macklem Flags Energy-Price Risk to Inflation Outlook Ahead of Oct. 28 Decision

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem said on the sidelines of the IMF meetings in Bangkok that persistently high global energy prices “may hurt” Canada’s inflation outlook, days after a 68,300-job plunge in September pushed unemployment to 6.5%. The comments make the central bank’s October 28 rate decision genuinely two-sided: a weak labour market argues for a hold or cut, while Macklem’s energy-inflation warning opens the door to a hike instead. Treasurers and fixed-income desks should price both tails into their October 28 scenario planning, not just the consensus hold.

STEEL

Ottawa Says Resolution to Stelco-Cleveland-Cliffs Standoff a ‘Question of Days’

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

Industry Minister Mélanie Joly said it is “a question of days” before Ottawa expects to resolve its dispute with Cleveland-Cliffs over planned layoffs at Stelco’s Hamilton plant, warning the US owner it risks a forced sale of its Canadian assets if it does not uphold job-protection commitments made under the Investment Canada Act. This is the first real test of using Investment Canada Act net-benefit undertakings as enforcement leverage against a foreign owner responding to US tariffs — a template other foreign-owned Canadian industrial assets should watch. Boards with cross-border industrial holdings in Canada should reassess how enforceable their own net-benefit undertakings really are.

DEFENCE

Four Canadian Soldiers Killed in Fast-Moving Fire at CFB Wainwright

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

A fast-moving grass fire at CFB Wainwright in Alberta killed four Canadian Armed Forces members and injured dozens more during a training exercise, Prime Minister Carney confirmed, with the blaze spreading beyond the base’s designated training area. The tragedy will trigger a formal military board of inquiry into training-safety protocols at one of Canada’s largest bases, with implications for defence-sector risk assessments and base-community relations. Expect procurement and training-contract reviews across CAF bases in the coming weeks.

AI GOVERNANCE

Anthropic Commits Scaling Share of Equity to New Public Benefit Fund

Anthropic · anthropic.com ↗

Anthropic is directing an initial 5% of company shares, subject to stockholder approval, into a new wholly-owned Public Benefit Fund, with the board able to add 2 percentage points more for every 50% increase in company value above a set baseline; the fund cannot be used for lobbying or political giving. Combined with founders’ prior 80% equity pledge, Anthropic says more than a fifth of its shares are now earmarked for public benefit, a scaling-equity structure other AI labs may face pressure to match. Boards evaluating AI-vendor governance should treat this as a new benchmark question to ask competing labs, not just a PR move by one.

AI INFRA

Microsoft Pulls Out of UK’s Largest Planned AI Data Centre

BBC News · bbc.co.uk ↗

Microsoft has withdrawn from what was set to be the UK’s largest AI data centre project, though developer Nscale says it remains committed to building the Essex site regardless. UK-site contractors, utilities and local authorities banking on the original scope should revisit their assumptions now, not at the next earnings call.

EverWind Proposes $10-Billion AI Data Centre in Nova Scotia, Backed by Anthropic Lease

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

EverWind, the company behind a wind-and-hydrogen development in Nova Scotia, is proposing a $10-billion AI data centre in Cape Breton after signing a 20-year lease with what it describes as a “frontier AI developer” reported to be Anthropic. Cohere to Acquire European AI-Security Startup Aisle

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

Toronto-based Cohere announced plans to acquire European AI-cybersecurity startup Aisle, aiming to bolster its models with systems that detect and address software vulnerabilities. BONDS

Canada on Track to Become a Top-Three Holder of US Government Debt, Surpassing China

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

Canada is on track to overtake China as a top-three foreign holder of US government debt, with analysts pointing to Chinese transfers of US Treasuries into Canadian accounts as a possible driver of the shift. The move reflects deepening financial-system linkages between Canada and the US even as the two countries remain locked in a tariff dispute, a split between trade friction and capital-market integration worth tracking for FX and cross-border portfolio positioning. ENERGY

IEA Cuts 2026 Oil Demand Forecast, Warns Gulf Risk Leaves Diesel Recovery ‘Fragile’

International Energy Agency · iea.org ↗

The IEA’s October report cut its 2026 global oil demand forecast to 102.4 million barrels a day, a 2.6 million-barrel downward revision driven mainly by diesel, and warned that “renewed attacks in the Gulf leave the recovery fragile” after citing the September bombing of Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline and October’s Houthi attacks. On-land oil stocks are at their lowest since at least 2016, even as diesel cracks have eased from September’s records above $200 a barrel. Any board modelling 2027 energy costs should treat the IEA’s own base case — not just spot prices — as already pricing in continued Gulf disruption.

Cumulus Media Emerges From Chapter 11, Alden Global-Aligned Directors Join Board

SEC EDGAR · Cumulus Media · sec.gov ↗

Radio broadcaster Cumulus Media completed its Chapter 11 reorganization on October 16, cancelling legacy common stock and handing ownership to former lenders, who also received $50 million in new convertible notes; two directors affiliated with Alden Global Capital — known for aggressive cost-cutting at acquired media properties — joined the new board, and the company plans to deregister and go fully private.
