01
TRILATERAL SUMMIT

Trump, Xi and Putin Hold First-Ever Trilateral in Shenzhen as Xi Calls for a 'World Without Nuclear Weapons'

Il Sole 24 Ore · ilsole24ore.com ↗

Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin held the first-ever three-way meeting between the US, China and Russia on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, with Xi telling the room nuclear weapons must be “completely banned and utterly destroyed” and calling them a “sword of Damocles” over humanity; Trump said Washington “will not allow Iran to acquire a nuclear weapon” and that the US and China share that interest, while Putin tied the talks to ending the war in Ukraine. No signed agreement or joint statement followed. For anyone pricing nuclear-arms-control or great-power risk, the fact that the three leaders sat down at all — after New START’s February lapse — matters more than anything they signed.

MARKETS & MACRO

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 a Share, Above Range, in Biggest Debut on Record as ANT Begins Trading on Nasdaq

Anthropic · anthropic.com ↗

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share — above its $70-to-$78 target range — selling 980 million Class A shares through underwriters Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, with trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ticker “ANT” set to begin Thursday and the deal expected to close Friday. The pricing implies a valuation above the $1.4 trillion previously floated, with Amazon and Google together expected to hold close to 30% of the company once it lists. For allocators and AI-lab competitors alike, Thursday’s opening print becomes the new reference point for every other private AI valuation.

03
TECH & MARKETS

Nvidia Posts Record $115.8 Billion Quarter as Board Rejects Reinstating Jensen Huang Amid Espionage-Linked Scandal

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year-over-year, with net profit of $64.9 billion and fourth-quarter guidance of roughly $130 billion; board chair Harvey Jones told the earnings call the board “unanimously accepted” Jensen Huang’s resignation and “does not intend to reinstate” him as CEO, despite an open letter signed by nearly 200,000 people, including Elon Musk, urging his return. Huang resigned amid scrutiny of his relationship with a former Megaspeed executive accused of working with Chinese intelligence, and separately, the Commerce and Justice Departments confirmed a criminal probe into whether Megaspeed diverted advanced chips to China. For anyone holding AI-infrastructure exposure, the real open question is no longer the earnings beat but who runs Nvidia once interim CEO Colette Kress hands off, and to whom.

04
GAZA CEASEFIRE

Israeli Tanks Cross Into Hamas-Controlled Central Gaza for First Time Since Ceasefire, Hamas Calls It a 'Flagrant Violation'

RTE · rte.ie ↗

Israeli tanks advanced onto the Salahudeen road west of the “Yellow Line” in central Gaza on Wednesday, the first ground incursion into Hamas-held territory since the October 2025 ceasefire, as residents reported gunfire, tank shells and forced evacuations near Nuseirat camp; the Israeli military said it was striking “Hamas infrastructure” that was “regrouping and rearming,” while Hamas called the raid a “dangerous escalation” and accused Israel of trying to derail the US-brokered truce. Palestinian medics reported three deaths from separate incidents; Gaza health authorities say more than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire took effect, against five Israeli soldiers. For organizations tracking Gaza reconstruction or regional risk, a tank crossing of the Yellow Line — not just another air strike — is the clearest sign yet that the ceasefire’s territorial lines, not only its disarmament clauses, are now in play.

05
EU SANCTIONS

EU Extends Sanctions to Nearly 50 More Russian Banks, Targets Shadow Fleet and Oil Infrastructure

European Union (EEAS) · eeas.europa.eu ↗

The EU adopted a new sanctions package on November 16 covering nearly 50 additional Russian banks and financial institutions — building on more than 100 already listed — plus actors in Russian oil exploration, refining and the St Petersburg oil terminal, and shadow-fleet enablers based in the UAE, China, the Seychelles and Oman; a separate 30-person, two-entity listing targeted hybrid and disinformation networks including the Social Design Agency, which Brussels called its largest-ever package aimed at foreign information manipulation. For banks and trading houses with residual Russia exposure, the net is now wide enough that “not directly sanctioned” is no longer a reliable compliance shield.

MARKETS & MACRO

Nasdaq Edges Higher, Dow Falls as Fed's Waller Floats Bigger December Hike and Nvidia Dips Despite Strong Guidance

Business Recorder (Reuters) · brecorder.com ↗

The Nasdaq rose 0.54% to 24,460.40 while the Dow fell 0.73% to 50,386.70 in Wednesday trading, as Fed Governor Christopher Waller said the central bank may need either a half-point rate hike in December or faster hikes through 2027, with traders now pricing a quarter-point move as near-certain; Nvidia shares fell 1.6% even after guiding fourth-quarter revenue to about $130 billion, above analyst estimates, with one strategist noting the real question is what the company looks like in three to five years without Jensen Huang. For rate-sensitive portfolios, Waller’s comments are the more consequential data point than Nvidia’s own guidance beat.

07
UKRAINE WAR

Zelensky Says War Could End in a Single Trump-Putin-Xi Meeting, as Von der Leyen Proposes €45 Billion Ukraine Support Fund

Anadolu Agency · anews.com.tr ↗

Ahead of the Shenzhen trilateral, Zelensky told NBC News the war could end “in one meeting” if Trump, Putin and Xi “wanted to do so,” arguing that a joint US-China sanctions threat against Russia would be decisive, while insisting “Ukraine’s issues must be decided with Ukraine’s participation.” Separately, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen proposed a coordinated support instrument of up to €45 billion ($52 billion) to cover much of Kyiv’s 2027 budget and defense needs, which Zelensky urged the European Parliament and Council to approve quickly. For anyone modeling Ukraine’s financing runway into 2027, the €45 billion figure is now the number to watch move through Brussels.

TECH & TRADE

US Tightens AI Chip Export Rules to Close Loophole Letting China Reach Advanced Chips Through Overseas Data Centres

South China Morning Post · scmp.com ↗

The US Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim rule requiring a license for exports and transfers of advanced AI chips and servers to any data centre worldwide — with at least 12 million aggregate processing-performance points — that supplies significant computing power to companies headquartered in mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau or countries under a US arms embargo, closing a loophole that let Chinese developers reach Nvidia-class chips through facilities in places like Southeast Asia. For chip makers and cloud operators with Asia-based capacity, the rule means due diligence on an overseas data centre’s ultimate customer base, not just its physical location, now determines export-control exposure.

US-ASIA TRADE

Trump and Marcos Announce 'Very Big' US-Philippines Trade Deal on Energy and Food Security

Manila Times · manilatimes.net ↗

Trump said he and Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. “consummated” what he called “the biggest trade deal we’ve ever made, by far” at a state luncheon in Manila, with Malacañang describing an understanding on “key areas of economic cooperation, particularly in energy and food security” that negotiators will now turn into concrete initiatives; Trump separately cited a planned Alaska LNG pipeline and support for the Philippines’ first civilian nuclear project. No tariff rates or dollar figures were disclosed, and a Philippine lawmaker criticized the deal as favoring American goods over Filipino farmers. For energy traders and Philippine import-dependent sectors, the deal’s real terms — not its superlatives — are the thing to watch for once negotiators publish specifics.

ANTITRUST & M&A

DOJ Signals Openness to Clearing Mergers Without Asset Sales After Approving Fox's $22 Billion Roku Deal

Insurance Journal (Bloomberg) · insurancejournal.com ↗

DOJ deputy antitrust chief Charlie Beller said the department is now open to clearing mergers based on behavioral commitments rather than requiring asset sales, calling such commitments “a mitigating factor” especially where divestitures are hard to arrange — a shift from the asset-sale-first posture of recent years; the remarks came after DOJ closed its investigation into Fox’s $22 billion acquisition of Roku on commitments to carry non-Fox content for at least three years. The FTC’s David Shaw pushed back, calling behavioral remedies “disfavored and rare.” For dealmakers, Beller’s comments are also being read as a signal for how DOJ will handle its pending review of Nvidia’s roughly $13 billion acquisition of Hugging Face.

CANADA TRADE

Carney Meets Trump at APEC as Canada and China Announce Tariff Relief for Pork and Seafood Exporters

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney met President Trump on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, with Canadian officials saying Canada’s trade relationship with China is not Trump’s top concern ahead of the meeting, even as Canada and China separately announced tariff relief that benefits Canadian pork and seafood producers specifically. Carney has described the push for a broader US-Canada deal as requiring a “very careful balancing act” against the political risk of being seen as too close to Beijing. For Canadian exporters, the pork and seafood relief is concrete and immediate; a comprehensive US trade deal remains, as of Wednesday, still unresolved.

CANADA ECONOMY

Canada's Inflation Holds at 3.1% in October as Gas and Grocery Prices Climb

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

Statistics Canada data show the annual inflation rate held at 3.1% in October, unchanged from September, as rising gasoline and grocery prices offset relief in other categories; the reading keeps inflation above the Bank of Canada’s 1-to-3% comfort range for a second straight month. For the Bank of Canada heading into its December rate decision, a third consecutive above-target print narrows the room to cut further even as growth forecasts for 2026 sit near 1%.

ENERGY & OIL

Oil Extends Declines as US Crude Stockpile Builds Outweigh Hormuz Risk Premium

Business Recorder (Reuters) · brecorder.com ↗

Energy was the worst-performing S&P 500 sector, down 2.6%, as oil prices slipped more than 1% on larger-than-expected builds in US crude and fuel inventories, outweighing the ongoing Strait of Hormuz standoff; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose a modest 0.7% as chip stocks traded mixed around Nvidia’s results. For hedging desks, a second straight session where inventory data beat geopolitical risk premium is a reason to question how much Hormuz is really adding to the oil price right now.
