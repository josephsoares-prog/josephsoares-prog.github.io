01
HORMUZ

An Iranian missile hits an ADNOC tanker in the Strait of Hormuz — the 16th strike on the state carrier's fleet since the war began — as the UAE and Qatar demand Tehran reopen the strait unconditionally

The National · thenationalnews.com ↗

Abu Dhabi says an Iranian missile struck an ADNOC tanker transiting Hormuz early Saturday with no casualties, the 16th attack on ADNOC vessels since the war began on 28 February. The UAE called it a flagrant violation of UN Security Council Resolution 2817, Qatar rejected use of the strait as a tool of pressure, and Iran's Revolutionary Guard insists any reopening is conditional on Washington accepting Tehran's terms. Treat the shipping lane as an active combat zone, not a diplomatic near-miss: for anyone pricing Gulf freight, insurance or crude, the risk premium is anchored to the war's terms, not a technical transit fix.

02
MARKETS

Odds of a September Fed hike collapse into cut territory after a July jobs report that showed the US economy shedding jobs — unwinding weeks of war-driven inflation repricing

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Markets had spent weeks bracing for a September rate hike to counter war-driven oil inflation; a sharply weak July payrolls print flipped that to pricing a cut instead. That is a regime change in the rate outlook, but a dangerous one to read simply: a Fed easing into labour-market weakness while an active Gulf war keeps a floor under oil is not the same as a Fed easing because inflation is tamed. The next CPI print is the real test of which regime the market is actually in.

03
OIL

Brent crude jumps 2.9% to $86 a barrel — up more than 18% in a month — as the Hormuz war premium keeps rebuilding into a fragile market

Fortune · fortune.com ↗

Brent settled near $86.04, up $2.40 (2.9%) on the day and roughly 18.5% over the month, as renewed Hormuz strikes rebuild a war premium that had partly deflated on ceasefire hopes. The move matters beyond energy desks: it keeps a floor under headline inflation just as the Fed pivots toward cuts, complicating the easing story. Model oil as a two-sided risk into the autumn — a genuine Hormuz reopening could unwind the premium fast, while any tanker escalation reprices it higher overnight.

04
CANADA

Ottawa races Washington's August 19 tariff deadline with 'all strategic sectors' on the table, as Carney warns a 50% levy threat is a live cliff, not a bluff

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

Prime Minister Carney says Canada and the US are trading proposals across 'all strategic sectors' with nine days left before a threatened 50% tariff, and describes Ottawa's tone as already 'quite firm.' For any business with cross-border supply chains, August 19 should be treated as a genuine cliff — officials themselves decline to guarantee a deal. The asymmetry is the point: CUSMA-compliant goods remain largely exempt, so the exposure concentrates in specific non-compliant lines that firms should be stress-testing now, not after the deadline.

05
CANADA

Canada adds a surprise 75,000 jobs in July and its jobless rate falls to a two-year low — a sharp divergence from a US labour market that just contracted

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

Statistics Canada reported roughly 75,000 new jobs in July, pushing the unemployment rate to its lowest in two years, the same week US payrolls went negative. That divergence hands Ottawa a stronger hand into the tariff talks and complicates any assumption that the Bank of Canada must follow a dovish Fed. For allocators, it argues against treating North America as a single macro bloc — the two labour markets are now pointing in opposite directions.

06
MACRO

US employers unexpectedly cut 23,000 jobs in July against forecasts of an 80,000 gain, with unemployment at 4.1% — the first payroll contraction in years

CBS News · cbsnews.com ↗

The July payrolls report showed the US economy losing about 23,000 jobs versus expectations of an 80,000 gain, even as the headline unemployment rate held near 4.1%. A negative print is a qualitatively different signal than a soft one — it moves the recession question from tail risk to base-case debate. Pair it with a Fed now expected to cut and elevated war-driven oil, and the setup is a policy easing into stagflation-lite conditions, which is precisely the regime that punishes portfolios positioned for a clean soft landing.

07
AI

Nvidia and OpenAI are in talks for a backstop of up to $250 billion to fund AI infrastructure — vendor and customer financing each other on an unprecedented scale

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Nvidia and OpenAI are reportedly discussing a backstop of up to $250 billion tied to a partnership to deploy roughly 10 gigawatts of Nvidia systems. The structure — the dominant chip supplier helping finance its largest customer's buildout — concentrates AI-cycle risk in a way regulators and credit desks will scrutinise, because it blurs the line between real end-demand and vendor-financed demand. For anyone underwriting the AI capex boom, this is the signal to watch: circular financing can extend a cycle, but it also amplifies the drawdown if utilisation disappoints.

08
TRADE

China's exports jump 23% in July, beating estimates on surging AI and high-tech demand, even as imports cool and the trade surplus widens

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Chinese exports rose about 23% year on year in July, well above forecasts, powered by AI-linked and high-tech shipments, with chip exports accelerating sharply while imports softened. Strong exports into weak imports is a familiar imbalance signal: external demand is carrying the economy while domestic demand lags. For trade-policy watchers, a widening surplus in the middle of a tariff standoff raises the odds of fresh friction, even as it confirms that the AI supply chain is now the single strongest current in global goods trade.

09
TRADE

Taiwan's July exports hit $75.3 billion, up 32.9%, as the finance ministry forecasts full-year exports will smash past $850 billion on AI demand

Taipei Times · taipeitimes.com ↗

Taiwan posted July exports of $75.30 billion, up 32.9% year on year, with the first seven months up 44.7%; the finance ministry now projects 2026 exports will surpass $850 billion, shattering last year's record. TSMC has raised sales guidance and capex in response to AI demand, and officials call the long-term uptrend intact. Read Taiwan's numbers as the cleanest real-economy readout on the AI cycle — if the boom were topping, this is where the strain would show first, and it isn't showing yet.

10
GOLD

Gold holds near a record $4,350 an ounce as a weak jobs report, rate-cut bets, central-bank buying and Hormuz risk all push the same way

TheStreet · thestreet.com ↗

Gold is trading near a record of roughly $4,350 an ounce after a string of 2026 highs, lifted by a weak July jobs report, firming rate-cut expectations, steady central-bank buying and Gulf geopolitical risk. When four independent drivers align on the same asset, the move is less a trade than a regime read: markets are hedging currency debasement and geopolitical tail risk simultaneously. The signal for allocators is that the safe-haven bid is structural right now, not a momentary flight — which is a different thing to underwrite than a spike.

11
ENERGY

OPEC+ presses ahead with another output increase — about 188,000 barrels a day for August — betting that recovering Hormuz flows can absorb more barrels

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

OPEC+ approved a further production increase of roughly 188,000 barrels a day for August as Gulf exports began to recover, continuing its move to unwind earlier cuts. The group is effectively wagering that Hormuz flows normalise; the ADNOC tanker strike cuts directly against that assumption. Watch the gap between the cartel's supply optimism and the war premium in the price — if strikes persist, OPEC+ is adding barrels into a lane the market does not yet trust, and that tension is where the next oil surprise lives.

12
UKRAINE

Russia and Ukraine sharply escalate their aerial war, trading massed overnight drone and missile barrages that killed civilians on both sides this weekend

CNN · cnn.com ↗

CNN reports Russia and Ukraine have markedly stepped up their aerial war, with weekend overnight barrages of drones and missiles killing civilians on both sides and Ukrainian strikes reaching Russian refineries and infrastructure. The escalation undercuts the fragile truce framework floated earlier in 2026 and reintroduces a supply-side risk to European energy just as Hormuz strains global oil. For risk desks, the read is that the two largest active conflicts — the Gulf and Ukraine — are escalating in parallel, compounding rather than offsetting each other.

13
CANADA

Alberta's pipeline buildout is set to spur a push for light-oil drilling as Ottawa moves to list a new West Coast export line as a national-interest project

Bloomberg · bloomberg.com ↗

Bloomberg reports Alberta's planned pipeline expansion is poised to trigger renewed light-oil drilling, as the Carney government moves to designate a new West Coast export pipeline a national-interest project, paired with the Pathways carbon-capture initiative. This is Canada's export-diversification play made concrete: reduce single-buyer dependence on the US precisely as tariff risk peaks. For investors, it reframes Canadian energy from a captive continental supplier into a prospective Pacific exporter — a multi-year re-rating story if the politics and permitting actually hold.

14
M&A

Electronic Arts goes private in a $55 billion buyout led by Saudi Arabia's PIF, with Silver Lake and Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners — one of the largest leveraged deals ever

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Electronic Arts has completed its take-private in a $55 billion deal, with Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund holding about 93.4%, Silver Lake 5.5% and Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners 1.1%, after regulators including the European Commission cleared it. The scale and the sovereign-fund control drew national-security scrutiny from US senators and questions about creative and labour direction. The through-line for dealmakers: Gulf sovereign capital is now writing some of the largest cheques in Western tech and media, and the political friction that buys is becoming a standing feature of megadeals, not an exception.

15
MIDEAST

Three Netanyahu aides are accused of leaking classified Egypt intelligence to Qatar while on Doha's payroll, with the opposition calling it treason and demanding an inquiry

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

Israeli reporting alleges three Netanyahu advisers — Jonatan Urich, Yisrael Einhorn and Eli Feldstein — leaked classified intelligence about Egyptian military movements to Qatar while being paid by Doha, with defence officials warning it could endanger the Egypt peace and hostage diplomacy. Opposition figures Yair Golan and Gadi Eisenkot called it treason and demanded a commission of inquiry. Beyond the domestic scandal, the decision-relevant risk is regional: anything that strains Israel-Egypt ties or Qatar's role as hostage mediator raises the odds of a wider diplomatic rupture at the worst possible moment.

16
MIDEAST

Netanyahu rejects a 15-point Gaza plan and vows Iran will never have nuclear weapons, after personally reversing an ordered IDF pullout from three areas near Rafah

The Jerusalem Post · jpost.com ↗

Netanyahu publicly rejected a 15-point Gaza framework and restated that Iran will never be allowed nuclear weapons, a day after reporting that he personally approved, then reversed, an IDF pullout from three areas near Rafah. The reversal signals a hardening line just as mediation channels are strained by the Qatar leak allegations. For anyone tracking the war's trajectory, the takeaway is that the near-term path is escalation-tilted, not toward a settlement — the diplomatic off-ramps are narrowing, not widening.

17
ENERGY

Yemen's Houthis claim a drone strike on a Saudi Aramco refinery at Jizan, reopening a second front against Gulf energy infrastructure

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

The Iran-aligned Houthis said they struck a Saudi Aramco refinery at Jizan with a drone, extending the Gulf conflict beyond Hormuz shipping to onshore Saudi energy assets. Even a limited hit matters as a signal: it reminds the market that Saudi downstream infrastructure remains inside the war's reach, not insulated from it. Combined with the tanker strikes, it argues for pricing Gulf energy risk as a multi-node problem — shipping lanes and fixed infrastructure both exposed — rather than a single chokepoint at the strait.

18
MARKETS

Wall Street enters CPI week off its biggest weekly rally in months — the Dow and Nasdaq gained over 1,300 points — with futures mixed and overbought risk rising

Goodreturns · goodreturns.in ↗

US equities enter the week after a powerful rally — the Dow and Nasdaq each added more than 1,300 points on the week and the S&P over 250 — as soft jobs data revived rate-cut hopes; Monday futures were mixed. The week's real catalysts are the CPI and PPI prints that will confirm or break the newly hardened cut narrative. The tactical read: a market this stretched into a binary inflation event is asymmetric — a soft CPI extends the melt-up, a hot one detonates the rate-cut trade that drove the rally.

19
CRYPTO

Bitcoin and ether climb after the weak US jobs report, trading as high-beta proxies for the market's hardening bet on Fed rate cuts

TheStreet · thestreet.com ↗

Crypto moved higher alongside equities after the soft July jobs report, with bitcoin and ether trading as leveraged expressions of the same rate-cut thesis lifting risk assets broadly. That correlation is the point worth noting: crypto is behaving less like an uncorrelated hedge and more like the high-beta tail of the macro trade. For portfolio construction, it means digital assets currently add to rate-and-liquidity exposure rather than diversifying it — useful on the way up, dangerous if the CPI print reverses the easing narrative.

TECH

Big Tech's latest earnings split the market on AI spending — Microsoft and Amazon rallied while Apple fell — as investors start policing the return on hundreds of billions in capex

Fortune · fortune.com ↗

Big Tech's recent quarter drew a divided market verdict: Microsoft and Amazon rose on the results while Apple slipped, as investors began to push back on the scale of AI capital spending relative to visible returns. The shift matters because it marks the moment the market starts demanding proof, not promise, on AI investment — the leash on hyperscaler spending is shortening. Watch which names can tie capex to revenue: that distinction, not the headline AI narrative, is what will separate winners from casualties if sentiment turns.

ASIA

Moody's warns India's growth could slow to about 6% if Washington imposes a 50% tariff, with analysts flagging up to $25 billion of exports at risk

Deccan Herald · deccanherald.com ↗

Moody's cautions that a 50% US tariff could pull India's FY26 growth down toward 6%, while trade researchers estimate up to $25 billion of Indian exports would be exposed without exemptions. India is the second major economy — alongside Canada — running a live tariff-cliff calculation this month, underscoring that the US tariff campaign is now a synchronized global-growth variable, not a series of bilateral spats. For allocators with emerging-market exposure, the tariff calendar has become a macro calendar.

Commentary Hooks

The AI Trade Is Now the Global Economy's Load-Bearing Wall

China's exports rose 23% and Taiwan's 32.9% on AI demand in the same week Nvidia and OpenAI discussed a $250 billion backstop — the AI supply chain is now carrying global goods trade and a large share of equity returns at once. That concentration is a strength on the way up and a single point of failure on the way down; the whole edifice now leans on one demand story staying intact.

A Rate Cut Into a War Is Not a Rate Cut Into Calm

Markets flipped from pricing a Fed hike to pricing a cut on one negative jobs print, but a central bank easing while an active Gulf war floors oil is running a fundamentally different experiment than one easing on tamed inflation. Investors pricing the two scenarios identically are carrying hidden risk into the next CPI report.

Ottawa's Two-Front Week: A Tariff Cliff and a Pivot to the Pacific

Canada faces a 50% US tariff threat on August 19 even as a surprise jobs surge and a national-interest West Coast pipeline hand Carney leverage and a diversification story. The near-term risk is real, but the strategic move — reducing single-buyer dependence on the US — is exactly the response the tariff era demands, and this week it stopped being rhetoric.

Watchlist

  • Whether the next US CPI and PPI prints confirm or undercut the market's newly hardened conviction in a September Fed rate cut.
  • Whether Iran's conditions harden further or a temporary Hormuz transit corridor materializes despite the continued tanker strikes.
  • Whether the Netanyahu-Qatar leak allegations trigger a commission of inquiry and strain Israel-Egypt ties and hostage mediation.
  • Whether Big Tech's AI-capex backlash resumes or the record China and Taiwan export data validate the spending into year-end.
