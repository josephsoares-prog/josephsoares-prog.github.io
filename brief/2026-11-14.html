NVIDIA GOVERNANCE

Nvidia Ousts Jensen Huang as CEO Amid Espionage Inquiry, Stock Swings Sharply

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

Nvidia’s board accepted Jensen Huang’s resignation as chief executive on Thursday, following an investigation into his relationship with a woman U.S. intelligence suspects of being a Chinese government agent; the stock plunged 14% at the open before paring the loss to close down 4.3%, leaving shares roughly 27% below October’s record. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is stepping in as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor, with rivals Broadcom and AMD rallying more than 5% on bets the AI chip trade can outlive any single company. For any board or allocator with AI exposure, the open question is no longer whether Nvidia can replace its founder — it’s how long markets will keep pricing AI infrastructure as a one-company trade while that search plays out.

FED POLICY

Hot Core Inflation Reading Revives Fed Hike Bets as 10-Year Yield Jumps to 4.99%

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

Tuesday’s inflation report showed underlying prices rose exactly the 0.3% threshold Fed Governor Christopher Waller had flagged as his trigger for a larger move, pushing the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.99% from 4.88% and sending the S&P 500 down 0.4% as traders repriced toward a December hike rather than a cut. Waller had warned that a smaller, quarter-point response “could put our credibility at risk” if inflation stayed this persistent — a signal now being read as a live option for a 50-basis-point move. For treasurers and portfolio managers who spent 2026 planning around a cutting cycle, the rate-path assumption behind every refinancing and duration bet needs to be rerun.

AI IPO

Anthropic Prices What Could Be AI’s Largest-Ever IPO as a “Pure-Play” Stock

Axios · axios.com ↗

Anthropic is reported to be pricing a listing that could value the company at up to $1.4 trillion, pitched to investors as AI’s first genuine “pure-play” public stock, just as its chip supplier Nvidia is consumed by a leadership crisis. The timing makes this listing the market’s cleanest test yet of whether investors will underwrite frontier-AI economics on their own merits rather than through Nvidia’s halo. Any allocator weighing exposure to the AI buildout should treat Anthropic’s reception — not Nvidia’s succession saga — as the signal on whether the broader AI financing story still holds.

CHIP POLICY

Huang’s Exit Reopens Washington’s Fight Over AI Chip Exports to China

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Jensen Huang’s abrupt departure, and the national-security questions attached to it, have reportedly revived a push in Washington to tighten export controls on AI chips to China — reopening a fight Nvidia had spent years lobbying to soften, just ahead of planned Trump-Xi engagement. The same crisis is also drawing scrutiny to the web of vendor financing and compute-for-equity arrangements that assumed Huang’s personal credibility as counterparty. Government-affairs and compliance teams at any chipmaker with China exposure should expect this to move on congressional and administration timelines, not corporate ones.

BANK OF CANADA

Bank of Canada Hikes to 2.5%, Flags Risk That Energy Costs Broaden Into Inflation

Bank of Canada · bankofcanada.ca ↗

The Bank of Canada’s Governing Council raised its policy rate a quarter point to 2.5% on October 28, judging that persistently higher gasoline, diesel and heating-oil prices risk passing through into broader costs even though CPI excluding gasoline still sits at 2.2%. Members explicitly weighed holding at 2.25% and chose the hike instead, reasoning that monetary policy “cannot lower global energy prices” but can stop them from becoming generalized inflation. Any Canadian borrower or fixed-income desk still pricing a cutting cycle needs to reckon with a central bank that just said, in its own words, it would rather act early on energy pass-through than wait.

TAIWAN-CHINA

Xi Says Taiwan “Reunification” Is Inevitable as PLA Runs 29-Aircraft Patrol

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Xi Jinping told Beijing’s Great Hall of the People that “the complete reunification of the motherland must be realised, and it will certainly be realised,” framing it as an “unwavering historical mission” even as he stopped short of renouncing the use of force Beijing has never ruled out. Taiwan’s defence ministry reported 29 Chinese military aircraft, including J-16 fighters and drones, running a “joint combat readiness” patrol the same day, while Taipei’s Mainland Affairs Council flatly rejected the “one country, two systems” framing Xi offered. For boards with Taiwan-exposed supply chains, this is a rhetorical escalation, not yet an operational one — but it lands a week before Xi is due to meet Trump at APEC, which is when rhetoric either gets walked back or hardens into policy.

OIL MARKETS

IEA Says Oil Market Is Tipping Into Surplus as Gulf Exports Recover

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

The IEA said Friday the global oil market will post a fourth-quarter surplus of 1.7 million barrels a day — reversing its own prior deficit call — as Gulf producers restore exports disrupted by the Iran war using workarounds like “dark crossings” through the Strait of Hormuz, with OPEC+ October output already back to 97% of pre-war levels. Brent eased to $95.71 a barrel on the report even as tanker attacks continue, which is the signal worth noting: supply is normalizing faster than the shooting is stopping. Energy-sensitive inflation hedges built on sustained Gulf disruption need to be tested against this data, not against the headlines about the war itself.

TECH INFRASTRUCTURE

Microsoft 365 Outage Drags Into Second Day, Hits 74 Countries

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

A global Microsoft 365 outage that began midday Thursday — disrupting Outlook, Teams and Copilot — ran into its second day Friday, with Downdetector logging reports from 74 countries within an hour of onset; Microsoft says it is seeing “encouraging recovery progress” but warns intermittent delays may persist. The episode is being cited by lawmakers outside the US as fresh evidence of the risk of concentrating critical business and government workflows on a single cloud vendor. For any enterprise or public body still treating multi-cloud resilience as a someday project, this is the cost of not doing it sooner, measured in a second straight day of disrupted operations.

AI MARKETS

AI Stocks Rebound as CoreWeave Jumps 24.7% on Cash-Cushion Reassurance

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

A rebound in AI stocks lifted the S&P 500 1.3% on Wednesday, nearly erasing the prior week’s rout, led by CoreWeave’s 24.7% jump after it reported smaller-than-expected summer losses and CEO Michael Intrator said the company has roughly $9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits due over the next three months to help fund operations. The reassurance matters because the specific worry hanging over AI infrastructure names has shifted from “will AI pay off” to “can these companies keep funding themselves at current rates” — and CoreWeave just offered the clearest answer yet. Allocators should treat this balance-sheet detail, not the headline index move, as the more durable signal.

FEDERAL BUDGET

Carney’s $142-Billion Budget Clears Confidence Vote, Averting a Winter Election

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

The Carney government’s Budget 2026 — C$142 billion in new spending and tax measures aimed at growth and affordability, including lower tax rates on new business investment — cleared its first confidence vote, removing the near-term threat of a snap winter election. The budget’s business-investment incentives are now the operative fiscal framework for anyone planning capital spending in Canada through the rest of this government’s mandate, regardless of how the opposition continues to frame the added debt. For corporate planners, the question has shifted from “will this budget pass” to “how to actually use the new deduction structure before year-end.”

LABOUR MARKET

Canada’s Unemployment Rate Rises to 6.7% as Private Sector Sheds 53,000 Jobs

Statistics Canada · www150.statcan.gc.ca ↗

Statistics Canada’s October Labour Force Survey showed headline employment essentially flat (-4,700) but unemployment rising 0.2 points to 6.7%, masking a sharper story underneath: private-sector employment fell 53,000 (-0.4%), its first decline since February, offset by public-sector hiring gains. For anyone assessing Canada’s underlying labour-market health independent of government payrolls, the private-sector number — not the headline rate — is the one that just turned negative for the first time in eight months.

Ottawa Serves Stellantis a Default Notice Over Its Brampton Plant Sale to Roshel

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

Industry Minister Mélanie Joly has served Stellantis with a notice of default on its federal funding agreement after the automaker signed a deal to sell its idled Brampton assembly plant to armoured-vehicle maker Roshel, saying bluntly “I’m fed up,” while Prime Minister Mark Carney says Stellantis still owes a “global solution” for the plant’s workers. This moves Ottawa’s leverage from rhetoric to an actual enforcement mechanism — a formal default notice, not just criticism — against a company that took public funding tied to production commitments. Any company holding a federal investment agreement should read this as confirmation that Ottawa will enforce default clauses on asset sales it wasn’t consulted on, not merely threaten to.

Greer Confirms Canada, US Have Spoken “Within the Past Few Days” on a Trade Deal

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told reporters Thursday that Canada and the US have been in contact “within the past few days” and that Mark Carney and Donald Trump spoke directly, with the stated aim of a “mutually beneficial” deal — the first confirmed high-level contact since Canada walked away from talks in August. Russia, Ukraine Agree to Large Prisoner Swap as Abu Dhabi Talks End With No Breakthrough

The Atlanta Journal-Constitution · ajc.com ↗

Russia and Ukraine agreed to a large prisoner exchange during the first US-mediated talks in nine months, but the two-day Abu Dhabi session ended with no major breakthrough toward ending the nearly five-year war, and fighting continued on the front line even as the talks were underway, with Ukrainian officials reporting three killed in a daytime strike on an Odesa-region market. For anyone pricing the war’s endgame, a prisoner swap without a security-guarantees framework is a confidence-building step, not evidence the harder territorial questions are any closer to resolved.

OPEC+ OUTPUT

OPEC+ Core Seven Hold December Output Steady, Next Call Due November 29

OPEC Secretariat · opec.org ↗

Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman agreed on November 1 to hold December production at September-approved levels, reaffirming their “commitment to market stability” in the group’s own release, with the next review set for November 29. This is the primary-source confirmation behind the IEA’s surplus call above: output policy has been steady for a month even as the surplus narrative builds, which makes November 29 the next real catalyst for anyone pricing where OPEC+ goes from here, not this routine hold itself.
