OIL

Oil Slides as Washington Weighs Easing Sanctions on Russia's 'Shadow Fleet'

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

Brent fell roughly 2% to near $104/bbl and WTI slid about 1.4% to near $94 on Wednesday as traders weighed a possible US easing of sanctions on Russia's shadow-fleet tankers against escalating Saudi-Houthi strikes near the Bab el-Mandeb shipping lane. We read this as a market pricing two offsetting supply risks at once — sanctions relief that could add barrels, and Red Sea escalation that could remove them — so treat the pullback as fragile, not a trend, for hedging and budget purposes.

02
CEASEFIRE

Russia Strikes Six Ukrainian Energy Facilities a Day After Trump's 'Energy Ceasefire'

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

A day after Trump announced an "energy ceasefire" between Russia and Ukraine on October 12, Russian strikes hit six Ukrainian energy facilities and wounded 33, while Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov said Moscow will continue its campaign and Washington had separately eased sanctions on Russian diesel. We see a pattern here: brokered ceasefires that don't survive 48 hours are becoming the baseline, not the exception, and firms with Russian-trade or sanctions-compliance exposure should assume the easing is tactical and reversible, not durable policy.

YEMEN

Houthis Strike Aden Airport as Saudi-Backed Forces Launch Red Sea Counteroffensive

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Houthi missiles struck the runway and terminal at Aden International Airport on October 7, diverting a Cairo-Aden flight to Jeddah, as Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces pressed a counteroffensive to retake Mocha and the Bab el-Mandeb coastline. This is a direct escalation astride one of the world's busiest crude and container chokepoints — any board with Red Sea transit, freight or marine-insurance exposure should be re-pricing that risk now, not waiting for a ceasefire that isn't on the table.

OPEC

OPEC+ Holds November Output Flat, Next Review Set for November 1

OPEC · opec.org ↗

The eight-country OPEC+ group — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman among them — agreed on October 4 to hold November production at September's required levels, with the next review set for November 1. That removes one variable from near-term price forecasting: barring a Gulf security shock, supply growth from this group is off the table until at least next month, so budget and hedging assumptions can treat OPEC+ output as a known constant for now.

MARKETS

S&P 500 Drops 1% as Hot Inflation Data Collides With an AI-Stock Selloff

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

The S&P 500 fell about 1% on October 13 as a hotter-than-expected inflation print landed at the same time AI-related stocks sold off, following a memory-chip decline the day before. For allocators, the signal is that rate expectations and AI-capex skepticism are no longer separate risk factors — they're now moving the index together, which argues for stress-testing portfolios against both shocks hitting at once rather than either alone.

OPENAI

OpenAI's Annualized Revenue Put at Nearer $50 Billion, $20 Billion Below Earlier Reports

Bloomberg Tax · bloombergtax.com ↗

OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue was nearing $50 billion at the end of September, roughly $20 billion below the ~$70 billion figure widely reported in September, with the Financial Times attributing part of the gap to investor pressure for accounting more comparable to rival Anthropic. A 30% downward revision to a frontier AI lab's own reported run-rate is exactly the kind of fact that should recalibrate anyone pricing AI valuations off top-line growth claims — verify the denominator before you trust the multiple.

ANTHROPIC

Anthropic's Quarterly Revenue Jumps 46% to $16.8 Billion as Backers Eye $2 Trillion IPO Valuation

Euronews · euronews.com ↗

Anthropic's Q3 2026 revenue rose 46% to $16.8 billion, and some backers are reportedly pricing a potential IPO as high as a $2 trillion valuation — a figure that would rival or exceed the largest listings on record. We'd treat this, alongside the OpenAI revision above, as the live test of whether AI-industry growth can actually justify current multiples: one lab's numbers are holding up, the other's just got cut by a third, and allocators need both data points before taking either story at face value.

TSMC

TSMC's September Revenue Jumps 54.6% Year-on-Year to NT$511.9 Billion

TSMC (SEC Form 6-K) · sec.gov ↗

Unlike announced capex plans or roadmap slides, foundry revenue is a hard, shipped-silicon number — this print confirms actual AI-chip demand is still accelerating even as AI-stock sentiment wobbled this week, a useful corrective for anyone discounting the buildout as hype.

CHIPS

Microsoft to Shift AI Inference Workloads to Its Own Maia Chips, Cutting Nvidia Reliance

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Microsoft plans to primarily run AI inference workloads on its own Maia chips rather than Nvidia hardware, aiming to cut costs and reduce reliance on its largest chip supplier. A top-three hyperscaler publicly pivoting its own silicon to inference work is a direct, near-term margin threat to Nvidia and a signal other hyperscalers may follow — chip-supply-chain allocations should assume this trend widens, not reverses.

GOLDMAN

Goldman Sachs Posts $5.29 Billion Q3 Profit as Equities Trading Revenue Surges 72%

Goldman Sachs (SEC Form 8-K) · sec.gov ↗

Goldman Sachs reported Q3 net revenues of $18.68 billion, up 23% year-on-year, and net earnings of $5.29 billion, up 29%, with equities trading revenue surging 72% to $6.44 billion even as its consumer-card platform revenue fell 72% on Apple Card markdowns. The split result is the headline: institutional risk appetite is clearly strong, but the consumer-card drag is a real cost line that shouldn't get lost in the trading-desk celebration.

JPMORGAN

JPMorgan Chase Posts $15.3 Billion Q3 Net Income, Up From $14.4 Billion a Year Earlier

JPMorgan Chase (SEC Form 8-K) · sec.gov ↗

JPMorgan Chase reported Q3 2026 net income of $15.3 billion, or $5.57 per share, up from $14.4 billion and $5.07 per share a year earlier. Continued double-digit EPS growth at the largest US bank by assets is a useful read-through on consumer and corporate credit health heading into year-end, and a baseline against which the week's more mixed bank results — Bank of America's decline, Citigroup's surge — should be measured.

A double-digit profit decline at the second-largest US bank in the same week Goldman and JPMorgan both posted gains is a clear signal that Q3 bank earnings are a stock-picker's market this quarter, not a uniform read on trading or credit conditions.

CITI

Citigroup Posts Best Third-Quarter Revenue in a Decade at $23.8 Billion

Citigroup (SEC Form 8-K) · sec.gov ↗

Citigroup's Q3 2026 net income rose to $5.2 billion from $3.8 billion a year earlier, with revenue of $23.8 billion marking its strongest third quarter in a decade and $4.1 billion returned to shareholders via buybacks and dividends. GOLD

Gold Holds Above $4,180 an Ounce, Up 25% Since Early 2025 on Hedging Demand

Fortune · fortune.com ↗

Gold traded at $4,182 an ounce on October 13, up roughly 25% since early 2025, with the rally attributed to sustained inflation and economic-uncertainty hedging even as the metal sits slightly below last month's peak. The Standard (Hong Kong) · thestandard.com.hk ↗

Canada Loses 68,000 Jobs in September, Unemployment Rate Climbs to 6.5%

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

PM Mark Carney will attend two "Forever Canadian" rallies in Alberta ahead of the province's October 19 referendum on launching a separation process, even as Premier Danielle Smith — who called the vote — is herself campaigning against a "yes." Carney Fast-Tracks West-Coast Pipeline and Cuts Project-Approval Timelines to One Year

Bloomberg · bloomberg.com ↗

PM Mark Carney has designated a new west-coast oil pipeline a "project of national interest" and is advancing Bill C-39, which cuts federal project-decision timelines to one year and requires concurrent rather than sequential environmental assessments — using the US tariff war as political cover to diversify Canada's energy exports away from a market that currently takes 85% of them. A G7 government compressing permitting to under a year is a structural, multi-year signal for capital allocators in Canadian energy infrastructure, though critics warn the compressed timeline weakens environmental and Indigenous review.

FRANCE

France Unveils €54 Billion Austerity Budget as Debt Nears Record 120% of GDP

Euronews · euronews.com ↗

France's government unveiled its 2027 draft budget on October 1, proposing roughly €54 billion in savings — including limits on pension indexation and a public-sector pay freeze — as public debt nears a record 120% of GDP and the deficit could reach 6.5% without the cuts, against a 5% target.
