What mattered on Saturday, 25 July 2026

21 items · 15 sources · ranked by decision impact · EN/FR
01
WAR ESCALATION

The US carries out a 13th straight night of strikes on Iran as Tehran hits US targets in Bahrain, Kuwait and Jordan and rejects a ceasefire

CNN · cnn.com ↗

A 13th consecutive night of US bombing, Iranian retaliation now reaching bases in three Gulf states, and Tehran's rejection of an Iraqi-brokered ceasefire together kill the de-escalation trade the market had begun to price. With Trump telling Axios he is weighing larger operations, the war is widening, not winding down. Treat every "ceasefire" headline as a countertrend rally and plan around a conflict measured in months.

02
OIL SHOCK

Oil tops $100 for the first time since May as the Houthi Red Sea blockade adds a second chokepoint to the Iran war

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Brent hit $100.65, up nearly 7% on the day, as the Houthi blockade layered a Red Sea chokepoint on top of a closed Strait of Hormuz. This is no longer a headline spike — it is two of the world's oil arteries priced as contested at once, and the pass-through is already showing in $4.09 gasoline. Rebuild every budget, margin and inflation forecast at triple-digit crude, not the pre-war base.

03
ENERGY

A third front opens: Kazakhstan's oil output falls 21% after drone strikes force the CPC to halt Black Sea loadings

Times of Central Asia · timesca.com ↗

Kazakh output dropped to 1.63 million bpd — Tengiz alone fell 56% — after drone strikes on Black Sea tankers forced the Caspian Pipeline Consortium to suspend loadings at Novorossiysk, which handles over 80% of the country's exports and about 2% of global supply. A third artery is now constrained alongside Hormuz and the Red Sea, and Chevron and ExxonMobil sit directly in the blast radius. This is the supply-side reason the oil premium is a floor, not a spike.

04
MARKETS

Wall Street limps to a losing week: the S&P ends flat at 7,412, the Nasdaq falls again, the Dow claws back 0.5%

The Motley Fool · fool.com ↗

The S&P 500 closed essentially flat at 7,412 and the Nasdaq slipped 0.64% to 24,976, sealing a down week after Thursday's megacap rout erased roughly $797 billion from the Magnificent Seven. The tape is digesting an AI-capex scare and a live oil-and-tariff double shock at the same time. Read the Dow's 0.5% bounce as a pause, not an all-clear — the repricing of margins and rate expectations is only starting.

05
CHIPS

Intel beats with revenue up 25% and a swing to profit — then reverses to close down 7.9% on foundry and capex fears

The Motley Fool · fool.com ↗

Intel posted a genuine beat — revenue up 25% and a return to profit — but the stock round-tripped to close at $92.32, down 7.89%, as investors balked at foundry losses and the capital-spending bill needed to compete. The message: even a real turnaround gets punished if the payback horizon is long and dilution is on the table. The market is now separating AI winners from AI spenders, and it is pricing discipline over ambition.

06
MEMORY

A Korean chip rout drags US memory names down: Micron and Western Digital fall 6%, SanDisk drops 9%

24/7 Wall St. · 247wallst.com ↗

An overnight selloff in Korea's KOSPI, led by Samsung and SK Hynix, spilled into US memory stocks — Micron and Western Digital off 6%, SanDisk down 9% — despite year-to-date gains that still run to 227% and 526%. The move is a valuation wobble in the year's hottest trade, not a demand break: AI memory remains scarce. Treat it as the first air-pocket in a crowded rally and a test of who has real conviction versus momentum.

07
CANADA

Carney says "everything is on the table" as he and Trump agree to intensify talks under a 50% tariff on wine, cement and hockey sticks

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

With Trump's 50% tariff on select Canadian goods now signed and a 30-day clock running, Carney and the premiers are unified and racing toward a comprehensive deal while refusing to retaliate pre-emptively. CUSMA-compliant trade may be spared, but the direction is fixed: US market access is now a rising, politicised cost. Canadian exporters should model the tariff as permanent and accelerate diversification rather than wait on the negotiation.

08
WAR POWERS

The House votes 214–208 to order Trump to end the Iran war or seek authorization; Senate Republicans block the companion measure

Just Security · justsecurity.org ↗

A 214–208 House vote directing the president to halt the campaign or seek explicit authorization is the first hard sign of congressional resistance, but Senate Republicans killed the matching bill, leaving the war's funding and momentum intact. The political constraint is real but not yet binding. Watch the vote count as a leading indicator of how much longer Washington's appetite for an open-ended Gulf war holds.

09
SHIPPING

The Houthi Red Sea blockade targets Saudi, Israeli and US-linked tankers — with enforcement calibrated to a ship's flag, not its cargo

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

The blockade declared July 20 is shaping who moves Saudi crude, not whether it moves — Chinese-owned tankers passed Bab el-Mandeb unharmed while Saudi-linked ships were hit. That selectivity is the tell: this is coercive signalling, not a total closure, which keeps a partial flow but a permanent risk premium. Shippers should reassess exposure by flag, reroute via the Cape where economics allow, and price war-risk insurance as a standing cost.

10
OIL

Crude retreats on Friday — WTI down 3.5% to $88.93, Brent off 3.8% to $96.88 — as profit-taking and ceasefire chatter cool the spike

TheStreet · thestreet.com ↗

Oil gave back ground on Friday, with WTI at $88.93 and Brent at $96.88, as traders banked gains and leaned on ceasefire speculation. Do not mistake the pullback for a top: with three chokepoints contested and strikes ongoing, this is volatility around a higher base, not a return to calm. The trading range has reset into the high-$90s — hedge the whipsaw rather than betting on a clean de-escalation.

11
FED

The Fed is expected to hold again next week as an oil-and-tariff inflation pulse ties its hands

CBS News · cbsnews.com ↗

Consensus has the Fed on hold at its meeting next week, pinned by the same twin shock — $100 oil and near-universal tariffs — that is lifting prices everywhere. A rate cut into a live inflation impulse is off the table, which means no monetary relief for borrowers pricing higher-for-longer. Finance to the current curve, not to a pivot the data will not allow.

GOLD

Gold holds near $4,057, up more than 21% over the year, as the safe-haven bid outlasts the daily noise

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Gold sat near $4,057, barely changed on the day but up 21% over twelve months, holding its bid ahead of the Fed. The flat session is noise; the annual run is the signal, and it says the market is paying up for insurance against war, tariffs and sticky inflation all at once. Treat gold as the hedge that is already working, not a call to chase the top.

SOUTH CHINA SEA

China's coast guard water-cannons two Philippine vessels near Scarborough Shoal for a second day as diplomats meet in Manila

The Washington Post · washingtonpost.com ↗

Chinese coast-guard water-cannon attacks on Philippine ships for a second straight day — the third faceoff of the week — show Beijing pressing its South China Sea claims even as the West is fixated on the Gulf. For anyone with regional supply chains, the read is that maritime friction is structural, not episodic.

UKRAINE

Zelensky reverses course, submitting a bill to restore anti-corruption agencies' independence after EU pressure and street protests

RFE/RL · rferl.org ↗

After days of the largest wartime protests and blunt EU warnings, Zelensky submitted a new bill that Ukraine's anti-corruption agencies say "restores" the independence he had just curtailed. Read it as evidence that external leverage over Kyiv still works, and that governance risk, not just the front line, gates the aid.

CRYPTO

Bitcoin slips to about $65,000, down 45% over the year, trading as risk rather than the hedge it is sold as

Fortune · fortune.com ↗

Bitcoin eased to roughly $65,030 and is down about 45% over twelve months, even as gold climbs on the same fears — the clearest tell that crypto still trades as high-beta risk, not insurance. On days when war and tariffs lift havens and push bitcoin down, the correlation speaks for itself. Size it as a liquidity and risk position, not as protection, in a tape defined by geopolitics and inflation.

16
OPEC+

Seven OPEC+ nations agree to trim output by 188,000 barrels a day from August, tightening supply into the shock

Energies Media · energiesmedia.com ↗

Seven OPEC+ members signed off on a 188,000 bpd cut starting in August — a modest volume, but a directional signal that the cartel is guarding price rather than rushing barrels to a market already short. With three chokepoints disrupted, the absence of a compensating supply surge removes the usual pressure valve. Do not count on OPEC+ to cap this rally; the group's incentives now point the same way as the war premium.
