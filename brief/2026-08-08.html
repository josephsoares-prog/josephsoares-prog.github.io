01
MARKETS

US employers shed 23,000 jobs in July against forecasts of an 80,000 gain, with unemployment easing to 4.1% and labour-force participation at a five-year low of 61.4%

TheStreet · thestreet.com ↗

July payrolls fell by 23,000 — the first monthly decline in months and a stunning miss versus the roughly 80,000 gain economists expected — even as the jobless rate ticked down to 4.1% because more people left the workforce, dragging participation to 61.4%. This was the last major labour reading before the September Fed meeting, and a headline this soft pulls a rate cut squarely back into view. The decision-relevant point: the softness is being read as demand weakening, not supply tightening, so allocators should treat a September cut as the base case and reprice rate-sensitive risk accordingly.

02
MARKETS

The S&P 500 closes at a record 7,757.64 and posts its strongest week since April as the soft jobs print revives rate-cut hopes; the Nasdaq jumps 1.3% and the Dow holds above 54,000

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Stocks read the weak payrolls as good news: the S&P 500 rose 0.6% to a record 7,757.64, the Nasdaq gained 1.3% to 26,690, and the Dow added 152 points to 54,037, capping the best week since April with the S&P up 3.6% and the Nasdaq up 5.2%. The market is now trading a soft-landing-plus-cut narrative rather than pricing recession, and momentum is concentrated in the rate-sensitive and AI-chip complex. The risk in owning this rally is that it needs the Fed to deliver — any hawkish surprise or hot inflation print now cuts against a tape priced for easing.

03
HORMUZ

The emerging deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz reportedly recognises Iran's control of the waterway, a strategic win for Tehran that months of US–Israeli strikes failed to break

Fortune · fortune.com ↗

Reporting on the Iran–Oman framework says it would grant Iran effective control over inbound traffic and let Tehran bar US and Israeli vessels — an outcome analysts call a de facto recognition of Iranian authority over the strait. Iran is pressing for a 5%–7% transit fee on cargo value while Oman has floated about 3%, and Washington insists any temporary route carry no tolls, so the money is where the deal could still break. For anyone pricing energy or shipping risk, the signal is that the strait may reopen on Tehran's terms, not the West's — a structurally different Gulf than the one before the blockade.

04
ENERGY

Oil holds a heavy war premium with Brent near $83.64, up almost 16% on the month, as the Hormuz standoff keeps a structural floor under crude

Fortune · fortune.com ↗

Brent settled near $83.64 a barrel, up about 15.8% over the past month and nearly 24% on the year, with the Gulf conflict and the closed Strait of Hormuz keeping a persistent premium in the price even as diplomats talk up a reopening. The operative assumption for planners is a structurally elevated floor, not a snap-back to pre-crisis levels — a reopening on Iran's terms with transit tolls could keep costs high even after ships move. Budget energy-input and freight assumptions to a world where crude stays in the low-to-mid $80s, and treat any deal-driven dip as a fade rather than a trend.

05
GULF SECURITY

Houthi missile and drone strikes hit Saudi-backed forces and camps in Yemen's Marib, killing at least two and wounding 14, as a Saudi source warns the coalition 'won't stand idly by'

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

Iran-aligned Houthis struck a Saudi-backed camp at Sahn al-Jinn and hit displacement camps and residential areas in Marib, killing at least two and wounding 14, while a source close to the Saudi military signalled the coalition will not 'stand idly by.' The threat surface is widening from the Red Sea into Yemen's interior at the same moment Gulf security is already stretched by the Hormuz crisis. Operators exposed to the peninsula should plan for a second front and a higher ceiling on escalation, not a contained shipping problem.

06
ENERGY SECURITY

Abu Dhabi's ADNOC reports 15 vessels attacked since the Gulf conflict began — three this week alone — with one crew member killed and 20 injured

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

Abu Dhabi's national oil company disclosed that 15 vessels have been attacked since the conflict began, including three in the past week, leaving one crew member dead and 20 injured. The tally is the hard evidence behind the war premium: the risk is not hypothetical closure but an active, ongoing campaign against tankers that raises insurance, crewing and routing costs right now. Anyone modelling a quick return to normal freight economics on a Hormuz deal is under-pricing how long the security overhang — and the cost of it — will linger after any reopening.

07
CANADA

Carney hits back at Trump's 'nasty' jab — 'this is a question of Canadian jobs' — as Washington threatens 50% tariffs from 19 August and US aluminium prices climb 58%

Fortune · fortune.com ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney answered Trump's 'nasty' characterisation of Canada bluntly — 'You can say nasty. But this is a question of Canadian jobs' — as the US threatens to widen tariffs to 50% on additional Canadian goods from 19 August, with Carney noting existing aluminium duties have already pushed US aluminium prices up 58%. The tell for boards is that Ottawa is now framing the fight around jobs and prepared to let American buyers absorb the cost, not fold. Treat the 19 August date as a live cliff for cross-border supply chains and price pass-through, and stress-test both sides of the border for a prolonged tariff war rather than a quick settlement.

08
CANADA

Ottawa moves to curb non-US steel imports as the domestic industry signals distress under US tariffs, tightening quotas to protect Canadian mills

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

Carney promised new measures to curb steel arriving from non-US countries, tightening tariff-rate quotas to shield Canadian producers being squeezed between US tariffs and cheap global oversupply. It is a defensive industrial-policy pivot: with the US market partly closed, Ottawa is trying to stop Canada from becoming the dumping ground for redirected foreign steel. For manufacturers and builders, the near-term effect is higher and more volatile domestic steel input costs, and for allocators it signals a government now willing to intervene sector-by-sector as the trade fight drags on.

09
COMMODITIES

Gold hovers near a record around $4,350 an ounce as the weak jobs report and revived rate-cut bets stoke safe-haven demand

TheStreet · thestreet.com ↗

Gold traded near a record around $4,350 an ounce, extending a run of 2026 highs as the soft July jobs data lifted odds of a Fed cut — which lowers the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset — on top of central-bank buying and Gulf tensions. The metal is now doing double duty as a rate-cut trade and a geopolitical hedge, which is why it keeps grinding higher even on risk-on equity days. For allocators, gold's persistence is a market-implied vote that real rates fall and tail risks stay elevated — a signal worth weighing against a stock tape priced for a clean soft landing.

10
TECH

Big Tech storms back as AI fears fade and euphoria resumes, with megacap chip and platform names leading the week's rally

Bloomberg · bloomberg.com ↗

Megacap tech snapped back hard as the late-July AI wobble — sparked by fears over the cost of financing OpenAI's buildout — gave way to renewed euphoria, with chip and platform leaders driving the Nasdaq's 5.2% weekly gain. The round-trip in under two weeks shows how thin the line is between 'AI capex is a bubble' and 'AI capex is the growth story,' and how quickly sentiment reprices the same facts. For allocators the lesson is concentration risk: the index is again leaning on a handful of AI names, so the rally's durability is only as strong as the next few megacap earnings and the capital-spending guidance behind them.

11
TECH

Tesla and SpaceX commit $16.8 billion to a Texas 'Terafab' chip plant spanning roughly 100 million square feet to make silicon for vehicles, robots and spacecraft

Tech Startups · techstartups.com ↗

Musk's two companies are jointly investing $16.8 billion in a Texas semiconductor 'Terafab' of roughly 100 million square feet to make chips for cars, humanoid robots and spacecraft — an aggressive bet on vertical integration and domestic supply. The move reads as both a hedge against chip-supply concentration and a play to control the silicon roadmap for autonomy and robotics. For competitors and suppliers, it signals that the largest AI-hardware buyers increasingly want to own the fab, not just the order book — a structural threat to merchant chipmakers over the next cycle.

12
REGULATION

Meta is ordered to pay $567 million over child-safety failures on Facebook and Instagram, bringing total penalties in the case to $942 million

Tech Startups · techstartups.com ↗

A court ordered Meta to pay $567 million over failures to protect minors on Facebook and Instagram and to build stronger age-assurance systems, pushing total penalties in the case to $942 million. The fine itself is immaterial to Meta's balance sheet, but the mandated age-verification remedy is not — it foreshadows compliance cost and friction that could reshape how large platforms onboard and monetise younger users. For anyone allocating to the platform economy, the durable risk is regulatory, not financial: age-assurance mandates are spreading, and they raise the cost of the ad-driven growth model.

13
AI INFRA

Nvidia-backed Firmus raises $2 billion at a $10.5 billion valuation to build AI data centres across the Asia-Pacific

Tech Startups · techstartups.com ↗

Australian AI-infrastructure startup Firmus raised $2 billion at a $10.5 billion valuation, backed by Nvidia, to build out data centres across the Asia-Pacific. The raise is another data point that the capital is flowing not to models but to the power, cooling and real estate under them — the physical layer that determines who can actually train and serve at scale. For investors, the AI value chain is visibly shifting toward infrastructure economics; the question that decides returns is whether compute demand keeps pace with the tens of billions now being committed to build it.

14
AI / CHINA

ByteDance is training a 10-trillion-parameter AI model, reportedly triple the size of leading rivals, in a push to match US frontier systems despite export curbs

Tech Startups · techstartups.com ↗

TikTok owner ByteDance is reportedly training a 10-trillion-parameter model — about three times the size of leading Western systems — as China pushes to close the frontier-AI gap despite US chip-export controls. The scale claim matters less than the signal: Chinese labs are still finding compute, whether stockpiled, domestic or offshore, and are willing to spend to stay in the race. For policymakers and allocators, it undercuts the assumption that export curbs alone will hold a durable US lead, and it raises the odds of a genuinely bipolar AI ecosystem with separate model, chip and standards stacks.

15
TECH M&A

AMD acquires Toronto-based Taalas, a specialised AI-inference chip startup, to deepen its challenge to Nvidia in low-cost inference

Tech Startups · techstartups.com ↗

AMD acquired Taalas, a Toronto startup building specialised AI-inference silicon that had previously raised about $219 million, in an undisclosed deal aimed at the fast-growing inference market. Inference — running models in production rather than training them — is where the volume and margin increasingly sit, and where challengers can most credibly chip at Nvidia's dominance. For Canada, it is another case of homegrown deep-tech IP exiting to a US acquirer; for the chip sector, it signals the inference battleground is now consolidating, and the winners will be decided on cost-per-token, not raw training horsepower.

TECH EARNINGS

Cloudflare posts $696 million in Q2 revenue, up 36% year over year, and lifts its full-year outlook toward $2.87 billion

Tech Startups · techstartups.com ↗

Cloudflare reported $696 million in second-quarter revenue, up 36% year over year, and raised full-year guidance to roughly $2.86–2.87 billion. Thirty-six-percent growth at that scale, with a raised outlook, is exactly the profile the market is paying up for — durable expansion tied to AI-adjacent networking and security demand. For allocators reading the software tape, Cloudflare sits on the winning side of a sharp earnings dispersion this season: infrastructure names with clear AI pull-through are re-rating while application-software laggards are being punished hard.

MARKETS

Earnings dispersion widens: Doximity soars ~81% and Atlassian jumps ~32% while The Trade Desk sinks ~28% and QuidelOrtho drops ~27%

TheStreet · thestreet.com ↗

Single-stock reactions this week were violent and two-sided: Doximity surged about 81% and Atlassian about 32% on strong results and raised guidance, while The Trade Desk fell roughly 28% and QuidelOrtho about 27% on disappointments. Moves of this magnitude on earnings signal a market with little tolerance for a miss and a big appetite to reward a beat — classic late-cycle dispersion. For investors, the message is that index-level calm is masking brutal stock-level risk; position sizing and event hedging around earnings matter more now than a benign headline tape would suggest.

M&A

Electronic Arts' $55 billion take-private closes under a Saudi PIF-led consortium, one of the largest leveraged buyouts on record

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

EA's $55 billion take-private completed with Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund holding about 93.4%, Silver Lake 5.5% and Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners 1.1%, after all regulatory approvals cleared. The close puts one of the world's largest game publishers under sovereign-fund control and marks another leg of Gulf capital moving from passive stakes into outright ownership of Western marquee assets. For dealmakers, it signals that mega-LBOs are viable again when a patient sovereign anchor supplies the equity — a template likely to recur across media, gaming and sports.

TECH

OpenAI is developing a puck-sized smart speaker priced above $300, co-designed with Jony Ive's LoveFrom, targeting a 2027 launch

Tech Startups · techstartups.com ↗

OpenAI is reportedly building a hockey-puck-sized smart speaker priced above $300, co-designed with Jony Ive's LoveFrom, aimed at a 2027 launch. It confirms the ambition to own a dedicated hardware surface for AI rather than renting attention on phones controlled by Apple and Google. The strategic stakes are large: if AI assistants migrate onto purpose-built devices, the platform toll shifts, and the incumbents' grip on the consumer interface — and the data behind it — comes under real threat. Watch this as an early marker of the coming fight over the AI hardware layer.

DIPLOMACY

Israel and Lebanon agree on a shortlist of countries to verify Hezbollah's disarmament under a US-brokered deal, with the next talks set for early September

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

At US-brokered talks in Rome, Israel and Lebanon agreed on a shortlist of countries to verify Hezbollah's disarmament, with Washington to make the final pick and the next round scheduled for early September. Progress on a verification mechanism is the connective tissue that could turn a fragile truce into a durable one — or expose how far apart the sides remain if it stalls. For regional-risk watchers, the September session is the near-term hinge: a workable verification regime lowers the odds of a wider Hezbollah–Israel flare-up that would compound the Gulf's energy and shipping stress.

HORMUZ

Iran says a Hormuz reopening agreement now awaits its Supreme National Security Council's approval — the last procedural step before any deal

The Times of Israel · timesofisrael.com ↗

Tehran says an agreement with the US and Oman to reopen the strait without toll charges now hinges on approval from Iran's Supreme National Security Council, framing a deal as one procedural step away. The signalling cuts both ways: it lets Iran claim it is negotiating in good faith while keeping the timeline — and the terms — under domestic control, so 'awaiting approval' can stretch. For markets pricing a near-term reopening, treat the council step as a real option, not a formality; the war premium in oil should compress only on confirmed transit, not on another round of optimistic language.

Commentary Hooks

Bad News Is Good News, Until the Fed Has to Deliver

A payrolls report that showed the economy shedding jobs sent the S&P to a record — because a weaker labour market means a likelier rate cut. That logic works right up to the moment the Fed either eases into re-accelerating inflation or disappoints a market that has already priced the cut. The interesting question isn't whether stocks like soft data today; it's what happens when 'soft enough to cut' tips into 'soft enough to worry.'

Reopening Hormuz Isn't the Same as Reopening the Old Gulf

Headlines will read 'Hormuz deal' as a return to normal. Read the terms instead: if Iran wins recognition of its control and a transit fee, and tankers are still being attacked, the strait reopens into a structurally costlier, higher-risk regime. The trade isn't 'crisis over, sell oil' — it's that the war premium may compress but the security and toll overhang becomes a permanent feature to be budgeted, not a headline to be faded.

The AI Money Has Moved From Models to Concrete

In one day: Tesla and SpaceX commit $16.8 billion to a Texas fab, Nvidia-backed Firmus raises $2 billion for Asia-Pacific data centres, and ByteDance is said to be training a 10-trillion-parameter model. The common thread is that the marginal AI dollar now buys silicon, power and real estate — the physical layer — not another chatbot. That reframes the bubble debate: the risk isn't overpaid models, it's tens of billions in hard infrastructure betting that compute demand keeps compounding.

  • Whether the brutal earnings dispersion — Doximity and Atlassian up sharply, The Trade Desk and QuidelOrtho down hard — widens into a broader repricing as more names report.
