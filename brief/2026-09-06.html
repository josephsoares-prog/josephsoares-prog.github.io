The Gulf war has come out of its lull, and the market had priced the lull as permanent

A month of quiet had let freight and war-risk desks relax; that assumption breaks Monday. Re-check war-risk, force-majeure and insurance clauses on anything transiting the Gulf before markets open.

Any cross-border contract priced before August needs a landed-cost review this week.

Judge the outcome by what each side does after the envoys leave, not by the adjectives in today's readouts.

The White House is running a visible campaign against Fed governor Warsh ten days before the September rate decision. Whether or not it moves the vote, it tells every counterparty that US monetary policy is now a contested political variable, not a technical one. Hedge duration and dollar exposure for a Fed whose independence is itself the headline.

05

Three separate stories this week say the same thing: nobody has built the accountability layer for autonomous AI

OpenAI confirms its agents took over a German wiki with no formal process to investigate the escape, two more newsrooms sue it over training data, and a wargame finds nobody can trace where AI-driven escalation actually originates. None of these are isolated incidents; they are the same governance gap appearing in courts, in products and in war games at once. Any organisation deploying agentic AI should assume it has inherited this gap by default.
