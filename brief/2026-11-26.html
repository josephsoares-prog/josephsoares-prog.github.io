TRADE & TARIFFS

USTR Proposes New Section 301 Tariffs on 14 Economies, Topping Out at 20% for China and Vietnam

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

The US Trade Representative capped an eight-month Section 301 investigation into “structural excess capacity” in steel, autos, chips and solar with proposed new duties on 14 of 16 economies probed — a 20% rate on both China (layered on top of roughly 25% from Trump’s first term, and on a truce that expires January 10) and Vietnam, with the EU, Japan and South Korea capped at 15% and Malaysia at 19%; Mexico and Norway were spared. A senior US official told Reuters the target is countries “promoting production and capacity divorced from the realities of demand,” and trade-policy expert Dmitry Grozoubinski called the tool “probably the most legally robust” of Trump’s tariffs, since it survives the kind of court challenge that already struck down the broader IEEPA duties. Sourcing and pricing models still built on last year’s tariff assumptions need updating now, before the Federal Register notice, not after.

MARKETS & MACRO

Hot October PCE Pushes Fed-Hike Odds to 98% as Wall Street Absorbs an AI-Driven Chip Selloff

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

October’s core PCE inflation — the Fed’s preferred gauge — came in at 3.3% year-over-year, above the 3.2% consensus, while the headline rate held at 3.8%; traders are now pricing a 98% probability of a 25-basis-point Fed hike in December and the 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.85%. The Dow nonetheless closed up 0.33% at 50,182.95 while the S&P 500 (-0.25% to 7,182.78) and Nasdaq Composite (-0.62% to 23,585.80) slipped as chipmakers led a fresh AI-profitability-driven selloff — Nvidia fell as much as 8% intraday before closing down 3.91% at $149.30. A market absorbing a hawkish inflation surprise and a chip-sector risk-off move in the same session, heading into a holiday close, is not showing conviction either way; treat Friday’s thin half-day session as noise and wait for next week’s volume before reading the signal.

UKRAINE

US Negotiators Privately Rule Out Troops or Bases in Ukraine Under Any Security Guarantee

The Kyiv Independent · kyivindependent.com ↗

US negotiators have privately ruled out stationing troops or setting up bases in postwar Ukraine as part of any security guarantee, people familiar with the talks tell Kyiv Independent, pushing instead an “Article 5-like” arrangement that does not specify who would respond to a future attack or whether force would be used. Zelensky told Kyiv Independent directly that Ukraine still lacks “a clear answer” on how such a group would defend it, that any guarantee “has to be on paper” — “so far, I haven’t seen any such steps on paper” — and that Kyiv needs guarantees lasting “preferably forever, or at least for decades.” A security guarantee built with no troops, no bases and no named trigger is, by design, harder for Russia to violate in a way that forces a US response — price the deterrent value of any eventual deal well below its face value on paper.

GAZA

Israeli Strike Kills Hamas Internal Security Chief, Wife and Two Daughters in Gaza’s Shati Camp

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

An Israeli air strike at dawn on Gaza’s Shati refugee camp killed Muhammad Majid Abu al-Awf, the Internal Security Service’s director for northern Gaza, along with his wife and two daughters, 15 and 13, Gaza’s Interior Ministry said — the second senior ISS official killed in under 24 hours, after Brig. Gen. Naim Abu Nahal on November 24, in what the ministry called an “ongoing attempt” to dismantle the territory’s post-ceasefire security apparatus. Israel offered no public military justification for either strike, and with at least 1,680 Palestinians now dead since the October 2025 ceasefire took effect, a pattern of killing the officials meant to police Hamas’s own ranks suggests it is the ceasefire’s internal-security architecture, not just its front line, that is now being dismantled — a distinction that matters for anyone modeling how much longer the truce holds.

BUSINESS & M&A

CrowdStrike Posts Record Net-New ARR, Raises FY2027 Guidance as Its $43.9 Billion Zscaler Deal Advances

CrowdStrike (SEC filing) · sec.gov ↗

CrowdStrike’s fiscal third-quarter results, released Monday, showed revenue up 28% to $1.58 billion and non-GAAP EPS of $0.34, with net-new annual recurring revenue hitting a record $419.2 million — up 58% year-over-year — prompting the company to raise its full fiscal-2027 net-new ARR growth guidance by 1,060 basis points to 45% at the midpoint. The results land as its all-stock, roughly $43.9 billion acquisition of Zscaler advances, which CrowdStrike’s CFO says will be “significantly and immediately accretive” to free cash flow per share, and as its year-old Falcon Guardian AI-agent-security product already clears $100 million in ARR. For competitors watching the largest cybersecurity merger on record take shape, a target that just posted record organic growth on top of the deal is the harder combination to out-compete on price alone.

UKRAINE

Ukraine Says Its Blysk Combat Laser Intercepted a Russian Jet-Powered Drone for the First Time

The Kyiv Independent · kyivindependent.com ↗

A Ukrainian air-defense laser called “Blysk,” built by the company Carmine Sky and rated at 4 kilowatts with an effective range up to one kilometer, shot down a Russian jet-powered Geran drone while protecting a critical-infrastructure site, presidential adviser Serhii “Flash” Beskrestnov said — the first such intercept in combat conditions, he said, cautioning that “it is still too early to talk about serial production.” The laser is one of roughly 100 companies’ projects under Ukraine’s Brave-1-backed Private Air Defense program, about half already fielding active units that had downed more than 60 aerial targets as of mid-October. A laser that runs on electricity rather than an interceptor missile is a direct attack on Russia’s drone-swarm cost-exchange math — even a single verified combat kill is worth tracking as a leading indicator for how Ukraine’s air-defense economics could shift if production scales.
