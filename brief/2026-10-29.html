IRAN WAR

Tehran Presses 7-Day Hormuz Ceasefire Plan as Trump Rejects It and Vows Strikes Could Resume After Midterms

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Iran is pressing a seven-day plan to partially reopen the Strait of Hormuz, but President Trump publicly rejected it and said on October 28 that “anything’s possible” on resuming US strikes once the November 3 midterms are past, even as he separately confirmed Vice President Vance sent Tehran a letter on medical supplies. The war entered its eighth month this week with no resolution path, per the Bank of Canada’s own policy statement citing it as the driver of persistently high oil prices. Executives and portfolio managers hedging Gulf-transit energy, shipping, and war-risk insurance should treat November 3 itself — not the general level of tension — as the trigger date markets are now pricing.

02
OIL & ENERGY

Brent Slips to $114 as Traders Weigh Reported Vance Outreach to Iran, Trump Downplays It

The National · thenationalnews.com ↗

Brent crude eased to about $114.10 a barrel and WTI held above $101 on October 29, down roughly 1% from Wednesday’s close, after the Wall Street Journal reported Vice President JD Vance sent a letter to Iran’s National Security Council chief, Mohsen Rezaei, aimed at reviving peace talks. Trump downplayed the move, saying Vance acted on his own and the letter concerned only medical supplies — a distinction that matters because it signals Washington has not softened its actual negotiating position. Energy buyers should read this as a modest, sentiment-driven dip rather than a structural turn, given the price remains roughly 55% above pre-war levels near $72.

03
FEDERAL RESERVE

Fed Holds Rates at 3.75%-4% in 10-2 Vote as Cook and Hammack Dissent for a Hike

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

The Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate at 3.75%-4% on October 28 in a 10-2 vote, with Fed Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack both dissenting in favor of a quarter-point hike — a notably hawkish split given the committee had been leaning toward raising rates just a month earlier. The statement upgraded growth language from “solid” to “strong” and softened its commitment language on the 2% inflation target, while the 10-year Treasury yield sat near 5.02% going into the decision. Rate-sensitive desks and corporate treasurers should treat the dissent itself as the signal: even with oil-driven inflation running hot, a majority of the committee chose to wait rather than tighten further.

04
BANK OF CANADA

Bank of Canada Hikes to 2.5%, Citing “Higher-for-Longer” Oil Prices From the Middle East War

Bank of Canada · bankofcanada.ca ↗

The Bank of Canada raised its overnight rate by 25 basis points to 2.5% on October 28, explicitly blaming “persistently high” oil prices tied to the Middle East conflict, now in its eighth month, for inflation running near 3% since April and expected to hit 3.5% by year-end. Governing Council flagged new US tariffs as the key reason it expects Q4 growth to slow even as Q2 GDP came in at a stronger-than-expected 3.3%, and left the door open to a further hike while promising to “proceed carefully.” Canadian mortgage holders, exporters, and bond investors should note the Bank’s own framing: this was a hike to contain an external oil shock, not a response to domestic overheating, and the next test comes December 9.

AI CAPEX

Hyperscaler AI Spending Hits $725 Billion for 2026 as Meta Falls 6% and Alphabet Jumps 7% on Capex Guidance

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, and Alphabet all reported quarterly results the same week, pushing combined 2026 AI capital spending toward $725 billion, up from a pre-earnings estimate near $670 billion — Meta raised its forecast to $125-145 billion and fell about 6% on the news, while Alphabet guided to $180-190 billion and rose roughly 7%. Microsoft now expects $190 billion in calendar-2026 capex, including $25 billion tied to higher component pricing, and Amazon said its roughly $200 billion plan remains “largely the same.” Boards weighing AI infrastructure exposure should note the market’s split verdict: investors are rewarding capex tied to visible cloud growth and punishing capex framed as defensive cost absorption, with underspending still seen as the bigger risk than overspending.

ANTITRUST

Nvidia's $12.9 Billion Hugging Face Acquisition Draws DOJ Antitrust Scrutiny

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

The US Department of Justice is inquiring into Nvidia’s $12.9 billion purchase of Hugging Face, the widely used open-source AI model-sharing platform, over concerns the chipmaker could use the deal to steer developers toward its own hardware and cloud ecosystem. No formal second request has been confirmed, but the inquiry itself signals Washington’s antitrust appetite now extends to AI infrastructure chokepoints, not just chips. General counsels and corporate-development teams at AI companies should treat this as a preview of how any large platform-layer acquisition by a dominant compute provider will now be read in Washington, deal structure and all.

07
VENEZUELA

Machado Returns to Venezuela After Months in Exile, Challenging Rodríguez for Power

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Opposition leader María Corina Machado returned to Venezuela on October 25 after months in exile, directly challenging acting leader Delcy Rodríguez for control following Nicolás Maduro’s removal, in a move analysts call a surprise bid to pressure Rodríguez’s camp without openly breaking with Washington. The transition’s legitimacy — and any US sanctions relief or oil-sector re-engagement tied to it — now hinges on whether Rodríguez’s government and Machado’s coalition can agree on a path to elections. Energy traders and anyone with Venezuela exposure should treat this as the opening move in a succession fight, not its resolution.

08
VENEZUELA

Rubio Warns Machado That Street Protests “Won't Get You Democracy” in Venezuela

Axios · axios.com ↗

Secretary of State Marco Rubio publicly warned María Corina Machado on October 27 that mobilizing street protests “won’t get you democracy,” pushing her instead toward a negotiated electoral path with Rodríguez’s government — a notable public distancing from a figure Washington has backed for years. The signal matters because it suggests the Trump administration wants an orderly, negotiated transition it can manage, not a popular uprising it cannot control. Investors and policy desks should read this as Washington capping its own tolerance for instability in Caracas, regardless of its past rhetorical support for Machado.

09
BRAZIL

Flavio Bolsonaro Flees to the US After Lula Wins Brazil's Election; His Party Accepts Defeat, He Doesn't

NPR · wunc.org ↗

Flavio Bolsonaro left Brazil for the United States after losing the presidential runoff to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, who won with 51.8% of the vote, according to sources cited by Reuters; his own Liberal Party has accepted the result even as he personally refuses to concede and signals a legal challenge. The split between party and candidate matters for governability — Lula needs a functioning opposition to negotiate with in Congress, and a delegitimized loser who still commands street support is a harder partner than one who concedes cleanly. Investors in Brazilian assets should watch whether the party’s acceptance holds or erodes under pressure from Bolsonaro’s base.

CANADA-US TRADE

Carney Says No Further Trade Pressure on US for Now, as Trump's Import Bans Keep Hitting Canadian Goods

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney said he has no plans to apply further trade pressure on Washington as the November 3 US midterms approach, even as Trump has escalated with outright import bans on nearly $1 billion of Canadian goods including alcoholic beverages, dairy products, and motorcycles. The restraint is deliberate: Carney is betting that patience now preserves leverage for a deal once the midterms are past, a wager that is already costing jobs — Stelco is laying off hundreds at its Hamilton plant, which Carney said the company “betrayed” its workers over. Canadian exporters and provincial trade officials should treat the current calm as tactical, not a sign the dispute is cooling.

CANADA-EU

Carney Embraces Proposal for Canada to Become the EU's First “Associate Member” Ahead of Montreal Summit

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

Prime Minister Carney has embraced a European proposal to make Canada the EU’s first “associate member,” a status that would deepen defence, trade, and labour-mobility ties without full membership, as Ottawa hosts a Canada-EU summit in Montreal aimed at an “ambitious new partnership.” The push is explicitly a hedge against Washington: with the US trade relationship unresolved and Trump’s tariffs still biting, Carney is diversifying Canada’s economic dependency in real time rather than waiting for a US deal. Canadian firms with European supply chains or EU market ambitions should treat this as a credible medium-term shift, not a symbolic gesture — the EU side has already given Carney a notably warm reception in Brussels and Strasbourg.

CANADA BUDGET

Champagne Previews “Generational Investment” as Ottawa Sets November 4 Budget With a Bigger Deficit Than Last Year

CBC · cbc.ca ↗

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne says Canadians “should be prepared for a generational investment,” comparing the moment to 1945 and framing the November 4 budget as a down payment on reducing dependence on the US; Prime Minister Carney has already confirmed the coming deficit will exceed last year’s $61.9 billion. Champagne is pairing that spending with a promise to cut operational costs 7.5% in 2026-27, rising to 15% by 2028-29, while the interim Parliamentary Budget Officer says Ottawa still lacks clear fiscal anchors. Bond investors and the opposition Conservatives, who are withholding support until they see the full document, should treat the gap between “generational investment” and credible fiscal anchors as the budget’s central tension.

UKRAINE

Kremlin Says European Countries Excluded From Next Round of Ukraine Talks

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

The Kremlin said on October 28 that European countries will not be included in the next round of Ukraine talks, a direct rebuff to Kyiv’s European backers who have pushed for a seat at the table. Moscow’s insistence on a US-Russia-Ukraine format over any broader European involvement signals it still sees Washington, not Brussels, as the only interlocutor worth negotiating with — a structural preference that will outlast any single round of talks. European capitals and NATO allies funding Ukraine’s war effort should treat their own exclusion as a bargaining signal, not a procedural footnote.

GULF SECURITY

Oman Proposes Gulf-Iran Non-Aggression Pact as Tehran Talks With Saudi, Qatari Officials Yield No Deal

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Oman’s foreign minister proposed a regional security framework — including a non-aggression pact — between Gulf states and Iran that would end US primacy as the region’s security guarantor, as Saudi, Qatari, Pakistani, and Turkish officials held the first high-level contact with Tehran since the war began, on October 26, without reaching an agreement. Iranian officials dismissed Qatar’s proposals as unacceptable the following day, and analysts at Chatham House and the International Crisis Group say a real framework is unlikely for months or years given how tied Gulf states remain to the US security umbrella. Energy and defence planners should read this as a hedging conversation among US allies, not an imminent realignment — the sequencing dispute between Washington and Tehran remains the real obstacle.

IRAQ-SYRIA

Iraqi PM Makes First Postwar Visit to Syria, Advancing Oil Pipeline Deal With Chevron-Linked Consortium

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi met Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa in Damascus on October 28 — the first visit by an Iraqi premier since Bashar al-Assad’s fall — to advance exports of Iraqi oil through the Haditha-Baniyas pipeline, which Iraq only recently began using in limited volumes during the regional war, under a July agreement involving a consortium that includes Chevron. The visit also covered trade, agriculture, and security coordination, signaling both governments see deeper integration as useful insulation against the wider conflict. Energy logistics planners should note this adds a second Gulf-adjacent export route to watch alongside the Strait of Hormuz, even as volumes remain limited for now.

RUSSIA-AFRICA

Ethiopia Receives 12 Russian Su-35 Fighter Jets, Plans S-400 Purchase

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Ethiopia has taken delivery of 12 Russian Su-35 fighter jets and is planning to acquire S-400 air-defence systems, deepening a defence relationship with Moscow at a moment when Russia is courting African partners while stretched by its own war footing and Western sanctions. The deal extends Russia’s arms-export reach into the Horn of Africa even as its own military-industrial capacity is under strain, suggesting Moscow is prioritizing strategic partnerships over near-term battlefield needs. Defence planners and investors tracking African security alignments should note Ethiopia is signaling a clear tilt toward Russian hardware over Western alternatives.
