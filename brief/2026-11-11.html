US INFLATION

October CPI Rises 0.4% as BLS Says It Cannot Publish an Annual Inflation Rate for the First Time on Record

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics · bls.gov ↗

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Tuesday that its all-items Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% in October, with core inflation (excluding food and energy) up 0.3% for a third straight month, but the agency said it cannot publish a 12-month inflation rate at all because last year’s shutdown-related data gap left it with no October 2025 baseline to compare against — a first in the index’s history. Energy costs jumped 0.7% after a 5.2% spike in September, while shelter rose a comparatively modest 0.3%. For anyone pricing the Fed’s December decision, a hot monthly print with no annual anchor to judge it against is exactly the kind of data vacuum that lets both hawks and doves claim the number supports them.

02
NVIDIA GOVERNANCE

Nvidia Board Confirms Jensen Huang Had Affair With Executive U.S. Intelligence Flagged as a Possible Chinese Asset

Associated Press via OPB · opb.org ↗

Nvidia’s board said Sunday it is investigating whether CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, after Huang told directors he had been intimate with a Singapore-based chip-buying executive the New York Times reported U.S. intelligence believes was gathering information for Beijing. Huang told employees he had been unfaithful to his wife and apologized, while the board said it has “no reason to believe” company secrets were compromised and that he will remain CEO and fully cooperate with the review; Trump called Huang “an honorable guy.” For anyone holding the world’s most valuable chipmaker, the question isn’t whether Huang survives this politically — it’s whether the review turns up anything that touches export-control compliance or trade secrets, which is the only version of this story that would actually move the stock for fundamental rather than headline reasons.

AI IPO

Anthropic’s IPO Valuation Talk Drops to $1.1-$1.3 Trillion as Roadshow Opens This Week

Investing.com via Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Anthropic began formally marketing its IPO as early as Monday, with pricing and a Nasdaq debut expected the week of November 16 under a compressed timeline meant to beat Thanksgiving, but Bloomberg reports the valuation being discussed with investors has fallen to $1.1-$1.3 trillion, down from the $1.5-$2 trillion range floated earlier this month. The slide tracks a rough week for AI stocks broadly and a deceleration in Anthropic’s own growth — third-quarter revenue grew 47% sequentially, versus 144% in the second quarter. For anyone with secondary-market exposure or allocation decisions to make before pricing, the valuation compression itself is the signal: it means the company’s own bankers are reading the AI-trade mood more cautiously than the headlines from a month ago suggested.

04
US POLITICS

Democrats Win Full Control of Congress, Setting Up Two Years of Divided Government and Investigations

NPR via KUOW · kuow.org ↗

Democrats won control of both the House and Senate in last week’s midterms — their first full control of Congress since 2023 — flipping seven Senate seats including North Carolina, Ohio and Texas, and winning House seats in Florida, Pennsylvania and even a Kentucky district Trump carried by 17 points in 2024. The result puts Trump’s final two years under investigation from both chambers, with Senate control also ending the steady pace of judicial confirmations and requiring Democratic votes for Cabinet nominees going forward. For anyone modeling policy risk into 2027, the practical ceiling here is lower than the headline suggests: Democrats still need Republican votes for the twelve annual appropriations bills, so expect this to shape messaging toward 2028 well before it reshapes actual legislation.

05
UKRAINE PEACE TALKS

Kushner’s Push for a New European Security ‘Framework’ Catches European Capitals Off Guard as Abu Dhabi Talks Open

Meduza · meduza.io ↗

US, Russian and Ukrainian delegations opened two days of trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday — Washington represented by Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, Russia by Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky, Ukraine by Rustem Umerov and Kyrylo Budanov — with Secretary of State Rubio framing it as a chance to advance post-war security “architecture.” European officials are attending in a separate track after Kushner posted about a new European security framework that, Meduza reports, caught EU and NATO capitals off guard, and after Putin reportedly told Finland’s and Norway’s leaders in a late-October call that Russia will not drop its goal of taking all of Donbas. For anyone pricing European defense or reconstruction exposure, the fact that Europe is negotiating a framework it didn’t help draft is the detail to watch, not the talks’ “constructive” tone.

06
IRAN

Iran Submits Nuclear Proposal via Qatar as Both Sides Report Ships Hit Near Strait of Hormuz

Reuters via Khaleej Times · khaleejtimes.com ↗

Iranian state media suggested explosions near Qeshm Island on Saturday evening were tied to Iranian military activity rather than a US strike, as Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said two tankers caught fire after hitting mines on an “unauthorised route” and the US military said it disabled a commercial vessel in the Gulf of Oman — with Reuters unable to confirm either side’s account. The more consequential detail: Iran has submitted a nuclear proposal to Washington via Qatari mediation, even as a new US task force moves against sanctioned goods reaching Iran overland and via the Caspian. For anyone pricing Gulf shipping or insurance risk, a live nuclear track running in parallel with contested strikes is a meaningfully different situation than a straight military standoff, and worth separating from the noise around any single tanker incident.

07
CHINA TRADE

China’s Trade Surplus Tops $1 Trillion for a Second Straight Year as AI-Linked Exports Surge 35%

Euronews / AFP · euronews.com ↗

China’s customs administration reported October exports rose 35.2% year-on-year to $412.3 billion and imports rose 41.6% to $307.1 billion, pushing the January-October trade surplus to $1.04 trillion — already ahead of last year’s pace, when the trillion-dollar mark wasn’t reached until November. Euronews reports the surge is driven largely by global demand for AI-related hardware — chips, data-center servers, networking gear — with exports to the US alone up 32.9% last month, data that lands just ahead of a planned Xi-Trump meeting at APEC in Shenzhen on November 18-19. For anyone assessing how much AI capex is actually flowing through Chinese supply chains versus US and allied ones, this is the clearest monthly signal yet that the buildout is global, not a US-only story.

08
VENEZUELA

Washington Pushes Phased Democratic Transition Plan for Venezuela as Machado Weighs Return

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

The US has urged Venezuela to follow a phased plan toward a democratic transition in the aftermath of Maduro’s ouster, as opposition leader María Corina Machado weighs returning to the country and has said elections could realistically happen within a year rather than immediately. The plan’s pacing — rather than any snap vote — appears to be the operative US position, with Machado publicly cautioning against letting Maduro-era figures retain influence during the handover. For anyone tracking when Venezuelan crude actually returns to global markets at scale, the transition’s political timeline, not the energy deals already being signed, is the real constraint on that supply.

09
TAIWAN

Taiwan Detects 49 Chinese Aircraft Sorties in a Single Day as Analysts Warn of a ‘Quiet Exercise’ in Pressure

Taipei Times · taipeitimes.com ↗

Taiwan’s defense ministry reported 49 Chinese military aircraft sorties around the island in the 24 hours before Wednesday, alongside six warships and six government vessels, with analyst Su Tzu-yun calling it a “quiet exercise” of pressure that stops short of a formally announced drill. The report coincided with the US carrier Theodore Roosevelt transiting the region and China’s Liaoning operating east of the Philippines, with a possible Shandong exercise flagged for mid-November. For anyone pricing Taiwan Strait risk into supply-chain or insurance decisions, the absence of a declared exercise is itself the point — this kind of unannounced, rising-tempo activity is designed to normalize a presence that would otherwise draw a formal response.
