MARKETS

Wall Street’s AI Trade Cracks as Nasdaq Sinks 2.6%, Chipmakers Lead Rout

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

The S&P 500 fell 1.2% and the Nasdaq tumbled 2.6% Thursday as investors reassessed whether AI infrastructure spending will ever pay for itself — the semiconductor ETF dropped roughly 4% with Lam Research down about 9%, while money rotated into bonds and pushed the 10-year Treasury yield down 11 basis points to 5.02%. IBM’s own CEO is now publicly flagging that large enterprises are scrutinizing AI return on investment, which is the kind of admission that turns a pullback into a re-rating. Boards and CFOs with AI capex on the books should expect financing costs and investor patience to tighten in tandem from here, not independently.

02
SAUDI ARABIA

Houthi Strikes Kill Several in Saudi Arabia as Attacks on Energy Infrastructure Widen

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

An attack claimed by the Houthis hit King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, killing two people and wounding eight, hours after a separate drone strike near Saudi Aramco’s Shaybah oilfield killed two more; the group says it struck Saudi infrastructure — including Abha airport, the defence ministry in Riyadh and Aramco’s Yanbu facilities — over just 48 hours. Aramco reportedly ordered an evacuation of its Dhahran headquarters, and the East-West pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz already saw flows halted once this month after a strike on a pumping station. Risk and continuity teams with Saudi operations should treat the breadth and frequency of targets, not any single strike, as evidence the campaign has shifted from episodic to sustained.

03
OIL

Brent Tops $111 as Saudi Attacks and Iran-Strike Fears Drive Oil to Five-Month High

The National · thenationalnews.com ↗

Brent crude climbed toward $113 and WTI near $100 — levels last seen in May — after the Houthi strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure compounded fears that Washington and Israel are weighing fresh strikes on Iran once the US midterms pass. The risk premium is now layered three deep: direct Saudi infrastructure risk, Strait of Hormuz disruption risk, and a geopolitical calendar that discourages escalation only until November 3. Energy buyers and hedgers should lock in near-term exposure now; the premium is more likely to widen than fade before the midterms resolve.

04
IRAN

Trump Accuses Iran of Staging Fake Strikes to Inflate Oil Prices Before Midterms

CBS News · cbsnews.com ↗

Trump posted that Iran is “making up fake hits, mines, and everything else” in the Strait of Hormuz to drive up oil prices ahead of the midterms, after Iran’s Revolutionary Guard claimed it struck seven vessels; the Financial Times separately reported Trump blocked an Israeli plan for multiday strikes on Iran earlier this month specifically to avoid pre-election escalation. Washington has quietly reinstated a 100-day naval blockade of Iranian ports and is thinning its Middle East military footprint even as it withdraws non-emergency staff from Saudi Arabia. The gap between Trump’s public rhetoric and the administration’s actual de-escalation moves is the real signal here — executives exposed to the Gulf should price in restraint through November 3, then a sharp reassessment after.

05
OPENAI

OpenAI Nears $30 Billion Raise at $1.25 Trillion Valuation, With UAE Capital in the Mix

TechCrunch · techcrunch.com ↗

OpenAI is finalizing a funding round of up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation led by Coatue alongside UAE investors, who may pair their capital with a separate deal for OpenAI to draw on UAE computing capacity — terms that could still shift before signing. The round would make OpenAI the most valuable private company ever if it closes above $1 trillion, and comes as the company reportedly leans toward more private capital over rushing its confidentially-filed IPO. Anyone benchmarking AI-sector valuations, including Anthropic’s own IPO planning, should treat this number as the new reference point, not the $1.4 trillion figure floated a month ago.

06
ANTHROPIC

Anthropic in Talks for $45 Billion More AI Compute From Nvidia-Backed Nscale

Reuters · investing.com ↗

Anthropic is negotiating a second $45 billion deal with Nvidia-backed Nscale for a new building at its West Virginia Monarch campus running 460 megawatts of Nvidia’s next-generation Vera Rubin chips, on top of the $45 billion it already committed to the same site in August. The talks surface just as rival CoreWeave’s roughly $50 billion debt load has unsettled AI-infrastructure investors, and as Nscale’s total contracted value has ballooned from $100 million to more than $103 billion in two and a half years — a growth rate that is itself becoming a credit-risk story. Capital-markets teams financing AI data centers should watch whether Anthropic’s appetite here survives reported investor pressure to lower its own IPO valuation target.

07
META

Meta Beats on Revenue and Announces 2-for-1 Split — Then Shares Reverse and Fall 8%

SiliconANGLE · siliconangle.com ↗

Meta posted Q3 revenue of $64.60 billion, up 26% year over year and above the $63.3 billion Wall Street expected, and said it will split its stock two-for-one through a dividend of new nonvoting Class C shares effective January 2027 — a structure that leaves Mark Zuckerberg’s roughly 60.6% voting control untouched. Diluted EPS came in at $3.83, weighed down by a $9.89 billion legal charge from Meta’s August settlement with 52 state attorneys general; excluding that charge, EPS would have been $7.40. Shares initially rose about 2% after hours on the results, then reversed and fell nearly 8% the next session, with investors reportedly worried the new share class will fund even heavier AI-infrastructure spending rather than return capital — the overnight reversal, not the split itself, is the signal boards should be watching.

08
TESLA

Tesla Beats on Adjusted EPS but Net Income Falls 43% as Capex Doubles

Electrek · electrek.co ↗

Tesla’s Q3 revenue held flat at $28.05 billion and adjusted EPS of $0.48 beat the $0.45 estimate, but GAAP net income fell 43% to $787 million as quarterly capital expenditure more than doubled year over year to $4.73 billion. Nine-month capex of $13.01 billion is only about half of management’s “more than $25 billion” full-year guidance, meaning Tesla would need to roughly double its quarterly capex pace in Q4 just to hit its own target. Auto-sector analysts modeling Tesla’s free cash flow should treat that Q4 capex catch-up, not this quarter’s narrow beat, as the number that will actually move the stock next.

09
MODERNA

Moderna Shares Plunge 25% After Cancer-Vaccine Trial Data Leaks — Despite Positive Results

Forbes · forbes.com ↗

Moderna shares fell about 26% to $181.14, erasing nearly $20 billion in market value, after Phase 3 results for its personalized melanoma vaccine — given with Merck’s Keytruda — leaked early on a medical news site ahead of Saturday’s scheduled presentation in Madrid; Merck fell about 2%. The data itself was reportedly positive, but Moderna had already run up roughly 290% since mid-August on anticipation, and Citigroup had flagged in September that the stock would need about $13 billion in annual oncology revenue to justify that run — a bar its own model says the drug won’t clear. The lesson for biotech investors is that a leak removes the catalyst even when the underlying news is good; positioning ahead of binary data events now carries event risk on both sides.

SALESFORCE

Salesforce to Buy Braze for $5.66 Billion, Betting on AI Agents in Marketing

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Salesforce agreed to pay $47.50 a share in cash for Braze — a 58% premium to its pre-announcement close — in an all-cash deal expected to close in fiscal 2028, folding the customer-engagement platform into Data 360, Agentforce and Marketing Cloud. Salesforce says the deal is growth-accretive but dilutive to near-term profitability, and it is explicitly betting that owning the layer where brands reach customers across apps, SMS and AI agents matters more now than margin protection. Marketing-tech competitors should expect Salesforce to move aggressively on cross-sell once antitrust clearance comes through, and should reassess their own AI-agent roadmaps now.

BUMBLE

Bumble Goes Private in $1.3 Billion Deal as Dating Apps Face a Reckoning

TechCrunch · techcrunch.com ↗

Bumble agreed to be acquired by Ithaca Holdings for $1.3 billion in cash, a roughly 138% premium to its depressed share price, as the dating app grapples with a steep decline in paying users and intensifying competition. The size of the premium relative to Bumble’s beaten-down valuation says more about how far dating-app stocks have fallen than about the price being paid; Ithaca is reportedly betting on an AI-driven relaunch of the platform. Consumer-tech investors should treat this as a signal that the entire dating-app category is now a turnaround trade, not a growth trade.

TAIWAN

Taiwan Drives Off 16 Chinese Vessels in What Coastguard Calls ‘Hybrid Warfare’

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Taiwan’s coastguard confronted and drove away 16 Chinese coastguard, research and fishing vessels operating simultaneously around the main island and the disputed Pratas Islands, calling it an “unusually large” coordinated operation and accusing Beijing of trying to unilaterally alter the status quo in the Strait. The incident fits a documented escalation pattern — Beijing deployed a record 244 vessels around Taiwan’s main island in July, more than four times July 2025’s count — shifting from occasional pressure to a near-daily operating tempo. Firms with Taiwan-dependent supply chains, especially in semiconductors, should treat the frequency trend, not any single incident, as the metric to track.

US MIDTERMS

Democrats Favored to Retake the House as Midterms Near, Senate Races Tighten

Forbes · forbes.com ↗

With the midterms less than two weeks out, prediction markets give Democrats a 90% chance of retaking the House, and pollsters show Democratic Senate candidates leading in at least four of seven toss-up races, including Georgia, Texas and Ohio, against a Republican majority currently at 53-47. Trump’s approval average sits at 38%, and the tariff dispute with Canada is reportedly resonating with voters beyond border states — a dynamic Canadian officials are explicitly counting on. Anyone planning around US fiscal, trade or regulatory policy into 2027 should start modeling a divided Congress as the base case, not the tail case.

BANK OF CANADA

Canada’s Inflation Climbs to 3.1% as Jobs Market Sheds 68,000 Positions Ahead of BoC Call

Capital.com · capital.com ↗

Statistics Canada reported annual inflation accelerated to 3.1% in September from 3.0% in August — driven by a 19% jump in energy prices and 27.8% higher gasoline — even as the economy shed a net 68,300 jobs the same month and the unemployment rate rose to 6.5%, the worst in years outside the pandemic. Money markets are pricing a hold at 2.25% on October 28 with a 25-basis-point hike priced for December, meaning the Bank of Canada is now navigating a stagflation-adjacent mix — weak growth and rising inflation — rather than the simple slowdown it faced a quarter ago. Canadian treasurers and mortgage-sensitive sectors should stop assuming the next BoC move is a cut; the data argues for a longer hold than markets currently expect.

CANADA-US TRADE

No Quick Canada-US Trade Deal Expected Before Midterms, Despite Faint Hopes

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

Canadian officials have not formally closed the door on a trade deal before the US midterms, and Carney spoke with Trump this week, but sources describe the hope as faint after talks collapsed in August and triggered fresh US tariffs. Chief trade negotiator Janice Charette says Canada’s “pens are down and the doors are open,” while Canada’s ambassador to Washington reportedly told officials the tariff fight is now hurting Republican prospects in races outside the border states — a point Ottawa is using as leverage rather than a reason to concede. Canadian exporters should plan for current tariff levels to persist at least through November 3, with any deal more likely as a post-election move than a pre-election one.

CANADA-TURKEY

Carney in Ankara to Deepen Defence and Energy Ties as Canada Diversifies From the US

Cyprus Mail · cyprus-mail.com ↗

Mark Carney is meeting Turkish President Erdogan in Ankara Thursday — the first dedicated bilateral visit by a Canadian prime minister — with Natural Resources Minister Tim Hodgson and Foreign Minister Anita Anand in tow, ahead of meetings with Istanbul business leaders Friday. The agenda centers on a possible CANDU nuclear bid by Canadian firm AtkinsRéalis for one of Turkey’s planned reactors, LNG and critical-minerals cooperation, and defence ties following Canada’s 2024 removal of arms-export controls on Turkey — though officials say no binding deals are expected to be signed this trip. This is a diversification signal, not a transaction: Turkey represents under 0.3% of Canada’s merchandise trade, so the real audience for this visit is Washington, not Ankara.

17
CANADA ENERGY

Canada Fast-Tracks Pacific Link Pipeline to Cut Dependence on US Oil Markets

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Ottawa designated the proposed 1-million-barrel-per-day Pacific Link pipeline a project of national interest, fast-tracking approval for a route meant to carry Alberta crude to Asian markets and reduce reliance on the US, which still buys the vast majority of Canadian oil exports. Analysts caution the project won’t revive Canada’s oil-jobs boom and faces the same financing, Indigenous-consultation and construction-timeline risks that have killed or delayed prior west-coast pipeline proposals. Energy-sector investors should treat the “national interest” designation as a signal of political will, not a signal that the pipeline is now likely to be built on the original timeline.

CRYPTO

Bitcoin Slides Below $82,000 as Risk Appetite Fades Across Markets

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Bitcoin fell below $82,000 Thursday, moving in the same direction as equities rather than acting as a hedge, as the broader selloff in risk assets swept up crypto alongside AI-exposed tech stocks. Treasury and portfolio-risk teams holding bitcoin as a diversifier should re-test that assumption against Thursday’s trading pattern before year-end rebalancing.

CURRENCIES

Yen Jumps to One-Month High as Wall Street’s Tech Rout Revives Haven Demand

Bloomberg · bloomberg.com ↗

The yen strengthened to its highest level in a month as Thursday’s tech-driven selloff sent investors back into traditional havens, reversing weeks of yen softness tied to expectations the Bank of Japan would skip an October hike. The move shows how quickly safe-haven flows can overwhelm a currency’s own domestic policy narrative when risk sentiment turns fast enough. FX desks should treat this as a reminder that yen weakness driven by BoJ dovishness is a slow-moving trade that can be interrupted abruptly by unrelated risk-off events in US equities.

BANK OF JAPAN

Bank of Japan Heads Into October 29-30 Meeting With Hike Debate Still Unresolved

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Governor Kazuo Ueda says the BOJ will weigh fresh data, including conversations in Washington, before its October 29-30 meeting, with inflation having run above the 2% target for three straight years and the policy rate still at just 0.5%. Market odds for an October move faded after two board members’ September hike proposal failed and after Sanae Takaichi’s win in the ruling party leadership race, since she favors looser policy; most analysts now expect 0.75% only by January. Anyone hedging yen exposure or Japanese rate-sensitive positions should treat the October meeting as a coin-flip rather than a formality, with the real decision point pushed to December.
