01
APEC TRILATERAL

Trump, Xi and Putin Set Thursday Trilateral at APEC as White House Frames It Around Nuclear Arms

CTV News / CP24 (AP) · cp24.com ↗

The White House confirmed Friday that Trump, Xi and Putin plan to meet Thursday on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, the first-ever three-way sit-down of its kind, with a senior US official calling it the “beginning of hopefully a conversation” toward a new trilateral deal on nuclear arsenals after New START expired in February. Trump is expected to also raise the US war with Iran, but the same official said he will steer clear of Ukraine. For anyone pricing nuclear-arms-control risk or Iran-war escalation, the first substantive three-way forum among these leaders arrives this week, not as an abstract possibility.

02
IRAN-US CONFLICT

Third US Carrier Joins Iran Buildup as Tehran Drone-Strikes Bahrain Radar Site, Sets Hormuz Conditions

Malay Mail (AFP) · malaymail.com ↗

The USS Theodore Roosevelt has joined the George H.W. Bush and George Washington carrier strike groups near Iran, CENTCOM confirming it as a “scheduled deployment,” after Iran fired drones at a Bahraini radar and helicopter base that Manama said it intercepted; Iran has blockaded the Strait of Hormuz since the war began and says it will reopen shipping within two days if Washington lifts its naval blockade, releases frozen assets and eases sanctions. Trump is expected to raise the war directly with Xi and Putin this week, after reportedly telling aides he expects to resume strikes by month’s end. For shipping, insurance and energy desks, three US carriers now sit in theatre against an unresolved blockade whose reopening terms Washington has not accepted.

03
UKRAINE WAR

Zelensky Calls US Donbas-for-NATO Pitch “Dangerous,” Says Any Referendum Needs Security Guarantees First

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

President Zelensky said US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner raised, in Abu Dhabi talks, whether Ukraine could join NATO if it withdrew from Donbas — an idea he called “dangerous” and said he would only put to a referendum alongside security guarantees, a capable army and reconstruction funding; Kushner called some proposals “maximalist” but said “several new ideas” are under private discussion. The Kremlin has not responded, and has long treated Ukrainian NATO membership as a red line while demanding Donbas, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson outright; analysts quoted by Al Jazeera call the NATO half of the trade “very doubtful” to survive Moscow’s veto. For anyone pricing a Ukraine settlement, the live question is no longer whether territory changes hands, but what security guarantee — if any — Kyiv’s public would accept in exchange.

04
RUSSIA-US DIPLOMACY

Witkoff and Kushner Leave Moscow for Dublin After Putin Rejects European Troops, Says NATO Expansion Is Over

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Putin in Moscow to restart direct US-Russia talks after a nine-month pause and an inconclusive Abu Dhabi round; the Kremlin called the talks “useful,” said the US side backed a joint humanitarian mission to eastern Ukraine, and relayed that Putin rejects any ongoing European troop presence in Ukraine and insists “NATO cannot expand further.” The envoys then flew to Dublin to brief Zelensky on the sidelines of a European Political Community summit, before Putin resumes the conversation with Trump at APEC. For European capitals weighing any security-guarantee architecture, Moscow is negotiating NATO’s own limits as a precondition, not a side issue, to any Ukraine deal.

05
ISRAEL POLITICS

Eisenkot’s Mandate to Form Israel’s Next Government Enters Its Second Week With Neither Bloc Near 61 Seats

Swarajya · swarajyamag.com ↗

President Isaac Herzog tasked former military chief Gadi Eisenkot with forming Israel’s next government after 67 lawmakers recommended him against 46 for Netanyahu, giving Eisenkot 28 days, extendable by 14, to assemble a coalition; neither Likud’s 27 seats nor Eisenkot’s own bloc reach the 61-seat majority, and Eisenkot has ruled out governing with the Arab-led parties that backed him. If his mandate lapses, Netanyahu is likely to get a turn next, and a fresh election follows if that also fails. For anyone pricing Israeli political risk, the clock on a durable government is now running past its first week with no majority path yet visible on either side.

06
SOUTH CHINA SEA

ASEAN and China Fail to Conclude South China Sea Code at Manila Summit Despite 80% Draft Agreement

Reuters (via Internazionale) · internazionale.it ↗

Philippine Foreign Minister Theresa Lazaro said Sunday “there is no conclusion yet” on the long-sought South China Sea code of conduct, even after the two sides provisionally agreed on roughly 80% of the draft text, with whether the code is legally binding still the core sticking point; analyst Collin Koh cited intra-ASEAN divisions over the code’s geographic scope as needing “major concessions” to close. Manila, which had pledged to finish the deal this year, now hands the unresolved 20% to Singapore’s 2027 chairmanship. For companies routing trade or energy through contested waters, the unresolved fifth is precisely the part — binding force — that decides whether the code changes anyone’s behaviour.

07
ENERGY & OIL

Gold Sinks 3% to a 13-Month Low as Trump Shuts the Door on an Iran Deal, Oil Gains on the Same News

Khaleej Times (Reuters) · khaleejtimes.com ↗

Spot gold dropped 3% to $3,867.96 an ounce — its lowest since October 2025 and a third straight losing session — after Trump said the US did not need a deal with Iran, quashing hopes of an end to the war that began in February; oil futures rose more than 1% on the same signal. The drop compounded hawkish Fed commentary and a hot US CPI print that have pushed the dollar index near a 19-month high, with markets now pricing a 91% chance of a December rate hike and rising odds it is 50 basis points rather than 25. For portfolio managers who treated gold as a one-way hedge against both inflation and Middle East risk, both legs of that trade just moved against them at once.

08
MARKETS & MACRO

Anthropic Targets November 19 Nasdaq Debut at Up to $1.4 Trillion, With a $26 Billion Private Placement Alongside

The Wealth Advisor · thewealthadvisor.com ↗

Anthropic is targeting a Thursday, November 19 Nasdaq debut under ticker ANT, pricing 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each in an offering that could raise more than $64 billion, alongside a separate $26 billion private placement of nonvoting shares — implying a valuation of roughly $1.4 trillion, with final pricing and regulatory approvals still outstanding. For allocators positioning ahead of the listing, the gap between the IPO share price and the private-placement terms is the clearest read yet on how skeptical late-stage investors are that the headline valuation holds on its first trading day.

09
CANADA ECONOMY

Carney’s “Masters of Our Destiny” Budget Commits C$142 Billion to Cut Canada’s Reliance on the US

AP (via AJC) · ajc.com ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney’s budget, titled “Masters of Our Destiny,” commits C$142.1 billion in multiyear spending and tax breaks — including C$36 billion in immediate business-investment expensing, a new C$25 billion sovereign wealth fund, and C$7.7 billion for defence with a goal that 70% of military contracts go to Canadian firms — against total 2026-27 spending of C$603 billion and a C$62.3 billion deficit, C$3 billion better than April’s forecast. Carney is targeting a doubling of exports outside the US by 2035 as Canada seeks closer ties with Europe and Asia, after walking away from US trade talks in August over demands he said threatened sovereignty. For anyone modelling Canada’s trade exposure, the budget treats reduced US dependence as an explicit multi-year target, not a hedge.

10
CANADA-AUTO

Ottawa Declares Stellantis in Default on Up to C$529 Million in Aid Over Brampton-to-Roshel Sale

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

The federal government has served Stellantis formal notice of default on up to C$529 million in funding tied to its Brampton assembly plant, after the automaker moved to sell the facility to armoured-vehicle maker Roshel and shifted the production it had promised to keep in Canada south to the US; Prime Minister Carney called the sale “unacceptable.” The notice opens the door to Ottawa clawing back the aid or suing to enforce the original agreement. For any company holding a Canadian government funding deal tied to production commitments, Ottawa has just shown it will enforce the fine print rather than simply protest a reroute.

CHINA TRADE

China Extends Rare-Earth and Battery Export-Control Suspension to January 10, 2027, Hours Before It Was Set to Lapse

S&P Global · spglobal.com ↗

China’s Ministry of Commerce extended its suspension of rare-earth and battery-material export controls to January 10, 2027, acting hours before the prior pause was due to expire, as talks with Washington continue; the extension keeps six separate export-control actions on hold rather than lifting them permanently. For any manufacturer dependent on Chinese-processed rare earths, the supply reprieve remains a renewable truce tied to the broader US-China trade relationship, not a settled policy.
