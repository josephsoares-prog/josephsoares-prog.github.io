What mattered on Tuesday, 18 August 2026

01
CANADA

Washington's 50% tariff on Canadian goods lands tomorrow and small exporters say it could halve their sales

CBC · cbc.ca ↗

Owners of small and mid-sized Canadian exporters told CBC they expect sales to fall by as much as half when the Section 338 duty takes effect on 19 August. The measure covers roughly C$28 billion in annual exports — about 5% of shipments to the United States — with Ontario, Quebec and British Columbia most exposed. This is the last business day to act: fix cross-border pricing, confirm which goods still qualify under CUSMA, and secure inventory positions before the duty applies.

02
HORMUZ

Brent tops $91 after an unidentified projectile strikes a vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz

OilPrice · oilprice.com ↗

A vessel in transit was hit by an unidentified projectile, pushing the international benchmark above $91 a barrel as hopes of a US-Iran settlement faded. The strike removes the argument that recent attacks were confined to state-carrier tankers — any hull in the strait is now a target. Price your fuel, freight and war-risk insurance off a sustained $90-plus band rather than waiting for a reopening announcement.

03
MACRO

The 30-year Treasury yield hits 5.31% — a 19-year high — as war, deficits and foreign selling compound

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

The long bond advanced past 5.31%, its highest since June 2007, with the Fed holding at 3.50-3.75%. Rising deficits, heavy issuance and reduced holdings by the UK, China and Japan are pushing the long end up independently of policy rates. Any decision priced off long-term borrowing costs — capital projects, refinancing, real-estate underwriting — should be re-run at these levels, not at the rates assumed earlier this year.

04
HORMUZ

Trump floats strikes on Oman if it interferes in the Iran peace effort, putting the mediator in the crosshairs

WSJ · wsj.com ↗

The President raised the possibility of striking Oman, the state that has hosted and brokered the Hormuz talks. A former ambassador characterised the remark as an attempt to get Muscat's attention rather than a stated intent. Either way the mediating channel is now itself contested, which removes the most likely route to a negotiated reopening — plan operations on the assumption that the strait stays constrained.

05
DEFENCE

Trump orders the Pentagon to cut joint drills with Seoul as the Iran war stretches US carrier availability

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

The President instructed the Pentagon to substantially reduce exercises involving 18,000 South Korean troops, citing cost, signalling toward Pyongyang, and Seoul's refusal to help on Iran. President Lee told South Koreans to prepare for a worst-case scenario. This is the clearest evidence yet that one theatre is now consuming the force structure assumed available in another — treat allied security guarantees in Asia as a variable, not a constant.

06
RUSSIA

More than 600 Ukrainian drones strike the Moscow region in the largest assault on the capital in two years

The Moscow Times · themoscowtimes.com ↗

Ukraine launched 637 drones at Moscow and its surrounding region overnight; 180 were downed over the Moscow region alone. A Wildberries e-commerce warehouse was hit, more than 5,000 customers lost power, and three people were injured. Deep-strike capability against logistics and energy infrastructure inside Russia is now routine rather than exceptional — a material input to any Russian supply, insurance or counterparty exposure.

07
RUSSIA

Moscow says Britain 'will pay' for supplying the drones used against Russian targets

BBC · bbc.co.uk ↗

Russia issued an explicit warning of consequences for the United Kingdom following reports that British-made drones struck Russian territory. The statement moves the argument from weapons supply to direct attribution — Moscow is naming a NATO state as a party to the strikes. Watch for retaliation in the domains Russia actually uses against European states: energy, subsea and air infrastructure, and cyber.

08
SHIPPING

Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz slows further as owners withdraw tonnage after the tanker attacks

CGTN · cgtn.com ↗

Shipping volumes through the strait declined again as owners pulled vessels rather than accept the risk premium. Reduced transits compound the price effect of the attacks themselves: the constraint is now commercial withdrawal as much as physical interdiction. Any supply plan that assumes carriers will return once a ceasefire is announced is likely to under-estimate the lag.

09
EU

ECB's Lane sets out how the rise in defence spending reshapes the euro area economy

ECB · ecb.europa.eu ↗

The ECB's chief economist delivered a formal assessment of defence expenditure as a macroeconomic force in the euro area, not merely a budget line. Read it as the central bank signalling that rearmament is now a structural input to growth, inflation and fiscal paths. European industrial and procurement opportunities follow this money — track where the spending is actually contracted rather than where it is announced.

10
DEFENCE

Raytheon receives a $23 billion contract to expand Tomahawk production

Breaking Defense · breakingdefense.com ↗

The award is one of the largest single munitions contracts on record and is directed at production capacity rather than new capability. It confirms that Washington is buying magazine depth for a long conflict, having drawn down stocks in the Middle East campaign. Munitions supply chains, castings, propellant and guidance subcontractors are where this money reaches the wider industrial base.

11
ENERGY

Libya seeks up to $40 billion in investment to lift oil output toward 2 million barrels a day

OilPrice · oilprice.com ↗

Tripoli is courting foreign capital to roughly double production at exactly the moment Hormuz supply is constrained and prices sit above $90. The barrels are real but the political risk is unchanged, and the timeline runs to years rather than months. Treat this as a signal about where non-Gulf supply capital will be directed, not as near-term relief on price.

12
CANADA

Canadian home prices post their first month-over-month increase since 2024

Financial Post · financialpost.com ↗

July brought the first month-over-month gain in national home prices in roughly two years. The turn arrives as long-term borrowing costs push higher and a major tariff shock lands on export-exposed regions. Read the two together before drawing conclusions: a price floor forming under falling rates is a different market from one forming under a trade shock.

13
INTELLIGENCE

Investigators report an 'unprecedented' number of Apple users received mercenary-spyware alerts

TechCrunch · techcrunch.com ↗

Researchers say the latest round of Apple threat notifications reached an unusually large group of targets. These alerts historically cluster around journalists, executives, lawyers and officials — precisely the population that carries deal and government information on personal devices. If your organisation has people in those categories, device hygiene and communications discipline are an operating control this week, not an IT preference.

14
NATO

An explosive drone at a German airport raises the question of a widening Russian threat to NATO infrastructure

CBC · cbc.ca ↗

An explosive-carrying drone at Leipzig has prompted analysts to ask whether Russia is extending pressure to civil infrastructure inside NATO territory. Attribution is not established, and the reporting is careful on that point. The planning question is unaffected: cheap aerial systems can now impose costs on airports, ports and energy sites that were secured against a different threat model.

15
EU

A Greek shipowner's fleet carried €2.35 billion of Russian Arctic gas to Europe this year

EUobserver · euobserver.com ↗

An investigation traced €2.35 billion in Russian Arctic gas cargoes delivered into Europe by ships tied to a single Greek billionaire. The finding illustrates how sanctions architecture is routed around through ownership and flag rather than defeated head-on. Any sanctions-compliance posture built on counterparty names rather than vessel and beneficial-ownership tracing is weaker than it appears.

16
ENERGY

China lifts refined-fuel exports as domestic stockpiles swell

OilPrice · oilprice.com ↗

Chinese refiners are pushing more product into export markets as domestic inventories build. That flow partially offsets the Hormuz constraint for Asian buyers and gives Beijing pricing influence in markets that normally source from the Gulf. For anyone modelling refined-product costs, the marginal barrel increasingly carries a Chinese export decision inside it.

17
INTL LAW

Can the United States actually dismantle the International Criminal Court?

Radio-Canada · ici.radio-canada.ca ↗

Radio-Canada examines whether sustained US pressure — sanctions on officials, and recent state withdrawals from the Rome Statute — can degrade the Court as an institution. The analysis matters beyond The Hague: it is a live case study in how a rules-based body behaves when its most powerful non-member sets out to weaken it. Enforcement risk for cross-border businesses is set by which institutions survive this pressure.

18
HUMANITARIAN

The Ebola outbreak in the DR Congo becomes the deadliest in the country's history

UN News · news.un.org ↗

UN reporting confirms the current outbreak has surpassed every previous Congolese epidemic in deaths. The escalation comes as the World Health Organization manages the announced US withdrawal from the organisation, reducing the funding and coordination capacity available for exactly this kind of response. Organisations with people, suppliers or operations in central Africa should re-check contingency and evacuation assumptions.

19
CHINA

Beijing closes 670 rural banks as the financial-sector overhaul accelerates

Caixin Global · caixinglobal.com ↗

Chinese regulators removed 670 rural lenders in a consolidation aimed at concentrating weak balance sheets into stronger institutions. The scale indicates authorities are choosing to absorb losses now rather than let small-bank stress accumulate. For anyone assessing Chinese credit exposure, the relevant signal is the pace of forced consolidation, not the headline health of the largest banks.

20
RUSSIA

'Voluntary donations' by Russian businesses to the state budget hit a record for 2026

Meduza · meduza.io ↗

Meduza reports that business payments recorded as voluntary contributions to the Russian budget have reached their highest level since the start of the year. The mechanism functions as informal taxation applied through pressure rather than statute. It is a direct indicator of fiscal strain and of the deteriorating predictability of operating conditions for any firm still exposed to the Russian market.

TECHNOLOGY

Anthropic's annualised revenue reaches $65 billion

TechCrunch · techcrunch.com ↗

The figure marks another step change in enterprise AI spending and in the concentration of that spending among a small number of model providers. Growth of this speed sets the pricing and dependency terms for every organisation building AI into its operations. If AI tooling is becoming core to how your business runs, the negotiating question is vendor concentration, not feature comparison.

GEOPOLITICS

Russia summons Japan's ambassador as the Kuril Islands dispute flares

The Moscow Times · themoscowtimes.com ↗

Moscow escalated the long-running territorial dispute with Tokyo at the same moment Washington is scaling back exercises in Korea. The two moves point the same direction: Russia is testing where Japan sits if American attention in the Pacific thins. Japanese defence procurement and regional supply-chain decisions should be read against that backdrop.

INDIA

The Tata Sons board splits over Chandrasekaran's exit

Economic Times · economictimes.indiatimes.com ↗

Directors are divided on whether to seek reconsideration of the chairman's departure from India's largest conglomerate. Succession contests at this scale set the terms for capital allocation across dozens of operating companies and for India's most visible corporate governance benchmark. Watch whether the board resolves this through process or through personality — the answer is the governance signal.

DEFENCE

German drone maker Helsing enlists Rakuten to broker a Japanese military deal

Defense News · defensenews.com ↗

Helsing is using a Japanese commercial partner to reach a defence customer, rather than the traditional government-to-government route. The structure is the story: European defence firms are entering Asian markets through corporate intermediaries with existing local standing. Expect more defence entry to look like commercial partnership and less like state export promotion.

SHIPPING

Global air freight rates dip as volumes lose altitude

The Loadstar · theloadstar.com ↗

Rates softened as air cargo volumes eased, even with sea freight disrupted through Hormuz. That combination is worth noting: when ocean disruption does not lift air rates, it suggests underlying demand is weakening rather than merely shifting mode. Treat softening air freight as a demand signal, not only a capacity one.

26
TECHNOLOGY

A proposed US policy framework would test, standardise and restrict Chinese AI models

Just Security · justsecurity.org ↗

Just Security sets out a framework for evaluating and limiting Chinese AI models in US systems. Proposals of this kind tend to become procurement rules before they become law, and they travel quickly to allied jurisdictions including Canada. Organisations selecting AI vendors now should assume model provenance will become a compliance question within the planning horizon.

CANADA

Where all the new power behind the Churchill Falls energy build actually comes from

La Presse · lapresse.ca ↗

La Presse examines the generation sources behind Quebec's expanded Churchill Falls programme, and Hydro-Québec's role as the negotiating party. Electricity supply is now a binding constraint on industrial siting, data centres and electrification commitments across the province. POLITICS

Pakistan's Supreme Court orders Imran Khan moved from prison to hospital

The Hindu · thehindu.com ↗

The court directed the government to transfer the former prime minister to hospital over health concerns. Judicial intervention on behalf of a jailed opposition leader is a measurable test of whether Pakistan's courts still constrain the executive. For a nuclear-armed state facing recurring tension with India, the durability of that constraint is a stability indicator worth tracking.

AFRICA

Zambia's Hichilema wins re-election with 60% despite unprecedented tensions

Jeune Afrique · jeuneafrique.com ↗

President Hichilema secured a decisive second-term margin in a contest marked by tensions without precedent in recent Zambian politics. Zambia is a significant copper producer, and copper sits directly in the critical-minerals competition between Western and Chinese buyers. Continuity in Lusaka matters more to mineral supply planning than the margin itself suggests.

Japan launches a second US military payload to monitor space above the Indo-Pacific

Breaking Defense · breakingdefense.com ↗

Tokyo placed another American surveillance payload in orbit as part of deepening space-domain cooperation. The launch shows allied capability integration continuing at the technical level even while Washington trims exercises with Seoul. Commentary Hooks

The Last Business Day

Canada has one working day before a 50% tariff it cannot prevent, after months in which the deadline was treated as a negotiating position rather than a date. A piece on the discipline of separating what you can still decide from what you can now only absorb — and why leaders lose the former by spending it on the latter — would land immediately with Canadian and US operators.

When One Theatre Pays for Another

The order to cut drills with Seoul was justified on cost and signalling, but the operative constraint is carrier availability consumed by the Iran campaign. A piece on how commitments get quietly repriced when a leader's resources are committed elsewhere — and how to read the difference between a stated reason and a binding one — is a governance lesson well beyond defence.

The Sanction That Sails Under Another Flag

€2.35 billion of Russian Arctic gas reached Europe this year aboard ships tied to one owner, without the sanctions regime being formally defeated. A piece on why control frameworks fail at the ownership layer rather than the rule layer — and what that means for anyone who believes their compliance list is their compliance system — would be useful to boards and executives alike.

  • The daily Hormuz transit count and whether Brent holds above $91 or retraces as Turkey and Iran discuss reopening the strait.
  • Whether the 30-year Treasury yield pushes toward the 5.60-5.70% range strategists have flagged, and whether foreign holdings fall again in the next Treasury data.
  • How far the US-South Korea exercises are actually cut once the Pentagon issues orders, and how Pyongyang responds in the following week.
  • Whether Russia acts on its warning to Britain, and in which domain — energy, subsea and air infrastructure, or cyber.
