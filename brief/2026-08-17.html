01
HORMUZ

Strait of Hormuz shipping grinds to a halt as the US-Iran ceasefire expires with no deal in sight

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Traffic through the strait sank to a new low ahead of today's expiry: five cargo ships crossed on Saturday and none were registered on Sunday. The 60-day negotiating window closes with the waterway effectively shut and no framework agreed. Treat the closure as the operating condition rather than a temporary disruption — reroute, re-insure and re-price on that basis.

02
DIPLOMACY

Trump threatens to bomb Oman if it 'gets in the way' of an Iran deal

Livemint · livemint.com ↗

With the 60-day negotiating period ending in stalemate, the President publicly threatened military action against Oman — the state hosting the Hormuz talks. Threatening the mediator removes the only functioning channel between Washington and Tehran. Assume the diplomatic architecture is broken, not stalled, and that the next moves are military and economic rather than negotiated.

03
CANADA

'All strategic sectors' on the table as the 19 August US tariff deadline closes to two days

CBC News · cbc.ca ↗

Prime Minister Carney says every strategic sector is in the negotiation and that Canada will not accept a bad deal, with the threatened 50% duty covering roughly $20 billion in annual exports spanning autos, alcohol and dairy. He has called immediate retaliation counterproductive while talks continue. For cross-border operators the planning assumption should be that the duty applies Wednesday and any relief arrives afterwards, not before.

04
IRAN

Behind the peace talks, Iranian hard-liners are preparing for a longer and costlier war with the US

The Wall Street Journal · wsj.com ↗

Reporting on Tehran's internal debate indicates the faction planning for a protracted conflict is ascendant even as negotiations continue. That matters more than any single round of talks: it sets the ceiling on what Iranian negotiators can concede. Assume Tehran's reservation price is rising, not falling, and that time favours neither side's stated position.

05
MARKETS

Wall Street's 'fear gauge' hits a 2026 low — and strategists warn it will not hold

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

The VIX has fallen to its lowest level of the year even as a war deadline expires and a major tariff cliff approaches. Calm of this kind usually reflects positioning and hedging cost, not an absence of risk. The asymmetry favours buying protection now rather than after the first headline moves the tape.

06
ENERGY

China's crude imports jump 22% even as its economic data disappoints

OilPrice · oilprice.com ↗

Beijing is buying barrels well ahead of what its own demand indicators justify, which points to stockpiling rather than consumption. Strategic accumulation during a Hormuz closure changes the shape of the market: it absorbs supply now and creates a buyer that can step away later. Read Chinese import strength as a policy signal about war duration, not as a demand recovery.

07
DIPLOMACY

Trump envoy Kushner meets Netanyahu after a rare direct meeting with Hamas on the peace plan

BBC News · bbc.co.uk ↗

Washington's envoy has moved from talking to Hamas directly to briefing the Israeli prime minister, an unusual sequencing that signals the US is brokering rather than backstopping. The Gaza track is now running on personal envoy diplomacy rather than institutional channels. That makes progress faster but far more fragile — outcomes will turn on a handful of individuals.

DEFENCE

Trump says the US will reduce military drills with South Korea after Seoul stayed out of the Iran war

BBC News · bbc.co.uk ↗

Alliance commitments in Asia are now being adjusted explicitly as payment for participation in a Middle East campaign. That converts a security guarantee into a transactional instrument and every treaty ally will read it that way. Expect allied capitals to hedge — through indigenous capability, diversified suppliers, and quieter alignment.

09
RUSSIA

BBC News · bbc.co.uk ↗

Kyiv continues to impose costs inside Russian territory rather than confining the fight to the front line. Deep strikes keep Russian air defence and political attention divided, which is their strategic purpose. For risk managers the relevant variable is not the casualty count but the widening geography of insurable loss inside Russia.

10
RUSSIA

VEB's chief economist fired after warning Russia cannot win a 'war of attrition'

The Moscow Times · themoscowtimes.com ↗

The state development bank has removed its senior economist over an internal assessment that Russia's economy cannot sustain a long war. Dismissing the analyst does not change the arithmetic, but it does degrade the quality of information reaching Russian decision-makers. Treat official Russian macro guidance as increasingly filtered, and weight independent data accordingly.

11
EU

South China Morning Post · scmp.com ↗

The EU is preparing a package described as its widest yet, extending well beyond the incremental listings of previous rounds. Firms with residual Russian exposure — including through third-country intermediaries — should assume the compliance perimeter widens again. Map counterparty chains now; retrofitting compliance after a package lands is the expensive path.

12
INDIA

South China Morning Post · scmp.com ↗

With Hormuz closed and Red Sea transit degraded, Moscow is pitching a land corridor that would move crude to India outside contested waters. Watch this as the leading indicator of whether the Eurasian energy map is being redrawn around chokepoint avoidance.

Defense News · defensenews.com ↗

Component contracts are the earliest reliable indicator of where the US expects to expend magazines. For suppliers and investors, the interceptor supply chain is where defence spending converts into revenue first.

DEFENCE

Defense News · defensenews.com ↗

Industry is declining to commit to a Ukraine Patriot production arrangement while officials publicly dispute where the delay originates. Ukraine's air defence depth depends on decisions being made now, not on stockpiles already fielded. DEFENCE

Defense News · defensenews.com ↗

US Central Command has stood up a coalition formation dedicated to offensive drone operations, institutionalising a capability that was improvised earlier in the campaign. Standing task forces outlast the crises that create them and set procurement patterns for allies. Expect regional partners to be asked for contributions in platforms and basing rather than in troops.

Read this as guidance on where European rates and issuance are heading over the medium term.

Commentary Hooks

When the Mediator Becomes the Target

Oman was the channel through which the Hormuz talks ran, and it has now been publicly threatened by one of the parties. A piece on what leaders actually do when the last neutral broker is removed — and why preserving a channel you dislike is a discipline, not a concession — would land with an operator audience well beyond the Gulf.

Governing to a Deadline You Do Not Control

Canada has two days before a 50% tariff it cannot prevent unilaterally, and its own negotiators have said the deadline could end the talks. A practical piece on decision-making when the clock belongs to the counterparty — how to separate what you can decide from what you can only prepare for — would be immediately useful to Canadian and US executives alike.

The Quiet Tape

Wall Street's fear gauge sits at a 2026 low in the same week a war deadline expires and a tariff cliff arrives. A short piece arguing that market calm measures positioning rather than risk — and offering a simple test for telling the two apart — would give a finance audience a framework they can apply the same day.
