01
HORMUZ

Oil slides as Iran-Oman corridor talks advance and Hormuz risk is repriced

Multiple (Al Jazeera, CNN, FX Leaders, Vantage Markets) · aljazeera.com ↗

Brent fell to roughly $88-89, down over 5% on the week, as traders shift from pricing an imminent supply cutoff to pricing a sanctions standoff. Qatar's prime minister is mediating in Tehran while Iran and Oman advance a temporary shipping-corridor proposal, and Iran still ties any full reopening to ending every Middle East war rather than treating closure as a bargaining chip. For decision-makers, the trade has changed: watch corridor terms and IRGC statements, not headline risk alone.

02
ENERGY

Washington takes majority control of 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan oil reserves

CNBC / Al Jazeera / NBC News · cnbc.com ↗

Trump announced a deal giving a new US-linked private company 55% effective output and an ownership stake in Venezuela's proven reserves, negotiated by Rubio and Hegseth with interim President Delcy Rodriguez at no direct cost to US taxpayers. The structure — equity capture rather than military occupation or a purchase agreement — sets a template for how Washington may approach other resource-rich, politically unstable states. Any operator with Western Hemisphere energy exposure should read the contract structure, not just the headline barrel count.

03
INTELLIGENCE

CIA director's secret Moscow trip warned Russia off NATO while flagging Iran-linked sanctions

CNN / CBS News / Washington Post (via Meduza) · cnn.com ↗

John Ratcliffe made the first CIA director visit to Moscow since 2021 after intelligence assessed that Putin sees Washington as weakened by the Iran war's toll on readiness and ammunition stocks. He warned Russia against testing NATO and raised further sanctions if the Strait of Hormuz stays closed, but the Kremlin says Putin was not present. The trip itself is the signal: Washington believes its own overstretch is now visible to adversaries and is managing perception directly rather than through public statements.

04
AFRICA

Coordinated attack hits Niamey airport, presidency perimeter and a military base

Reuters (via Al Jazeera, Arab News) · aljazeera.com ↗

Attackers struck Diori Hamani International Airport, attempted to breach the perimeter around Niger's presidency, and targeted Base 101, a major military installation, in the third such incident in the capital this year. Security forces say they regained control and neutralised some attackers while others fled. The recurrence — not the single incident — is the signal: Niamey's core state infrastructure remains a repeatable target.

05
HUMANITARIAN

Nepal-China flood death toll passes 620 with roughly 2,500 still missing

CNN / Washington Times / Detroit News · cnn.com ↗

Flash floods and mudslides along a 72km stretch of the Trishuli River killed at least 616 in Nepal and 7 in Tibet, with two glacial lakes posing an ongoing burst risk that has repeatedly paused and resumed rescue operations. The scale of the missing-persons count relative to confirmed deaths suggests the toll will keep rising through the weekend. Watch the second lake's water level before assuming the crisis has stabilised.

06
MACRO

Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as Fed Chair pulls forward bank rate-hike bets

Bloomberg · bloomberg.com ↗

Kevin Warsh's inaugural policy address at the Kansas City Fed's Jackson Hole symposium prompted several major banks to move up the timing of expected rate hikes, leaving markets split on the September decision. The reaction matters more than the speech content: a new Fed Chair's first major address is being read as a signal of reaction function, not just current conditions. Position sizing into September should account for this repricing, not last month's consensus.

07
TECHNOLOGY

Meta's $18B teen-safety settlement is a contingency bet designed to force TikTok and YouTube to match it

CNBC / Washington Post / Fortune · cnbc.com ↗

Meta will pay $12.7 billion of its $18 billion multistate settlement regardless, but the remaining $5.3 billion is contingent on TikTok, YouTube and Snapchat adopting comparable limits — 60-minute daily caps, overnight blocks, and school-hours notification bans for minors. States say they are prepared to sue the holdouts directly. This converts a legal settlement into a private enforcement mechanism that platform-dependent businesses should expect to see replicated in other sectors.

CANADA

Ottawa trims its counter-tariff list as Washington drops cultural-policy demands, and Carney's Quebec support climbs

La Presse · lapresse.ca ↗

Canada pulled seafood from its 8 September counter-tariff list as the US dropped its push on Canadian cultural and French-language policy, narrowing the trade dispute's non-economic dimension. A new Synopsis-La Presse poll shows Carney's support rising in Quebec, tying his handling of the US file directly to provincial political capital. The de-escalation on cultural policy is the more durable signal than any single tariff-list adjustment.

DEFENCE

Pentagon reviews allied burden-sharing and European troop options for 6 November as NSA Bahrain damage draws scrutiny

Defense News · defensenews.com ↗

A Pentagon review is auditing allied contribution levels and preparing European troop deployment options ahead of a 6 November decision point, while Defense News separately flagged the state of NSA Bahrain as a "national security warning" tied to regional force posture. Together they point to a US military reassessing footprint and readiness after six months of the Iran war, not just planning contingencies. Watch for which allies are asked to increase contributions first.

INTL LAW

ICC "strongly rejects" newest round of US sanctions designations

International Criminal Court · icc-cpi.int ↗

The International Criminal Court issued a formal rejection of the latest US sanctions designations against its officials, continuing an escalating institutional confrontation that began with earlier Trump-administration sanctions actions. The court's public, on-record rejection — rather than quiet protest — signals it is treating the sanctions as a direct threat to its functioning rather than a diplomatic irritant. This affects any organisation whose compliance posture depends on ICC-adjacent designations.

Moscow Times · themoscowtimes.com ↗

Read the ICBM test alongside the Ratcliffe Moscow trip: both point to Russia signalling capability while probing Western resolve.
