01
HORMUZ

Trump claims ‘100%’ US control of the Strait of Hormuz and demands Iran pay reparations, opening a new front in the standoff

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

President Trump says the US Navy has swept Hormuz of mines and now holds full control of the waterway, calling the posture a ‘steel wall’ even as Iran sporadically lays mines. Tehran is demanding a lifted blockade, sanctions relief, troop withdrawal and war-damage compensation before it fully restores shipping — Trump countered by suggesting Iran instead owes Washington reparations for decades of attacks. Treat reparations, not the physical waterway, as the actual chokepoint now: a transit fix without a settled damages question will not hold.

INTELLIGENCE

Iranian state media releases images purporting to show damage to multiple US military bases — an unverified claim that could reset the reparations fight

CGTN · cgtn.com ↗

CGTN and Iranian state outlets published imagery Tehran says documents strikes on multiple US bases in the region, days after Washington began demanding its own reparations from Iran. No independent verification exists yet, and the timing — landing squarely inside the reparations dispute — argues for treating this as an information operation until confirmed. If authenticated, it materially strengthens Iran's negotiating position; if not, it still signals Tehran will contest the narrative publicly rather than concede the diplomatic ground.

03
RUSSIA

Zelensky says Putin used North Korean ballistic missiles in a lethal strike on Ukraine, confirming deepening Pyongyang-Moscow military integration

Politico EU · politico.eu ↗

President Zelensky says Russia used North Korean-supplied ballistic missiles in a strike that killed civilians, the clearest confirmation yet that Pyongyang's military support has moved from artillery shells and troops into precision strike systems. That escalation ladder matters beyond Ukraine: it is a live test of DPRK missile technology in combat and deepens a Moscow-Pyongyang axis that Seoul and Washington will now have to price into their own deterrence planning. Expect renewed calls in Washington and Seoul for tighter sanctions enforcement on the supply chain.

TECHNOLOGY

Nvidia lines up $500 billion in financing as Jensen Huang tells CNBC his chips are an ‘investable asset’ — vendor financing at unprecedented scale

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Nvidia is arranging roughly $500 billion in financing with Wall Street asset managers, with CEO Jensen Huang framing the chips themselves as a financeable, investable asset class rather than a depreciating cost line. That reframing is the story: it invites pension funds and private credit into AI infrastructure at a scale that concentrates cycle risk in fewer, larger balance sheets. For allocators, the question is no longer whether AI capex continues but who ultimately holds the residual risk if utilisation disappoints.

TECHNOLOGY

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Watch whether the capital gets deployed into committed foundry customers or remains a balance-sheet cushion — that split will tell you if the turnaround is real or optics-driven.

HUMANITARIAN

Colombia's magnitude-7.4 earthquake kills more than 130, injures 570 and prompts a national emergency — the country's strongest quake in the 21st century

NBC News · nbcnews.com ↗

The UN has offered support and Bogotá has declared a national emergency, positioning this as the dominant humanitarian and reconstruction story in the Americas this week. For insurers and infrastructure investors with Andean exposure, expect a multi-week window of elevated claims activity and reconstruction contracting.

Both Assad brothers remain in Russia, so the sentence is symbolic in the near term but sets a legal precedent Damascus's new government can use to press Moscow on extradition and to anchor broader accountability prosecutions. Track whether this triggers a parallel push at the ICC or a diplomatic rupture with Russia over shelter for the Assads.

Commentary Hooks

Reparations Are the New Hormuz — the War Moved From the Water to the Ledger

Trump's demand that Iran pay reparations, met by Tehran's own compensation demands, reframes the Hormuz standoff from a military and shipping problem into an accounting dispute — and accounting disputes between adversarial states historically take years, not weeks, to close. Anyone pricing a near-term reopening should study how long comparable reparations negotiations have taken historically, then double it.

The AI Trade's Financing Is Becoming More Interesting Than Its Technology

Nvidia arranging $500 billion with asset managers, Intel raising $20 billion on an AI re-rating, and OpenAI completing a $7 billion tender offer in the same week shows the AI story has become primarily a capital-markets story wearing a technology costume. The interesting question for 2026 is no longer which model wins — it is who ultimately holds the paper if the demand assumptions underneath all this financing turn out to be wrong.

Ignored Crises Are the Most Dangerous Ones for Operators

Sudan's displacement crisis and DRC's Ebola toll crossing 2,000 are both accelerating with almost no international attention, while Colombia's earthquake and Hormuz dominate coverage. History shows underfunded, under-watched crises are exactly where operational surprises — sudden instability, logistics disruption, workforce risk — originate, because nobody was pricing them in.

Watchlist

  • Whether Iran's reparations demand and Trump's counter-demand harden into a formal negotiating deadlock or produce a face-saving formula within the week.
  • Whether Iranian state media's claimed images of US base damage are independently verified or debunked, and how Washington responds either way.
  • Whether Libya's National Oil Corporation issues a formal force majeure declaration on Zawiya exports, which would confirm North African supply risk is compounding Gulf risk.
  • Whether Colombia's earthquake death toll and damage assessment continue rising, and how fast reconstruction financing and insurance claims mobilise.
  • Whether more Canadian municipalities disclose Russian-linked critical-infrastructure intrusions, confirming this is a coordinated campaign rather than isolated incidents.
