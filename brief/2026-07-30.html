Commentary Hooks

The Decision That Held and the Split That Didn't: why three hawkish dissents matter more than the rate

Warsh kept rates steady and kept the Fed independent, but three officials voting to hike tells you where the internal risk really sits — up, not down. The market priced a dovish pivot; it got a hawkish minority. That gap is the trade.

One Night, Two Verdicts: how Microsoft and Meta ended the argument about AI spending

Same sector, same capex wave, opposite reactions — because the market stopped asking who spends on AI and started asking who earns from it. Azure past $100B is proof; Meta guiding to $145B without it is a promise. Proof beats promise now.

The Premium That Won't Die: why a resumed strike put the war back into every barrel and bond

Markets tried to price the Iran conflict as over and were wrong within days. The lesson for allocators is that a pause is not a settlement, and pricing peace before it is signed is the most expensive mistake in the current tape.
