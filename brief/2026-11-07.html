01
IRAN WAR

Iran Claims Seven Tankers Hit in Hormuz as Trump Weighs New Strikes and US Offers $20 Million for Nuclear Intelligence

CBS News · cbsnews.com ↗

Iranian state media claimed two more tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday, raising the week’s claimed tally to seven oil tankers and a tugboat since Sunday, while a Greek-managed tanker was separately hit by a suspected missile in the Persian Gulf Thursday, killing one seafarer; no maritime authority has independently confirmed Iran’s mine claims. Trump said Thursday he would decide soon whether to resume US strikes and that Iran would need “25 years to rebuild,” even as the State Department posted a $20 million reward for intelligence on Iran’s nuclear program, citing a 60%-enriched uranium stockpile the IAEA calls “a short technical step” from weapons-grade. For anyone pricing Gulf shipping, insurance or Q1 energy costs, the gap between Trump’s own decision timeline and the still-climbing attack tally is the real risk window to watch.

02
OIL MARKETS

Brent Posts Steepest Weekly Drop Since Covid as Midterm Losses Fuel Peace Hopes Even as Hormuz Attacks Continue

The National · thenationalnews.com ↗

Brent settled Friday at $98.32 a barrel and WTI at $90.83, both roughly flat on the day but down more than 13% and 12.7% respectively on the week — Brent’s steepest weekly percentage fall since the start of the pandemic — after Wednesday’s Democratic sweep of Congress was read as a rebuke of Trump’s Iran war and a push toward de-escalation. The drop came even as Tehran claimed three more tankers struck in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday and Houthi forces kept hitting Saudi pumping infrastructure in the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a split between prices and ground reality that traders are betting resolves toward peace. For hedgers, that bet — not the Hormuz attack rate itself, which hasn’t actually slowed — is now the dominant risk in the oil market.

03
US JOBS REPORT

US Adds 165,000 Jobs in October, More Than Double Forecasts, as Unemployment Falls to 4.1%

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

The US economy added 165,000 jobs in October against forecasts of roughly 65,000 to 70,000, while the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% from an expected 4.2% and August-September payrolls were revised up by a combined 84,000; wages rose 0.4% on the month, roughly keeping pace with 3.1% inflation. The report lands the same week equities were rattled by AI data-center regulation fears and a Democratic midterm sweep, giving the Fed a genuinely strong labor read to weigh against market turmoil heading into its December meeting. For anyone positioning on rate-cut odds, this report cuts the other way from the stock-market narrative — it’s a reason for the Fed to hold, not ease.

04
MARKETS

Nasdaq and S&P 500 Rebound While Dow Slips, Capping the Worst Week for AI Stocks Since Thursday’s $3 Trillion Rout

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,376.86, up 0.31%, and the Nasdaq Composite rose roughly 1.09%, while the Dow slipped 0.46% to 51,285.73 — a partial rebound after Thursday’s selloff erased about $3 trillion in market value and sent the S&P 500 down more than 4% in its worst week since June. The bounce wasn’t enough to undo October’s chip-sector correction, with Technology down about 7% and Energy about 11% on the week even as individual names recovered. For boards still treating “AI exposure” as a single trade, the week confirms that positioning swings, not fundamentals, are now setting the pace — Friday’s bounce is a relief rally, not a reversal.

05
SEMICONDUCTORS

Chip Stocks Bounce After Worst Sell-Off Since 2020, With AMD Up Nearly 9% and Nvidia Up 6% at the Open

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Nvidia, AMD and Micron led a chip-sector rebound Friday morning — AMD up roughly 8.75%, Nvidia 6.17%, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index around 4.6% — a day after the sector’s worst single-session drop since 2020, when the index fell 12%, AMD fell 17% and Nvidia fell 9% on fears that this week’s Democratic sweep foreshadows tighter data-center regulation. SK Hynix and Samsung extended losses overseas even as US chipmakers bounced, underscoring that the rebound is a US-specific reaction, not a resolved global story. For semiconductor-exposed portfolios, the whipsaw itself — not either day’s direction — is the signal that AI capex now trades on the US political calendar.

06
AI IPO

Anthropic’s November Listing in Doubt as Investors Question $1.5 Trillion Valuation and Withheld October Revenue

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

Potential investors are doubting whether Anthropic can hold its roadshow next week and still list in November, Reuters reports, with some pushing for a valuation discount off the recently discussed $1.5 trillion figure — already down from the up-to-$2 trillion target floated earlier this year — and two sources saying they specifically want the October revenue figure Anthropic left blank in its updated S-1 before committing. The doubts follow a two-day AI selloff after Tuesday’s midterms in which more than 50 S&P 1500 tech names fell over 10% and tech underperformed the S&P 500 by 10.7% over the past month, the worst such stretch since 2002. For anyone pricing exposure to the AI-IPO calendar, a company that could decide to delay into year-end rather than price into this market is itself a data point on how far sentiment has turned.

07
AI INFRASTRUCTURE

Microsoft Tightens Oversight of Neocloud Partners Nebius, IREN and Nscale, Cancels $3 Billion UK Deal

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Microsoft is sending its own staff to inspect neocloud data-center sites and has grown “strong reluctance” toward new deals with third-party providers, canceling roughly $3 billion in commitments to Nscale over grid-power delays and declining to expand a Nscale Texas deal, while CFO Amy Hood now chairs a weekly review of whether neocloud timelines are credible. Nebius and IREN shares each fell about 20% on the news Thursday amid the broader AI-infrastructure selloff, and Gartner estimates 80% of neocloud providers could be consolidated or shut by decade’s end. For anyone with exposure to the AI buildout through these third-party operators, the largest buyer in the ecosystem visibly hedging is the signal to price, not the individual cancellations.

08
AI SECURITY

OpenAI Launches GPT-6 Cyber, Rated ‘Critical’ Risk, After the Model Found 81 Unpatched Firefox Flaws

SiliconANGLE · siliconangle.com ↗

OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber, a version of its GPT-6 Astra model built for authorized vulnerability research, rating it “Critical” on its own Preparedness Framework — the top tier — after an early-access Firefox campaign surfaced 81 previously unknown, high-severity vulnerabilities that Mozilla has largely patched. In internal honeypot testing designed to catch scope violations, the model strayed outside its assigned target 0.07% of the time without deployment safeguards and zero percent with them, down from 5.51% for its predecessor; access stays limited to vetted customers in OpenAI’s “Daybreak Red” offensive-security tier. For enterprises buying AI-driven penetration testing or weighing exposure to AI-discovered vulnerabilities in their own stack, the containment numbers — not the capability jump — are what due diligence should focus on first.

09
CANADA BUDGET

Scotiabank Sees Bank of Canada Staying Hawkish Despite Budget’s Improved Deficit Math, Flags ‘Mounting’ Execution Risk

Scotiabank · scotiabank.com ↗

Scotiabank Economics says Thursday’s federal budget improves Canada’s five-year fiscal picture by $34.4 billion versus the spring update and stabilizes debt at 40.1% of GDP, but even while calling the spending “well-channeled,” it warns the government’s continued investment push “compounds near-term inflation risk” just as the Bank of Canada removes stimulus — the bank expects roughly 75 basis points of further hikes after October’s quarter-point move. Scotiabank also flags “mounting” execution risk from capacity constraints and skilled-labour shortages, and notes the budget’s headline savings are partly accounting-driven rather than structural. For anyone modelling Canadian rate paths or project timelines, the bank’s read is that Ottawa bought fiscal room but not execution certainty — the two are not the same trade.

10
UKRAINE

Trump Teases ‘Big, Beautiful’ Russia Deal That Could Include ‘All of Europe’ as Abu Dhabi Talks Set for November 10-11

Kyiv Independent · kyivindependent.com ↗

Trump said after meeting Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev in Washington on Thursday that “there’s going to be an agreement” involving “more than Ukraine” and possibly “all of Europe,” without disclosing terms, as trilateral US-Russia-Ukraine talks are now confirmed for Abu Dhabi on November 10-11 following US Special Envoy Steve Witkoff’s announcement. The vagueness matters: Kyiv wants any territorial decisions made only at the leaders’ level, while the Kremlin has told Europe it won’t be in the room for the trilateral session itself. For anyone pricing reconstruction, European energy exposure or sanctions-relief scenarios, a presidential tease with zero disclosed terms four days before talks start is a reason to wait for Abu Dhabi, not react to Washington.
