UKRAINE PEACE TALKS

Abu Dhabi Talks End With Prisoner-Swap Agreement as Kushner and Witkoff Head to Moscow, Then Dublin

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Trilateral US-Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi — the first since February — ended Wednesday with Russia’s Vladimir Medinsky saying both sides had agreed to a large-scale prisoner swap, though envoy Jared Kushner said the exact numbers would only be finalized Thursday and TASS put the exchange two to three weeks out. Kushner and Steve Witkoff are now flying to Moscow to brief Vladimir Putin before continuing on to Dublin to meet Volodymyr Zelenskyy, with both sides describing progress on a “post-war security framework” but no breakthrough on territory, which Kushner called the hardest issue to settle. For anyone pricing the war’s endgame, the shuttle itinerary — Abu Dhabi to Moscow to Dublin inside a week — is itself the signal: Washington is now running parallel tracks with each side rather than waiting for them in the same room.

02
NVIDIA GOVERNANCE

Nvidia CEO’s Exit ‘Looms’ as Board Meets Wednesday Night to Weigh Jensen Huang’s Future

The Information · theinformation.com ↗

Nvidia’s board met Wednesday night to discuss Jensen Huang’s future, The Information reported, three days after announcing an inquiry into whether he was the target of a foreign intelligence effort over his admitted affair with a Singapore-based executive U.S. intelligence has flagged as a possible Chinese asset. The report says Huang’s time as CEO “appears to be coming to an end,” though it does not identify the reports it cites, and Nvidia’s board had said days earlier it had “no reason to believe” company secrets were compromised. For anyone holding the world’s most valuable chipmaker, the distance between a board reviewing a CEO’s conduct and a board meeting specifically to weigh his exit is the whole story here — and it is still playing out in real time, with nothing on the record from Nvidia itself since the inquiry was announced.

ISRAEL POLITICS

Herzog Taps Eisenkot Over Netanyahu to Form Israel’s Next Government

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

President Isaac Herzog tasked former military chief Gadi Eisenkot with forming Israel’s next government after 67 lawmakers backed his candidacy over Benjamin Netanyahu’s, a formal setback for the prime minister that opens a fraught coalition-building process against a divided opposition. Eisenkot has said Netanyahu’s own party would be welcome to join a government under him, a signal he is angling for breadth rather than a narrow bloc. For anyone tracking Israeli policy continuity on Gaza, Iran or US relations, the open question isn’t whether Eisenkot has a mandate — he already does — it’s whether he can assemble a majority before it lapses, and what he has to trade away to the parties he needs to get there.

OIL MARKETS

Oil Edges Up From a 20% Monthly Slide as Iran Reports a Fresh Hormuz Mine Strike

Euronews · euronews.com ↗

Brent rose 0.2% to $91.71 a barrel and the US benchmark gained 0.1% to $86.12 on Wednesday, a modest bounce after both contracts lost roughly 20% this month on hopes of a US-Iran peace deal, after Iran’s army said a tanker struck a mine and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz and India’s shipping minister reported a separate projectile strike that injured four crew on a Liberia-flagged tanker. The US military says it recently cleared all mines from the strait, which normally carries about a fifth of global oil, and Washington has been helping tankers transit it since the summer. For anyone pricing Gulf shipping risk, prices rising on a tanker attack rather than falling on peace hopes is the tell: traders are now treating a US-Iran deal as priced in and the live war risk as the variable still unresolved.

05
CANADA-EU

Carney Touches Down in Dublin to Push Canada’s Bid to Become the EU’s First Associate Member

CP24 · cp24.com ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney arrived in Dublin early Thursday to address the European Political Community — only the second time he has spoken to the forum, and Canada remains the only non-European country to do so at all — with his office saying Canada and the EU are “working toward Canada becoming the EU’s first Associate Member” under the countries’ new Alliance for the Future. Before addressing the EPC, Carney was set to meet Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis; he is expected to spend less than 24 hours in Ireland before flying more than 15,000 kilometres to Manila, then Cambodia and Shenzhen for APEC. For anyone tracking Canada’s post-tariff diversification, an EU associate-member track running in parallel with an APEC swing the same week is the clearest sign yet that Ottawa is hedging its US exposure on two fronts at once, not picking one over the other.

06
CANADA AUTO

Carney Calls Stellantis’s Sale of Its Idled Brampton Plant to an Armoured-Vehicle Maker ‘a Betrayal’

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney said Stellantis’s sale of its idled Brampton, Ontario assembly plant to Roshel, a Canadian armoured-vehicle maker, amounts to “a betrayal” of thousands of workers and vowed Ottawa would “fully enforce” its rights, while CTV reports the federal government was “actively and intensively” involved in the talks that led to the deal, according to three sources with direct knowledge. The sale has drawn criticism from Unifor and from Ontario’s auto caucus over Stellantis walking away from commitments tied to the plant’s planned EV retooling, which had been supported with public incentives. For anyone tracking Ottawa’s leverage over automakers that took public money for Canadian production promises, Carney calling his own government’s negotiating partner’s outcome a “betrayal” — rather than staying neutral — signals Ottawa expects to extract something from Stellantis elsewhere to compensate.

07
MARITIME M&A

Transocean Says All Regulatory Approvals in Hand for Valaris Merger, Eyes December 1 Close

Transocean · investor.deepwater.com ↗

Transocean CEO Keelan Adamson said in the company’s third-quarter results this week that “all regulatory approvals” for its all-stock merger with Valaris are “now in hand” and that closing is expected December 1, with the combined offshore driller now focused on integration and capturing $200 million in identified annual synergies. The deal, agreed in February as an all-stock combination leaving Transocean shareholders with roughly 53% of the merged company, creates one of the industry’s largest deepwater drillers by fleet size at a moment when Gulf and Middle East tensions have kept offshore day rates elevated. For anyone pricing offshore-drilling exposure, a completed regulatory clearance with a firm date — rather than another “subject to approval” update — is the signal that this consolidation is now locked in, not speculative.

08
AVIATION

Avolon Finalizes Record Lessor Order for 140 Boeing 737-10 Jets

Boeing · boeing.mediaroom.com ↗

Aircraft lessor Avolon finalized its purchase of 140 Boeing 737-10s, which Boeing calls a record 737 MAX order by a lessor and which lifts Avolon’s backlog past 240 unfilled aircraft, as lessors have now placed firm orders for more than 230 737-10s in 2026 alone — five times the entire lessor backlog for the variant at the end of 2025. The 737-10 seats up to 230 passengers, and the order adds to Avolon’s fleet of 1,092 owned, managed and committed aircraft leased to 138 airlines in 62 countries. For anyone tracking narrow-body supply into 2027 and beyond, lessor demand outpacing last year’s entire backlog in a single year is the clearest read yet that airlines are locking in capacity through leasing rather than waiting on direct order slots from Boeing, which remain backed up for years.

09
SPACE

SpaceX Could Attempt First-Ever Double Catch of Starship and Its Booster as Early as This Month

Space.com · space.com ↗

SpaceX plans to try catching both Starship’s Super Heavy booster and its Ship upper stage on the vehicle’s 15th test flight, Elon Musk said on the company’s Q3 earnings call, calling it a “profound technology milestone” that would mark the first time any orbital launch vehicle has been fully recovered. Musk gave no firm date beyond “next month” and put the odds at “more likely than not” to succeed, with a steady stream of further attempts planned through year-end if it doesn’t; SpaceX has already caught the booster on three prior flights but has never attempted to catch the more fragile Ship. For anyone modeling launch-cost curves into 2027, a successful double catch would be the moment full reusability stops being a roadmap slide and becomes an operational fact — which is precisely why Musk is hedging the timeline rather than naming a date.
