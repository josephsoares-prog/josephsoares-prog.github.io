01
HEALTH

AstraZeneca and Bristol Myers Squibb are in early talks on a roughly $400 billion merger that would create one of the world's largest drugmakers

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

The Financial Times reported Sunday that AstraZeneca (about $265 billion) and Bristol Myers Squibb (about $133 billion) have held talks on a cash-and-stock combination worth close to $400 billion — a deal that would reshape global pharma and rank among the largest mergers ever. We read it as a signal as much as a transaction: with drug pipelines maturing and US-listing pressure mounting, expect a fresh wave of mega-scale consolidation — but the talks are early and may collapse, so position for the theme, not the single name, until a term sheet is signed.

02
ENERGY

OPEC+ agrees to raise output by another 188,000 barrels a day from September, completing the unwind of its voluntary cuts as crude slides to about $84 Brent

Benzinga · benzinga.com ↗

OPEC+ approved a further 188,000 barrels-a-day increase for September, finishing the restoration of the output it had voluntarily withheld and pushing Brent toward $84 and WTI near $81 — down from roughly $91 a week earlier. The decision-grade read is that supply, not the Middle East headlines, is setting the oil price: with the group signalling a likely fourth-quarter pause, the barrels are now in the market and the geopolitical premium is thin — position an energy book for range-bound crude, and treat any Hormuz flare-up as the tail that reprices it fast, not the base case.

03
GEOPOLITICS

Trump says a new round of US-Iran talks begins Monday and a deal is 'imminent' — after he says he called off 'the biggest attack since World War II'

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

President Trump said negotiations with Iran resume Monday afternoon and that agreements on Strait of Hormuz shipping and Iran's nuclear programme are close, adding he had cancelled a massive planned strike at the urging of Gulf allies. For allocators this is the most consequential de-escalation signal in weeks — a durable Hormuz deal would drain the war premium from oil and lift risk — but discipline still applies: price the signed, verified agreement, not the announcement, because a prior ceasefire on the Strait has already collapsed once.

04
MACRO

Friday's July jobs report — payrolls seen near 85,000 — headlines a 'Big Four' data week that will set the Fed's September path

Bloomberg · bloomberg.com ↗

Economists expect about 85,000 jobs added in July with unemployment near 4.3%, the capstone of a week that stacks ISM manufacturing, JOLTS and ADP ahead of Friday's payrolls. With Big Tech earnings behind us, the labour read is now the market's swing factor: a soft number hardens the case for a September rate cut and lifts duration, a firm one arms the hawks — so this is the print to size rate-sensitive risk around, and the earlier data are the tells that move the odds before Friday.

05
GEOPOLITICS

Israel raises 'serious concerns' over the Hamas disarmament deal, demanding weapons be destroyed and rejecting Qatari-Turkish verification — pushing the plan toward deadlock

NPR · npr.org ↗

Netanyahu's office says Hamas remains committed to rebuilding its arsenal rather than disarming, and set three conditions — freedom to keep operating militarily, destruction rather than storage of confiscated weapons, and no Qatari or Turkish verification. With Israel controlling more than 60% of Gaza and refusing to withdraw until Hamas fully disarms, the Board of Peace roadmap now faces a structural deadlock. The read confirms the caution: this is an announcement, not a settlement — do not price a Gaza de-escalation dividend until the verification mechanism is agreed and the first weapons are actually surrendered.

06
SECURITY

Zelensky warns Ukraine's air defences are being overwhelmed as Russia pounds Kyiv, and presses Washington for a decision on Patriot interceptors

CNN · cnn.com ↗

After Russian strikes killed at least nine in Kyiv, President Zelensky warned that Ukraine's air defences are being overwhelmed and pressed the United States for a faster decision on Patriot interceptor supply. The decision-grade signal is direction: even as Washington talks de-escalation with Iran, the European war is intensifying and now hinges on an interceptor bottleneck as much as on diplomacy — keep the European-security and energy hedges on, and read any 'peace' talk against a battlefield that is getting worse, not better.

07
TRADE

Mexico secures a 90-day tariff extension from Trump even as Canada's 35% rate takes hold — North America splits into two trade tracks

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

President Sheinbaum announced a 90-day extension of the tariff pause with Washington, keeping most US-Mexico trade duty-free under USMCA — the mirror image of Canada, where Trump's 35% rate on non-CUSMA-compliant goods just took effect. For decision-makers with North American supply chains, the divergence is the story: Mexico bought time while Canada is negotiating under a live tariff, so route sourcing and inventory decisions to the country with the clearer runway, and watch whether Ottawa can convert its CUSMA carve-out into a Mexico-style extension.

08
MARKETS

US stock futures rally into a jobs-and-earnings week, with the S&P 500 near records after a tech-led July close

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Equity futures pushed higher to start the week as investors positioned for Friday's jobs report and a dense earnings slate, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq holding near records after a July driven by Amazon's roughly 15% post-earnings surge. The read for allocators is a market climbing on narrow leadership into a two-sided catalyst: strong AI monetizers are carrying the tape while the macro verdict is still out, so respect the momentum but keep hedges — a soft jobs print or an earnings stumble in this week's non-tech names could puncture the calm quickly.

09
RATES

The Fed's September path now rides on Friday's payrolls as a 'teetering' market leans toward another rate cut

Reuters · investing.com ↗

With the Fed on hold, markets head into the week leaning toward a September rate cut — but Reuters notes a 'teetering' tape that needs Friday's jobs data and a heavy earnings slate to confirm the bet. The decision-grade point is asymmetry: a soft payrolls number likely locks in a September cut and rewards duration and rate-sensitive equities, while a firm one forces the market to unwind cut bets it has already partly priced — so the risk into Friday is skewed, and the prudent move is to avoid adding rate-sensitive exposure before the print.

BUSINESS

Berkshire Hathaway reports its first full quarter under Greg Abel with a record cash pile near $397 billion — and signs of a large buyback

The Motley Fool · fool.com ↗

Berkshire is reporting its first full quarter with Greg Abel as CEO, sitting on a record cash pile of roughly $397 billion, with analysts flagging the possibility it repurchased as much as $11 billion of its own stock. The read for allocators is a tell on valuations: when the most disciplined capital allocator in the market buys back its own shares rather than deploying cash into deals, it is signalling it sees more value at home than in a richly priced market — a caution worth heeding as the AI-led rally runs into a pivotal data week.

MARKETS

TaiwanPlus · taiwanplus.com ↗

US and Chinese negotiators have resumed trade talks, extending the uneasy truce that has kept the two economies from a fresh tariff escalation. The read for decision-makers is that the tail risk of a full-blown US-China rupture is being managed, not resolved — the talks lower the near-term probability of a shock, but the structural rivalry over chips, rare earths and technology access is unchanged, so treat the calm as a negotiated pause and keep supply-chain diversification on the agenda rather than assuming détente.

ECONOMY

Taiwan's economy grows a blistering 12.9% in the second quarter as AI-chip demand booms — but the boom is dangerously concentrated

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Taiwan's GDP grew 12.92% year-on-year in the second quarter, powered by AI and semiconductor exports, with TSMC alone making up about 40% of the stock market and US imports from Taiwan hitting $201 billion in 2025. The decision-grade signal is concentration risk: the island is now a critical node in the global AI supply chain, but with only about 350,000 people working in chips, the growth is a K-shaped boom exposed to any AI-spending slowdown or Trump-era trade renegotiation — an essential read for anyone with exposure to the AI hardware complex.

CANADA

The Bank of Canada holds its policy rate at 2.25% as it warns the economy will decelerate under the weight of US tariffs

Bank of Canada · bankofcanada.ca ↗

The Bank of Canada has kept its policy rate at 2.25%, pairing the hold with a forecast that growth will slow as US tariffs bite. For Canadian decision-makers the message is that monetary policy is now largely on the sidelines of the tariff fight: with the 35% US rate live and only the CUSMA carve-out shielding most exports, the central bank is signalling it will not pre-emptively cut into trade-driven weakness — so plan for a soft-growth, steady-rate backdrop through the autumn and treat the trade file, not the Bank, as the variable that moves the Canadian outlook.

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

Commentary Hooks

Two Tracks Through One Border: Mexico buys time, Canada trades under fire

Mexico just secured a 90-day tariff extension while Canada's 35% rate went live — the same North American market, split into two negotiating tracks. The lesson for operators is that in this trade regime, timing and leverage matter more than geography: the winners are the ones who route sourcing and inventory to the jurisdiction with the clearer runway, and who know their CUSMA compliance line by line.

The Megadeal Returns: what a $400 billion pharma merger says about the cycle

AstraZeneca and Bristol Myers Squibb talking about a near-$400 billion combination is not just a health-care story — it is a signal that boards believe scale, not organic growth, is the way to defend margins as pipelines mature and rates ease. When the largest players start hunting each other, expect a wave, and expect regulators to become the swing variable in every big deal for the next year.

Announcements Are Not Agreements: three conflicts, one discipline

Trump says an Iran deal is imminent while Tehran says there are no talks; Israel raises serious concerns as the Gaza roadmap stalls; and Ukraine's war intensifies even as Washington courts diplomacy. The market keeps reaching for the de-escalation dividend, and gold near its record — even amid the equity rally — shows the smart money still prices the signed, verified outcome, not the headline.
