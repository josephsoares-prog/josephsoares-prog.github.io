What mattered on Thursday, 22 October 2026

01
SAUDI ARABIA

US Pulls Diplomats From Saudi Arabia as Houthi Strikes Push Brent Past $110

Bloomberg · bloomberg.com ↗

Washington ordered some non-emergency diplomatic staff out of Saudi Arabia after a fresh wave of Houthi drone and missile strikes on the kingdom, including one near the Shaybah oil field, pushed Brent crude above $110 a barrel for the first time since the Iran war began. The drawdown signals Washington now rates the threat to its own personnel, not just to Saudi infrastructure, as serious enough to act on — a marked escalation from the calibrated-harassment pattern of recent months. Energy desks and corporate security teams with Gulf exposure should treat the diplomatic drawdown itself, not the oil-price spike, as the leading indicator of how Washington is pricing escalation risk.

02
FEDERAL RESERVE

Fed’s Waller Signals More Hikes Ahead as 16 of 18 Officials See a 2026 Move Beyond September’s

Federal Reserve Board · federalreserve.gov ↗

Governor Christopher Waller said the Fed’s September quarter-point hike to 3.75–4% reflected months of stalled inflation progress — driven by Middle East-linked energy prices, AI-buildout cost pressure and new tariffs — and that 16 of 18 FOMC participants now see at least one further 2026 hike, with futures markets pricing an 85% chance of one by December. With core PCE running at 3% and Waller favoring a “signal the destination, not the pace” approach, the Fed has shifted from a holding pattern to an active tightening bias heading into its October 28 meeting. Treasurers, CFOs and fixed-income desks should reprice year-end funding costs around a live December hike, not a pause, as the base case.

03
BANK OF CANADA

Bank of Canada’s Macklem Warns Inflation Risk Has Risen as Oil Surge Complicates October 28 Call

Reuters · investing.com ↗

Governor Tiff Macklem said from Bangkok that the risk of broader, more persistent inflation “has increased,” citing the Middle East conflict, curtailed shipments and refinery-capacity damage, and that the Bank is prepared to raise its quarterly inflation forecast significantly ahead of its October 28 decision. Markets were not pricing a change on October 28 or in December as of Saturday, but were pricing roughly 100 basis points of hikes over the next year — a sharp reversal from the hold-or-cut consensus that prevailed before oil prices spiked. Canadian corporate treasurers and mortgage-exposed sectors should stop treating October 28 as a formality and build a hike scenario into Q4 funding plans now.

04
TESLA

Tesla Profit Falls 43% to $787 Million as AI and Optimus Spending Outpaces Flat Revenue

TechCrunch · techcrunch.com ↗

Tesla reported Q3 revenue roughly flat at $28.04 billion but net income fell 43% to $787 million, which the company attributed to AI and Optimus-robot spending plus lower regulatory-credit sales, even as it began producing the Optimus humanoid robot at its Fremont factory and energy-storage revenue fell 16% on tariff-related refunds. Services revenue, up 33% to a record $4.6 billion, is now the clearest bright spot in the results, suggesting Tesla’s near-term earnings story is shifting away from vehicle margins entirely. Auto and robotics investors should read this quarter as confirmation that Tesla is deliberately trading current profit for Optimus capacity — a bet that will not be validated or refuted for several more quarters.

05
NETFLIX

Netflix Posts Slowest Growth in Three Years, Cuts 2026 Ad Target to $2.7 Billion, Shares Slide

The Next Web · thenextweb.com ↗

Netflix’s Q3 revenue rose 12% to $12.87 billion — its slowest growth in nearly three years — and the company guided Q4 earnings to 67 cents a share against a 73-cent estimate while cutting its 2026 ad-revenue target to about $2.7 billion from roughly $3 billion, with co-CEO Greg Peters admitting the company is “a bit behind” on its ad build-out; shares fell almost 6% after hours. The company still spent a record $4.8 billion on buybacks in the quarter, signalling management does not see the growth slowdown as a capital-allocation problem. Media buyers and streaming-ad planners should treat the ad-target cut as a near-term inventory constraint, not a demand-side warning — Netflix’s core subscription business is still growing, just more slowly.

06
INDUSTRIAL AI

Schneider Electric to Acquire PTC for $22.6 Billion in Largest-Ever Industrial Software Deal

PTC Inc. · ptc.com ↗

Schneider Electric agreed to buy US software maker PTC for $22.6 billion, or $205 a share — a 42.3% premium — in an all-cash deal expected to close by Q3 2027 and funded by roughly €5–6 billion in new equity and €16–17 billion in debt backed by Morgan Stanley and Société Générale. The deal roughly triples Schneider’s addressable industrial-software market by fusing PTC’s design and engineering tools with Schneider’s energy and operations platforms, targeting €250 million in annual cost synergies and €800 million in revenue synergies by year three. Industrial-automation and engineering-software competitors should expect Schneider to move fast on cross-selling once the deal closes, and should reassess competitive positioning in discrete and hybrid manufacturing now, not after close.

07
MEDIA M&A

Fox Shareholders Approve $22 Billion Roku Acquisition, Creating Third-Largest US Media Distributor

TheWrap · thewrap.com ↗

Fox shareholders overwhelmingly approved the company’s $22 billion acquisition of Roku — 197.36 million of 197.48 million eligible Class B shares voted in favor — putting the deal on track to close in the first half of 2027 pending a Department of Justice antitrust review that was extended by a second information request in September. The combination pairs Roku’s reach of more than 100 million streaming households with Fox’s existing Tubi platform, creating the third-largest US media distributor behind YouTube and the combined Paramount-Warner Bros. Discovery; Roku holders will own about 27% of the merged company. Streaming advertisers and content licensors should start renegotiating distribution terms now, since the DOJ’s extended review is the only remaining obstacle to a deal shareholders have already cleared.

08
HEALTHCARE M&A

Buyout Firm EQT Agrees to Acquire Molecular-Testing Company Qiagen for $10.8 Billion

Reuters · tradingview.com ↗

Qiagen said Wednesday it agreed to be acquired by Swedish buyout firm EQT for roughly €9.6 billion ($10.78 billion) in an all-cash deal, capping months of speculation after KKR and Advent were also reported circling the molecular-diagnostics company. The deal hands EQT a dominant position in a diagnostics and genomics-testing supplier that serves pharmaceutical, clinical and research customers globally, at a moment when AI-driven drug discovery is increasing demand for high-throughput molecular data. Life-sciences and diagnostics-sector investors should expect EQT to run a buy-and-build strategy around Qiagen, making it a more active consolidator in genomics tooling rather than a passive financial holding.

AI CHIPS

ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent Place Over $35 Billion in Nvidia Chip Orders Despite Export Limits

Reuters (via CTV News) · ctvnews.ca ↗

ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent have placed orders for more than three million of Nvidia’s export-compliant RTX PRO 5500 chips, worth a combined sticker price above $35 billion, Reuters reported — a volume that shows Chinese hyperscalers are scaling AI infrastructure through the export-control-compliant product tier rather than waiting for restrictions to ease. The order size suggests China’s AI buildout is proceeding at a pace US export rules were specifically designed to slow, raising the stakes on whether Washington tightens the compliant-chip carve-out further. Semiconductor and AI-infrastructure investors should treat this order volume as evidence that export controls are reshaping, not restraining, Chinese AI capex — demand is simply routing through whatever tier remains legal.

10
CANADA TARIFFS

Carney, Ford Unveil $8.7 Billion Plan for Steel, Auto and Critical-Minerals Sectors Hit by US Tariffs

Global News · globalnews.ca ↗

Prime Minister Mark Carney and Ontario Premier Doug Ford unveiled an $8.7 billion joint plan to support steel, auto and critical-mineral development — including Ontario’s Ring of Fire — against the backdrop of Cleveland-Cliffs shifting Stelco steel production away from Hamilton, a move Industry Minister Mélanie Joly says breaches the company’s Investment Canada Act commitments and could trigger legal action or a forced divestiture within days. The announcement lands as Canada’s Parliamentary Budget Officer cuts 2026-27 growth forecasts to 0.9% and 1.5% from 1.1% and 1.6%, and just two weeks before a federal budget due November 5. Boards with Ontario industrial exposure should watch the Stelco enforcement precedent as closely as the funding itself — it will set the template for how Ottawa treats foreign owners who cite US tariffs to justify Canadian layoffs.

11
UKRAINE

Russia Strikes Kyiv Hours After Witkoff Meets Putin in Push to Revive Peace Talks

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

US envoy Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin in St Petersburg on October 21 to push for renewed peace talks, and Russia released jailed American teacher Stephen Hubbard as a goodwill gesture while Trump delayed new sanctions — but Russian strikes on Kyiv overnight killed at least one person and injured 22, and Ukraine answered with 630 drones targeting Russia, two of them wounding people in Moscow. The Kremlin said it was “in principle” open to restarting talks while dismissing Ukrainian and European proposals as lacking “substance,” and Witkoff separately thanked Putin for a diesel deal that lets Russia sell diesel to the US — a transaction that has angered Kyiv and European capitals. Sanctions-compliance and energy-trading desks should watch the US-Russia diesel channel as the live wedge issue; it is doing more to strain the Western coalition right now than the battlefield picture is.

12
VENEZUELA

Venezuela’s Third Round of US-Backed Transition Talks Delivers Supreme Court Reform, More Prisoner Releases

US Department of State · state.gov ↗

A third round of US-backed reconciliation talks between Venezuela’s interim government and the 2015 National Assembly opposition began October 14, following two earlier rounds in August and September, and has already produced a new law reforming Supreme Court justice selection — with 32 new judges to be seated by December — plus the release of 177 additional political prisoners since talks began and restored access to more than 70 previously blocked websites. The talks, which exclude opposition leader María Corina Machado directly, are now working toward reforming the National Electoral Council and guaranteeing participation rights ahead of credible elections. Firms evaluating Venezuela re-entry, particularly in oil, should treat the Supreme Court and electoral-council reforms — not the political-prisoner releases — as the leading indicators of whether the legal environment is becoming investable.

13
NORTH KOREA

North Korea Fires Two Ballistic Missiles Over 900 Kilometres, Its Second Launch in Ten Days

The Korea Times · koreatimes.co.kr ↗

North Korea fired two ballistic missiles from the Pyongyang area on October 12, with at least one travelling more than 900 kilometres, South Korea’s Joint Chiefs of Staff said — the regime’s second launch in ten days after an October 3 test from Wonsan. US Pacific Command said the launches pose no immediate threat to US personnel, territory or allies but reaffirmed its defence commitments, a measured response that leaves North Korea’s testing cadence unconstrained for now. Defence and insurance underwriters pricing Korean-peninsula risk should note the shortened interval between tests — ten days versus the usual multi-week gaps — as the more significant signal than the missiles’ range.

14
TAIWAN

Taiwan Probes Organised-Crime Links to China’s ‘Transnational Repression’ Campaign

Daily Maverick · dailymaverick.co.za ↗

Taiwan’s National Security Bureau is investigating whether local criminal gangs and private investigation firms behind an attack on a comedian who mocked Xi Jinping were directed and funded by the Chinese Communist Party, bureau director-general Tsai Ming-yen told parliament, adding that Taiwan’s current legal framework is inadequate to counter this kind of “transnational repression.” The probe comes alongside rising Chinese Coast Guard patrols east of Taiwan since June and sightings of maritime militia vessels that officials believe may be preparatory moves toward a blockade posture, even as Beijing calls its operations lawful. Risk teams at firms with Taiwan-based supply chains should treat the legal-framework gap Tsai flagged as a near-term policy catalyst — new legislation criminalizing proxy intimidation networks is now plausible within this legislative session.
