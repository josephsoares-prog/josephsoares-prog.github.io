01
IRAN WAR

Iran Says Four Tankers Have Now Hit Mines in Hormuz as Saudi Arabia Intercepts Houthi Missiles Aimed at Riyadh

Gulf News · gulfnews.com ↗

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard says a fourth tanker has now hit a mine on an “unauthorized southern route” in and near the Strait of Hormuz, while Saudi air defences shot down two Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and a third targeting the Eastern Province — all as Washington weighs a week-long bombing campaign against Iranian leadership, nuclear sites, and energy infrastructure once the November 3 midterms pass. Brent is trading near $112 this morning after touching $117 earlier this week, its highest since March, even as Tehran says it is negotiating a seven-day plan with Qatari mediators to reopen the strait. Executives with Gulf-transit exposure — energy, shipping, war-risk insurance — should treat November 3 itself, not the general temperature of the conflict, as the date markets are now pricing as the strike trigger.

02
MARKETS

Alphabet Sheds $482 Billion in a Single Day — the Second-Largest One-Day Market-Cap Loss in US History — Despite 103% Cloud Growth

The Motley Fool · fool.com ↗

Alphabet shares fell as much as 13% on October 29 — their steepest one-day drop since the 2004 IPO — erasing roughly $482 billion in market value, the second-largest single-day loss in US stock-market history after Nvidia’s January 2025 rout. The results themselves were strong: revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion and Google Cloud more than doubled to $30.7 billion, but capex hit a record $51.4 billion, free cash flow was negative for a second straight quarter, and search growth slowed to 12%. The market’s verdict is now explicit: investors will punish AI capex that is not visibly converting to cash flow, which boards at every hyperscaler and every company leasing their compute should read as a tightening window before “trust us” stops working as an answer.

03
CANADA-US TRADE

Trump Signs Fifth Section 338 Proclamation Reshuffling the Canada Tariff List, Effective Today

The White House · whitehouse.gov ↗

Effective today, a fifth Section 338 proclamation removes plywood, some paper products, and certain machinery and refrigeration equipment from the Canada tariff list while adding valves, carpets, and waste products — tariffs the White House says stack on top of Section 232 duties and apply regardless of USMCA eligibility. The administration frames this as refining, not ending, the campaign it says began when Canada “walked away” from a near-final deal and retaliated against roughly $20 billion in US exports. For Canadian exporters and their US buyers, the lesson of a fifth modification in three months is that the tariff list itself is now a moving target — procurement and pricing teams need a standing process to re-check HS codes against each new proclamation, not a one-time compliance check.

04
LITIGATION

Meta’s Profit Falls 37% as a $9.89 Billion Teen-Safety Settlement Charge Masks a 26% Revenue Jump

SEC EDGAR · sec.gov ↗

Meta’s Q3 operating income fell 37% after the company booked a $9.89 billion charge tied to its August settlement with 52 state attorneys general over teen mental-health and social-media litigation — a deal whose total payments could reach $18.59 billion. Strip out the charge and the underlying business is fine: revenue rose 26% to $64.6 billion and the company raised its 2026 capex guidance to $135-145 billion. For any company whose product reaches minors, this is the first hard number attached to that liability category, and general counsels should be pricing a comparable multi-state exposure into their own risk models now, not after their own settlement talks start.

05
M&A

DoorDash in Advanced Talks to Buy Instacart for About $17 Billion

BNN Bloomberg · bnnbloomberg.ca ↗

DoorDash is in advanced talks to acquire Instacart at a roughly $17 billion valuation — about 30% above Instacart’s prior market value — with a deal possibly closing within days; Instacart’s parallel talks with Uber are no longer active. Instacart shares jumped and DoorDash fell on the news, the market’s shorthand for “the buyer is paying up for scale.” If it closes, this consolidates restaurant delivery and grocery marketplace logistics under one operator just as Instacart separately winds down its Caper Cart hardware business — a signal that the delivery sector’s next round of competition is about retailer relationships and route density, not standalone grocery-tech bets.

06
WORKFORCE & AI

Microsoft Offers Voluntary Buyouts to About 60,000 Employees as It Reshapes for the AI Age

TechCrunch · techcrunch.com ↗

Microsoft confirmed it is offering buyouts to roughly 60,000 employees at the VP level and below across Xbox, Windows, Copilot, and Finance in the US, Canada, India, and the UK — on severance terms similar to standard packages, with data-center, sales, and marketing staff excluded. It is the company’s second workforce-reduction move this year, after more than 30% of 8,750 retirement-eligible staff took an April buyout, and it comes as Microsoft’s own quarterly capex more than doubled year over year. For any enterprise buyer negotiating with Microsoft right now, the signal is explicit: headcount is being reallocated away from legacy product groups toward AI infrastructure, and account teams on the losing side of that reallocation are a near-term relationship risk worth flagging to your own vendor-management function.

07
OPEC+

OPEC+ Holds November Output Steady Even as the Hormuz Crisis Pushes Oil Toward $117

OPEC · opec.org ↗

The eight OPEC+ countries steering output policy — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, UAE, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman — reaffirmed their existing production levels for November and will revisit the decision December 1, choosing not to respond to a war-driven price spike with extra barrels. That restraint, taken before the latest round of tanker mine strikes, means the group now has to decide at its next meeting whether to keep sitting on spare capacity while Brent trades near its highest since March, or release it and risk being blamed for flooding a market already spooked by the Gulf conflict. Energy buyers locking in winter supply contracts should price both outcomes rather than assume OPEC+ steps in to cap prices if Hormuz escalates further.

08
ENERGY

Shell Takes Final Investment Decision to Double LNG Canada’s Export Capacity at Kitimat

Shell · shell.com ↗

Shell Canada Energy has taken a final investment decision on LNG Canada’s Phase 2 expansion, doubling production capacity at the Kitimat, BC terminal — one of the largest single capital commitments in Canadian LNG to date. The decision locks in Canada’s bet that Asian buyers will pay for North American gas for decades, at the same moment the country is trying to diversify export markets away from the US. For Canadian energy investors and BC supply-chain and labour-market planners, the multi-year construction window this opens is the more immediate opportunity than the eventual export volumes themselves.

09
UKRAINE

Localized Oleshky Ceasefire, Brokered by Melania Trump, Holds as Aid Reaches the Kherson Front

Al Jazeera · aljazeera.com ↗

The localized truce around Russian-occupied Oleshky that the White House credits to Melania Trump’s personal intervention has held since taking effect October 27, letting mine-clearance crews and aid convoys reach a city that had roughly 2,000 of its pre-war 24,000 residents still inside. It is a narrow, humanitarian carve-out — not a step toward the broader ceasefire Kyiv and Moscow remain far apart on, with Ukraine’s negotiators still set to meet Russian counterparts in Abu Dhabi in early November under terms neither side has conceded. For anyone tracking Ukraine reconstruction or war-risk exposure, the realistic read is that localized, personality-brokered truces like this one are now the mechanism doing the actual humanitarian work, while the diplomatic track remains stalled.

CANADA SECURITY

Ottawa Set to Unveil Canada’s First National Security Strategy Since 2004 Within Weeks

CP24 · cp24.com ↗

Mark Carney’s senior adviser David Morrison told a House committee the government’s first overarching national security strategy since Paul Martin’s 2004 “Securing an Open Society” is close — covering espionage, foreign interference, and terrorism alongside a new emphasis on economic security and “strategic autonomy” amid shifting alliances and trade patterns. The effort, originally ordered by Trudeau, stalled in March 2025 when officials warned its assumptions no longer matched Canada’s strategic environment following Trump’s tariffs and statehood threats. For corporate security, legal, and government-affairs teams, “economic security” language in a document like this typically precedes new investment-screening rules or supply-chain disclosure requirements — worth reading in full the week it lands, not weeks later.

11
CANADA STEEL

Stelco to Idle Hamilton Finishing Lines, Lay Off Up to 500, in Standoff With Ottawa Over Cleveland-Cliffs Jobs Pledge

CP24 · cp24.com ↗

Stelco is idling finishing operations and laying off up to 500 workers across its Hamilton and Lake Erie plants, citing a 58% drop in Canadian cold-rolled and coated steel exports to the US and import penetration still at 45% of the market despite Ottawa’s trade protections. Industry Minister Mélanie Joly is pushing back because US-based owner Cleveland-Cliffs’ 2024 takeover approval required it to hold unionized headcount steady for five years — a commitment Stelco argues the layoffs do not technically breach. Any company that agreed to foreign-investment job commitments as a condition of a takeover should watch how this dispute resolves: it is the live test case for whether Ottawa will actually enforce those conditions once market conditions make honoring them unprofitable.

12
CANADA HOUSING

Canadian Housing Starts Rose 3% in September, CMHC Says

CTV News · ctvnews.ca ↗

CMHC’s seasonally-adjusted annualized pace of housing starts rose 3% in September from August, a modest gain that will feed directly into how the Bank of Canada and federal budget planners read housing-supply momentum heading into the November 4 budget. It is not a dramatic number on its own, but with the Bank having just surprised markets with a hike to 2.5% on oil-driven inflation, every incremental housing-supply data point now carries extra weight in the rate debate. Homebuilders and mortgage lenders should read this as confirmation that supply is inching up, not surging — financing costs, not permitting, remain the binding constraint.
