01
HORMUZ

Trump insists the US controls the Strait of Hormuz as shipping traffic sinks to a three-month low

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

Trump told reporters the US Navy has swept Hormuz for mines and controls the waterway, threatening Iran would be 'blown away' if it interfered. Kpler data shows the opposite on the water: transit has weakened every week since the war began, from 120 vessels a day before the war to a fraction now. Treat the rhetoric and the physical reality as two separate signals -- price shipping and insurance risk on vessel counts, not press-conference claims.

02
CANADA

Six days from Washington's 50% tariff, Ottawa still has no deal after a third round of talks

National Post · nationalpost.com ↗

Dominic LeBlanc met US trade officials for the third time in three weeks with no agreement, as Washington's 50% tariff on nearly $20 billion of Canadian goods -- dairy, alcohol, electronics, building materials -- takes effect August 19 regardless of CUSMA compliance. Canada is reportedly prepared to drop retaliatory tariffs and provincial procurement restrictions for partial relief, but no framework has been signed. Operators exposed to cross-border goods flow should assume the tariff lands as scheduled and plan pricing now, not after August 19.

03
ENERGY

IMF: the oil market absorbed the war shock, but its buffers are running low

IMF · imf.org ↗

The IMF warns global oil supply is now forecast to fall 4.3 million barrels a day to 102 million, a sharper drop than projected in July, as losses compound from the Middle East and Russia simultaneously. OPEC and the IEA separately cut their 2026 demand growth outlooks the same week, even as prices held. The combination -- falling demand forecasts alongside falling supply buffers -- is the real vulnerability: a single new supply shock now has far less slack to absorb it than it did in the spring.

04
SHIPPING

Sanctioned tanker's oil spill reaches Oman's coast as Red Sea attack adds to supply-chain uncertainty

UN News · news.un.org ↗

Oman's Environment Authority confirms a spill from a sanctioned tanker carrying Russian crude has reached beaches at Ras Madrakah, deploying specialized teams as it spreads. Separately, the UN reports a deadly Red Sea ship attack is compounding global supply-chain uncertainty already strained by Hormuz disruption. The shadow fleet carrying sanctioned crude is now an environmental and insurance liability on top of a geopolitical one -- expect claims disputes wherever this oil originated.

05
RUSSIA

Meduza · meduza.io ↗

Zelensky has moved from general appeals to a specific, quantified ask of Washington -- a fraction of the US interceptor missile stockpile, precisely sized to Ukraine's winter air-defence gap. That specificity forces a decision rather than a debate: Western governments can no longer default to vague commitments once the number is on the table. Watch whether Washington responds with a figure of its own in the next 72 hours -- silence is itself an answer.

Putin's first visit to the Kuril Islands -- held by Russia but claimed by Japan since 1945 -- is a deliberate signal that Moscow is unconcerned about opening new fronts of friction while stretched in Ukraine. Japan's protest will stay rhetorical for now, but the timing matters: it tells Tokyo, and by extension Washington's Indo-Pacific allies, that Russia reads Western attention as fully consumed by Hormuz and Ukraine. Expect no near-term escalation, but log this as evidence Moscow is testing bandwidth, not making a mistake.

Track prime contractor announcements over the next quarter for early positioning.

Corridor investors with European industrial exposure should model this as a base case, not a tail risk.
