01
HORMUZ

Trump insists the US controls the Strait of Hormuz as Iran warns it could 'prolong' the war

CNN · cnn.com ↗

President Trump again insisted the US now controls the Strait of Hormuz, while Tehran countered that Washington's stance could 'prolong' the conflict and pressed demands including a toll on shipping passage. The gap between claimed control and an actual reopening is what matters for anyone pricing energy and freight risk: rhetoric has hardened even as an Oman-brokered arrangement remains unsigned. Treat the strait as contested, not reopening, and keep insurance and routing contingencies in place.

02
CANADA

Canada's top negotiator warns Washington the August 19 tariffs could halt trade talks entirely

The Globe and Mail · theglobeandmail.com ↗

Ottawa's lead negotiator told US counterparts that letting the 50% tariff take effect on August 19 could end the trade talks outright, as Carney presses for broader relief across 'all strategic sectors.' This reframes the deadline from a bargaining chip into a rupture point — the downside case is no deal and retaliation, not just higher duties. Canadian operators should finalize August 19 pricing and supply contingencies now rather than assume a last-minute reprieve.

INFLATION

US inflation holds at 3.4% in July as prices rise 0.1%, keeping a September Fed cut in play

CNBC · cnbc.com ↗

July headline CPI rose just 0.1% on the month and held at a 3.4% annual pace, roughly in line with expectations and cool enough to keep a September rate cut on the table. But 3.4% is still well above target, and Warsh's Fed has signalled less urgency to ease than markets want. Allocators should treat a September cut as probable but not certain — the tariff-driven goods pressure building beneath the headline is the risk to watch.

04
ENERGY

Brent holds near $92 as Iran floats a Hormuz 'protection toll,' up nearly 38% on the year

Fortune · fortune.com ↗

Brent settled around $91.60 a barrel, off about 1% on the day but up 37.7% year-on-year, with Iran floating what analysts called a 'mafia-style protection toll' for Hormuz passage. A toll — rather than a closure — would institutionalize a permanent risk premium into every barrel that transits the strait. Budget for structurally higher energy input costs through Q4, not a quick reversion to pre-crisis levels.

HUMANITARIAN

Colombia's earthquake death toll climbs to 239 with hundreds still missing

The death toll from Colombia's magnitude-7.4 quake has risen to 239 with hundreds still missing as rescue teams push into the Choco epicenter for a third day. Foreign aid is arriving — the US pledged $15.5 million — but the gap between confirmed dead and the missing means the count will keep climbing. Reconstruction financing and insurance exposure across the western Andes will dominate the next several weeks.

TECHNOLOGY

Trump says Apple, Nvidia and Tesla will build chips with Intel in a US manufacturing push

Yahoo Finance · finance.yahoo.com ↗

Trump said Apple has agreed to design and build chips with Intel in the US, alongside Nvidia's 'first-level' chips and Musk's planned Intel-built 'TerraFab' — part of a manufacturing push backed by an $8.9 billion US government stake in Intel now valued near $52 billion. Details remain thin, but the direction is unmistakable: Washington is anchoring domestic chip capacity with state capital and marquee customers. Supply chains built around Asian foundries should model a slower but real reshoring of leading-edge production.

MARKETS

A capitalized Intel foundry changes the competitive map for TSMC and Samsung and gives Washington's reshoring bet a balance sheet. Investors treating Intel as a value trap should re-underwrite it as a policy-backed foundry play.

Investing.com · investing.com ↗

Commentary Hooks

A Toll Is Not a Reopening

Iran floating a 'protection toll' for Hormuz passage is not a step toward normalization — it is an attempt to institutionalize a permanent tax on the world's most important oil chokepoint. Anyone pricing a return to pre-crisis freight and insurance costs is mispricing what a toll actually means.

Ottawa's Deadline Just Became a Cliff

When Canada's own negotiator tells Washington the August 19 tariff could end the talks, the deadline stops being leverage and becomes a cliff edge. Canadian leaders should be building for rupture and retaliation, not banking on the constructive tone Carney describes.

Gold Is Voting, Crypto Isn't

The clearest market tell of the week isn't equities near records — it's gold at $4,400 while Bitcoin sits 46% off its high. When real fear enters, capital still runs to the metal, not the token; the safe-haven case for crypto keeps failing exactly when it is supposed to work.
