Venezuela

Last updated: September 15, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. Venezuela's oil revenue now clears through Washington, and that single fact organises everything else. Executive Order 14373, signed 9 January 2026, created Foreign Government Deposit Funds — US Treasury accounts holding the proceeds of Venezuelan natural-resource sales, with the Secretary of State deciding what is disbursed and when. On 31 August 2026 the White House announced 100-year concessions over 17 fields to North American Blue Energy Partners, with the Pentagon's Office of Strategic Capital taking a 35% equity stake and the State Department holding a right to buy 20% of output at cost. The constitutional track has not moved at the same speed. Delcy Rodríguez has been acting president since early January and no election date has been set. Commercial access is being written faster than constitutional order, and the gap between the two is the story.

The security picture

The US military campaign in the Caribbean has been institutionalised rather than wound down. On 6 August 2026 US Southern Command replaced Operation Southern Spear with Joint Task Force Western Hemisphere, a standing command structure spanning 18 partner nations. The Pentagon's own quarterly report to Congress, covering 1 April to 30 June 2026, records at least 19 strikes on vessels, approximately 56 people killed, and $820.9 million obligated cumulatively through 30 June. SOUTHCOM's newsroom records a further lethal strike on a vessel in the Caribbean on 9 September and refuelling-vessel interdictions in the eastern Pacific on 5, 7 and 8 September. Inside the country, armed colectivos deployed across Caracas after Maduro's detention and remain positioned near the presidential palace. Interior Minister Diosdado Cabello put the homicide rate at 1.2 per 100,000 at mid-year — a government figure with no independent series behind it.

Politics

The constitutional clock ran out and nobody restarted it. Article 234 gave the acting presidency 90 days; that window closed on 5 April 2026 without the National Assembly convening to debate it, and the 180-day ceiling passed in early July with no public electoral commitment. Talks opened in Caracas on 2 August between National Assembly president Jorge Rodríguez and Dinorah Figuera of the 2015 Assembly: the opposition is seeking to rebuild the electoral council and the Supreme Tribunal, the government is seeking further sanctions relief. The Judicial Postulations Committee has been widened to 12 civil-society representatives alongside 11 deputies, and a seven-jurist technical commission has been floated to vet candidates. María Corina Machado said on 23 May she will run again and return before the end of 2026. Foro Penal counted 328 political prisoners at the end of August.

Economy

Output is rising slowly while prices run fast, and the two figures come from different reporting chains. OPEC secondary sources put August crude output at 1.145 million barrels a day, up 23,000 on July; PDVSA's own direct communication claimed 1.201 million. Exports held near flat at 1.17 million barrels a day in August. The central bank reported 7.14% year-on-year growth in the second quarter — oil 9.10%, non-oil 5.79% — a 21st consecutive quarter of expansion. Against that, annual inflation reached 575.9% and monthly inflation 19.9% in July. The IMF's April 2026 outlook projects 4.0% growth and 387.4% average inflation for the year. Washington keeps adjusting the terms of access: OFAC published General Licences 50A and 51A on 11 September and amended General Licence 52C on 14 September. Roughly 7.8 million Venezuelans remain abroad.

What to watch

The G20 energy ministerial in Houston through 15 September, where the Venezuelan delegation — Hydrocarbons Minister Paula Henao and PDVSA executive vice-president Jovanny Martínez — is admitted to bilaterals and side events but not to the closed ministerial sessions. The Human Rights Council's interactive dialogue on the report of the Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Venezuela, on 18 September. Whether a Trump–Rodríguez meeting materialises around the UN General Assembly in late September, which Trump raised on 13 September. The reporting trail General Licence 52C creates: exporters of Venezuelan-origin oil must report to the State and Energy Departments within ten days of a first transaction, then every 90 days, which will make the flows visible for the first time. And whether the Figuera–Rodríguez table converts judicial appointments into an electoral calendar, or stops at the appointments.

Today's cross-source snapshot — 15 September 2026

Venezuela's acting president Delcy Rodríguez is preparing to travel to New York for the UN General Assembly and could meet with Donald Trump there, according to people familiar with the planning. The trip has not been officially confirmed by either government, and Bloomberg Línea frames it as a planned rather than settled itinerary.Bloomberg Línea
The Trump administration is positioning Venezuela as a pillar of a new hemispheric oil strategy meant to reduce US dependence on Middle East supply, with Interior Secretary Doug Burgum naming the country inside that map. Washington officials describe the goal as building Venezuela into a major global energy hub, a characterization from US sources that has not been independently verified against actual output or investment figures.El Nacional · Infobae
A newly formed Venezuelan opposition coalition is demanding presidential elections in which María Corina Machado would run as candidate, along with the lifting of her political disqualification. No electoral timeline or eligibility ruling has been set by Venezuelan authorities, so the coalition's demand remains a position, not a scheduled process.Infobae
Alex Saab, a businessman closely tied to the former Maduro-era government's finances, is expected to plead guilty to a federal money-laundering charge at a Miami court hearing. The case moves a long-running US prosecution toward resolution and links directly to the broader financial-flow scrutiny of the prior administration's overseas dealings.El Nacional
Secretary of State Marco Rubio closed out a South America tour by signing new anti-organized-crime cooperation agreements with Colombia, Ecuador and Peru, expanding a US-led security network in the region. Venezuela was left out of that network, a gap Efecto Cocuyo notes without a stated reason from either government.Efecto Cocuyo
The US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued General License 52C, easing restrictions on US firms' commercial and operational transactions with PDVSA while keeping underlying sanctions in place and requiring reporting every 90 days. Independent Venezuelan outlet Últimas Noticias and state broadcaster teleSUR both report the same license terms, indicating the easing itself is not in dispute even where its political framing differs.Últimas Noticias · teleSUR
InSight Crime reports that US missile strikes on suspected drug-trafficking vessels near Venezuela have resumed after a pause, reviving a tactic the outlet's reporting says has not previously reduced trafficking volumes. The renewed strikes sit inside the broader US military posture toward Venezuela rather than a declared new policy shift.InSight Crime

What's circulating — 15 September 2026

A network of roughly 75 accounts on X, plus more than a dozen recently built web pages and fake Instagram news outlets, has been amplifying support for businessman Alejandro Betancourt and the Rodríguez administration while attacking independent Venezuelan media and María Corina Machado. Venezuelan fact-checking group Cazadores de Fake News traced the accounts to dormant profiles created years earlier and reactivated in a coordinated burst — this is a documented inauthentic-amplification pattern, not a spontaneous groundswell.
Opposition-aligned accounts are amplifying the new opposition coalition's demand that María Corina Machado be recognized as the unified presidential candidate, treating her candidacy as effectively settled online. No electoral authority has confirmed a race, a date, or her eligibility, so this online framing is advocacy staking a claim, not a confirmed nomination.
Government-aligned outlets and social accounts are pushing Venezuela's reported 7.14% second-quarter economic growth figure as vindication of current economic policy. Independent Venezuelan reporting the same week describes shrinking bank credit to households and businesses despite acknowledged financing needs, so the growth figure and the credit picture are being amplified as competing framings of the same economy rather than a single agreed reality.
A viral video showing María Corina Machado at a highway tollbooth has been recirculated on Facebook, TikTok, Instagram and Threads by more than 10,000 accounts as supposed footage of her entering Venezuela from Colombia. Cazadores de Fake News traced the clip to an October 2023 campaign event, meaning the amplified 'return' footage predates and does not confirm any actual border crossing.
