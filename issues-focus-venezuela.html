Venezuela

Last updated: September 18, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. Venezuela's oil revenue now clears through Washington, and that single fact organises everything else. Executive Order 14373, signed 9 January 2026, created Foreign Government Deposit Funds — US Treasury accounts holding the proceeds of Venezuelan natural-resource sales, with the Secretary of State deciding what is disbursed and when. On 31 August 2026 the White House announced 100-year concessions over 17 fields to North American Blue Energy Partners, with the Pentagon's Office of Strategic Capital taking a 35% equity stake and the State Department holding a right to buy 20% of output at cost. The constitutional track has not moved at the same speed. Delcy Rodríguez has been acting president since early January and no election date has been set. Commercial access is being written faster than constitutional order, and the gap between the two is the story.

The security picture

The US military campaign in the Caribbean has been institutionalised rather than wound down. On 6 August 2026 US Southern Command replaced Operation Southern Spear with Joint Task Force Western Hemisphere, a standing command structure spanning 18 partner nations. The Pentagon's own quarterly report to Congress, covering 1 April to 30 June 2026, records at least 19 strikes on vessels, approximately 56 people killed, and $820.9 million obligated cumulatively through 30 June. SOUTHCOM's newsroom records a further lethal strike on a vessel in the Caribbean on 9 September and refuelling-vessel interdictions in the eastern Pacific on 5, 7 and 8 September. Inside the country, armed colectivos deployed across Caracas after Maduro's detention and remain positioned near the presidential palace. Interior Minister Diosdado Cabello put the homicide rate at 1.2 per 100,000 at mid-year — a government figure with no independent series behind it.

Politics

The constitutional clock ran out and nobody restarted it. Article 234 gave the acting presidency 90 days; that window closed on 5 April 2026 without the National Assembly convening to debate it, and the 180-day ceiling passed in early July with no public electoral commitment. Talks opened in Caracas on 2 August between National Assembly president Jorge Rodríguez and Dinorah Figuera of the 2015 Assembly: the opposition is seeking to rebuild the electoral council and the Supreme Tribunal, the government is seeking further sanctions relief. The first cycle closed on 12 August with an agreement to fully renew the Supreme Tribunal; a second round opens on 17 September. The Judicial Postulations Committee has been widened to 12 civil-society representatives alongside 11 deputies, and a seven-jurist technical commission has been floated to vet candidates. María Corina Machado said on 23 May she will run again and return before the end of 2026. Foro Penal counted 328 political prisoners at the end of August; the registry of Justicia, Encuentro y Perdón counts 452.

Economy

Output is rising slowly while prices run fast, and the two figures come from different reporting chains. OPEC secondary sources put August crude output at 1.145 million barrels a day, up 23,000 on July; PDVSA's own direct communication claimed 1.201 million. Exports held near flat at 1.17 million barrels a day in August. The central bank reported 7.14% year-on-year growth in the second quarter — oil 9.10%, non-oil 5.79% — a 21st consecutive quarter of expansion. Against that, annual inflation reached 575.9% and monthly inflation 19.9% in July. The IMF's April 2026 outlook projects 4.0% growth and 387.4% average inflation for the year. Washington keeps adjusting the terms of access: OFAC published General Licences 50A and 51A on 11 September and amended General Licence 52C on 14 September. Roughly 7.8 million Venezuelans remain abroad.

What to watch

The Human Rights Council's interactive dialogue on the report of the Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Venezuela, on 18 September. Whether a US–Venezuela meeting materialises at the UN General Assembly, which Rodríguez plans to attend from 20 to 26 September: Trump raised a meeting on 13 September, and Secretary of State Rubio has said he will “probably” meet her. The reporting trail General Licence 52C creates: exporters of Venezuelan-origin oil must report to the State and Energy Departments within ten days of a first transaction, then every 90 days, which will make the flows visible for the first time. And whether the Figuera–Rodríguez table converts judicial appointments into an electoral calendar, or stops at the appointments.

Today's cross-source snapshot — September 18, 2026

Venezuela's government and the opposition-recognized 2015 National Assembly opened a second round of US-backed transition talks on September 17 at Caracas's Hotel Meliá, with civil and political guarantees and freedom of expression on the agenda after a first round in August produced a judiciary-renewal pact and a deal to recover Venezuelan reserves held at the Bank of England for earthquake relief. The talks opened the same day police detained opposition ex-mayor Javier Oropeza in Barquisimeto, days after he returned from two years' exile in Spain and had a prior case against him dismissed; he was released hours later after public pressure and an intervention by opposition delegation head Dinorah Figuera. María Corina Machado and Edmundo González remain outside the talks.Agencia EFE · La Croix
A United Nations Independent Fact-Finding Mission told reporters in Geneva on September 17 that Venezuela's repressive apparatus remains structurally intact despite prisoner releases and increased public activity, naming 18 current and former officials — 12 flagged for possible criminal responsibility, including former Attorney General Tarek William Saab and Interior Minister Diosdado Cabello, and six still in senior posts, including interim President Delcy Rodríguez, who oversaw intelligence service Sebin as vice president until 2021. Mission chair Sofía Macher said "nothing has changed" structurally even as individual freedoms have expanded. The same day, an Amnesty International representative told the UN Human Rights Council that between 328 and 400 people remain jailed on political grounds, citing continuing arbitrary detentions, torture and forced disappearances.Runrun.es · Bloomberg Línea · Efecto Cocuyo
Venezuela's telecom regulator Conatel ordered internet providers on September 17 to restore access to more than a dozen previously blocked news sites, including Efecto Cocuyo, El Pitazo, Runrun.es and the EFE wire service, at the request of a body created by Delcy Rodríguez. Digital-rights monitor VE Sin Filtro counted 17 domains restored simultaneously with that request, while Espacio Público, using Open Observatory of Network Interference data, called the move "real" but "not universal": more than 200 domains remain blocked nationally, over 60 of them news outlets, including El Nacional itself, which reported this finding while still inaccessible inside Venezuela. Tal Cual's director said the outlet lost 60% of its traffic during a block dating to July 2024 and voiced skepticism the reversal will be complete.El Nacional · La Croix
The White House removed Venezuela from its list of major drug-transit and producing countries for fiscal year 2027, with President Trump crediting "positive steps taken under interim President Delcy Rodríguez" — a US government characterization, not an independent drug-flow assessment. The same week, Venezuela's military deployed aircraft over the Bauxilum bauxite complex in Bolívar state after reports that Colombian ELN guerrilla cells were kidnapping youths, displacing communities and killing Indigenous leaders in the area; a former opposition deputy alleged, without independent confirmation, that other criminal factions have taken over the zone once run by slain Tren de Aragua leader "Niño Guerrero" with the acquiescence of military authorities.InSight Crime · Al Jazeera · Infobae
Venezuela signed a wave of US-linked resource deals this week at the G20 energy ministerial in Houston, all falling under Treasury sanctions carve-outs OFAC issued August 27 covering Venezuelan minerals, gold and PDVSA transactions. Continental Resources signed a memorandum with state oil company PDVSA to develop the Ayacucho 2 block, an estimated 30 billion barrels in place, seeking full operating control with output possible within 18 months. New York-based Heeney Capital, partnered with commodities trader Mercuria, separately signed a 30-year deal for gold-mining and export rights at the Choco mine in El Callao, an initial investment of up to $1 billion, while state producer Venalum prepared its first US-bound aluminum shipment in years, about 15,000 metric tons. Government-aligned outlet teleSUR characterized Venezuela's G20 participation as reaffirming its position "as one of the world's leading energy powers," a government framing not independently verified; Delcy Rodríguez separately signed a 25-year, fully privately financed deal with GeoPark for heavy-crude output at the Bare field.Oil & Gas Journal · Efecto Cocuyo · Bloomberg Línea · teleSUR (government-aligned)
Alex Saab, a Colombian-born financier and Maduro-era aide, pleaded guilty to money laundering in a Miami federal court, agreeing to forfeit $195 million and cooperate with US prosecutors, raising the possibility he could testify against Maduro at his own trial; FBI Director Kash Patel said Saab had "stole from the people of Venezuela." Separately, Cilia Flores, Maduro's wife, held since her January 3 capture, asked a New York court to move her to house arrest, citing chest pain, arrhythmia and a 24-pound weight loss; the judge set an October 8 hearing, with the Maduro-Flores trial expected to begin June 1, 2027.BBC News · Efecto Cocuyo
Interim President Delcy Rodríguez is expected to attend the UN General Assembly in New York next week, which would make her the first Venezuelan head of state at the annual gathering in nearly a decade; both President Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio have said they could meet her if the trip goes ahead, according to people familiar with the planning, though her office, the UN and the White House have not confirmed it.Bloomberg Línea · El Nacional

What's circulating — September 18, 2026

Political-prisoner and human-rights monitors, including Foro Penal and accounts aligned with Provea, are pointing to today's two developments together — Javier Oropeza's brief arrest and the UN mission's finding that repression remains "structurally" intact — as proof that liberalization gestures have not reached the security state. Weigh this framing against the dialogue and UN Fact-Finding Mission items in the Media Monitor before treating it as a confirmed policy shift.
Finance-watch accounts are circulating competing numbers on this week's resource-deal wave: government-aligned channels are repeating Delcy Rodríguez's promised $100 billion in investment and $200 billion in tax revenue from the oil concessions, while independent finance commentary is amplifying analysts who doubt the pace of execution after a decade of underinvestment in the sector. Check both figures against the oil and mining items in the Media Monitor rather than taking either at face value.
Violence- and crime-monitoring accounts are presenting the Bolívar mining-zone military deployment as evidence that criminal control of the region has changed hands rather than ended, pointing to reported ELN presence where a slain Tren de Aragua leader once operated. This claim is circulating mainly on opposition-aligned and crime-monitoring accounts and remains independently unconfirmed, so weigh it against the Media Monitor's security item.
US officials and Venezuela's chavista-aligned National Assembly account are framing the drug-list removal and new Treasury licenses as validation of Rodríguez's "positive steps," while opposition-aligned accounts are recirculating the same news as evidence Washington is normalizing ties with a government it does not fully recognize. Both framings describe the same facts covered in the Media Monitor's drug-delisting and resource-deal items, so the positioning, not the news itself, is what's new here.
