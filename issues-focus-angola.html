Continental watch · Country deep dive

Angola

Last updated: September 17, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. President João Lourenço is in the final stretch of a second term he cannot constitutionally extend, and the succession question now dominates Angolan politics more than any single policy dispute. The ruling MPLA holds its December 2026 congress without an incumbent president also seeking the top state job for the first time in over a decade, while Lourenço has told the party he intends to stay on as MPLA leader even after he leaves the presidency in 2027 — a "two-headed" model that would let him keep a hand on his successor's selection. Angola's oil-dependent economy is stabilising on paper (inflation fell from 28.2% in 2024 toward single digits through 2026, and Sonangol has cut its China-collateralised debt by more than half since 2020) even as production keeps sliding and the government has not disclosed the exact figures behind that recovery. A sweeping military and security reshuffle on September 5 was explicitly linked by reporting to "heightened political tension" ahead of the 2027 electoral cycle.

Politics

Lourenço's MPLA will hold its party congress in December 2026, the first since he took power in which an incumbent president is not simultaneously standing for state office. The party has said it will not change its statutes at that congress and will shrink its Central Committee by 14.5%, and Lourenço has separately signalled, according to Jornal de Angola as reported by Bloomberg, that he wants to keep the MPLA leadership after his presidential term ends in 2027 — separating the party chieftaincy from the presidency so he retains influence over the succession. Analysts point to two competing MPLA currents: officials Lourenço has promoted to the Politburo, and a bloc of dos Santos-era veterans, including Isabel dos Santos and General Higino Carneiro, both of whom were recently cleared or partly exonerated by the Supreme Court on corruption charges that had been used to sideline them. National Assembly Speaker Adão de Almeida, elected in November 2025, is widely read as Lourenço's preferred successor. Angola's next general election is due in 2027, though no date has yet been set.

The opposition UNITA, which came within seven points of the MPLA in the 2022 election and swept the capital, has faced an escalation in pressure: on August 8, security forces allegedly targeted a UNITA gathering in Uíge province, injuring 34 people, prompting a formal condemnation from Renew Europe, UNITA's partner group in the European Parliament, which demanded an investigation and warned of the stakes for assembly and political-participation rights ahead of 2027. Angola's government carried out a sweeping reshuffle of its military and security leadership on September 5 — retiring 26 generals and admirals for age limits across the armed forces, police, military justice and intelligence services — which regional reporting linked explicitly to the pre-election climate. Rights groups continue to track the aftermath of July 2025's fuel-subsidy protests, in which Human Rights Watch's latest count puts the death toll at 29, and roughly 200 people remain imprisoned over 2024's Cabinda autonomy protests.

Economy

Angola's macro picture is genuinely improving on the metrics the government controls most closely, and more ambiguous on the ones it does not. Inflation fell from 28.2% in 2024 to 12.4% by March 2026, with one Angolan bank forecasting single-digit inflation by August — the first such reading in over two decades — and the central bank cut its policy rate for a second time this year on July 14. The kwanza has been unusually stable against the dollar through 2026, though the IMF's own May 2026 Article IV statement explicitly urged "improved exchange rate flexibility," suggesting the Fund sees that stability as partly managed rather than fully market-driven. Oil production, Angola's overwhelming revenue source since it left OPEC in December 2023, fell below one million barrels a day for the first time in mid-2025 before a partial recovery; the government still calls declining output and prices "the government's biggest challenge." Sonangol's oil-collateralised debt to China has fallen from a 2020 peak of $16.3 billion to $6.83 billion by June 2026, a reduction the state oil company has achieved mainly through natural loan amortisation rather than accelerated repayment, and it is now negotiating a $4.8 billion Chinese loan for a new Atlantic-coast refinery that, notably, would not be collateralised by oil — a first since 2017. The IMF has no active lending arrangement with Angola; it monitors the country instead under a Post-Financing Assessment following the conclusion of Angola's last Extended Fund Facility.

Foreign policy

Angola's external relationships increasingly track its effort to diversify away from a China-and-oil-only model without breaking either relationship. The Lobito Corridor — the US-backed rail link moving copper and cobalt from the DRC and Zambia to Angola's Atlantic port at Lobito — remains the centrepiece of the US relationship and was cited by an American Africa envoy as "a model" for the wider US-Africa partnership; Angola also featured in a February 2026 US Critical Minerals Ministerial as a supply-chain link. Portugal's Vice President-level contacts with Angola continue on cooperation, even as the Portuguese broadcaster RTP's expulsion from a presidential event in 2025 sits as an unresolved friction point beneath the official warmth. On the continent, Angola's most consequential recent role was chairing the African Union through the 2025-26 cycle, during which Lourenço pushed for a ceasefire and verification mechanism in eastern DRC alongside Presidents Tshisekedi, Gnassingbé and Olusegun Obasanjo; Angola handed the AU chairmanship to Burundi in February 2026, and by this September its DRC-mediation role, if any, is that of a regional actor rather than an active AU chair.

What to watch

Two dates anchor the calendar: the MPLA's December congress, where Lourenço's bid to keep the party leadership after 2027 will be tested against the dos Santos-era faction and against Adão de Almeida's rising profile; and the still-undated 2027 general election itself, for which MPLA candidate selection is the single largest open variable. Watch also for whether UNITA's Adalberto Costa Júnior faces further restriction on assembly and campaigning after the August Uíge incident, and whether Sonangol's Chinese refinery financing closes on its reported non-collateralised terms — a genuine departure from Angola's post-2004 borrowing pattern if it holds.

Today's cross-source snapshot — 17 September 2026

Angola's central bank cut its policy rate for the fifth time this year, from 15.75% to 14.75%, and lowered the local-currency reserve requirement from 17.5% to 16.5%. The Monetary Policy Committee of the Banco Nacional de Angola (BNA) attributed the move to progress in the inflation trajectory rather than to growth concerns.Expansão · Novo Jornal
Angola's goods trade turned over $34.6 billion in the first half of 2026, up 127% year on year, according to central bank data. Crude oil, sold at an average $91.9 a barrel versus $71.3 a year earlier, generated 95% of export revenue, while non-oil, non-mineral exports held at just 2% of the total.Expansão
Angola's state development bank, BDA, moved 261.92 billion kwanzas of non-performing loans off its books, transferring 434 debtors to Recredit, the state's dedicated bad-debt vehicle. Minister of State for Economic Coordination José de Lima Massano oversaw the September 15 agreement, which BDA says will cut its bad-loan ratio by roughly 27 points and strengthen its standing with the BNA.Novo Jornal
Angola marked National Heroes Day around President Agostinho Neto, and opposition parties UNITA and FNLA said the commemoration excludes their own founding figures. UNITA's Ernesto Mulato said the country "has to have many heroes," while FNLA's Ngola Kabangu called the holiday "exclusivist" and urged genuine reconciliation. The ruling MPLA did not respond to the criticism in the reporting reviewed.Novo Jornal
Angola widened its push to diversify investment beyond oil this week. The World Bank moved a $300 million "AgriConnect" program for the Lobito and Malanje corridors into its pipeline, with board approval expected in January 2027, and Angola and Brazil separately signed a memorandum easing Brazilian investment in Angolan grain production. The World Bank program targets 700,000 new agricultural jobs and $2.2 billion in added value once implemented between 2027 and 2030.Expansão · Novo Jornal
Angola's Minister of Fisheries and Marine Resources, Carmen do Sacramento Neto dos Santos, called it "urgent" to formalize 116 of the country's 222 fish-landing sites, which currently operate outside state oversight and conflict with other coastal uses. The ministry is working with the UN Food and Agriculture Organisation on registration and data collection to bring the sites into the formal economy.AllAfrica
Angola used an Arab-African investment conference in Cairo to pitch itself as a future regional logistics hub, presenting opportunities in transport, energy, water and agriculture rather than oil. Angolan representatives framed the push as part of a broader strategy of economic diversification and regional connectivity, alongside fellow presenters Libya, Namibia, Zambia and Uganda.AllAfrica

What's circulating — 16 September 2026

Novo Jornal is amplifying UNITA leader Adalberto Costa Júnior’s public appeal for all Angolans to complete voter registration and identity verification as soon as possible, a mobilization message that should be checked against the National Electoral Commission’s own registration timeline and stated turnout targets.
Novo Jornal is tracking the MPLA’s internal candidate-validation process, reporting that cleared provincial secretaries – including Luís Nunes in Luanda – have opened campaigns explicitly framed as a counterweight to UNITA’s influence, a party-line narrative worth checking against the MPLA’s own official statements.
Expansão is circulating an interview alleging that Angola's tax authority (AGT) resists outside scrutiny because officials fear what oversight would uncover. AGT has not responded; check the claim against its reply when it comes.
