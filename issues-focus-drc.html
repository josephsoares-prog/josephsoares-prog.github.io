Democratic Republic of Congo

Last updated: September 18, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. Two negotiated peace tracks sit on paper, and neither has stopped the fighting. Kinshasa and Kigali signed a peace agreement in Washington on 27 June 2025 and reaffirmed it as the Washington Accords on 4 December 2025, alongside a separate US-DRC Strategic Partnership Agreement on critical minerals. In a parallel, Qatar-led track, the DRC government and the AFC/M23 armed group signed the Doha Framework on 15 November 2025. Talks in Switzerland from 17 to 21 August 2026 produced a roadmap with "sequenced steps and timelines" and a common method for reporting ceasefire violations. Yet Kinshasa outlets reported on 13 September 2026 that Rwanda accuses the Congolese government of financing armed groups, and that the peace framework's own implementation sits at only about 18.6% after thirteen months. The World Bank estimates the conflict has displaced roughly 5.3 million people.

The security picture

On the ground, the diplomatic paper and the military reality diverge. Critical Threats' Congo War Security Review for the first two weeks of September reports the AFC/M23 consolidating control in southern Masisi and pushing west toward Walikale for the first time, with fighting continuing along the RP1030 corridor through 11 September. Around the Minembwe highlands, pro-government forces and M23-allied Twirwaneho fighters clashed near Point Zéro, and Colombian private military contractors reportedly withdrew to Uvira on 6 September. In Walikale, the army and Wazalendo briefly retook the centre of Kibati after fighting on 13 September, then withdrew; AFC/M23 held it again by 15 September. No significant peace negotiations were reported during this fighting.

Politics

President Félix Tshisekedi is pushing a national dialogue that the mainstream opposition has so far rejected. The opposition publicly rejected his initial offer on 29 August 2026; by 13 September, Kinshasa outlets reported Tshisekedi had stood up an organizing committee regardless, and that both the National Assembly and the Senate were reconvening. The C64 opposition coalition marched on 15 September in Kinshasa, Mbandaka and other cities. Police used tear gas and made at least ten arrests; the opposition calls the detentions arbitrary, while a provincial police commander says only non-peaceful protesters were held. At least a dozen C64 members had already been arrested in Kindu, Maniema and Lubumbashi before the march.

Economy

The government adopted a 2027 budget of 57.5 trillion Congolese francs — about $24.8 billion, up 12% from 2026 — at its 12 September cabinet meeting. Prime Minister Judith Suminwa Tuluka tabled it in the National Assembly on 15 September. The DRC remains the pivot of the global cobalt market: the US Geological Survey put its 2025 output at 230,000 of the world's 310,000 tonnes, with roughly half of known reserves. Kinshasa suspended cobalt exports outright from February to October 2025 and has since moved to a quota system rather than an open ban, a structural fact that continues to shape battery-metal pricing and the competition between Washington's and Beijing's mining agreements with Kinshasa.

Public health

Two outbreaks run concurrently. The Bundibugyo-strain Ebola outbreak, for which no vaccine or treatment is approved, was declared a WHO Public Health Emergency of International Concern on 17 May 2026. The DRC's Ministry of Communications reported 6,041 confirmed cases and 2,911 deaths as of 31 August; Kinshasa outlets put the toll at roughly 6,800 cases and 3,310 deaths by 11–13 September, with new cases confirmed among displaced people at Djugu on 13 September and South Ubangi newly affected. Uganda has imposed a mandatory 21-day quarantine on students arriving from the DRC. This outbreak's case counts have been revised sharply before — treat any single day's figure as provisional. A separate cholera outbreak in Haut-Katanga's Pweto territory had produced 85 cases and 12 deaths over two weeks as of 11 September.

What to watch

Whether the C64 keeps marching after the 15 September arrests, and how security forces respond; the Geneva Joint Security Coordination Mechanism talks of 16–17 September, where Rwanda contests Kinshasa's July protocol with an FDLR faction; whether the national dialogue draws in any credible opposition or armed-group participation, or remains a Tshisekedi-only exercise; the Ebola case count over the next reporting cycle, given this outbreak's history of large revisions; and whether the Geneva roadmap's ceasefire-violation reporting mechanism produces any verified reduction in fighting on the Masisi-Walikale and Minembwe axes.

Today's cross-source snapshot — September 18, 2026

The National Assembly formally rejected AFC/M23 participation in President Tshisekedi's proposed national dialogue on September 17. Assembly President Aimé Boji Sangara said dialogue "cannot legitimize violence or reward those taking up arms against the Republic." Part of the opposition, including the C64 coalition, continues to demand a fully inclusive process covering all political and armed actors.Actualite.cd
The C64 opposition coalition said on September 17 it is adopting undefined "self-defense measures" against future disruption of its public activities. C64 — led by Martin Fayulu, Jean-Marc Kabund, Moïse Katumbi, Augustin Matata and Delly Sesanga — says one activist died and several were injured during its September 15 march against a feared third presidential term. A government spokesperson called the coalition's casualty claim "lies and manipulation." The National Human Rights Commission (CNDH) opened its own inquiry the same day into violence reported on three streets in Kinshasa's Limete district, condemning "all violence" without confirming a death and praising police professionalism during the march.Actualite.cd · 7sur7.cd
Ruling UDPS party secretary-general Augustin Kabuya told a party meeting on September 17 that discussion of a Tshisekedi successor is premature, saying one "cannot speak of a successor while the king still sits on his throne." The remark followed the September 15 C64 march against constitutional revision and a possible third presidential term. Kabuya's comments position the ruling party against the succession debate the opposition has put at the center of its case for a broader dialogue.MediaCongo.net
Fighting between AFC/M23 and a Wazalendo-FARDC alliance continued along the Masisi territory front in North Kivu between September 15 and 17, with village control changing hands multiple times. The Wazalendo-FARDC alliance retook Remeka, Katuunda, Miko and Kareta in the Ufamandu I grouping, according to 34th Military Region spokesperson Major Dieudonné Kasereka. Heavy and light weapons fire was reported through the night of September 16 around Burora Hill near Ngungu, as government-aligned forces pushed toward the Rubaya mining corridor. Critical Threats' most recent review, dated September 11, had already reported M23 pressing westward into previously uncontested territory near Walikale.Actualite.cd · Radio Okapi · Critical Threats
The World Health Organization said on September 16 that its Ebola response in DRC is showing progress but remains "far from over," with confirmed cases surpassing 7,000 as of September 11. WHO's Maria Van Kerkhove said "we have many, many months ahead of us," while Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus cited declining transmission in most of Ituri province alongside continued risk from population movement. In Lubero health zone, North Kivu, local health authorities recorded 16 confirmed cases and 11 deaths in the Munyakondo health area as of September 17, with four patients having abandoned treatment, according to zone health director Dr. Charles Kaviri.Al Jazeera · Actualite.cd
Kasaï provincial governor Chrispin Mukendi Bukasa declared a new mpox epidemic on September 16, affecting five health zones. Luebo territory recorded more than 30 suspected cases and four deaths in the Mukuandianga and Kambangoma health areas, with three additional suspected cases within one family in Tshikapa. The governor said treatment would be free at targeted health facilities and urged residents to follow WHO- and INSP-recommended preventive measures.7sur7.cd
Eight civilians held by ADF fighters were freed and reunited with their families on September 16 in Téturi, Mambasa territory, Ituri province. Roughly 30 other captives from the same group remain unaccounted for. The freed hostages had been held for more than a month in the Seseo forest and reported severe fatigue on release; Radio Okapi's report does not identify which force carried out the release.Radio Okapi

What's circulating — September 18, 2026

Accounts aligned with the DRC presidency (@Presidence_RDC), Communications Minister Patrick Muyaya and the government's @Com_mediasRDC channel are pushing the National Assembly's rejection of AFC/M23 dialogue participation as evidence Kinshasa's peace approach is principled, and are amplifying the government spokesperson's "lies and manipulation" characterization of C64's claim that a march participant died on September 15 — a claim the CNDH's new inquiry has not yet confirmed or denied.
Accounts aligned with Radio Okapi (@radiookapi) and Actualite.cd (@actualitecd) are circulating the CNDH's September 17 announcement of a probe into Limete march violence side by side with C64's unconfirmed death claim, framing the episode as a disputed-facts story pending the CNDH's own field report.
Accounts aligned with independent conflict trackers — the Congo Research Group/Ebuteli (@GEC_CRG/@ebuteli), the Kivu Security Tracker (@KivuSecurity) and Critical Threats (@criticalthreats) — are tracking village-by-village control changes around Ufamandu and Walikale in Masisi territory as evidence AFC/M23 is pressing toward the Rubaya mining corridor, a read consistent with Critical Threats' September 11 assessment and that sits alongside the Wazalendo-FARDC recapture narrative Congolese military sources have given national outlets.
Accounts aligned with MONUSCO's police component (@MonuscoPolice) are likely to present the September 16 release of eight ADF-held hostages in Mambasa as a security success story, a framing that runs ahead of Congolese reporting, which does not name the force that carried out the release and notes roughly 30 captives are still missing.
