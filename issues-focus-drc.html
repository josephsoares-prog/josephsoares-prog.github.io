Democratic Republic of Congo

Last updated: September 15, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. Two negotiated peace tracks sit on paper, and neither has stopped the fighting. Kinshasa and Kigali signed a peace agreement in Washington on 27 June 2025 and reaffirmed it as the Washington Accords on 4 December 2025, alongside a separate US-DRC Strategic Partnership Agreement on critical minerals. In a parallel, Qatar-led track, the DRC government and the AFC/M23 armed group signed the Doha Framework on 15 November 2025. Talks in Switzerland from 17 to 21 August 2026 produced a roadmap with "sequenced steps and timelines" and a common method for reporting ceasefire violations. Yet Kinshasa outlets reported on 13 September 2026 that Rwanda accuses the Congolese government of financing armed groups, and that the peace framework's own implementation sits at only about 18.6% after thirteen months. The World Bank estimates the conflict has displaced roughly 5.3 million people.

The security picture

On the ground, the diplomatic paper and the military reality diverge. Critical Threats' Congo War Security Review for the first two weeks of September reports the AFC/M23 consolidating control in southern Masisi and pushing west toward Walikale for the first time, with fighting continuing along the RP1030 corridor through 11 September. Around the Minembwe highlands, pro-government forces and M23-allied Twirwaneho fighters clashed near Point Zéro, and Colombian private military contractors reportedly withdrew to Uvira on 6 September. Unverified reporting alleges Tanzanian, Angolan and Belgian troops now support pro-government forces, and that Ukrainian operatives are assisting the AFC/M23 with drone operations. No significant peace negotiations were reported during this fighting.

Politics

President Félix Tshisekedi is pushing a national dialogue that the mainstream opposition has so far rejected. The opposition publicly rejected his initial offer on 29 August 2026; by 13 September, Kinshasa outlets reported Tshisekedi had stood up an organizing committee regardless, and that both the National Assembly and the Senate were reconvening. The C64 opposition coalition has called demonstrations for 15 September. The National Human Rights Commission has publicly warned about calls for participants to carry weapons, and at least a dozen C64 members were already arrested in Kindu, Maniema and Lubumbashi by 13 September, with the coalition saying it will proceed regardless.

Economy

The government adopted a 2027 budget of 57.5 trillion Congolese francs — about $24.8 billion, up 12% from 2026 — at its 12 September cabinet meeting. The DRC remains the pivot of the global cobalt market: the US Geological Survey put its 2025 output at 230,000 of the world's 310,000 tonnes, with roughly half of known reserves. Kinshasa suspended cobalt exports outright from February to October 2025 and has since moved to a quota system rather than an open ban, a structural fact that continues to shape battery-metal pricing and the competition between Washington's and Beijing's mining agreements with Kinshasa.

Public health

Two outbreaks run concurrently. The Bundibugyo-strain Ebola outbreak, for which no vaccine or treatment is approved, was declared a WHO Public Health Emergency of International Concern on 17 May 2026. The DRC's Ministry of Communications reported 6,041 confirmed cases and 2,911 deaths as of 31 August; Kinshasa outlets put the toll at roughly 6,800 cases and 3,310 deaths by 11–13 September, with new cases confirmed among displaced people at Djugu on 13 September and South Ubangi newly affected. Uganda has imposed a mandatory 21-day quarantine on students arriving from the DRC. This outbreak's case counts have been revised sharply before — treat any single day's figure as provisional. A separate cholera outbreak in Haut-Katanga's Pweto territory had produced 85 cases and 12 deaths over two weeks as of 11 September.

What to watch

The 15 September C64 demonstrations and how security forces handle them; whether the national dialogue draws in any credible opposition or armed-group participation, or remains a Tshisekedi-only exercise; the Ebola case count over the next reporting cycle, given this outbreak's history of large revisions; and whether the Geneva roadmap's ceasefire-violation reporting mechanism produces any verified reduction in fighting on the Masisi-Walikale and Minembwe axes.

What's circulating — 16 September 2026

Government spokesman Patrick Muyaya's channel is framing the national dialogue launched by the September 13 ordinance as a sovereign, Congo-based process rather than a foreign-brokered one. Check whether opposition parties actually join the organizing committee before treating that framing as settled.
Actualite.cd's live coverage of the September 15 C64 marches is amplifying opposition claims of arbitrary arrests alongside a police commander's competing account that only non-peaceful protesters were detained. The full arrest count and grounds for each detention still need independent confirmation.
Accounts tracking the Kivus, including Critical Threats and KivuSecurity, are recirculating an M23-aligned outlet's claim of roughly 470 FARDC deaths near Fizi in early September. Treat the figure as a contested claim, not a confirmed toll, pending independent verification.
Ebuteli's own channel is amplifying its September 8-9 hackathon findings on draft association and religion laws, positioning civic-freedom safeguards as a governance test to watch alongside the national dialogue. The bills' final wording still awaits parliamentary debate.
