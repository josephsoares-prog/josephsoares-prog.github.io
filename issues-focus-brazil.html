Regional watch · Country deep dive

Brazil

Last updated: September 17, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. Brazil enters the final three weeks of a presidential campaign with a dispute inside its own Supreme Court running alongside it. The first round falls on 4 October 2026, with a runoff, if needed, on 25 October. On 15 September the Court's full bench took up a Federal Police report on contacts between Justice Alexandre de Moraes and the former chief executive of the liquidated Banco Master. The vote stood at four to three when Justice Flávio Dino requested a review period of up to 90 days, which can push a decision past the 4 October first round. Macroeconomics is the calmer half of the picture. The Selic rate has been cut four times since March and sits at 14.00%, August consumer prices fell 0.32% month on month, twelve-month inflation is 4.22%, and unemployment is 5.3%. Politics is where the volatility is.

Politics

Voters elect a president, governors, 54 of 81 senators and all 513 federal deputies on 4 October. Candidate registration closed 15 August, campaigning opened 16 August, and free radio and television airtime for the first round runs 28 August to 1 October. Polling is contested and moving, which is the most important thing to understand about it: one survey fielded 1–3 September put the runoff at 46–44 one way, a second fielded 10–13 September put it at 42–40 the other way, and a third fielded 11–13 September put it at 47–46 back the first way. All three sit inside their stated margins, so none of them supports a directional claim. In Congress, the veto on the sentencing bill covering the 8 January 2023 cases was overridden on 30 April and the law promulgated on 8 May; five constitutional challenges to it are consolidated before Justice Moraes, who has suspended its application with no judgment date set.

Economy

Disinflation is doing the work. The monetary policy committee has cut the Selic from 15.00% to 14.00% across four meetings — 19 March, 30 April, 18 June and 6 August — and meets again on 15–16 September, with the central bank's own survey of market economists expecting 13.75%. August consumer prices fell 0.32% month on month, bringing twelve-month inflation to 4.22% from 4.72% in May. Growth is modest: GDP rose 0.5% in the second quarter and 2.0% year on year. The fiscal position is the constraint. Gross general government debt reached 82.56% of GDP in July. The 2027 budget bill went to Congress on 31 August with a headline primary surplus of R$18.6 billion; on the fiscal framework's adjusted basis it shows R$83.4 billion against a target worth R$73.2 billion, a cushion of R$10.1 billion. The real traded at 5.1696 to the dollar on 14 September.

Trade, energy and the external position

Washington applied two Section 301 measures in July 2026: a 25% Brazil-specific tariff effective 22 July, and a 12.5% forced-labour tariff applied to Brazil among 41 economies, stacking to 37.5% where both bite. Brazil's own trade ministry puts 23.1% of exports under the new tariffs and 52.7% untouched, with coffee, meat, aircraft and orange juice exempt. The effect is visible: exports to the United States fell 9.7% to US$24.105 billion in January–August, while China absorbed US$77.07 billion over the same months, 30.7% of Brazil's US$250.86 billion total. The EU–Mercosur interim trade agreement has applied provisionally since 1 May 2026, but the European Parliament's January referral to the Court of Justice suspends its consent for 16 to 18 months. Oil output hit 4.498 million barrels a day in July, with the pre-salt supplying 82.4% of national production. Brussels' suspension of Brazilian meat, honey, fish and egg imports, in force since 3 September, covers up to US$2 billion of annual trade.

What to watch

Justice Dino's review period on the Moraes case, and the 23 September session on a separate request to investigate Justice André Mendonça. The monetary policy decision on 15–16 September, with consensus at 13.75%. The 19 September point in the electoral calendar, from which candidates may not be detained except in flagrante. The end of first-round free airtime on 1 October and the vote itself on 4 October, polls open 8am to 5pm. The two remaining rate meetings of the year, 3–4 November and 8–9 December. COP31 in Antalya from 9 to 20 November under a Türkiye–Australia co-presidency, the first real test of what Belém's implementation agenda delivers. And the negotiation over the European import suspension, which is open-ended.

Today's cross-source snapshot — 17 September 2026

Brazil's central bank cut the Selic policy rate to 13.75% a year on September 16, its fifth consecutive reduction, after the national statistics agency IBGE reported an August deflation of -0.32%, the sharpest monthly price drop in four years. The bank's rate-setting committee voted unanimously and flagged the arrival of El Niño as a fresh risk to food prices in coming months. Annual inflation stood at 4.22% in the twelve months to August, down from 4.44% in July, according to IBGE data cited by the bank.Agência Brasil
An AtlasIntel/Bloomberg poll released September 17 puts Senator Flávio Bolsonaro at 47.2% against President Lula's 46.8% in a simulated presidential runoff, a gap the pollster describes as a technical tie. The survey of 5,018 voters, fielded September 11-16 with a one-point margin of error, shows Lula leading the first-round field at 44.1% to Bolsonaro's 41.7%. Lula's job approval stood at 45.4% positive against 53.6% negative in the same poll.Poder360
Supreme Court justice Gilmar Mendes formally protested the handling of the Banco Master case by fellow justice Luiz Fux on September 17, saying he received only 22 minutes' notice before a session called to ratify the removal of the Federal Police director. The dispute follows a rapid internal sequence: justice André Mendonça removed the director on September 8 over the Vorcaro investigation, and justice Flávio Dino reinstated him the next day. Retired Supreme Court justices separately asked the court to hold a public session on the underlying case, Operation Compliance Zero, which investigates former Banco Master owner Daniel Vorcaro over a multibillion-real fraud that led to the bank's collapse.Agência Brasil · JOTA
Brazil's Internet Steering Committee, CGI.br, published 46 guidelines for regulating social media platforms on September 17, covering content-moderation transparency, algorithmic accountability and competition rules scaled to platform size. Committee coordinator Renata Mielli said the framework is meant to give lawmakers, courts and regulators predictable benchmarks rather than to create binding law on its own.Agência Brasil
Brazil's national development bank, BNDES, opened enrollment on September 17 for a third phase of its Brasil Soberano credit line, worth R$22.6 billion, R$13.5 billion from the National Treasury and R$9.1 billion from BNDES itself, for companies affected by trade disruption. Eligible borrowers include exporters hit by US tariffs, strategic industrial sectors and firms trading with Gulf states, with interest rates ranging from 4.3% to 17.5% depending on company size and use of funds.Agência Brasil
The Indigenous Missionary Council, Cimi, found that 54% of the 272 bills before Congress affecting Indigenous peoples are unfavorable to their interests, in an analysis released September 15 covering 110 roll-call votes cast between January 2019 and June 2026. Cimi tallied 41,391 individual votes across those measures and found 67.6% cast against Indigenous-rights positions, against 31.6% in favor. Cimi published the findings on a new tracking platform it named Congresso Antindígena, a label that is the organization's own characterization rather than an official designation.Agência Brasil
A political analysis published by Americas Quarterly on September 17 frames Brazil's presidential race as centered on fallout from the Vorcaro banking scandal, noting that allegations linking Flávio Bolsonaro to former banker Daniel Vorcaro surfaced in May while allegations tying Vorcaro to Supreme Court justice Alexandre de Moraes surfaced later and affected Lula. The piece states that both camps deny wrongdoing and describes most polls as showing a tied race ahead of the first round on October 4 and a likely runoff on October 25.Americas Quarterly
