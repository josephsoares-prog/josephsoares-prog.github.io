Regional watch · Country deep dive

Brazil

Last updated: September 15, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. Brazil enters the final three weeks of a presidential campaign with a dispute inside its own Supreme Court running alongside it. The first round falls on 4 October 2026, with a runoff, if needed, on 25 October. The Court's full bench sits on 15 September to examine a Federal Police report on contacts between Justice Alexandre de Moraes and the former chief executive of the liquidated Banco Master; Chief Justice Edson Fachin took the case file on 12 September and ordered thousands of pages unsealed. Macroeconomics is the calmer half of the picture. The Selic rate has been cut four times since March and sits at 14.00%, August consumer prices fell 0.32% month on month, twelve-month inflation is 4.22%, and unemployment is 5.3%. Politics is where the volatility is.

Politics

Voters elect a president, governors, 54 of 81 senators and all 513 federal deputies on 4 October. Candidate registration closed 15 August, campaigning opened 16 August, and free radio and television airtime for the first round runs 28 August to 1 October. Polling is contested and moving, which is the most important thing to understand about it: one survey fielded 1–3 September put the runoff at 46–44 one way, a second fielded 10–13 September put it at 42–40 the other way, and a third fielded 11–13 September put it at 47–46 back the first way. All three sit inside their stated margins, so none of them supports a directional claim. In Congress, the veto on the sentencing bill covering the 8 January 2023 cases was overridden on 30 April and the law promulgated on 8 May; five constitutional challenges to it are consolidated before Justice Moraes, who has suspended its application with no judgment date set.

Economy

Disinflation is doing the work. The monetary policy committee has cut the Selic from 15.00% to 14.00% across four meetings — 19 March, 30 April, 18 June and 6 August — and meets again on 15–16 September, with the central bank's own survey of market economists expecting 13.75%. August consumer prices fell 0.32% month on month, bringing twelve-month inflation to 4.22% from 4.72% in May. Growth is modest: GDP rose 0.5% in the second quarter and 2.0% year on year. The fiscal position is the constraint. Gross general government debt reached 82.56% of GDP in July. The 2027 budget bill went to Congress on 31 August with a headline primary surplus of R$18.6 billion; on the fiscal framework's adjusted basis it shows R$83.4 billion against a target worth R$73.2 billion, a cushion of R$10.1 billion. The real traded at 5.1696 to the dollar on 14 September.

Trade, energy and the external position

Washington applied two Section 301 measures in July 2026: a 25% Brazil-specific tariff effective 22 July, and a 12.5% forced-labour tariff applied to Brazil among 41 economies, stacking to 37.5% where both bite. Brazil's own trade ministry puts 23.1% of exports under the new tariffs and 52.7% untouched, with coffee, meat, aircraft and orange juice exempt. The effect is visible: exports to the United States fell 9.7% to US$24.105 billion in January–August, while China absorbed US$77.07 billion over the same months, 30.7% of Brazil's US$250.86 billion total. The EU–Mercosur interim trade agreement has applied provisionally since 1 May 2026, but the European Parliament's January referral to the Court of Justice suspends its consent for 16 to 18 months. Oil output hit 4.498 million barrels a day in July, with the pre-salt supplying 82.4% of national production. Brussels' suspension of Brazilian meat, honey, fish and egg imports, in force since 3 September, covers up to US$2 billion of annual trade.

What to watch

The Supreme Court's full bench on 15 September, and a second petition listed for 23 September. The monetary policy decision on 15–16 September, with consensus at 13.75%. The 19 September point in the electoral calendar, from which candidates may not be detained except in flagrante. The end of first-round free airtime on 1 October and the vote itself on 4 October, polls open 8am to 5pm. The two remaining rate meetings of the year, 3–4 November and 8–9 December. COP31 in Antalya from 9 to 20 November under a Türkiye–Australia co-presidency, the first real test of what Belém's implementation agenda delivers. And the negotiation over the European import suspension, which is open-ended.

Today's cross-source snapshot — 15 September 2026

Brazil's Supreme Court decides Tuesday whether to open a first-ever criminal investigation into a sitting justice, Alexandre de Moraes, over alleged contacts with a jailed banker. Chief Justice Luiz Edson Fachin set the session's procedure after two prior sittings were canceled amid the dispute among justices. The trigger was Justice André Mendonça's September 1 release of a federal police report describing messages between Moraes and Banco Master founder Daniel Vorcaro.Agência Brasil · Al Jazeera
The Banco Master fraud collapse, which Agência Pública ties to roughly R$52 billion in losses to Brazil's deposit-guarantee fund, has drawn senators, judges and the Bolsonaro family into overlapping investigations three weeks before Brazil's presidential vote. Lawmakers aligned with the Bolsonaro family have filed 54 impeachment requests against Moraes since the police report surfaced, per Agência Pública's reconstruction. Separately, police allege Vorcaro moved about $12 million to a US-based fund tied to Eduardo Bolsonaro meant to finance a film about his father, a transfer Eduardo says was intended to shield the money from judicial interference, Al Jazeera reports.Agência Pública · Al Jazeera
Two pollsters give conflicting reads on Brazil's presidential runoff three weeks before the October 4 election. Datafolha, commissioned by Globo and Folha, puts President Lula at 46% against Flávio Bolsonaro's 44%, while PoderData/Aya shows Bolsonaro ahead 45% to 44%; both gaps sit inside each survey's margin of error. Poder360, which published both surveys, notes the PoderData finding marked the first time since May that Bolsonaro numerically led Lula in second-round matchups.Poder360 · Poder360
Brazil and the United States expect to meet face-to-face around the G20 summit, on September 30 or October 1, to negotiate the tariff dispute that cut Brazilian exports to the US by 13% in the first half of 2026, to $17.4 billion. Trade Minister Márcio Elias Rosa said on September 14 that Brazil continues "exchanging documents" with the US Trade Representative's office and does not expect a full resolution before Brazil's October elections. The dispute layers a 25% tariff imposed in July over alleged unfair trade practices with a further 12.5% tied to labor and deforestation complaints.Poder360
Brazilian retail sales fell 0.8% in July from June, the first monthly drop after two months of growth, according to IBGE's Monthly Commerce Survey (PMC). Five of eight tracked retail segments contracted, led by furniture and appliances, down 4.9%. Sales still rose 1.2% year-on-year and are up 1.8% for 2026 through July, per the same IBGE data.Poder360
Lula will pause his re-election campaign to open the United Nations General Assembly's general debate in New York on September 20-22, continuing Brazil's tradition of delivering its opening address. The trip, two weeks before Brazil's October 4 vote, will run with a reduced delegation likely including Foreign Minister Mauro Vieira. Officials expect possible bilateral contact with President Trump on the tariff dispute during the visit, Infobae reports.Infobae

What's circulating — 15 September 2026

Bolsonarista lawmakers and supporters are amplifying impeachment messaging against Justice Moraes on X ahead of Tuesday's Supreme Court vote, framing the Banco Master revelations as proof of judicial bias — a claim the Court's own proceedings have not resolved and should be read as a position, not a finding.
Eduardo Bolsonaro, posting from the United States, has branded the US tariffs on Brazil the "Tarifa-Moraes," attributing Washington's trade measures to the justice's conduct rather than the US government's stated rationale — a causal claim circulating online that outlets have not corroborated.
The PT is training and deploying a network of online "spokespeople" to counter right-wing narratives on social media through the campaign's final weeks, an organized amplification effort whose claims should be checked independently rather than taken as settled fact.
Fact-checkers at Agência Lupa have flagged PL-distributed campaign content containing false claims and personal attacks on opponents circulating on social platforms; the volume of shares reflects reach, not accuracy, and should be weighed accordingly.
