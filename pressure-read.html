The Pressure Read

You already know the decision.
You cannot see it clearly.

Send me one decision. In five business days you get it back in writing — structured, pressure-tested, and with the thing you are avoiding named out loud.

Send me the decision — US$950

A written read on one high-stakes decision.

Not a call. Not a relationship. Not an engagement with a scoping phase.

You write out the situation. I read it against the five SPARK pillars — Strategic Alignment, People and Talent, Adaptive Innovation and AI, Relationship and Communication, and Key Decision-Making and Execution. Five business days later a document lands in your inbox.

You can act on it the same afternoon.

Why in writing
Advice given out loud disappears.

You nod, you feel clearer, and a week later you cannot reconstruct the reasoning — only the conclusion.

A document does not do that. You can reread it. You can disagree with it in the margins. You can hand it to your CFO, or your co-founder, or the board member who is going to push back hardest, and let them argue with the reasoning rather than with you.

That is the difference between advice and an asset.

What arrives
Fifteen to twenty pages.
One

The decision, restated

In my words, not yours. Most of the value is here. If I have understood your situation differently than you described it, that gap is the finding.

Two

Where the pressure actually is

Your five pillars scored, with the one that is carrying the load named.

Three

The case against your instinct

Argued properly, as your sharpest critic would argue it.

Four

Three options, and what each costs

Including the option you have not considered.

Five

What to do on Monday

Specific. Not “align stakeholders.”

Written in plain language, because you are going to read it on a plane.

Who this is for

Founders and chief executives holding a decision that is too consequential for the people around them to be neutral about.

A carve-out. A co-founder exit. A market you are not sure you should enter. A senior hire you already know is wrong. A board that has stopped agreeing with each other.

It works when the decision is real and the clock is running.

Who this is not for

If you want a thinking partner over six months, this is the wrong thing. That is the Sprint, and I will tell you so.

If you want someone to confirm what you have already decided, this is the wrong thing entirely. You will not enjoy the document.

Who is reading it

I served in the Prime Minister’s Office during the 2008 financial crisis, and I was Chief of Staff in the Senate of Canada during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Both jobs were the same job: decisions of consequence, made with incomplete information, on a clock, in rooms where everyone present had a position to protect.

I am not going to tell you about those decisions — what happens in those rooms stays in them, permanently. What transfers is the pattern, and the pattern is what you are buying.

MBA, PMP, Adm.A., ACC. Published in Forbes and Newsweek.

Questions people ask

Can we just talk instead?

No. That is deliberate, not a scheduling problem. A conversation produces a feeling of clarity. A document produces clarity you still have next month.

What if my situation is confidential?

Assume it is. Nothing you send is ever used as content. I hold professional obligations as an Adm.A. under Québec’s professional code, and confidentiality under it is absolute.

Five days seems fast.

It is fast because the method is fixed. I am not inventing an approach for your situation. I am running a documented framework against it.

What if I want more afterwards?

The 30-Day SPARK Sprint exists for that, and I will mention it once at the end of your document. Once.
