Biography

About Joseph Soares

Primary press headshot. Free for editorial use with credit “Courtesy of Joseph Soares.” For additional formats or high-resolution files, email info@josephsoares.com.

Joseph Soares is a strategic advisor, author, and commentator on crisis leadership, institutional decision-making, and global business risk. He served in the Prime Minister’s Office of Canada during the 2008 financial crisis and as Chief of Staff in the Senate of Canada through the pandemic — two of the highest-pressure rooms in the country, in two of its hardest years. His commentary has been published in Forbes and Newsweek. His first book, Leadership Under Fire: Forged in the Rooms of Power, arrives October 6, 2026. He holds an MBA, PMP, Adm.A., and ACC, and works in English, French, and Portuguese. He is based in the Ottawa–Gatineau region of Canada.

Joseph Soares is a strategic advisor, author, and commentator who has spent his career at the intersection of government, business, and high-stakes decision-making. He brings institutional-grade credibility earned in the rooms where the pressure was real and the stakes were national.

He served in the Prime Minister’s Office of Canada during the 2008 financial crisis and later as Chief of Staff in the Senate of Canada through the pandemic — leadership at the centre of Canadian government through two national emergencies. That experience shapes how he reads crisis, institutions, and the decisions leaders cannot afford to get wrong.

His analysis and commentary on leadership, geopolitics, and global business risk have been published in Forbes and Newsweek. He is the host of Corridor Intelligence, a program that examines how power, capital, and resources actually move — analytical, not adversarial. He is the creator of the SPARK framework, a method for building organizations that hold up under pressure, taught in full in his first book.

That book, Leadership Under Fire: Forged in the Rooms of Power, is published October 6, 2026 — a field manual for the decisions that don’t allow a second draft.

Joseph holds an MBA, the Project Management Professional (PMP) certification, the Chartered Administrator (Adm.A.) designation, and the Associate Certified Coach (ACC) credential. He is trilingual in English, French, and Portuguese, and gives interviews and keynotes in all three languages. He is of Azorean Portuguese heritage, with roots in Fenais da Ajuda, São Miguel, and is based in the Ottawa–Gatineau region of Canada — available for interviews and commentary across North America, Europe, and the Lusophone world.

Interview Preparation

Suggested Questions

Starting points for journalists, podcast hosts, and moderators. Joseph is comfortable going off-script.

You served in the Prime Minister’s Office during a financial crisis and as Chief of Staff in the Senate through a pandemic. What did that teach you about leadership under real pressure?

+
The quality of a decision is determined less by the information available than by the decision structure around it. In a crisis you never have perfect information — no one does. What matters is the speed and clarity of the response, the alignment of the people in the room, and the discipline to adjust course as new information arrives. Most organizations fail in crises not because they lack intelligence, but because they lack a decision framework that works under pressure. I keep the specifics of those rooms confidential — but the pattern is what I teach.

Your book, Leadership Under Fire, comes out October 6. What is it, and who is it for?

+
It’s a field manual for the decisions that don’t allow a second draft — the calls leaders can least afford to make alone. It sets out the SPARK framework: the characteristics that let organizations hold up under pressure, and the ones whose absence makes them fail. It’s written for founders, executives, and anyone who has built something worth protecting and wants a structured way to test whether they’re actually prepared for disruption, or just hoping it doesn’t come.

What are the biggest geopolitical risks business leaders should be watching right now?

+
Three that I’d flag. First, energy and shipping chokepoints — escalation around the Strait of Hormuz or the Red Sea moves directly into energy prices and supply-chain stability. Second, the restructuring of global trade: the world is drifting from a rules-based order toward a power-based one, and businesses that haven’t stress-tested their supply chains and market dependencies are exposed. Third, the speed of AI adoption opening a competence gap between organizations that integrate it strategically and those that treat it as a technology problem. Each is a strategic risk, not a headline — and the leaders who thrive are deciding about them now.

What is the SPARK framework?

+
SPARK is the method at the core of the book: five characteristics shared by organizations that perform under pressure, and typically missing in the ones that collapse. It isn’t a theory — it’s a synthesis of what I’ve seen work in the rooms where the stakes were highest, turned into a structured way for leaders to evaluate whether they’re genuinely prepared for disruption.

What’s the single biggest mistake leaders make in a crisis?

+
Waiting. The riskiest move in a crisis is doing nothing. When pressure hits, the instinct is to gather more information and wait for conditions to clarify — but conditions don’t clarify during a crisis, they deteriorate. The leaders who navigate crises successfully accept imperfect information, make a decision, communicate it clearly, and adjust in real time. Speed and clarity beat perfection. That’s not recklessness — it’s discipline.

You’re trilingual with Azorean heritage. How does that shape your perspective?

+
Growing up between languages and cultures gives you an instinct for reading a room — understanding not just what people say, but what they mean and what they’re not saying. Practically, fluency in English, French, and Portuguese opens North American, European, and Lusophone conversations that most commentators can’t reach. I interview and speak in all three.
