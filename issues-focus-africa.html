Continental watch

Africa

Last updated: September 11, 2026 · Reviewed and improved in place, not re-published as a new post

Where things stand. The African Union counts 55 member states, including the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic. Colonial-era languages and currencies still shape how these states trade and align. The CFA franc zone covers 14 states: eight in the West African Economic and Monetary Union and six in the Central African CEMAC. Language and currency do not line up neatly. Guinea-Bissau and Equatorial Guinea use the CFA franc, yet both also belong to the Community of Portuguese Language Countries, alongside Angola, Cabo Verde, Mozambique and São Tomé and Príncipe. Both CFA francs keep a fixed euro parity, and the French Treasury commits to supply euros when needed. The Central African central bank still deposits part of its reserves in an operations account at the French Treasury. The West African central bank has had no such account since January 2020. On 19 July 2026, ECOWAS leaders restated their "firm commitment to the launch of the ECO in 2027". Only states that meet the convergence criteria will join the first group.

The decision point

The sharpest break in West Africa now runs between ECOWAS and the Confederation of Sahel States (AES). Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger adopted the treaty creating the confederation in Niamey on 6 July 2024. Their withdrawal from ECOWAS took effect on 29 January 2025. The three states inaugurated a joint investment bank, the BCID-AES, in Bamako in December 2025. They launched a unified military force headquartered in Niamey the same month and enlarged it from 5,000 to 6,000 troops in February 2026. In June 2026 they deposited notices of withdrawal from the International Criminal Court's Rome Statute; each withdrawal takes effect one year after notice. On 24 August 2026 the confederation's 45-member parliament opened its first session in Niamey. ECOWAS has chosen to keep talking. Its July communiqué extended its chief negotiator's mandate to December 2026 and said talks with the three states will proceed "exclusively as a unified block". It also directed the President of the ECOWAS Commission to work with the AES towards "a pragmatic security cooperation mechanism". ECOWAS named General Birame Diop President of its Commission from 1 September 2026. The open test is whether the AES holds together under armed pressure. In Mali's 2026 fighting, the other two members "have failed to deploy forces under the group's regional defense agreement."

Who holds the leverage

Outside powers are competing directly for what lies under African ground. The Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) mined 230,000 of the world's 310,000 tonnes of cobalt in 2025 and holds half of known reserves, according to the US Geological Survey. On 4 December 2025, Washington signed a Strategic Partnership Agreement with Kinshasa and a separate economic framework with Kigali, alongside the DRC-Rwanda Washington Accords. In January 2026, Congolese lawyers asked the DRC's Constitutional Court to rule that the US agreement needed approval by parliament or referendum. At a US Critical Minerals Ministerial in February 2026, Guinea and Morocco were among the countries that signed new bilateral critical minerals frameworks or memoranda of understanding with the United States. Beijing keeps its own strong position. China and the DRC reached a new mining agreement in March 2026, and China remains the DRC's biggest creditor. Armed groups also hold leverage on the ground. In Mali, the al-Qaeda-linked group JNIM declared a fuel blockade on 3 September 2025. More than 300 fuel tankers have since been destroyed, and fuel now reaches Bamako under military escort.

What to watch

Demographics remain the continent's largest long-term story. Africa's population grew from 283 million in 1960 to more than 1.5 billion in 2024. A 2024 analysis by staff of the UN Economic Commission for Africa (ECA) projects 2.5 billion by 2050, or 28% of the world's people, up from 10% in 1960. The World Bank expects more than 620 million people to enter Africa's labour force by 2050. The same ECA analysis states plainly that "a demographic dividend is by no means guaranteed". Each year more than 10 million young people enter the African labour market, but current growth creates only about 3 million formal jobs. Of 304 million young workers, 104 million live in extremely poor households. The World Bank projects 4.1% growth for Sub-Saharan Africa in 2026. Key dates: ECOWAS's negotiating mandate with the AES runs to December 2026. Angola's ruling party holds its congress in December 2026. The Sahel states' ICC withdrawals take effect in June 2027. An outbreak of Bundibugyo-strain Ebola, declared in the DRC on 15 May 2026, had killed more than 2,000 people by 11 August, and no approved vaccine exists for this strain.

Country watch: twelve to track

Côte d'Ivoire. Alassane Ouattara won a fourth term in October 2025 with 89.77% of the vote. Two leading rivals were excluded: Laurent Gbagbo was barred over a criminal conviction, and Tidjane Thiam was disqualified for having acquired French citizenship. The World Bank puts 2025 growth at 6.3% and projects 5.8% for 2026 if Middle East tensions persist. In his August 2026 independence-day address, Ouattara linked his development goals to the 2026–2030 National Development Plan. France has withdrawn its troops or handed over its military bases in Côte d'Ivoire, as it has in Chad, Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger. Abidjan keeps working ties with Bamako: in August 2026, Ivorian and Malian experts agreed a draft treaty fixing their shared border. Both states are expected to sign it before the end of 2026.

Nigeria. Bola Tinubu's government faces pressure from Washington alongside gains at home. In late 2025 the United States named Nigeria a "Country of Particular Concern" for religious freedom. Defence spending rose from ₦2.98 trillion in 2023 to ₦4.91 trillion in 2025. The World Bank puts 2025 growth at 4%. Nigeria's upstream regulator, the NUPRC, reported crude output of 1.56 million barrels a day in June 2026, the highest since April 2020. Nigeria told OPEC that output rose by 35,000 barrels a day to 1.573 million barrels a day in August 2026.
