Colombia

Last updated: September 14, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. Colombia is five weeks into a new government and one month into recovery from a major earthquake. Abelardo de la Espriella took the oath of office in Cali on 7 August 2026, having won the 21 June runoff narrowly. Three days after the inauguration, a magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck southeast of San José del Palmar, Chocó, at 108 km depth; the national disaster agency counted 331 dead and 4,439 injured. The new government has moved fast on two fronts at once: it has dismantled the negotiated-peace architecture it inherited, and it has deepened the security and resources relationship with Washington, signing memoranda on critical minerals and civil nuclear cooperation with Secretary of State Marco Rubio in Barranquilla on 8 September. Congress, which the government does not control, is the constraint on both.

The security picture

The negotiating tables are closed. Resolutions 390 and 391 of 2026, issued 9 September, revoked the designated delegations for talks with the ELN and with the Ejército Gaitanista de Colombia; earlier decisions had already terminated the tables with the EMBF, the Coordinadora Nacional Ejército Bolivariano and the ACSN. On 13 September the Attorney General's office reactivated three suspended capture orders against Clan del Golfo commanders. The underlying numbers cut both ways. UNODC measured 261,000 hectares of coca at the end of 2024, up 3.5% year on year, with the Pacific region up 14% and Catatumbo up 11%; cocaine seizures rose 19% to 889,201 kg while forced manual eradication fell 54% to 9,403 hectares. Indepaz, counting independently, recorded 89 massacres and 336 victims to 11 September 2026, against 78 and 256 for all of 2025.

Politics

Congress is fragmented and the president does not command it. In the 8 March legislative elections the Pacto Histórico took the largest Senate bloc at 25 seats, ahead of Centro Democrático with 17; allocations below the top two are reported inconsistently and should be treated as contested. When Congress installed on 20 July, Honorio Henríquez of Centro Democrático won the Senate presidency 56 to 45, with Pacto Histórico votes deciding it against the incoming government's preference — an early signal that floor arithmetic, not the presidency, sets the agenda. Institutional friction is now routine: on 14 September the Supreme Court's Special Instruction Chamber opened a preliminary inquiry into the financing of Iván Cepeda's October 2025 primary campaign, after the National Electoral Council had cleared the same contributions in January. Two state bodies, two working positions, no final ruling.

Economy

Growth has accelerated while inflation has moved the wrong way. The statistics agency recorded GDP growth of 3.5% year on year in the second quarter and 2.9% for the first half; annual inflation reached 6.24% in August, against 5.10% a year earlier. Banco de la República holds its policy rate at 12.0%, in force since 1 July after a four-to-three vote to hold on 31 July, and meets again on 30 September. Unemployment was 8.1% in July. Oil output fell 2.46% year on year in July to 727,920 barrels a day and gas fell 8.36%, while exports rose 5.9% in July with fuels and extractives at 40.0% of the total. Two external pressures sit on top of that: a 12.5% US Section 301 tariff on Colombian goods ordered 23 July over forced-labour enforcement, and a current account deficit running at 3.5% of quarterly GDP, cushioned by US$4,131 million in remittances.

What to watch

The 2027 budget: Congress's joint economic committees must fix the ceiling by 15 September and approve the bill by 20 October, against an independent fiscal committee projecting a primary deficit of 4.5% of GDP, a total deficit near 10% and net debt near 67.3%. The US Presidential Determination on major drug transit countries for fiscal 2027, which had not appeared as of 14 September — last year's designated Colombia as having failed demonstrably while granting a national-interest waiver. The aerial spraying pilot authorised on 18 August: seven days, 1,000 hectares, glufosinate ammonium rather than glyphosate, with technical reports due within 60 days, and environmental licensing and prior consultation with ethnic communities still required before anything wider. Banco de la República's 30 September rate decision. And El Niño through the 2026–27 season, which the fiscal committee flags as a risk to reduced energy and gas subsidy funding.

Today's cross-source snapshot — 15 September 2026

Colombia's government under President Abelardo de la Espriella has ended negotiation tables with three armed groups, closing the "Total Peace" framework pursued by predecessor Gustavo Petro. The government also reversed a decade-long suspension on aerial glyphosate spraying for coca eradication. InSight Crime notes that glyphosate spraying alone has not resolved Colombia's coca-replanting problem in the past, and warns the shift toward enforcement risks pushing fragmented armed factions back into open conflict rather than into demobilization.InSight Crime · The City Paper Bogotá
The United States was set to decide on September 15 whether to recertify Colombia as a cooperating partner in counter-narcotics enforcement, a designation tied to continued security assistance. El Tiempo reports Washington is weighing whether the De la Espriella government's early anti-narcotics measures will satisfy the Trump administration. Bloomberg Línea frames the decision as running against a narrowing timeline for Colombia to show results before facing a harsher designation.El Tiempo · Bloomberg Línea
Armed men identified as ELN fighters used heavy machinery to destroy the police station in Betoyes, Arauca, on September 15, according to Infobae. El Tiempo situates the attack within a broader pattern of ELN actions across Arauca and Chocó coinciding with Colombia's change of national government.Infobae · El Tiempo
Colombia's Prosecutor General's Office formally charged three brothers of alias "Iván Mordisco," top commander of the FARC-EMC dissident faction, with homicide, kidnapping and torture over a killing in El Peñón, Cundinamarca. Semana and El Espectador report the brothers will face trial on all three charges.Semana · El Espectador
Ecopetrol's board sought the resignation of interim president Juan Carlos Hurtado after a leaked audio recording of his conversations with Catalan businessman Manel Grau became public, and Hurtado stepped down on September 14. Infobae reports board member Hildebrando Vélez resigned as well, and El Colombiano names Camilo Barco as Hurtado's likely successor at the state-controlled oil company.Infobae · El Colombiano
President De la Espriella removed DANE director Ricardo Valencia and named Juan Manuel Alvarado in his place. La Silla Vacía reports the move followed Valencia's public statement that former president Petro did not manipulate government statistics. Cambio notes the timing coincides with ongoing negotiations over the 2027 national budget.La Silla Vacía · Cambio
US Secretary of State Marco Rubio toured Ecuador, Colombia and Peru in early September, and Al Jazeera reports the trip followed recent right-wing electoral victories across the region. The City Paper Bogotá describes the visit as opening a new phase of US-Colombia security cooperation under the "Shield of the Americas" framework, which Peru also joined this month.Al Jazeera · The City Paper Bogotá

What's circulating — 14 September 2026

The inherited-disaster frame is being pushed from two capitals at once, in Bogotá and in Washington. It is a position, not a finding — check it against the fiscal committee's own sequence, which attributes most of the 2027 budget's growth to a 54.7% rise in debt service and to spending the previous bill had not fully recognised.
“Total peace has failed” is circulating as settled. It is an argument with an interested author. The competing position, worth watching as it plays out, is that dismantling the tables may fragment armed groups further rather than fold them into the state.
The return of aerial spraying is being amplified as a decision already taken. It is not one yet: the government's own framing of the September pilot says it is not equivalent to resumption or definitive authorisation, and uses glufosinate ammonium rather than glyphosate. Watch whether environmental licensing and prior consultation actually advance.
Cocaine production figures are circulating with more confidence than the underlying data supports. UNODC and the Colombian government jointly agreed in April 2026 not to publish the potential-production indicator in the June report, judging the existing methodology unsound. Cultivated area and seizures are published and can be cited; production potential currently cannot.
