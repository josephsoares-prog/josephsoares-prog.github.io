Colombia

Last updated: September 17, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. Colombia is five weeks into a new government and one month into recovery from a major earthquake. Abelardo de la Espriella took the oath of office in Cali on 7 August 2026, having won the 21 June runoff narrowly. Three days after the inauguration, a magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck southeast of San José del Palmar, Chocó, at 108 km depth; the national disaster agency counted 331 dead and 4,439 injured. The new government has moved fast on two fronts at once: it has dismantled the negotiated-peace architecture it inherited, and it has deepened the security and resources relationship with Washington, signing memoranda on critical minerals and civil nuclear cooperation with Secretary of State Marco Rubio in Barranquilla on 8 September. Congress, which the government does not control, is the constraint on both.

The security picture

The negotiating tables are closed. Resolutions 390 and 391 of 2026, issued 9 September, revoked the designated delegations for talks with the ELN and with the Ejército Gaitanista de Colombia; earlier decisions had already terminated the tables with the EMBF, the Coordinadora Nacional Ejército Bolivariano and the ACSN. On 13 September the Attorney General's office reactivated three suspended capture orders against Clan del Golfo commanders. The underlying numbers cut both ways. UNODC measured 261,000 hectares of coca at the end of 2024, up 3.5% year on year, with the Pacific region up 14% and Catatumbo up 11%; cocaine seizures rose 19% to 889,201 kg while forced manual eradication fell 54% to 9,403 hectares. Indepaz, counting independently, recorded 89 massacres and 336 victims to 11 September 2026, against 78 and 256 for all of 2025.

Politics

Congress is fragmented and the president does not command it. In the 8 March legislative elections the Pacto Histórico took the largest Senate bloc at 25 seats, ahead of Centro Democrático with 17; allocations below the top two are reported inconsistently and should be treated as contested. When Congress installed on 20 July, Honorio Henríquez of Centro Democrático won the Senate presidency 56 to 45, with Pacto Histórico votes deciding it against the incoming government's preference — an early signal that floor arithmetic, not the presidency, sets the agenda. Institutional friction is now routine: on 14 September the Supreme Court's Special Instruction Chamber opened a preliminary inquiry into the financing of Iván Cepeda's October 2025 primary campaign, after the National Electoral Council had cleared the same contributions in January. Two state bodies, two working positions, no final ruling.

Economy

Growth has accelerated while inflation has moved the wrong way. The statistics agency recorded GDP growth of 3.5% year on year in the second quarter and 2.9% for the first half; annual inflation reached 6.24% in August, against 5.10% a year earlier. Banco de la República holds its policy rate at 12.0%, in force since 1 July after a four-to-three vote to hold on 31 July, and meets again on 30 September. Unemployment was 8.1% in July. Oil output fell 2.46% year on year in July to 727,920 barrels a day and gas fell 8.36%, while exports rose 5.9% in July with fuels and extractives at 40.0% of the total. Two external pressures sit on top of that: a 12.5% US Section 301 tariff on Colombian goods ordered 23 July over forced-labour enforcement, and a current account deficit running at 3.5% of quarterly GDP, cushioned by US$4,131 million in remittances.

What to watch

The 2027 budget: Congress's joint economic committees approved the 634.9 trillion-peso total on 14 September and must approve the bill by 20 October, against an independent fiscal committee projecting a primary deficit of 4.5% of GDP, a total deficit near 10% and net debt near 67.3%. The US Presidential Determination on major drug transit countries for fiscal 2027, which had not appeared as of 14 September — last year's designated Colombia as having failed demonstrably while granting a national-interest waiver. The aerial spraying pilot authorised on 18 August: seven days, 1,000 hectares, glufosinate ammonium rather than glyphosate, with technical reports due within 60 days, and environmental licensing and prior consultation with ethnic communities still required before anything wider. Banco de la República's 30 September rate decision. And El Niño through the 2026–27 season, which the fiscal committee flags as a risk to reduced energy and gas subsidy funding.

Today's cross-source snapshot — 17 September 2026

President Abelardo de la Espriella's campaign strategist now chairs Ecopetrol's board. The board elected Carlos Suárez unanimously on September 17, restructuring itself with six new independent members. It must next select a chief executive to replace outgoing CEO Juan Carlos Hurtado Parra, with another De la Espriella campaign associate, Joaquín Gutiérrez, positioned as the leading candidate.Infobae
A Bogotá criminal court ordered Colombia's Defense Ministry to disclose complete records of President de la Espriella's official flights within 48 hours. The ruling followed a challenge from opposition Pacto Histórico representative José Luis Bohórquez, who found the ministry's initial disclosure incomplete. Court filings cited in the ruling show the president took 103 flights over a 15-day period in August, at a reported cost exceeding 1.1 billion pesos.Infobae
The Ombudsman's Office (Defensoría del Pueblo) issued an early warning for rural Tibú, Norte de Santander, after three separate attacks killed two adolescents and wounded two civilians on September 15. One victim died triggering an antipersonnel landmine while farming; the Defensoría attributes the other's abduction and killing to the ELN. A separate explosive-laden drone attack wounded two civilians in the same municipality.El Tiempo
Armed-group membership in Colombia nearly doubled, from roughly 15,000 to 29,000, during former President Gustavo Petro's Total Peace policy, according to InsightCrime's review of its record. The approach unraveled after the ELN's 2025 offensive in Catatumbo killed close to 100 people and displaced 50,000 residents in 72 hours. President de la Espriella has since ended the ceasefires and negotiation spaces, reviving extradition and a militarized enforcement posture instead.InSight Crime
President de la Espriella says he has signed 85 extradition orders in the first month and a half of his term, a figure his office presents as proof of a harder line on organized crime. He signed the documents personally at the Presidential Legal Office and described a continuous pace. This is a government characterization; the cases still require processing through the Foreign Ministry and Interpol before execution, and that data has not yet been separately reported.Semana
Massacres in Colombia rose 14% in the first eight months of 2026, according to the independent conflict monitor Indepaz, which counted 89 incidents through September 6 against 78 for all of 2025. Indepaz director Leonardo González attributes the rise to territorial disputes among criminal and armed structures rather than confrontation with the state. Valle del Cauca, Catatumbo, Chocó, Nariño and Putumayo account for most of the recorded cases, a trend that runs alongside the government's own security-improvement claims.Semana
A Colombian-born U.S. citizen with direct access to Mar-a-Lago has become a connector between Trump-aligned figures and President de la Espriella's inner circle, according to La Silla Vacía. Andrés DePew, founder of CPAC Colombia, has facilitated introductions including a nominee for Colombia's ambassadorship in Bogotá. The relationships show personal networks, rather than formal diplomatic channels, shaping part of the access to Colombia's foreign and security policy circle.La Silla Vacía

What's circulating — 17 September 2026

@DefensoriaCol and @Indepaz are amplifying alarm over the Tibú violence and the rising massacre count, framing conflict escalation in ELN-contested zones as the new government's most acute internal-security problem, a framing to be checked against the administration's own security-success messaging.
Accounts aligned with @infopresidencia are amplifying President de la Espriella's extradition tally and the regional investment rollout as evidence of early governing success, a self-reported framing that independent outlets have not yet corroborated with agency-level figures.
@insightcrime is circulating its retrospective on the collapse of Total Peace, arguing the government's pivot to militarization repeats a previously failed approach, a position that directly rebuts official claims that a harder security line is already delivering results.
Peace-sector voices aligned with @ideaspaz are drawing attention to the Ecopetrol board reshuffle and the Trump-linked access network as signs that campaign loyalists, rather than technocrats, are consolidating control of state institutions, a framing government supporters are likely to dismiss as ordinary transition politics.
