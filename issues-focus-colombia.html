Regional watch · Country deep dive

Colombia

Last updated: September 16, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. Colombia is five weeks into a new government and one month into recovery from a major earthquake. Abelardo de la Espriella took the oath of office in Cali on 7 August 2026, having won the 21 June runoff narrowly. Three days after the inauguration, a magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck southeast of San José del Palmar, Chocó, at 108 km depth; the national disaster agency counted 331 dead and 4,439 injured. The new government has moved fast on two fronts at once: it has dismantled the negotiated-peace architecture it inherited, and it has deepened the security and resources relationship with Washington, signing memoranda on critical minerals and civil nuclear cooperation with Secretary of State Marco Rubio in Barranquilla on 8 September. Congress, which the government does not control, is the constraint on both.

The security picture

The negotiating tables are closed. Resolutions 390 and 391 of 2026, issued 9 September, revoked the designated delegations for talks with the ELN and with the Ejército Gaitanista de Colombia; earlier decisions had already terminated the tables with the EMBF, the Coordinadora Nacional Ejército Bolivariano and the ACSN. On 13 September the Attorney General's office reactivated three suspended capture orders against Clan del Golfo commanders. The underlying numbers cut both ways. UNODC measured 261,000 hectares of coca at the end of 2024, up 3.5% year on year, with the Pacific region up 14% and Catatumbo up 11%; cocaine seizures rose 19% to 889,201 kg while forced manual eradication fell 54% to 9,403 hectares. Indepaz, counting independently, recorded 89 massacres and 336 victims to 11 September 2026, against 78 and 256 for all of 2025.

Politics

Congress is fragmented and the president does not command it. In the 8 March legislative elections the Pacto Histórico took the largest Senate bloc at 25 seats, ahead of Centro Democrático with 17; allocations below the top two are reported inconsistently and should be treated as contested. When Congress installed on 20 July, Honorio Henríquez of Centro Democrático won the Senate presidency 56 to 45, with Pacto Histórico votes deciding it against the incoming government's preference — an early signal that floor arithmetic, not the presidency, sets the agenda. Institutional friction is now routine: on 14 September the Supreme Court's Special Instruction Chamber opened a preliminary inquiry into the financing of Iván Cepeda's October 2025 primary campaign, after the National Electoral Council had cleared the same contributions in January. Two state bodies, two working positions, no final ruling.

Economy

Growth has accelerated while inflation has moved the wrong way. The statistics agency recorded GDP growth of 3.5% year on year in the second quarter and 2.9% for the first half; annual inflation reached 6.24% in August, against 5.10% a year earlier. Banco de la República holds its policy rate at 12.0%, in force since 1 July after a four-to-three vote to hold on 31 July, and meets again on 30 September. Unemployment was 8.1% in July. Oil output fell 2.46% year on year in July to 727,920 barrels a day and gas fell 8.36%, while exports rose 5.9% in July with fuels and extractives at 40.0% of the total. Two external pressures sit on top of that: a 12.5% US Section 301 tariff on Colombian goods ordered 23 July over forced-labour enforcement, and a current account deficit running at 3.5% of quarterly GDP, cushioned by US$4,131 million in remittances.

What to watch

The 2027 budget: Congress's joint economic committees must fix the ceiling by 15 September and approve the bill by 20 October, against an independent fiscal committee projecting a primary deficit of 4.5% of GDP, a total deficit near 10% and net debt near 67.3%. The US Presidential Determination on major drug transit countries for fiscal 2027, which had not appeared as of 14 September — last year's designated Colombia as having failed demonstrably while granting a national-interest waiver. The aerial spraying pilot authorised on 18 August: seven days, 1,000 hectares, glufosinate ammonium rather than glyphosate, with technical reports due within 60 days, and environmental licensing and prior consultation with ethnic communities still required before anything wider. Banco de la República's 30 September rate decision. And El Niño through the 2026–27 season, which the fiscal committee flags as a risk to reduced energy and gas subsidy funding.

Today's cross-source snapshot — 16 September 2026

President Abelardo de la Espriella removed DANE director Ricardo Valencia on September 14, citing an unauthorized press briefing on unemployment data. Former DANE directors say the move risks breaching Law 2335 of 2023, which shields the statistics agency's technical independence from political pressure.El Tiempo · Cambio
Colombia's congressional economic commissions approved a 634.9 trillion-peso budget for 2027 on September 14, after the Finance Ministry defended its spending and revenue assumptions. Congress president Honorio Henríquez said lawmakers will separately review the decrees issued under the government's economic emergency powers.El Tiempo · Cambio
Former president Gustavo Petro used a September 15 address from Brussels to reject De la Espriella's claim that his government over-indebted the country, arguing external debt fell relative to GDP during his term because borrowing went toward debt service. He said the new 2027 budget would instead push total debt to 66% of GDP, up from the 61% he says his administration reached.El Espectador
De la Espriella's government closed its remaining negotiating tables with armed groups in mid-September and told them to submit to the law or face confrontation with security forces, El Tiempo reported September 14. InSight Crime's September 15 analysis frames this as the formal end of the "Total Paz" framework Petro launched in 2022, and notes it leaves open which armed structures now face renewed offensives.El Tiempo · InSight Crime
De la Espriella's government says it has recorded 18,750 arrests, 1,940 seized firearms and 1,746.5 hectares of coca eradicated since he took office on August 7, a tally he posted after a September 12 arms-cache seizure in Zaragoza, Antioquia attributed to the Clan del Golfo. The figures come from the government's own count; no independent audit of the total was found.Infobae
Colombia's EPS health insurers owed 40.35 trillion pesos against 21.65 trillion pesos in assets, according to data reported to the Health Superintendency and analyzed by the think tank Así Vamos en Salud in a study published September 14. Only 8 of the 23 EPS reviewed still hold positive equity, and insurers already under state intervention alone carry a combined 12.78 trillion-peso deficit.Cambio
Colombia recorded 39 killings of environmental and land defenders in 2025, close to a third of the 124 Global Witness counted worldwide, the London-based watchdog's report found. El Espectador reported September 15 that Colombia has topped Global Witness's ranking for four consecutive years.El Espectador

What's circulating — 16 September 2026

The presidency's own channel is amplifying De la Espriella's security tally — 18,750 arrests and 1,940 firearms seized since August 7 — as evidence the offensive against armed groups is working; the count comes from the government's own reporting and has not been independently audited, so the trend, not the precise total, is what to check.
Current and former DANE officials are pushing back online on the removal of director Ricardo Valencia, framing it as a breach of the 2023 law meant to shield the statistics agency from political interference; the presidency frames the same move as accountability for an unauthorized briefing, so the two readings should be checked against each other.
Peace-monitoring accounts, including Indepaz and Fundación Ideas para la Paz, are the ones to watch as the government's closure of dialogue tables with armed groups gets framed either as firmness toward non-compliant structures or as the collapse of the "Total Paz" framework, a framing dispute InSight Crime's own analysis is also feeding.
The national police account is highlighting specific operational wins — the Zaragoza, Antioquia arms cache linked to the Clan del Golfo and a heroin-and-fentanyl seizure in Nariño — as proof points for the government's offensive; these are worth checking against judicial follow-through, such as formal charges or forfeitures, rather than the raw seizure numbers alone.
