Attritional war

Russia–Ukraine

Last updated: September 11, 2026 · Reviewed and improved in place, not re-published as a new post

Where things stand. The war is in its fifth year, and the front line moves slowly. The Institute for the Study of War (ISW), a Washington research institute, assessed that Russian forces seized about 90 square kilometres in August 2026, against about 505 in August 2025, while Ukrainian forces took back nearly 130. Russian forces are present in most of Kostiantynivka but have not taken it. Moscow states that its forces have made "significant advances". UN monitors verified at least 437 civilians killed in Ukraine in July, the highest monthly toll since May 2022.

The decision point

The live question is whether negotiations restart. On August 28, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said peace talks were on a "deep pause". US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on September 5 and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Kyiv on September 6. Putin says he halted strikes on Kyiv for the visit, and Kyiv offered to spare Moscow. Zelenskyy credited the envoys with "restarting the negotiation process". After a Putin–Trump call on September 8, Putin's aide Yuri Ushakov said three-way talks could resume, possibly in Abu Dhabi. No date has been set.

Who holds the leverage

Territory remains one of the biggest obstacles. Moscow's condition for a ceasefire is that Ukraine cede full control of Donetsk region, which Kyiv has repeatedly rejected. Zelenskyy says "the territorial issue remains a relevant one" and that only leaders can settle it. Moscow says it outlined "tangible actions" Washington could take to end the fighting sooner. Washington has added no new economic pressure: the US Treasury's log shows no new Russia designations since early August, and its October 2025 sanctions on Rosneft and Lukoil remain in place. The EU's 21st sanctions package, adopted July 23, listed 41 more shadow-fleet vessels, and the EU has paid out €11.6 billion of a €90 billion loan for 2026 and 2027. By July 23, allies had contributed $6.7 billion for US-made weapons through PURL, a US–NATO scheme that delivers about 95% of Ukraine's Patriot interceptors. Canada signed a 100-year partnership declaration with Ukraine on September 10. Kyiv's own lever is range: ISW recorded its deepest drone strike yet, nearly 3,000 kilometres from the border.

What to watch

First, whether a three-way round is scheduled. Second, winter: Russia launched at least 429 missiles in July, more than double June's number, and Zelenskyy says Kyiv is counting on "substantial volumes of American LNG". Third, spillover into Europe. Germany's government concluded that Russia was behind an explosive-laden drone that fell near a Ukrainian cargo plane at Leipzig/Halle Airport on August 4, and is closing Russia's consulate general in Bonn. Russia's foreign ministry calls Europe's reaction "a pre-planned escalatory maneuver".
