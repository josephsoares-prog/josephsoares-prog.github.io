A tax credit for travelling in Canada: former president of the Federation of Canadian Municipalities revives the idea ahead of the federal budget

Yves Ducharme explains the reasoning behind his proposal, and the conditions for it to work, in the first interview episode of Corridor Intelligence.

GATINEAU, September 28, 2026 — Yves Ducharme, former mayor of Hull, first mayor of the amalgamated City of Gatineau and past president of the Federation of Canadian Municipalities (FCM), has shared his thinking with the Government of Canada on a tax credit that would encourage Canadians to travel in their own country. He explains the reasoning, and the conditions for it to work, in the first interview episode of Corridor Intelligence, Joseph Soares's analysis program, released today in French.

The idea is not new: Mr. Ducharme had it adopted unanimously by the FCM's general assembly during his presidency. It returns in a different context. "It's a good old new idea that carries even more weight now, given the tariff war with our neighbours to the south," he says.

The proposal requires that the entire stay take place in a province other than one's own or the one next door, over a five-year program. For Mr. Ducharme, the measure rests on two conditions: an amount large enough to change a destination, and absolute simplicity. "If it's too complicated to get your refund, people will give up. It has to be simple."

The interview asks the question Ottawa rarely asks before acting: how would anyone know the measure worked, rather than simply rewarding trips that would have happened anyway? Mr. Soares points to Ontario's 2022 precedent, a similar credit that was never evaluated.

"A good idea that actually gets through is rare. What interests me is what kills it along the way, and what decision-makers can do so that it doesn't," says Joseph Soares.

The episode also covers how Paul Martin freed municipalities from the GST in a single federal decision, and why cities have disappeared from Canadian political discourse. "It's as if we don't exist," the former mayor says.
