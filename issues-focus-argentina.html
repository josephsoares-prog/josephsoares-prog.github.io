Regional watch · Country deep dive

Argentina

Last updated: September 17, 2026 · The Brief is reviewed and improved in place; the Media Monitor and Social Media Monitor below are rebuilt every morning

The Brief

Where things stand. President Javier Milei passed 1,000 days in office on September 4, 2026, with disinflation the government's strongest card and growth the weakest: INDEC's August consumer price index rose 1.7% month-on-month, the slowest pace in 14 months, but private estimates point to a 0.5-1% seasonally adjusted GDP contraction in the second quarter and to poverty rising again ahead of INDEC's official release on September 24. Congress is fighting over two things at once — the 2027 budget the government sent on September 15 and a stalled push to scrap the PASO open primaries — while the IMF sends a mission the week of September 21 to test whether Argentina met this year's reserve targets. Milei has layered a new sovereignty campaign over the Falklands/Malvinas onto that agenda, escalating after Donald Trump questioned US neutrality on the islands, which raises his nationalist profile even as critics, including inside his own coalition, say he is changing the subject from the economy.

Politics

Milei governs with a stronger but still incomplete parliamentary hand than a year ago. La Libertad Avanza won more than 40% of the national vote in the October 2025 midterms and took 15 of Argentina's 24 electoral districts, up from eight in 2023, giving the party roughly a third of both chambers — enough to block override attempts on presidential vetoes but not enough to legislate alone, per analysts cited by Americas Quarterly. That math let the government push through a contentious labor-market overhaul, approved by the Senate in February 2026, and it is the same math the government needs to eliminate the PASO primaries, which requires supermajorities of 37 votes in the Senate and 129 in the Chamber of Deputies. As of the Senate's September 14 session, the government did not have those votes, lacking commitments from the Radical (UCR) bloc and from PRO senators aligned with Patricia Bullrich, the former security minister now a national senator negotiating directly with the chamber's leadership.

The cabinet changed hands mid-year under pressure, not by design. Manuel Adorni resigned as Chief of Cabinet on June 27, 2026, after Congress moved toward an interpellation and censure motion and prosecutors advanced a probe into his personal wealth; Diego Santilli was sworn in June 28 and now leads negotiations with provincial governors. Those talks are strained: non-automatic transfers to the provinces have fallen 66.4% in real terms under the fiscal-adjustment program, and Milei's sister and chief strategist, Karina Milei, has tied continued gubernatorial support for the PASO bill to resolving that transfer dispute, per Infobae's reporting on the September cabinet meetings. Public opinion has cooled: a Hugo Haime & Asociados poll found presidential approval at 33% against 64% disapproval, with about 12% of 2023 Milei voters — termed "repentant libertarians" — now dissatisfied over living standards rather than over Milei personally.

Economy

Disinflation is real and slowing growth is real at the same time. INDEC's monthly CPI fell for three straight months through August 2026 to 1.7% — the lowest since June 2025 — after 1.9% in June and an annual rate of 33.5% that month; the 2027 budget sent to Congress on September 15 projects 21.1% average inflation for next year (18% December to December) against the IMF's more optimistic 15.7%, and 4% growth. Growth has not kept pace: INDEC recorded 4.4% GDP growth for full-year 2025, led by mining, agriculture and financial intermediation, and Q1 2026 beat expectations, but private consultancies — Invecq, FMyA, Romano Group, Equilibra and EcoGo — converge on a 0.5-1% seasonally adjusted GDP contraction for Q2, driven by falling manufacturing, construction, retail and auto sales even as mining rose 10.2% and exports grew 19.6% year-on-year. Poverty tracks the same divergence: INDEC's official rate for the second half of 2025 was 28.2%, and while that remains the last confirmed figure, private projections from ExQuanti and UTDT economist Martín González Rozada put the first-half-2026 rate near 31.6% — about 1.7 million more people below the poverty line — against a government estimate of 30-31%; INDEC's own number is due September 24.

The peso and reserves show a government with room to maneuver but a program still under scrutiny. The wholesale dollar closed at $1,507.50 on September 15, its lowest since August 21, leaving a gap of roughly 25.9% to the exchange-rate band's ceiling — set to rise from $1,919.63 in September to $1,951.50 in October under the CPI-indexed formula — the widest cushion since May 2025. BCRA gross international reserves stood at $50.469 billion as of September 11, up from $41.095 billion at the end of 2025, though the bank's own dollar-purchase pace this month has run roughly 60% below August's, per La Nación. Argentina has drawn about $15.8 billion of the $20 billion IMF Extended Fund Facility approved in April 2025, after the Fund's board cleared a second review on May 21 that released roughly $1 billion despite Argentina missing one reserve-accumulation target — a miss the board waived. An IMF mission is due the week of September 21 for the arrangement's third review, which IMF spokesperson Julie Kozack said will examine reserves, financing risks and the budget. Country risk closed at 506 basis points on September 15, back above 500. Coverage points to global pressure — US Treasury yields near 5% and oil above $100 a barrel — and to local factors, including the 2027 debt-payment calendar and the 2027 election.

Foreign policy

The dominant story is a fast-escalating dispute with the United Kingdom over the Falklands/Malvinas, triggered less by anything Argentina did than by a shift in the US posture that Milei chose to seize on. In a nationally televised address on September 4, Milei declared "winds of change" after Trump told GB News he "always review[s] every position" on US backing for Britain there, and Milei announced sanctions on companies working the Sea Lion offshore oil project — Britain's Rockhopper Exploration and Israel's Navitas Petroleum — plus new funding for a naval base in Tierra del Fuego; Argentina filed criminal complaints against five oil companies on September 10 and is advancing a "Law for the Defense of National Sovereignty" toughening penalties on fishing and hydrocarbon activity on the continental shelf. The UK government, now led by Prime Minister Andy Burnham after Keir Starmer's resignation in July 2026, has not budged: Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband called London's commitment to the islanders' self-determination "unwavering," and on September 14 Defence Minister Luke Pollard confirmed the Falklands garrison will receive upgraded Typhoon Tranche 2 jets — armed with Storm Shadow, Brimstone and Meteor missiles — in a rotation in late 2027, replacing the older aircraft deployed there now. Milei called off a planned late-October visit to the UK on September 10 without public explanation, a sign the diplomatic channel has narrowed even as the rhetorical one has widened.

The government treats the episode as validating its alignment with Washington, and points to Argentina's full repayment, confirmed by US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent in January 2026, of the $20 billion currency swap line the Trump administration extended in 2025, as evidence the relationship pays dividends beyond rhetoric. Independent commentary at home reads the Malvinas push differently: La Libertad Avanza deputy Marcela Pagano publicly called it a "cortina de humo" (smokescreen) for the economy, opposition figures made the identical charge, and Página/12 ran a September 15 piece framing it as a way to avoid discussing the economy, while pro-government outlets treated the same week's events as a sovereignty win. Both readings are political framing rather than settled fact, and both are presented here as such.

What to watch

Four dates converge over the next ten days: the IMF's third-review mission arrives the week of September 21 to test reserves, financing risk and fiscal execution against the EFF's targets; INDEC publishes its official first-half-2026 poverty rate on September 24, the first confirmed number since the 28.2% recorded for the second half of 2025; the Senate's electoral-reform committee keeps negotiating the PASO-elimination and Boleta Única bills, still short of the 37/129 supermajority; and the 2027 budget, submitted September 15, moves to committee in the Chamber of Deputies carrying a 21.1% average inflation assumption, above the IMF's 15.7%. Watch also whether the exchange rate approaches October's band ceiling of $1,951.50 — a test of whether the BCRA needs to intervene for the first time in months — and whether Argentina's sanctions against Falklands oil operators escalate into a broader legal or diplomatic rupture with London.

Today's cross-source snapshot — 17 September 2026

President Javier Milei sent a National Sovereignty Defense bill on the Falklands/Malvinas to Congress on September 17, co-signed by Cabinet Chief Diego Santilli, Foreign Minister Pablo Quirno, Defense Minister Carlos Presti and Economy Minister Luis Caputo. The bill extends resource-extraction bans beyond hydrocarbons — targeting in particular the Sea Lion oil project — creates a presidentially led National Security Council, authorizes tariffs of up to 75% and asset freezes on companies operating in the islands, and sets prison terms of up to 20 years for unauthorized extraction.Infobae · Chequeado
Milei described his reversal on Malvinas policy as pressure to draw corporate investment into the Vaca Muerta oil and gas basin, not an electoral calculation, and criticized journalists who questioned the timing. Independent polling by the Analogías consultancy found 61% of Argentines distrust the president's new position, and Falklands Legislative Assembly president Jack Ford separately attributed the shift to the approach of Argentina's 2027 election cycle.Página/12 · MercoPress · Infobae
The Milei administration's 2027 budget, presented by Economy Minister Luis Caputo, projects 4% GDP growth, 18% inflation from December to December, and an exchange rate of 1,847.60 pesos per dollar by year-end. The Central Bank's own Market Expectations Survey (REM) of private economists is more cautious, putting 2027 growth at 2.9%, inflation at 20.5% and the year-end exchange rate at 2,015.7 pesos per dollar.Infobae · El Cronista
INDEC reported August consumer-price inflation of 1.7%, the lowest monthly reading in 14 months. Milei and Caputo publicized the figure on social media as vindication of the government's disinflation strategy, while the article carrying the data cited no independent economist assessment of the trend's durability.Buenos Aires Herald
Labor Ministry data analyzed by the consulting firm Politikón show private-sector employment fell 1.9% year-on-year through June 2026, a loss of 121,000 jobs. Only the oil-producing provinces of Neuquén and Río Negro have surpassed their November 2023 employment levels, while Buenos Aires province recorded the largest absolute job losses.Buenos Aires Herald
Bloomberg reporting, cited by Infobae and quoting Barclays analyst Iván Stambulsky, said Argentina's strong-peso policy is restraining economic activity because the peso has depreciated only 3.7% year-to-date while consumer prices rose 21.3% over the same period. The analysis pointed to a 4.9% year-on-year manufacturing contraction and a 4.5% decline in construction in August, and noted that private-sector dollar-hedging positions have more than doubled since March to roughly $12 billion.Infobae
Former president Cristina Fernández de Kirchner's defense team asked the UN Human Rights Committee to overturn her Vialidad-case conviction and lift the lifetime ban on holding office that excludes her from Argentina's 2027 presidential race. The petition included a new economic audit by Brazilian economist Antonio Corrêa de Lacerda, which found that companies controlled by Lázaro Báez received only 0.85% of the road-works funds at issue in the case.MercoPress

What's circulating — 17 September 2026

Milei and Caputo used their own accounts to celebrate the August INDEC inflation print as proof the 'peso fuerte' strategy is working, a framing that independent analysis reported by Infobae (via Bloomberg and Barclays) instead casts as a drag on manufacturing and construction output.
As formal co-signatories of the Malvinas sovereignty bill, Milei, Cabinet Chief Santilli and Foreign Minister Quirno are the accounts most likely to promote the measure as a sovereignty and security response, a framing at odds with the electoral-motive reading attributed to the government by the Falklands Legislative Assembly's own president.
Accounts sympathetic to Cristina Fernández de Kirchner, including opposition figures such as Buenos Aires Province Governor Kicillof, are positioned to amplify her defense team's UN Human Rights Committee petition as a due-process and political-rights issue ahead of 2027, a claim to be weighed against the judicial record MercoPress describes.
Independent economic consultancies such as Ecolatina and CEPA are positioned to contest the government's 2027 budget assumptions on growth and inflation, an argument the Central Bank's own REM survey of private economists already substantiates with more cautious figures than Caputo's.
